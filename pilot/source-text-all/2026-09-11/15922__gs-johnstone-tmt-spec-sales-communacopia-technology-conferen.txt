Communacopia + Technology Conference: Day 2 Wrap
Day 2 reinforced that AI is moving from experimentation into enterprise architecture. The clearest themes
were security as the control layer, and the risks agents bring, model optimisation over the last 3 months
has become a way to lower inference costs and protect software margins (SAP and Hubspot noting how
things have changed), and personal agents as a potential new interface layer. The next investor debate is
likely to focus on which platforms become the trusted control points for agents.
Key Themes
• Security: As AI agents move from assistants to systems that can take action, the security perimeter is
expanding from external traffic to internal systems, identity, data access, workflows and systems of record.
The key message from ServiceNow, Databricks, Cloudflare, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and SAP is that
security needs to be embedded directly into the platforms where work happens, with Zero Trust, agent
identity, runtime protection and automated remediation becoming core infrastructure for the agentic
enterprise.
• Model optimisation: A key theme was that companies can increasingly choose the right model for each
use case, combining frontier models with cheaper open-source or specialised models where quality allows.
SAP, HubSpot, ServiceNow, Microsoft and TSMC all pointed to this as a way to lower inference costs,
protect software gross margins and broaden AI adoption. SAP’s comment quite something that Mistral was
around 5x cheaper for HR queries than other Frontier models .
• Personal agents: Consumer and enterprise agents remain a major bull-case debate, potentially turning
each user into a small cloud tenant and driving demand across compute, memory, flash, payments, identity
and permission layers.
• AI demand broadening: The value chain is widening beyond GPUs into leading-edge logic, advanced
packaging, memory, process control, storage, software workflows, payments, edge infrastructure and
security.
Consumer / Media
Spotify was one of the most interesting presentations of the day, with Gustav Soderstrom positioning the
company as an increasingly interactive, AI-led media service rather than a passive music app. The key
message was that Spotify can use AI to make the product more useful and engaging — from asking in-app
questions about artists, tours or credits, to personalised features like Running Mode that can soundtrack
your life — while still offering a lower-regret form of engagement versus social platforms such as Meta or

Snap. Management also sounded confident on pricing, arguing that Spotify is now more expensive than
peers in most markets without seeing meaningful churn, because the value gap remains wide. Video is
becoming more important for discovery and retention, with songs that have video far more likely to be
streamed or saved, while Spotify’s approach to generative music is focused on working with artists and
rights holders so new AI-driven formats can still support royalties.
Hyperscalers
Microsoft – infrastructure and capex: Microsoft struck a confident tone, reinforcing its position at the
centre of the enterprise AI stack across infrastructure, applications, security, coding tools, intelligence and
agents. Azure demand remains supply-constrained, but management pointed to better execution, including
a 50% improvement in dock-to-live times, longer-term power and land planning, and a more diversified
silicon strategy across Cobalt CPUs and Maia accelerators. Capex was framed as demand-led and ROIC-
disciplined, focused on bringing revenue-generating capacity online quickly while preserving balance-
sheet flexibility. Microsoft – model optimisation and monetisation: Microsoft also reinforced the model-
optimisation theme, highlighting access to more than 11,000 frontier, open-source, specialised and internal
models so customers can pick the right model for each use case. This supports the shift from AI
experimentation to ROI-led enterprise adoption and from traditional seat-based licensing toward more
consumption- and outcome-based pricing across GitHub, Microsoft 365, Agent 365 and potentially
Copilot over time.
Read-across: Hyperscaler capex remains demand-led, but the debate is moving toward execution,
utilisation, model choice and monetisation discipline.
Software
ServiceNow was predictably bullish, with Bill McDermott positioning the company as a core “rules and
rails” platform for the agentic enterprise. The message was that the debate is moving beyond “token
maxing” individual components toward secure, scalable workflows built around enterprise context.
ServiceNow’s moat sits in the 100bn workflows and 8tn transactions running through its CMDB, giving
CEOs identity, visibility and control as AI agents begin to act across departments. Management argued this
is hard to replicate with fragmented DIY agent workflows, which can create technical debt, integration
complexity and total cost of ownership 5x to 10x higher than ServiceNow. Security is becoming a more
central differentiator as AI expands the attack surface: ServiceNow integrates with 47+ security tools, has
a security operations business above $1bn, and is extending into identity, asset management and
operational technology. On AI economics, the company remains model-agnostic, using the right LLM for
the right task while its zero-copy data fabric and high-ROI use cases should support token efficiency and
strong monetisation.
Cloudflare came across very positively, with the CFO positioning the company as well placed for the
agentic AI era. The core message was that Cloudflare is already growing at a 36% run-rate, but AI agents
could drive a broader shift toward inference, secure sandboxes, identity, agentic commerce and
consumption-based monetisation. Management argued that Cloudflare’s platform was built early for
inference-led workloads rather than capital-intensive training, giving it structurally lower capital intensity

than hyperscalers. Its developer ecosystem provides an early read on emerging use cases, while
partnerships with Visa, Mastercard, Amex and others point to future opportunities around payments,
wallets, identity and monetisation gateways.
HubSpot argued that AI is reshaping software from feature delivery toward measurable outcomes, with
proprietary customer context becoming the key differentiator. The company is positioning its CRM around
intelligence, action and conversational interfaces, while adapting B2B marketing from an SEO/email-led
playbook to a broader omnichannel model across social, video, podcasts, AEO and deeper personalisation.
SMB customers want simple, fast-to-deploy AI that works with their own data, supported by longer trials,
clearer spend controls and outcome-based credit monetisation. On costs, HubSpot’s internal agentic
platform can switch between LLMs by use case, balancing quality and inference cost; management said
the past three months have materially improved model-cost optimisation options, helped by cheaper and
open-source models with little impact on outcomes.
IBM was focused on execution after its recent guide down. Management said IBM is on track to deliver 4–
5% constant-currency revenue growth in 2026 while increasing free cash flow by around $1bn year on
year. Software should grow 6–8%, led by Red Hat and recurring revenue momentum, despite a spending-
priority shift that weighed on Transaction Processing and Automation. IBM also sees GenAI as a key
value driver for consulting and believes quantum computing could approach $1bn of revenue before 2030.
Read-across: Enterprise AI is shifting from pilots to governed workflows, with customer context, security,
model choice and outcome-based monetisation becoming the main differentiators.
Semicap
ASML remains supported by strong demand visibility and increasingly firm customer commitments.
Bookings now extend into 2028, with most of next year’s production already reserved, suggesting ASML’s
planned capacity ramp is being pulled by real customer demand rather than speculative orders.
Management also uses large down payments to calibrate true demand, giving confidence that build plans
reflect genuine market needs.
AMAT was broadly consistent with prior Q2/GIR takeaways but reinforced a constructive multi-year AI-
driven WFE story. Management expects leading-edge logic, advanced packaging and memory to account
for around 80% of WFE in 2026, with revenue still expected to grow around 40% next year and strength
continuing into 2027/28 given rising orders, backlog and longer-dated visibility. The strongest areas
remain advanced packaging, leading-edge foundry and DRAM/HBM, while Process Control should grow
more than 50% in 2026 as customers focus on yield improvement, performance optimisation and materials
engineering at advanced nodes. AMAT also highlighted solid execution, services growth, margin
expansion and internal AI productivity benefits, with regionalised semiconductor manufacturing adding a
further tailwind to the high-margin services business. Capital allocation remains focused on small strategic
deals and returning excess cash, as large M&A is seen as impractical in the current geopolitical
environment.

Read-across: AI demand is increasingly visible through the semicap supply chain, with leading-edge logic,
advanced packaging, memory and process control all benefiting from stronger multi-year visibility.
TSMC sounded very confident that AI remains an early-stage, multi-year growth driver, with open-source
models seen as a demand accelerator rather than a risk because lower token costs should broaden adoption
across customers and their customers’ customers. Management pointed to very strong customer demand
behind its Arizona expansion, where yields are already comparable to Taiwan, while reiterating that the
most advanced R&D and initial production will stay in Taiwan and that roughly 30% of advanced capacity
could eventually sit outside Taiwan, mostly in Arizona. The broader message was that there are no
shortcuts in leading-edge foundry: TSMC’s advantage rests on technology leadership, manufacturing
execution, scale and customer trust, with competitors still years behind. While cycles remain part of the
industry, management argued the key is getting the mega-trend right, aligning capacity closely with
customers, and continuing to execute on nodes such as N2 and future technology transitions without major
issues.
Semis & Hardware
Dell argued that the AI infrastructure cycle remains in its early innings but is accelerating, with enterprise
adoption moving from productivity gains toward full workflow redesign. That should create durable
demand for compute, storage and networking. Dell’s differentiation sits in its end-to-end portfolio,
hybrid/on-prem AI positioning, supply chain strength and services capability, supported by a $9.5bn AI
server backlog and growing enterprise demand. Management also sees runway from an ageing PC and
server installed base, post-quantum security upgrades and rising storage needs tied to AI workloads. The
key message was that AI demand looks durable rather than cyclical, while infrastructure refresh, services
and capital returns remain underappreciated.
NXP sounded constructive, broadly consistent with GIR’s takeaways. Demand is strengthening across
automotive, industrial and IoT, inventories remain lean, and visibility is improving even though a broad
restocking cycle has not yet fully emerged. Management framed the portfolio around “physical AI” at the
intelligent edge, with software-defined vehicles, industrial robotics and automation supporting longer-term
growth. Pricing was also highlighted as increasingly favourable, with cumulative increases expected to
benefit revenue and gross margin over the coming quarters.
Sandisk reiterated the constructive Q2 message: management remains confident in long-term financial
targets, supported by long-term agreements, share repurchases and efficient manufacturing at low capital
intensity. NAND demand is structurally improving as AI inference lifts storage requirements, with data
centres now representing roughly half of the market. Around one-third of volumes are pre-booked at
attractive gross margins, while HBF is positioned as a future AI inference enabler. The presentation added
little new information but reinforced confidence in sustained demand growth, pricing resilience and
strategic positioning.
Read-across: AI infrastructure demand is broadening into storage, edge compute, networking and physical
AI, supporting a more durable hardware cycle beyond accelerators alone.

Payments
Adyen reiterated its Q2 guide and current strategy, with interim CFO Hwa Tsao emphasising that growth
momentum remains strong. H1 revenue grew 21% in constant currency, largely driven by existing-
customer expansion, reinforcing the land-and-expand model. Product-led growth is becoming more
important through cross-sell, financial services and geographic expansion, while Talon.One and Orb
broaden the opportunity in loyalty and billing. Platforms remain the fastest-growing segment and a key
route to SMEs at scale, while financial products such as issuing, lending, FX and treasury tools should
become more meaningful over time. Competitive positioning sounded strong, with continued share gains
from incumbents, particularly in North America, and OpenAI cited as a new customer. The key now is
stringing together further consistent quarters.
Toast sounded positive, with vertical depth in complex restaurant subsegments continuing to drive share
gains while expansion TAMs scale quickly. Since IPO, Toast has tripled locations to more than 150,000
and grown share from around 4–5% to more than 20%, helped by a localised sales flywheel and focus on
segments such as quick service, hotels and sports entertainment. International, enterprise and retail ARR
should reach around $200m this year, doubling year on year from a small base, with these newer vectors
growing faster and delivering higher ARPU than the core US SMB business at a similar stage. Toast IQ
Grow also looks promising, with early adopters seeing around 48% sales growth and the product on track
to be Toast’s fastest ever to $10m ARR. Notably, Adyen recently won Toast’s US business.
