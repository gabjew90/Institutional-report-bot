ECB Recap—Higher Rates Ahead 
 
 
The Governing Council hiked its policy rate by 25bp to 2.5% at today’s meeting, as 
widely expected. 
The new staff projections showed somewhat higher growth and more persistent inflation 
(Exhibit 1). The projections for core inflation, in particular, were raised by 0.1pp to 2.6% 
in 2027 and by 0.1pp to 2.3% in 2028. At the same time, the staff reiterated that the 
revisions to core inflation were mostly due to indirect (rather than second-round) 
effects, as projections for wage growth were revised only slightly higher. 
 
The staff also updated their milder, adverse and severe scenarios of alternative energy 
price paths. The scenarios were constructed under the same framework as in June (using 
the 25th, 75th and 95th percentile of option-implied distributions for oil and gas prices 
as of August 19th) and with similar assumptions on the strength of direct, indirect and 
second-round effects.  
President Lagarde noted that the decision to hike had been unanimous and once again 
described it as “robust”. This is consistent with our updated policy simulations, which 
show at least two hikes across all scenarios (Exhibit 2). Our simulations point to the 
policy rate reaching 2.75% under the staff’s new baseline, 3% under their adverse 
scenario, and 4% under the severe scenario.

Although neither the Council nor Lagarde gave any details on the path ahead, some 
elements of today’s communication had a hawkish undertone. First, the Council stated 
that inflation would remain “well above target for an extended period”, a formulation 
last used during the 2022-2023 hiking cycle. Second, Lagarde stated that the next move 
was still undecided. Third, she put little emphasis on the downside surprise to inflation in 
August. Fourth, she did not suggest that higher bond yields would substitute for rate 
hikes and noted that estimates of the neutral rate (which the staff estimated to be as 
high as 2.5%) were of “no great importance” for policy decisions. Fourth, 
More Tightening Ahead 
Given the economy’s recent resilience, the renewed spike in energy prices and today’s 
hawkish message, we now expect the Governing Council to hike the deposit rate by a 
further 25bp in December to 2.75%, taking rates into mildly restrictive territory.  
As discussed above, the ECB staff projections—especially if updated with the latest 
energy forwards, which are close to the adverse scenario—would likely require 
tightening beyond this given the projected resilience in growth and persistence in 
inflation pressures. The early Q3 growth data, however, have been softer (with our 
tracking at 0.19%), and we forecast weaker pass-through pressures into underlying 
inflation (given the weaker run-rate of inflation so far) and our commodities team looks 
for some relaxation in energy prices compared to the forwards.  
That said, we see a low hurdle for hikes to 3% with stronger data and see the risks 
skewed in this direction. In particular, a hike is quite possible already in October with 
continued energy price pressures, an upside surprise in the September HICP data, or 
early signs of second-round effects in the ECB’s surveys (such as SAFE or the Corporate 
Telephone survey), especially if stronger US inflation tilts the Fed towards hiking.  
Looking further ahead, we maintain our view that the ECB will normalise the policy rate 
when inflation slows but push back the anticipated timing of cuts to 2027Q4 and 
2028Q1 (from 2027Q2 and 2027Q3 previously). We also nudge up our terminal rate 
forecast to 2.25% (from 2% before) given recent signs of a higher neutral rate.
