ECB review: Further tightening ahead
European economy
 
 Authors: Dean Turner, Economist, UBS AG, UBS AG London Branch; Maelle Quillevere, Economist, UBS AG London Branch; Rochus Baumgartner, Co-
Head CIO Credit, UBS Switzerland AG; Thomas Wacker, CFA, Head CIO Credit, UBS Switzerland AG; Constantin Bolz, CFA, Strategist, UBS Switzerland 
AG; Matthew Gilman, Head, CIO Europe Equity Strategy, UBS Switzerland AG
• What happened? The ECB raised all three policy 
rates by 25bps, taking the deposit rate to 2.5%, in a 
unanimous and widely expected decision.
• What changed? Updated staff projections showed a 
more persistent inflation outlook, with core inflation 
forecast to rise in 2027 and remain above target 
through 2028, leading us to expect another 25bps hike 
in December.
• How to invest? We remain constructive on 
EUR assets: favor a stronger EUR, attractive carry 
opportunities in fixed income and credit, and European 
equities supported by an improving earnings cycle 
despite moderately higher interest rates.
 
Source: UBS
 
The European Central Bank (ECB) raised its three policy 
rates by 25 basis points at its September meeting, 
lifting the deposit rate to 2.5%. The move was widely 
anticipated by markets and aligned with our expectations. 
The more important development came from the latest 
ECB staff projections, which delivered a distinctly hawkish 
message on the inflation outlook.
Most notably, core inflation is now projected to rise 
from 2.5% in 2026 to 2.6% in 2027 before easing only 
gradually to 2.3% in 2028. In other words, underlying 
inflation is expected to become more persistent rather 
than less, remaining above the ECB's 2.0% target 
throughout the forecast horizon. This leads us to conclude 
that the current tightening cycle is not yet complete.
As a result, we now expect the ECB to deliver one further 
25bps rate hike at its December meeting before pausing. 
The risks around that forecast remain skewed toward 
additional tightening in 2027 should inflation prove more 
persistent than currently anticipated.
Hawkish forecasts dominate the 
meeting
Beyond the rate decision itself, President Lagarde offered 
few surprises. She confirmed that the decision to raise 
rates was unanimous and reiterated that there is no 
predetermined path for policy. Future decisions will 
continue to depend on incoming data and the evolving 
inflation outlook.
The latest staff projections suggest that inflation pressures 
will prove more persistent than previously thought. 
Headline inflation is forecast at 3.0% in 2026, 2.5% 
in 2027, and 2.1% in 2028. Compared with the June 
projections, the forecasts for 2027 and 2028 were revised 
up by 0.2 and 0.1 percentage points, respectively, while 
the 2026 estimate was left unchanged.
Core inflation is projected at 2.5%, 2.6%, and 2.3% over 
the same period, while real GDP growth is forecast at 
0.9%, 1.4%, and 1.5% (Fig. 1).

The underlying assumptions are equally noteworthy. These 
incorporate higher energy prices than assumed in June, 
but they also embed a significant tightening in financial 
conditions. Short-term interest rates are assumed to 
approach 3% over the coming two years, while 10-year 
government bond yields are expected to rise toward 4% 
by 2028, around 50 basis points above current market 
levels. Yet despite these assumptions, inflation is still 
projected to remain above target throughout the forecast 
horizon.
This does not necessarily imply that additional hikes are 
unavoidable. However, it does suggest that adopting a 
restrictive policy stance will be necessary and that further 
tightening may ultimately be required to ensure inflation 
returns sustainably to 2%.
Why the ECB can still afford to 
move cautiously
Despite the hawkish projections, we continue to believe 
the ECB is likely to proceed cautiously.
Evidence of broader inflation persistence remains limited. 
Businesses continue to face higher energy and input 
costs, but productivity gains are helping offset some of 
this pressure. As a result, firms have not needed to pass 
through higher costs as aggressively as might otherwise be 
expected.
Tthere is little evidence that second-round effects are 
becoming entrenched. The ECB's wage tracker continues 
to point to negotiated wage growth of around 2.8% in 
early 2027—a pace that remains elevated but is broadly 
consistent with inflation gradually returning toward target. 
Unit labor cost growth has also moderated, while profit 
margins have recovered.
Taken together, the data suggest that underlying inflation 
pressures are proving persistent, but not yet in a manner 
that would require an aggressive tightening cycle.
Our ECB view
The major takeaway from the ECB's September meeting 
is that more tightening will be needed to bring inflation 
back to target. In our view, the ECB is unlikely to rush 
into a series of rapid rate increases, but neither can it 
declare victory on inflation. The latest projections show 
inflation remaining above target even under assumptions 
that already incorporate tighter financial conditions. That 
argues for maintaining a restrictive policy stance and 
supports our expectation of one additional rate hike in 
December.
However, the outlook remains highly sensitive to energy 
developments. A more favourable trajectory for gas, oil, or 
refined fuel prices could quickly reduce inflation pressures 
and lessen the need for further tightening. Equally, a more 
pronounced deterioration in growth would increase the 
attractiveness of a prolonged pause.
Investment implications
Foreign exchange
For currency markets, the policy decision and broadly 
hawkish policy guidance had largely been priced in before 
the meeting. As a result, EURUSD was largely unchanged 
in the hours following the announcement.
President Lagarde appears to have successfully balanced 
the need to preserve optionality while avoiding 
unnecessary market volatility. While near-term currency 
gains may be limited, we believe the prospect of additional 
ECB tightening and a reduced likelihood of policy easing 
should provide support for the euro over the medium 
term.
We therefore remain comfortable with our constructive 
EURUSD outlook and maintain our 1.20 forecast for 2027.
European fixed income
The ECB meeting does little to alter our broader 
convictions across EUR fixed income markets. While we 
now expect one further rate increase, front-end markets 
already discount policy rates reaching around 3.25% 
over the next 12 months. We therefore do not expect a 
substantial repricing beyond current market expectations.
With yield curves positively sloped and all-in yields 
remaining attractive, we continue to see value in 
extending duration through high-quality European 
government bonds. In particular, we favour intermediate- 
and longer-dated German, Dutch, and Austrian 
government bonds.

We also see selective opportunities in French and Italian 
government and agency bonds, where fiscal and political 
uncertainty continue to support attractive risk premia 
relative to fundamentals.
Credit
The credit backdrop remains constructive. Elevated all-in 
yields, resilient corporate balance sheets, and supportive 
technical factors continue to offset relatively rich spread 
valuations. Corporate fundamentals appear sufficiently 
robust to absorb a moderate further tightening in 
monetary policy.
Consistent with our recent "Conviction calls to put cash 
to work" publication, we favour intermediate- to longer-
dated debt issued by high-quality A- and BBB-rated 
borrowers, particularly in defensive sectors such as utilities, 
telecommunications, and energy. We also continue to 
identify opportunities within selected industrial issuers and 
systemically important banks.
Nevertheless, selectivity remains essential. Growth 
uncertainty, inflation developments, geopolitics, trade 
policy, and idiosyncratic corporate events are likely to 
remain important drivers of market dispersion. Within 
higher-yielding segments, we continue to favor issuers 
with strong balance sheets and limited refinancing needs. 
Selected short-call hybrid securities also continue to offer 
an attractive combination of income generation and 
diversification.
European equities
Although higher interest rates can weigh on valuations, 
we do not believe the currently anticipated tightening path 
is sufficient to derail the improving earnings backdrop for 
European equities.
What matters for equities is not simply that rates are 
rising, but why they are rising. When tighter monetary 
policy reflects resilient economic activity and persistent 
demand, stronger earnings growth can more than offset 
the impact of higher discount rates. We believe this 
remains the environment today.
European companies appear to be in the early stages of 
an earnings recovery following three years of stagnation. 
Reflecting this improvement, we recently upgraded our 
earnings growth forecast for this year from 10% to 15%.
Manufacturing indicators have strengthened, corporate 
cost discipline remains evident, and investment linked 
to structural themes—including artificial intelligence, 
electrification, reshoring and defence spending—continues 
to support activity. We therefore expect earnings growth 
to remain the primary driver of European equity returns 
over the coming year.
The principal risk to this view would be a material 
broadening of inflation pressures that forces the ECB into 
a significantly more aggressive tightening cycle. At present, 
we see little evidence that second-round inflation effects 
are becoming entrenched, suggesting such a scenario 
remains unlikely.
Historical experience also offers reassurance. While rate-
hike cycles often generate short-term volatility, equity 
markets have typically resumed their upward trajectory 
as long as tightening occurs alongside a strengthening 
economic backdrop. This continues to support our 
preference for European cyclicals, including information 
technology, industrials, and banks, as well as our broader 
Eurozone equity preference and thematic exposure to 
European Leaders and Swiss Mid Caps.
