1) ADAM CROOK – 2 Mins Read: In to CPI, Thoughts + Risk Views from the Floor
Consensus across the floor, given parameters laid out by the Fed Leadership, sees 25bp or above on Core
CPI as the threshold above which the Fed acts.
Some thoughts from the floor:
Andrew Lavenburg (US Inflation Trading): “We think a 20bp or lower PCE translation will keep the
committee on hold, while a 24bp+ would cement a hike, translating into 23bps for Core PCE.”
Brian Bingham (Short Macro Trading): “The market appears to be penciling the over/under at 25, but we
struggle to see a meaningful rally on an in-line 20bp core print following this week’s jobs report; in a
world where the meeting goes in pricing greater than 50% chance of a hike, the risk of the bond market
interpreting a hold as a policy error seem far greater than the harm of hiking into above-target inflation.”
Anshul Sehgal (Co-Head of FICC): “We've got two starkly different reads from Chairman Warsh and
Governor Waller… So it's a bit of a coin-toss in terms of whether they hike this cycle or not… We don't
have a strong opinion on that, whether they need to hike in the next few months…. A lot of it comes down
to what happens to energy prices, what happens with geopolitics…. As Chairman Warsh said, there are
parts of the economy that are clearly in recession, housing, for example, and there are parts of the
economy that are booming, AI CapEx, for example.. . We don't expect the Fed to engage in a full blown
hiking cycle… The 1y1y rate at 435 basis points is pricing in about two and a half hikes this cycle….
Sounds about right…”
Alan Stewart (Head of EMEA EM Trading and Head of G10 Spot Trading): “We think that a material
deviation away from consensus expectations from this week’s US inflation data looks unlikely, but any
material beat, particularly in core CPI, is likely to see the market price close to a full hike for next week’s
FOMC. Inevitably this would feed through into initial USD strength, especially given the steady
accumulation in dollar shorts over the last couple of weeks, but without an accompanying hawkish
forward guidance suggesting a cycle beyond the 2.5 hikes that are currently priced out of next week’s FED
meeting, we would expect any such USD strength to prove to prove short lived.”
We feel the greater asymmetry is to a dovish reprice/reaction in the event of a CPI miss, or even an in-line
release. The speed with which the market was willing to fade what was an unquestionably strong

dollar. Any notable negative deviation in CPI should eliminate the chance of a FED hike next week, and
even an in line outcome, whilst only likely to bring market pricing back to 40%-50% chance of a 25bp
hike, will in our opinion be sufficient to keep the FOMC on hold.”

2) EUROPEAN ECON (STEHN/ STOTT) – ECB Preview
GS Econ expects the ECB to hike by 25bp today, as widely expected and fully priced.
ALEXANDRE STOTT (EUROPEAN ECON): I think most people struggle to see how Lagarde could
beat market expectations, and yet there is no courage, no willingness to go out and, for instance, for rates
clients to receive rates next year or even try to play for cuts after that… even though the logic is that the
higher you hike the more likely it is that at some point you have to reverse that with some cuts.
I think there's just a little bit of concern that you're just taking a view on energy. The trades that some
clients in rates have been interested in is trying to price out some of the non-projections meetings. So far
the Governing Council has shown a clear preference for moving not in April but in June, not in July but in
September, and so people think that the bar for them to step up the pace and hike back-to-back, for
instance in October, is actually quite high. And instead that the more likely next move would be in
December.
On the press conference: we think she'll acknowledge the better inflation numbers we got over the summer
but then contrast that with the renewed increase in energy prices. On the policy path ahead, we think the
play is for her to say very little, keep her options open. Back in June, for instance, she said something of
the sort as the rate path will be what it will be. So sometimes that's as much as she's willing to give away.
We think this template should also apply, but the risks are probably that she says something a bit more
explicit. In the past for instance she stressed that the projections were conditioned on X amount of hikes.
This time around it would be 3 or 4 hikes, so that's something she could highlight. And in the past she also
went out of her way to say that the markets read their reaction function very well.
On staff projections: We think they'll show slightly higher growth and also somewhat higher inflation next
year. We have the staff raising the headline inflation projections by 4/10 in 2027 from 2.3% to 2.7%. The
feedback from clients so far is that this is at the hawkish end of expectations…

KAREN FISHMAN (FX STRATEGY): we are watching monthly flows from trust accounts and
investment management companies as the cleanest read on Japanese portfolio shifts — it captures most
