The S&P 500 earnings revision ratio has remained positive for four consecutive months, its longest streak since 2021. The ratio 
measures net revisions of constituent EPS estimates, calculated as: increases minus decreases, divided by total revisions. The positive 
ratio suggests that analysts remain constructive. Consensus currently expects 15% EPS growth in 2027. While no forecast is perfect, 
even a hypothetical one-third reduction in that growth rate would still leave earnings growth well above the long-term average of ~7%.
Friday, September 25, 2026
After rising 6.6% YTD and peaking on Sept. 3, the S&P 500 Financials sector slipped into negative territory this week. Even so, we're not 
ready to overweight the sector. Why? First, a flatter yield curve may pressure banks' net interest margins. Second, while IPO activity is 
rebounding, brokerages continue to face SPAC-related headwinds. Third, property insurers remain exposed to risks in high-cost states 
like California and Florida. Finally, despite the pullback, the sector still trades at 2.3 times book value, above its long-term average.
