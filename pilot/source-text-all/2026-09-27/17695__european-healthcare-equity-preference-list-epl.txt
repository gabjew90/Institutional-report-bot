Health care positioning

stocks in 2025, we believe the policy overhang has now largely receded, following
MFN pricing agreements reached between the US administration and major
European pharmaceutical companies in 4Q25. The MSCI European Healthcare

discount to its historical relative valuation versus broader markets. We believe the

Health care sector fundamentals
European health care fundamentals remain broadly resilient, supported by new
product launches and limited near-term patent-expiry risk. We expect mid-single-
digit sector earnings growth in FY26, followed by high-single-digit growth in
FY27. Looking ahead, we believe investor focus will increasingly shift back to
company fundamentals, with further narrowing of the sector’s valuation gap likely
to depend on pipeline delivery. Positive readouts could therefore provide
significant re-rating potential for the sector.
Strong 2Q26 earnings season
Most European pharma companies delivered solid results with several companies
raising their FY26 guidance, supporting upward revisions to earnings estimates.
Medtech performance was more mixed. While fundamentals were broadly stable,
valuations and investor sentiment continued to lag those of pharma companies,
reflecting concerns about procedure-volume recovery and visibility into 2027
growth.
Obesity opportunity still in focus
We remain constructive on the long-term opportunity in obesity treatment, as
access to effective therapies remains significantly underpenetrated and both Novo
Nordisk and Eli Lilly are well positioned to benefit from secular growth. We view
the MFN agreements on GLP-1 pricing as a significant long-term positive, as we
expect near-term net price reductions to be more than offset by the substantial
Medicare volume opportunity following the expansion of obesity-treatment
reimbursement from 1 July 2026. The launch of oral GLP-1 therapies should
further broaden the obesity-treatment market. We see Novo Nordisk’s oral
Wegovy as an underappreciated opportunity: its early-2026 launch has so far
exceeded expectations, supported by first-mover advantage and a strong clinical
profile.
Health care top picks
Relative to the MSCI Europe Health Care benchmark, our pharma top picks are
Galderma and UCB. For Galderma, we like the strength in its injectable aesthetics
business and Nemluvio, which continues to surprise to the upside. For UCB, we
continue to see upside to consensus forecasts from Bimzelx contributing to above-
sector earnings growth.
Sustainability considerations
The main sustainable investing topics for the health care sector relate to social
issues in the products and services category, particularly access to care and product
liabilities.

Alcon Inc
CH0432492467
Most Preferred
Weight: 3.5%
Added on 2025-03-26
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is Most Preferred
Alcon is a high-quality European medtech large cap with an industry-
leading position in ophthalmology. While the stock has faced a heavy
share price correction due to disappointing earnings and guidance
cuts, we still believe that Alcon offers a broad product pipeline that
should drive above-market growth going forward. However, we
maintain its weight close to its weight in the MSCI European health
care benchmark given the prospect for near-term volatility.
Company profile
Alcon designs, manufactures and distributes a full suite of eyecare
products within two businesses: Surgical (57% of sales) consists of
implantable products (intra-ocular lenses), consumables (surgical kits)
and equipment (lasers). Vision Care (43% of sales) includes contact
lenses and ocular health (over-the-counter eye drugs). Alcon is the
number one and number two global player in the surgical and vision
care markets, respectively. Alcon is headquartered in Geneva,
Switzerland.
What drives our opinion
Alcon holds leading positions in two attractive ophthalmology
markets, both of which should enjoy mid-single-digit percentage
demand growth over the next few years. It dominates the global
market for intra-ocular lenses, which are used to treat cataracts, and
is a major player in contact lenses. After its investments peaked in
2018—around the time of the spin-off out from Novartis—the
company is now benefiting from a series of product launches, which
should help to gain market share and steadily boost its operating
margin. Key recently launched new products include Vivity intraocular
lenses in China, its Unity vitreoretinal cataract system, and Precision7
soft contact lenses. This has also been followed by launches of the
Belkin Vision glaucoma laser device as well as the Tryptyr for dry eye.
Also, Alcon has low financial gearing.
Key risks include failure of pipeline products; new or tougher
competition; legal threats including product liability cases; access,
reimbursement and pricing challenges; a shifting political and
regulatory environment; currency and interest rates. Alcon-specific
risks include inability to trade patients up to multifocal intra-ocular
lenses and further intangible asset impairments.

Argenx SE
NL0010832176
Most Preferred

Why it is Most Preferred
Argenx offers a compelling investment case through Vyvgart’s
potential to become a multi-blockbuster sales opportunity across
multiple autoimmune indications. We believe strong execution, rising
profitability and substantial cash generation support the potential for
sustained double-digit growth at an undemanding valuation.
Company profile
Argenx is a European biotech company headquartered in the
Netherlands. The company’s lead asset is anti-FcRn biologic drug
Vyvgart for a range of autoimmune rare diseases. Vyvgart is approved
in myasthenia gravis with multiple trials in other indications.
What drives our opinion
Argenx is one of the highest-quality growth stories in European
biotech, in our view. The company’s strong growth outlook is
anchored by the rapid expansion of Vyvgart across multiple
autoimmune indications, with the potential to become a multi-
blockbuster sales franchise, while an increasingly diversified pipeline
offers additional long-term growth opportunities. The recent positive
Phase 3 myositis data further strengthens Vyvgart’s potential in
rheumatology and expands its addressable market. Supported by
strong commercial execution, rising profitability and substantial cash
generation, Argenx has the potential to evolve from a successful
FcRn-focused company into a durable immunology compounder
capable of delivering sustained double-digit growth over the coming
years. Meanwhile, the acquisition of Forte Biosciences broadens
Argenx’s exposure to larger immunology and inflammation
opportunities. Upcoming Phase 3 data for empasiprubart in
multifocal motor neuropathy, expected in 4Q26, will be an important
catalyst which could help demonstrate that Argenx is developing a
broader multi-franchise platform rather than remaining reliant on a
single product.
Key risks facing biotech companies include failure of pipeline
products or any regulatory setbacks, access, reimbursement and
pricing challenges, new competition, legal threats including product
liability cases, a shifting political and regulatory environment,
currency, and interest rates. Argenx specific risks include: 1) greater-
than-expected competition from other FcRn inhibitors and novel
immunology therapies 2) execution risk associated with expanding
Vyvgart across multiple indications and geographies.

Why it is Most Preferred
AZN is an innovation leader with underappreciated pipeline
optionality, in our view. On a 12-month forward P/E multiple, the
stock is trading well below its 10-year average of 18x. However, we
think the stock should trade at a higher multiple given the above-
sector earnings growth momentum (13% 2026-29 CAGR vs. 9% for
sector) and potential upside risks from upcoming pipeline readouts.
Company profile
AstraZeneca is a UK-based global pharmaceutical company. Its main
disease areas are oncology, biopharmaceuticals (focusing on
cardiovascular, renal and metabolism, respiratory and immunology)
and rare diseases (via the Alexion acquisition). AstraZeneca invests
heavily in research and development, and strives to be a first mover in
next-generation therapeutics and modalities. AstraZeneca is one of
the largest international pharma companies operating in China.
What drives our opinion
AstraZeneca is a pure-play pharma company delivering above-average
top-line growth and significant margin expansion driven by its
innovative and rejuvenated portfolio. The key drivers of growth are
the high-margin oncology assets (Tagrisso, Imfinzi, Calquence,
Enhertu), which continue to gain market share in their respective
therapeutic areas and require little incremental investment. Relative to
its large-cap pharma peers, we believe AstraZeneca offers one of the
strongest R&D pipelines. Among its pipeline offering, AZN offers
exposure to antibody drug conjugates for cancer treatment and next-
generation immuno-oncology assets, which could unlock multi-billion
dollar sales opportunities, in our view. Looking over the next 12-18
months, we see a rich line up of high-value Phase 3 pipeline
readouts, which, if successful, could trigger upwards earnings
revisions, in our view. These assets combined target a multi-billion
dollar peak sales potential, which in the event of trial success could
drive increased confidence in reaching its USD 80bn revenue ambition
by 2030 (2025e sales: about USD 59bn).
Key risks facing biopharma companies include access, reimbursement
and pricing challenges, new competition including generic drugs,
legal threats including product liability cases, a shifting political and
regulatory environment, failure of pipeline products, currency, and
interest rates. AstraZeneca-specific risks include: 1) a slowdown in the
Chinese business (13% of revenues) driven by government
investigations; 2) greater impact of US Part D redesign and IRA vs.
peers given its US oral oncology portfolio (Calquence, Lynparza,
Tagrisso); 3) more onerous volume-based procurement and related
sales pressure; 4) new product launches particularly in oncology could
also miss market expectations, driven by increased competition; 5)
and greater-than-expected sales erosion following patent expirations.

Why it is Most Preferred
Bayer offers an attractive rerating opportunity as improving pharma
and crop science fundamentals are overshadowed by glyphosate
litigation concerns, in our view. Resolution of this overhang,
alongside continued execution, could unlock the company’s
underlying earnings power and drive further upside.
Company profile
Bayer is a diversified life sciences company, based in Germany. Key
pharma franchises are women’s health, cardiovascular, imaging
agents and specialty/oncology. Consumer Health is one of the leading
OTC businesses globally, and includes dermatology, nutritionals and
GI. Following the 2018 acquisition of Monsanto, Crop Science
comprises seeds/germline and crop protection chemicals. Glyphosate
and PCB litigation overhangs remain but are in the process of
resolution.
What drives our opinion
Bayer offers an attractive rerating opportunity as investors continue to
heavily discount the shares for glyphosate litigation risk despite
improving fundamentals across both its Pharmaceuticals and Crop
Science businesses. The current valuation appears to underappreciate
the company’s underlying earnings power and the progress being
made across its key operating divisions. In Pharmaceuticals, Bayer is
building a more durable growth profile through strong momentum
from Nubeqa and Kerendia, which are increasingly offsetting the
impact of legacy product declines. In addition, pipeline opportunities,
including asundexian and further label expansions for existing growth
products, have the potential to support medium-term revenue
growth and narrow Bayer’s valuation discount relative to European
pharmaceutical peers. Crop Science is also showing encouraging
signs of operational improvement. Management continues to reaffirm
its five-year strategy, focused on driving profitable growth through
innovation in seeds, traits and crop protection. Improved execution,
coupled with productivity measures, should support margin
expansion, stronger cash generation and a more resilient earnings
profile over time. Importantly, successful resolution of glyphosate
litigation could remove the group’s largest valuation overhang,
allowing investors to focus on underlying earnings power and
supporting a meaningful rerating from current levels. Key Risks: The
primary risk remains glyphosate litigation, where adverse legal
outcomes, settlement delays or higher-than-expected liabilities. Key
risks facing the Pharma business include access, reimbursement and
pricing challenges, new competition including generic drugs, legal
threats including product liability cases, a shifting political and
regulatory environment, failure of pipeline products, currency, and
interest rates. Key risks facing the Crop business include weaker-than-
expected adoption of new seed and crop-protection products,
agricultural-cycle and weather-related volatility, intensifying
competition, regulatory scrutiny and litigation, and failure to deliver
targeted margin and cash-flow improvements.

Why it is Most Preferred
EssilorLuxottica is the clear leader in eyewear and eyecare, leveraging
its scale and vertical integration to drive unmatched investment and
sustained market share gains. Its multi-pronged strategy supports
strong double-digit growth and ongoing transformation. While the
stock is trading at a premium valuation to its medtech peers, we
believe its superior earnings outlook justifies an even higher
valuation.
Company profile
EssilorLuxottica is a fully integrated player in the design, manufacture
and distribution of ophthalmic lenses, frames and sunglasses. As of
FY 22, 47% of sales are generated from North America, and the
company continues to expand through investments in Europe and
fast-growing markets. The company has two divisions: Professional
Solutions (48% of FY 22 sales) and Direct to Consumer (52% of FY
22 sales). Having closed its own merger in October 2018, the
company acquired GrandVision, the largest optical retailer in Europe,
which has been consolidated since 1 July 2021.
What drives our opinion
We favor EssilorLuxottica for its transformation from a traditional
eyewear leader into a diversified healthtech innovator. The
management is actively investing in three high-potential growth
areas: smartglasses, hearing aid eyewear, and myopia management.
These ventures align with emerging consumer needs, such as digital
integration, aging populations, and preventive eye-care, while
leveraging the company’s brand strength, global retail footprint, and
expertise in optics.  Its distribution advantage, combined with its
innovation pipeline and willingness to disrupt its legacy business,
supports a robust and defensible growth outlook over the next
decade, in our view. Key risks include tighter competition and slower
consumer adoption in its growth areas.

Why it is Most Preferred
Fresenius is becoming a simpler, higher-quality healthcare company,
focused on the improving Kabi and Helios businesses while exiting
non-core assets. Kabi’s fast-growing Biopharma platform and Helios’
scale and efficiency provide catalysts for sustained revenue growth,
margin expansion and stronger cash generation. With the turnaround
gaining traction and the portfolio transformation progressing, we
believe the stock’s discounted valuation (vs. 10-year history of 13x)
does not yet reflect the group’s improving earnings quality.
Company profile
Fresenius is a diversified healthcare conglomerate. Its business
segment Helios operates hospitals in Germany and Spain. Kabi
provides infusion and clinical nutrition products, as well as sterile
injectable drugs. Fresenius Medical Care is separately listed, and
provides services and products related to dialysis care.
What drives our opinion
Fresenius is becoming a more focused health care company centered
on Kabi and Helios, with the planned reduction of its stake in
Fresenius Medical Care simplifying the group and sharpening capital
allocation. Kabi’s Biopharma business is emerging as a key growth
and margin driver, supported by commercial successes, new contract
wins and a growing biosimilar pipeline. As products scale and launch
costs normalize, Biopharma should provide meaningful operating
leverage and support Kabi’s ambition of reaching a 20% EBIT margin
by 2030. Helios’ scale, efficiency, and quality-focus position it to
benefit from industry consolidation and evolving reimbursement. We
believe the market is not yet pricing in the earnings and margin
potential of the simplified group, leaving room for a sustainable re-
rating as the Kabi and Helios turnarounds gain traction. Key risks
include: Integration risks from recent acquisitions. Given the
company’s leverage, rising interest rates could have an adverse
impact on earnings. The group is exposed to FX risks, and its
operations in emerging markets carry political risks. Kabi is exposed
to product innovations from competitors and potential failure to
obtain regulatory approval for its own products. The group’s venture
into the biotech space could prove unsuccessful. Increasing energy
costs and food prices could also constitute a risk to earnings.
Increasing prices for hospitals could limit its opportunities to acquire
hospitals, or create a risk of overpaying in a transaction.

Why it is Most Preferred
We believe Galderma stands out as a compelling buy due to its strong
commercial execution and clear path to margin expansion. The stock
is trading at around 36x on a 12-month forward P/E multiple, a
significant premium compared to European mid-cap pharma peers at
18x, reflecting its superior earnings growth outlook (30% CAGR for
2026-29 versus 13% for peers). Nevertheless, we believe an even
higher valuation premium is justified, given the sustainability of
Galderma’s franchise.
Company profile
Galderma is a dermatology-focused global biopharma and consumer
company, headquartered in Switzerland. Founded in 1981 as a JV
between L’Oréal and Nestlé, it was acquired by private equity in
2019. Galderma IPOed on the SIX Swiss Exchange in March 2024.
Key brands include Dysport, Restylane and Sculptra in injectable
aesthetics, and Cetaphil and Alastin in dermatological skincare.
Galderma also provides prescription and OTC therapeutics for acne
and rosacea. In prescription dermatology, Nemluvio (nemolizumab) is
approved in the US and the EU for atopic dermatitis and prurigo
nodularis.
What drives our opinion
Galderma is a Swiss pure-play dermatology-focused pharma. Since its
IPO in 2024, the company has demonstrated robust growth,
particularly in high-potential areas such as Injectable Aesthetics
(neuromodulators and fillers) and Therapeutic Dermatology, fueled by
the successful ramp-up of new products such as Nemluvio (atopic
dermartitis, prurigo nodularis). Its differentiated dermatology platform
and resilient business provide a solid foundation for continued
compounding growth. Despite strong share price performance, we
believe the market has yet to fully appreciate the scale of Galderma’s
transformation and long-term earnings potential. With blockbuster
biologic launches on the horizon, sustained double-digit top-line
growth, and significant EBITDA margin expansion expected, we
believe Galderma is well positioned to deliver attractive returns. Its
unique profile, which combines the strengths of both pharma and
consumer sectors with less exposure to binary risks, justifies further
valuation upside, in our view. Backed by a proven management team
and the flexibility to pursue targeted innovation, we think Galderma
offers an attractive long-term growth story.
Key risks facing biopharma companies include access, reimbursement
and pricing challenges, new competition including generic drugs,
legal threats including product liability cases, a shifting political and
regulatory environment, failure of pipeline products, currency, and
interest rates. Galderma-specific risks include: 1) slower-than-
expected launch of Nemluvio given US payer coverage hurdles from
leading players in atopic dermatitis, and 2) an economic slowdown or
out-of-pocket consumer pressures could also see a slowdown in the
self-pay markets.

Why it is Most Preferred
We believe Lonza is well placed to benefit from secular tailwinds
driven by increasing penetration of biologic drugs and uptake of new
novel modalities, which we believe should drive superior earnings
growth among the life sciences businesses over the long term. The
stock is trading at around 29x on a 12-month forward P/E multiple
and below its CDMO peers’ average, which we view to be
undemanding for a broadly in-line earnings growth outlook relative
to its peers.
Company profile
Lonza’s core businesses are: (1) Biologics CMDO (c45% of sales), i.e.
contract manufacturing of large molecules, such as monoclonal
antibodies and antibody drug conjugates; (2) Small molecules CDMO
(c15%), i.e. contract manufacturing of active pharmaceutical
ingredients; (3) Capsules and Health Ingredients (c25%), which
includes hard capsules for pharmaceutical (2/3) and nutritional
applications (1/3); and (4) Cell & Gene Therapy and Bioscience.
What drives our opinion
Lonza is the world’s largest contract drug manufacturer, with leading
positions in small molecules, biologics, and the emerging cell and
gene therapy space. We expect high-single-digit industry growth
rates over the coming years, given resilient demand and persistently
tight capacity. Lonza is particularly well positioned to benefit from US
demand due to its acquired Vacaville plant in California. Sales growth
prospects are very visible thanks to take-or-pay contracts and the
critical nature of Lonza’s services. While the production
discontinuation of COVID vaccines and the slowdown in biotech
financing pressured utilization rates and margins (2023-24), we
expect the latter to gradually recover in 2026/27. Key risks facing
healthcare companies such as Lonza include access, reimbursement
and pricing challenges, a shifting political and regulatory
environment, failure of pipeline products, new competition, legal
threats including product liability cases, currency, and interest rates.
Slower pharma/biotech R&D spending and lumpy order-book trends
are additional risks.

Why it is Most Preferred
With the US genericization of Entresto largely behind us, we expect
earnings momentum to improve from mid-2026, supported by key
growth brands and new launches. Remibrutinib’s positive Phase 3
results reinforce the medium-term outlook despite the pipeline
setbacks for pelacarsen and del-desiran. Following Novartis’s rerating
toward its 10-year average P/E of 15x, we remain constructive on
earnings growth but see a more balanced risk-reward at current
levels.
Company profile
Novartis is a global pharmaceutical company headquartered in
Switzerland. Following the spin-off of its eye care business, Alcon, in
April 2019, and generics unit, Sandoz, in October 2023, Novartis is a
pure-play innovative medicines business focused on oncology,
neuroscience, immunology and cardiovascular conditions. It has
invested heavily in novel therapeutic platforms, including radioligand
therapeutics, gene therapy and RNA therapeutics.
What drives our opinion
Novartis’ medium-term sales target (5-6% CAGR 2025-30) is one of
the lowest-risk amongst the large-cap peer group. A better
operational and pipeline performance in 2023-25 has improved the
outlook, with growth now mostly driven by approved products with
relatively low clinical and regulatory risk, including Kesimpta (multiple
sclerosis), Pluvicto (prostate cancer), Kisqali (breast cancer), and
Scemblix (blood cancer). To help offset competition such as from
Entresto generics (in 2025) and Cosentyx biosimilars (in 2029),
Novartis has a broad product development pipeline. It has also
invested in new modalities, such as radiopharmaceuticals and
cell/gene therapy, which may have lower generic risk than traditional
drugs. Cash generation is strong and the balance sheet robust.
Key risks facing biopharma companies include access,
reimbursement, and pricing challenges; new competition including
generic drugs; legal threats including product liability cases; a shifting
political and regulatory environment; failure of pipeline products; and
currency and interest rates. Novartis-specific risks include 1) worse
commercial success for key product assets; 2) more aggressive generic
erosion for Entresto and other drugs that are facing loss of exclusivity
in the coming years; 3) price/volume competition for Cosentyx given
competitor new launches; and 4) reduced expectations for
cardiovascular launches (e.g., Leqvio and pelacarsen); 5) the inability
for the company to reach its medium-term margin targets.

increasing competition and a pipeline setback. However, we still view
Novo Nordisk as one of the two clear leaders in a fast-growing
obesity market with strong brand equity and significant cash
generation. On a 12-month forward P/E multiple, Novo Nordisk is
trading around 14x, which is well below its 10-year P/E average of
24x. We still believe Novo should be part of a diversified health-care-
focused portfolio as any incremental positive news flow could drive a
share price rebound.
Company profile
Novo Nordisk is a global pharmaceutical company headquartered in
Denmark. It specialises in diabetes and obesity, and is a leading global
provider of GLP-1 analogues and insulin. Novo Nordisk’s portfolio is
dominated by peptide therapeutics and it is a pioneer in oral peptide
technology, used in the Wegovy pill. Other key areas are rare
diseases, focused on haemophilia and blood disorders.
What drives our opinion
Novo has strong presence in the fast growing GLP-1 market for
treating diabetes and obesity. Near-term growth will likely be driven
by Wegovy (Semaglutide for ‎obesity), which has faced supply
constraints in the past due to very strong consumer demand; we
expect production capacities to ‎increase significantly. Medium-term
earnings should be driven by next-generation obesity assets including
oral Wegovy (received US approval on 22 December 2025) and
‎CagriSema, a combination of semaglutide with an amylin receptor.
The clinical data from CagriSema came below market expectations—
it had a broadly similar weight loss profile to Eli ‎Lilly’s tirzepatide.
However, we believe CargriSema still provides another product option
for high BMI patients; the drug was filed for approval in late 2025,
and the US approval decision is expected in late 2026.
With the recent Most Favored Nation (MFN) drug pricing agreement
for its GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic and Wegovy) in November 2025—
where Novo has traded lower GLP-1 prices for broader Medicare
access—we view this as a significant long-term positive given the
major potential volume unlock. Furthermore, with lower GLP-1 prices
even in the cash channel, we think adoption of GLP-1 drugs in the
out-of-pocket (cash) channel will accelerate further from 2026
onwards, driving out the use of compounded GLP-1s.
With the share price now near trough valuations and its historical
premium to European large-cap pharma fully wiped out, we think the
market may now be pricing in an overly conservative growth outlook.
Ultimately, we do see Novo shares rerating over the medium term,
though we believe a recovery may not materialize until there is
greater visibility of a meaningful prescription turnaround for Wegovy
and the company rebuilds its R&D pipeline. That said, we believe the
market may be underappreciating the potential upside risk from the
launch of oral Wegovy, which has first-mover advantage relative to its
competitor. Furthermore, as Novo failed to demonstrate non-
inferiority for CagriSema compared to tirzepatide in the Phase 3
(REDEFINE-4) head-to-head study, we believe that, at current
discounted valuations, the market is assigning close to zero value to
CagriSema. The next key catalyst for Novo is the US approval decision
for CagriSema in late 2026.
Key risks facing biopharma companies include failure of pipeline
products; new competition including generic drugs; legal ‎threats
including product liability cases; access, reimbursement and pricing
challenges; a shifting political and regulatory ‎environment; and
currency and interest rates. Given Novo’s reliance on GLP-1 drugs for
growth, any slowdown in their ‎prescription volumes, significant price
cuts, or further supply challenges would be a risk to earnings
forecasts. An additional ‎risk is adverse clinical data for follow-on
obesity drugs such as CagriSema.
