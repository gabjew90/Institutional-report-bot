U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.75% | Nasdaq -1.13% | Dow -0.68%
Synopsis

US equities finished lower on Wednesday, not far from their worst levels, although the S&P 500 and Nasdaq held on to week-to-date
gains; the Russell 2000 fell 1.28%. The bond yield backup and further bearish flattening were the main story: the 2Y yield rose above
4.90%, its highest in more than two years, the 10Y moved back through 5.10% to new post-2007 highs, and the 30Y briefly traded at its
highest yield since 2004. The move was triggered by hotter September flash PMIs, the highest since 2022 with heightened price pres-
sures, by continued hawkish-leaning Fedspeak and by a very weak 5Y auction. Small caps and precious metals were among the worst
performers on the rate backup, and semis and memory were also weaker; other laggards included homebuilders, China tech, biotech,
airlines, auto retailers, managed care, HPCs and cruise lines, while big tech was mostly down. Energy, software, cybersecurity, networking
and communications equipment, machinery, HVAC, multis, exchanges, rails, hotels and quantum computing outperformed. The dollar
index rose 0.5%, gold finished down 1.3%, silver fell 2.4% and bitcoin futures were down 2%. WTI crude settled up 1.8%, off its best levels,
after a five-day losing streak, as Brent rebounded from declines driven by better Saudi export and Strait of Hormuz transit headlines;

Macro

Treasury selloff and hawkish Fed pricing: Rates rose sharply, with the 2Y yield above 4.90%, the highest since Jun-24, and the 10Y above
5.10%, the highest since Jul-07. Treasury's $70B 5-year auction tailed by 3.1 bp, with bid-to-cover and foreign interest both below six-
month averages, following Tuesday's lackluster 2Y auction; the auction produced the highest 5Y yield since 2006 and the second-largest
tail since 2018. Front-end pricing implies a 64% chance of an October hike, up 9 pp d/d, and 36 bp of hikes through year-end, up 3.5 bp
d/d. Fed Governor Barr confirmed support for the September 25 bp hike, citing persistent inflation above the 2% target, and said his base
case is that further policy adjustments are likely needed to bring inflation back to target in a timely fashion. Chicago Fed's Goolsbee (non-
voter) warned that large negative supply shocks tend to be far more persistent on inflation than previously assumed, so policymakers
should not look through them. Goldman Sachs noted that short-term yields have not fallen alongside the recent decline in oil, suggesting
the market sees nominal growth as robust and potentially too hot, while also citing Treasury issuance and hyperscaler debt funding as
supply factors. Treasury also announced another long-end buyback of up to $6B.

September flash PMIs well ahead of consensus: The flash composite PMI rose to 58.4 versus 56.1 consensus and 56.0 prior, the fastest
expansion since Jul-21 and a fourth straight monthly acceleration. Manufacturing increased to 57.0 versus 53.6 consensus and 53.9
prior, with output growth the strongest since Apr-22, and services climbed to 58.7 versus 56.0 consensus and 56.5 prior, with output
rising at the fastest pace in over five years. Employment growth accelerated to its strongest since Jun-22. Average input-cost inflation
across goods and services surged to the highest since Oct-22, driven largely by fuel and transportation costs, and supply chain delays
were the most widespread since Jul-22. Thursday brings initial claims and August new home sales alongside a $44B 7-year note sale,
with August durable goods and final University of Michigan sentiment due Friday.

US-Iran talks and oil supply: US and Iranian officials held several hours of talks in New York on Tuesday through a Qatari mediator, their
first meeting since June. Special Envoy Witkoff said he hoped the discussions would prove constructive, and President Trump said there
was a lot of momentum for Iran to make a deal, while reiterating that a deal would come after the November midterms. Iran used the
meeting to restate its conditions, including an end to the naval blockade and the release of Iranian assets, and President Pezeshkian told
the UN that Iran cannot be made to surrender or accept limits to its nuclear program. A Kyodo report cited a senior Iranian official con-
veying a proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days if the US eases military pressure. Saudi Arabia was reported to have
restarted East-West Pipeline operations with exports from Yanbu able to resume, though throughput is low and will take 6 to 8 weeks to
reach full capacity, and Brent put options logged record volumes on Tuesday. Goldman Sachs expects China's crude imports to stay
subdued in Q4 if prices remain elevated, rising only ~0.6M bpd q/q and remaining more than 3M bpd lower y/y, and sees Middle East
supply disruptions rather than Chinese buying as the main upside risk to oil.

Diesel export ban draws pushback: Politico reported the administration was preparing a 90-day ban on diesel exports, which the White
House called fake news; the idea has gained traction in parts of Congress and with Trump, and the White House has been exploring its
feasibility. Energy Secretary Wright said a ban "definitely doesn't work", and the American Petroleum Institute argued export restrictions
would destabilize refinery operations and could ultimately push US prices higher. The main concern is that Gulf Coast refineries producing
diesel for export may cut overall runs, reducing supplies of other products including gasoline, while major customers Brazil and the UK
could struggle for supply. The ban has become a talking point in some tight midterm races.

Muse momentum and cheaper frontier models: Meta's Muse and agentic proliferation remained the main AI story, with attention on
Meta's monetization advantage from existing customer relationships, trust concerns and expected competition from Google and Apple.
CPUs and memory were widely discussed as winners, while displacement risk was debated for businesses that rely on friction and inertia,
including telecom, insurance, banking, wealth management, subscriptions, lead-gen and comparison platforms, travel, credit cards, pay-
ment processors, job boards and digital advertising. Meta paused a human concierge feature for Muse after employee privacy concerns.
Separately, Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 was priced ~40% below its predecessor and OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol/Luna ~50% below prior GPT-
5.6 offerings, pointing to frontier-lab competition shifting from peak performance to cost efficiency; model price commoditization is a
margin headwind but a tailwind for enterprise adoption.

EU Market Recap
Germany -0.66% | France -0.4% | UK -0.03%
Synopsis

European equity markets closed lower after two firmer sessions. Energy, chemicals and financial services were the best performers, while
insurance, construction, retail and tech lagged. Stocks were softer despite easing crude prices, with analysts citing the ECB's hawkish
stance, resilient economic growth, low European gas storage levels and rising crack spreads as factors keeping yields supported; Euro-
pean bond yields also backed up. Read-across from Meta's Muse remained a headwind for sectors seen at risk of AI disruption and
displacement. The eurozone flash composite PMI came in firmer than expected on an upside surprise in services, with euro-area output
the fastest since April 2023, and Brightmine data showed easing UK pay settlements. On the sidelines of the UN, Trump held talks with
UK PM Burnham, who called for new global AI standards in his first UN speech, a proposal Trump rejected; Ukraine's Zelensky asked
Trump for a winter aid package and said he believes the US will push for an energy ceasefire. In the UK, the Treasury Select Committee
called for the OBR to produce a 10-year forecast, and Burnham's adviser Jim O'Neill argued that bringing Gilt yields down should take
priority over the growth-in-every-postcode pledge. On the corporate front, EFG International (EFGN-CH) reached a final settlement with
Kuwait's PIFSS resolving legacy UK civil proceedings, Raiffeisen Bank International (RBI-AT) benefited from an Austrian court ordering
Rasperia to pay €3.15B, KWS SAAT (KWS-DE) reported a 3% decline in FY 2025/26 sales to €1.63B with EBITDA below expectations, JD
Sports (JD-GB) posted broadly in-line H1 results and Adyen (ADYEN-NL) nominated Niclas Neglen as CFO from 1 February 2027. In insur-
ance, Sampo fell after Solidium announced an accelerated bookbuild of 70M A shares, and in construction Arcadis was lower after WSP
Global abandoned its takeover bid.
Macro

Eurozone activity accelerates despite energy shock: The September eurozone flash composite PMI rose to 53.1 versus 52.0 prior and
51.7 consensus, the fastest pace in over three years and a third consecutive expansion. Services delivered the surprise at 53.0, a 10-
month high, from 51.6 prior. Germany's composite jumped to 53.8 as services returned to growth after five months of contraction, and
France unexpectedly expanded for the first time in 10 months with services at 51.4, although French factory output slipped to a near-stall
50.3. New orders rose at the fastest rate since May-2022 and backlogs accumulated for the first time since mid-2022, while employment
was split between hiring in Germany and job cuts in France. Input and output prices rose at the steepest pace since May on fuel and
energy. S&P Global said the reading implies ~0.4% q/q GDP for Q3, and ING suggested another ECB hike is now harder for doves to
dismiss.

ECB keeps energy pass-through in focus: Bundesbank President Nagel said oil has become more relevant for ECB policy. He sees rates
at 2.5% as neither stimulative nor restrictive, but if high energy prices persist he cannot exclude a move into mildly restrictive territory,
citing second-round risk from German wage talks. Lagarde and Vujcic have rejected a lockstep link to oil and suggested the ECB may wait
until December for its next hike. Bank of America flagged product markets as tighter than crude and raised its H2 Brent forecast to
$95/bbl, while diesel is back at 2022 highs and Dutch TTF is up more than 140% y/y. Goldman Sachs sees an ECB hike to 2.75% in
December.

UK PMI eases while pay settlements cool: The UK composite PMI met expectations at 52.0 versus 52.5 prior, a three-month low, as
services missed at 51.7 versus 52.0 consensus and 52.5 prior; manufacturing reached a three-month high of 52.0 versus 51.5 forecast,
supported by AI investment and defence spending. Input prices accelerated at the highest rate since June on energy, fuel and raw material
costs, and prices charged also rose at the fastest pace since June, while total new work fell marginally and hiring appetite remained soft.
Brightmine data showed the median pay award for July revised down to 3.0% from 3.2%, with August holding at that level, consistent with
private sector earnings just below 3% and with the BoE's view that ~3% wage growth is compatible with its 2% target.

UK fiscal and mortgage pressure: The OECD raised its 2026 UK GDP forecast to 1.1% from 0.9% but trimmed 2027 to 1% from 1.1% on
the impact of the Iran conflict, and urged stronger efforts to contain spending and bring debt under control. Chancellor Healey's fiscal
headroom is estimated to have more than halved due to the Iran war, economists see tax rises as inevitable, and Centax researchers said
£26B could be raised through higher capital gains tax and wider national insurance. The Treasury Select Committee warned against
attacks on the OBR ahead of the October Budget. Households face a mortgage shock: one million homeowners have remortgaged from
cheaper fixed rates since February and up to 750K sub-3% deals expire by year-end, with Moneyfacts showing the average two-year deal
at 5.9% from 5.59% and the five-year near 6% from 5.63%.

French spread and German coalition strain: France's OAT-Bund spread rose above 100 bps for the first time since 2012 as a weak budget,
stalled growth and the 2027 presidential race collide; BNP Paribas said a budget pass on 30-Sep could bring a bounce but expects 2027
politics to keep French flows depressed. Goldman noted the CAC 40 has been Europe's weakest large-cap index for three years, and both
brokers prefer Germany while Merz's coalition holds. In Germany, Chancellor Merz is under pressure after the CDU failed to clear 5% in
the Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania election, and the SPD has seized on his remark about the dual health-insurance system to push for
a single Bürgerversicherung, which Eurointelligence views as a de facto end to private full cover and a large levy on higher earners.

Asian Markets
Japan closed (holiday) | Hong Kong -1.01% | China -0.39%
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mixed on Wednesday, with Japan closed for a holiday. The MSCI Asia Pacific ex Japan index rose around 0.5%, as the
overnight Nasdaq rally supported the Kospi, up 0.90%, and the Taiex, which crossed 48K for the first time; India also posted modest
gains, Southeast Asian benchmarks rose and the ASX was flat. Hong Kong underperformed noticeably, dragged lower by IT and internet
names, and mainland China boards fell. Sentiment was helped by positive messaging on US-China trade, as both sides look to extend
their trade truce and potentially cut tariffs on a range of products, and by developments in the Middle East. The US dollar was higher, with
weakness in the AUD, NZD and yen; Asian bond yields declined, crude futures were lower with Brent just below $100/bl, and precious
metals and cryptocurrencies were mixed. In regional developments, Seoul confirmed its first investment under its $350B investment deal
with the US will be a gas-fired power plant in Texas, and South Korean consumer sentiment improved as the stock market recovered.
Singapore headline inflation rose to a two-year high of 2.3% with core at 2.2%. S&P Global flash PMIs showed continued expansion across
industries in India but a contraction in Australian manufacturing, the first in six months, and the ADB warned Asia faces sustained inflation
pressure well into 2027. Taiwan export orders hit a record high on AI-related shipments. On the corporate side, SoftBank (9984.JP) is
marketing a $1B junk bond offering at yields of 8.75-9.875%, the highest ever for its dollar bonds of those maturities; Adani Group firms
paid INR15M to settle disclosure violations with SEBI; and Qantas (QAN.AU) will launch new ultra-long flights including a Sydney-New York
route despite high fuel prices.
Macro

Trump-Xi summit and trade truce extension: Xi Jinping visits the US over September 23-25. A senior US official told Bloomberg that Xi is
unlikely to be accompanied by Chinese CEOs, even though US business leaders including the CEOs of all Mag 7 firms are expected at the
state dinner, suggesting different expectations on the two sides. Reuters sources said Xi is expected to press Trump to halt Taiwan arms
sales, citing the 1982 joint communique, and a Global Times editorial placed the onus on the US to turn consensus into action, naming
Taiwan as the issue on which relations could go either way. The trade truce is due to expire on 10-Nov. USTR Greer said in a Bloomberg
TV interview that an extension will not be agreed this week, and he answered affirmatively when asked whether the two sides were dis-
cussing three to six months, saying the US wants incremental extensions to ensure compliance on rare earth exports. Nikkei reported
that Beijing prefers an agreement running through Trump's term to early 2029. After the Asia close, Bessent told Fox News he had reached
agreement with Vice Premier He Lifeng to extend the truce until 10-Jan and that Trump and Xi could meet twice more this year in Shenzhen
and Miami, with no confirmation from China yet.

Offshore revenue splits China's AI winners and losers: Bloomberg analysis found that Chinese tech stocks with the highest share of
overseas revenue are up 36% YTD, against 9% gains for those reliant on domestic business, as exporters benefit from global AI infrastruc-
ture demand while Beijing's self-sufficiency push has driven intense domestic competition and a price war. Zhongji Innolight (3308-HK)
and Eoptolink Technology (300502-CN), which earn more than 90% of revenue overseas, have both rallied about 50% this year. Localiza-
tion plays have lagged, with Moore Threads Technology (688795-CN) down ~25%, SenseTime Group (20-HK) down more than 40% and
Kuaishou Technology (1024-HK) down 51%, although the localization trade retains appeal as a geopolitical hedge.

Alibaba and Tencent advance AI programs: Alibaba (9988-HK) unveiled its Zhenwu V900 accelerator, developed by its T-Head unit, which
it described as China's most powerful AI chip and a direct competitor to Nvidia. CEO Wu said it triples its predecessor's performance, can
be combined into 500K clusters for frontier model training and will launch commercially in Q1-27. He set a target of 20 gigawatts of data
center capacity by 2032 and said Qwen 4.5 and 5.0 will be trained at 5-10T parameters, while Qwen 4.0 is in training. Alibaba will also
build data centers in Europe and the Middle East. Tencent (700-HK) launched its Hy Image 3.5 Preview image-generation model, which it
claimed is on par with ByteDance's Seedream 5.0 Pro. Separately, China was reported to be reviewing its dependence on Broadcom
switches in data centers.

Bank Indonesia holds and boosts rupiah support: Bank Indonesia held its base rate at 5.75% as widely expected, with the deposit facility
