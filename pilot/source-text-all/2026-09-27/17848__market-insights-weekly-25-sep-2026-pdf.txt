Surveys reinforce higher -for -longer expectations
 
The US Composite PMI rose to 58.4 in September, its highest level for more than five 
years, signalling a marked acceleration in activity across manufacturing and services. The 
report helped push the US 10
-year Treasury yield above 5% and provided broad sup
port 
for the dollar. Activity also strengthened in the 
Eurozone, where the Composite PMI 
increased to 53.1, a three
-and -a-half -year high. Hawkish comments from Fed officials 
maintained market expectations of at least one further rate rise this year, following the 
Fed’s first increase since 2023 earlier this month.
 
Sterling underperformed the other major currencies, weighed partly by a fall in the UK 
Composite PMI to 51.7 in September from 52.5. The survey continued to signal expansion, 
but at a more moderate pace, while businesses also reported stronger input and ou
tput 
price pressures (see chart 2). The GfK consumer confidence index provided a more 
positive signal, rising for a third consecutive month to 
-13. Ahead of the Budget on 28 
October, public sector borrowing was higher than expected in August, reaching £77.
3bn 
in the financial year to date
, tracking some £8.1bn above the OBR’s March forecas
ts . 
The Bank of England has stood apart from most other major central banks by keeping 
policy rates unchanged this year. However, some Monetary Policy Committee members 
warned this week that rates may need to rise. Clare Lombardelli, who voted for no change 
at the latest meeting, reiterated that global energy prices had evolved more closely with 
the Bank’s July “adverse” scenario. That scenario points to a higher near
-term inflation 
peak and a later return to the 2% target. Her remarks highlight growing concern about 
second -round inflation effects if a protracted Middle East conflict keeps energy prices 
elevated.

Over the week, GBP/USD fell towards 1.32, a level last breached in June. Broad dollar 
strength also pushed EUR/USD below 1.14 for the first time since July. UK 10
-year gilt yields 
remained above 5%, although they rose by less than their US and German count
erparts.

initially falling on hopes of US

amid renewed concerns about 
further escalation. 
Inflation and jobs data to test rate expectations
 
With markets increasingly focused on the inflation outlook and the prospect of further 
policy tightening, the coming week’s data releases will be particularly important. In the 
US, attention will centre on the PCE deflator, the Fed’s preferred inflation me
asure, on 
Wednesday and the labour market report on Friday. The main 
Eurozone release will be 
the flash estimate of September CPI inflation on Friday. The Reserve Bank of Australia is 
expected to respond to upside inflation risks by raising its policy rate by 25bp to 4.60% 
on Tuesday. The week also features several speeches 
by global central bank officials, 
including ECB President Christine Lagarde at the European Parliament on Monday.
 
In the UK, releases include Bank of England money and credit data, the Decision Maker 
Panel survey and the Lloyds Business Barometer. We expect mortgage approvals to ease 
to 53.5k in August, remaining below the levels recorded earlier in the year. Second
-
quarter GDP growth is expected to be confirmed at 0.4% q/q. The BRC shop price index 
may also attract attention as a timely indicator of food and core goods inflation. Sterling 
markets will also focus on the Labour Party Conference, with speeches from Chanc
ellor 
John Healey and Prime Minister Andy Burnham on Monday and Tuesday respectively. 
Several Bank of England MPC members are also scheduled to speak.
 
We expect US data releases to maintain pressure on the Fed to tighten policy further. The 
August PCE deflator is likely to reflect renewed increases in energy prices and underlying 
inflation. Annual headline and core PCE inflation are both forecast to rise by 0.1 
percentage point, t
o 3.8% and 3.4% respectively. Markets may nevertheless place 
greater 
weight on the timelier September CPI report, due on 14 October, ahead of the Fed’s 
policy announcement on 28 October.
 
Friday’s labour market report is also expected to reaffirm the resilience of the US 
economy. We forecast nonfarm payrolls to rise by 100k in September, below August’s 
stronger -than -expected increase of 162k but still a solid outcome. The unemployment 
rate is expected to remain at 4.1%, while average hourly earnings are forecast to rise by 
0.3% m/m. 
Eurozone data are also expected to support the case for further ECB policy tightening. 
We forecast the latest rise in global energy prices to lift headline CPI inflation to 3.5% in 
September, its highest level for three years. Core inflation is expected to
 remain at 2.4%, 
which would support a further rate rise at the December policy meeting rather than in 
October. A 
higher -than -expected outturn, however, would raise expectations of an 
earlier move.

Eurozone ‘flash’ CPI (Sep ) – Fri 2 October (10:0 0 BS T) 
Eurozone headline CPI inflation 
accelerated to 3.2% in August, up from 2.9% in July, 
mainly reflecting higher energy prices. However, core inflation moderated to 2.4% from 
2.5%, led by a decline in services inflation to 3.0% from 3.3%. The more muted readings 
for underlying inflation may
 enable the European Central Bank to 
continue to 
raise 
interest rates 
at a 
gradual pace . We expect September headline CPI inflation to 
accelerate to 3.5%, a three
-year high, reflecting the renewed increase in global energy 
prices, including higher oil prices with a 
Middle East agreement to reopen the Strait of 
Hormuz still elusive and 
with gas prices 
also rising 
as Europe approaches winter. 
Underlying core inflation is forecast to stay at 2.4%,

of greater risks of second
-round effects 
as the increase in 
energy price inflation
 further 
postpones the return to target for headline inflation

US labour market report
 (Sep ) – Fri 2 October 
(13:30 BS T) 
The August US labour market report surprised on the upside, with nonfarm payrolls 
increasing by 162k, well above the consensus forecast for a rise of 55k. It was the strongest 
outturn since March, following weaker jobs growth over 
the prior 
months. In the 
household survey, the unemployment 
rate stayed at 4.1%
, reflecting strong employment 
growth and an increase in the participation rate. Some of the August strength may reflect 
temporary factors, including seasonal -adjustment effects , but the underlying trend 
appears to have strengthened. For September, our baseline forecast is for nonfarm 
payrolls to rise by 100k and for the unemployment rate to stay at 4.1%. 
Average hourly 
earnings growth is expected to rise by 0.3% m/m, the same as in August. 
With a solid US 
labour market and inflation set to accelerate further, 
markets continue to expect another 
Fed rate rise in Q4.
