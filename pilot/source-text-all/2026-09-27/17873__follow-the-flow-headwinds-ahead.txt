Follow The Flow 
Headwinds ahead 
     
   
 
 
Rates uncertainty brings outflows 
Rates market jitters are having a direct impact on flow strength into bond markets. Last 
week, government bond, money market and high-yield funds recorded outflows. While 
inflows into high-grade funds resumed, we are sceptical at the current juncture that this 
will continue in a world of a higher rates vol backdrop. We note that while rates vol 
declined last week, it has moved notably higher over the past couple of days. We cannot 
see such a development as a tailwind for flows into credit funds. Rates vol at current 
levels of c.90pts (for SMOVEU3M index) is not supportive for flows into riskier assets 
like credit vis-à-vis flows into “risk-free” proxies such as government deb. 
Exhibit 1: Elevated uncertainty in rates is having a notable impact on flows into credit funds 
The higher the level of rates vol, the weaker the flow into credit funds 
Source: EPFR Global.

Over the past week… 
Overall, high-grade funds returned to inflows after two weeks of outflows. Across 
maturities, mid- and long-term funds drew inflows as short-term funds posted outflows. 
Short-term funds broke a six-week streak of inflows. 
High yield funds recorded their third consecutive week of outflows. For European-
domiciled funds, Globally-focussed HY funds have suffered the most vs. Europea and 
US-focussed ones (Exhibit 22). HY ETFs recorded inflows over the previous week, after 
two weeks of outflows.  
Government bond funds swung back into outflows territory. Money market funds 
suffered from another week of outflows, the second in a row. Global EM debt funds 
have now recorded inflows for eight weeks in a row. In aggregate, flows into fixed 
income funds reversed course over the previous week, turning back to inflows. Equity 
funds extended their outflow streak to two weeks, while equity ETFs posted inflows for 
an eighth straight week.
