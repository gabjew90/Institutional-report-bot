25 September 2026 | 5:56PM EDT
Amidst a broader tape dominated by tech/AI, geopolitical and macro news, in US 
Pharma this week, pre-3Q sellside calls began as we approach quarter end, 
beginning with JNJ and LLY (details within). Additionally, we fielded a number of 
follow-ups on our note BMY: Complicated Setup for Upcoming Admilparant Phase 3 IPF 
Readout which we view as the next most consequential near-term pipeline event for 
the company, and with implications for others in the IPF space (notably CTNM and 
UTHR). Novo held its Capital Markets Day, where it laid out its strategy post 
semaglutide LOE, however the stock has traded off since then, as with recent 
pipeline disappointments still fresh in investors’ minds, evidence of execution on 
both the pipeline and new launches will be required before investors give credit in 
the valuation for the potential for growth beyond the semaglutide LoE. 
From a macro perspective, we are monitoring the positioning unwind in biotech (XBI 
down ~8% off August local highs vs. SPY -1% off all-time highs), where our 
colleagues point out rising rates, reawakening of AI themes and a negative leaning 
bias in clinical updates and other news from pharma and biotech through this time 
period. Here, as we noted last week, prior Fed hiking cycle episodes show most of the 
XBI weakness is at the very start of the cycle (Exhibit 1) as small cap DCFs are 
disproportionately impacted. 
Next week, the EASD conference will feature Ph2 data from LLY’s eloralintide + 
tirzepatide combination, as well as updates from others in the space (e.g. 
ABBV/Gubra, Roche, Zealand, Hengrui) as they look to chart the course for the next 
leg of the obesity market evolution.

Big Picture: Biotechnology performance was mixed versus the broader markets this 
week (XBI/NBI -1.33%/+1.03% vs. the S&P 500 +0.72%) on rising treasury yields and 
renewed inflation fears/interest rate dynamics, alongside sector rotations. 
 This week, VRTX reported Phase 2b data from inaxaplin in APOL1-mediated kidney 
disease (AMKD), where patients with modest proteinuria saw 42.7% and 44.7% 
reductions in urine albumin to creatinine ratio (UACR) and urine protein to creatinine 
ratio (UPCR), respectively, above VRTX’s bar of ~20-40% and consistent with prior Phase 
2 data, and patients with co-morbid type 2 diabetes saw reductions of 17.3% (UACR) 
and 25.4% (UPCR), noting low investor expectations per the diabetic co-morbidity – 
overall, the data removes an overhang into an eventful catalyst path, and bodes well into 
the early-27 data from the ongoing Phase 3 AMPLITUDE study of inaxaplin. Separately, 
AMGN announced positive Phase 3 topline results for anti-CD40L antagonist fusion 
protein dazodalibep in moderate-to-severe systemic Sjögren’s disease, a systemic 
autoimmune condition, noting a statistically significant and clinically meaningful benefit 
at week 48 on the primary endpoint of EULAR Sjögren’s Syndrome Disease Activity 
Index, with improvements observed at week 4, and low rates of discontinuations and no 
imbalance in the incidence of thromboembolic and opportunistic infections across 
treatment arms, potentially addressing toxicity concerns associated with prior 
generation anti-CD40Ls - we see positive read-across to the Phase 3 trial in 
moderate-to-severe symptomatic disease (study completion in 4Q), and look to full data 
at an upcoming medical meeting to inform commercial positioning.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
