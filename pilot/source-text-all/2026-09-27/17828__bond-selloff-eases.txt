25 September 2026 
Bond selloff eases 
MARKET LINES 
Markets remain focused on oil prices and rates. After two sessions of sharp gains, the price of 
Brent is down today and interest rates are easing slightly. Global equity markets remain resilient. 
The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield falls back below the 5.20% mark at 5.16% this morning after 
touching 5.22% yesterday. What stands out is the relative stability of long-term market inflation 
expectations (U.S. 10-year inflation break-evens at 2.3%) compared to the real rate, which is 
driving up nominal interest rates. The 10-year U.S. real rate now stands at 2.85%, its highest level 
since December 2008. Key data will be the US August durable goods and the speech by Williams 
(Fed). 
Rates 
► Continued rise in bond yields amid hawkish comments from central bankers (ECB Schnabel, BoE 
Lombardelli and Breeden): +4bps on the 10-year Bund at 3.55%.  
► Sovereign spreads tightened slightly, particularly the OAT-Bund (-1 bp at 109bps), with no clear 
direction during the European session.  
► In the U.S., after reaching 5.22% yesterday, the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield fell back to 5.18% this 
morning as oil prices declined. The 2-year U.S. Treasury yield stands at 4.90%. 
► This morning, yields are falling: -3 bps for the 10-year U.S. Treasury, -4bps  for the 10-year OAT (OAT-
Bund spread at ~107 basis points). 
► Trade idea: Receive AUD 10yr vs JPY 10yr IRS - Trade Idea APAC Rates 
FX 
► The DXY rose by +0.23% to reach a two-month high at 101.33.   
► EUR/USD depreciated slightly to 1.1368, weighed down by dollar strength.   
► The CHF was the most affected G10 currency, with USD/CHF rising by +0.51% to 0.8293, and 
EUR/CHF also moving higher by +0.37% to 0.9426, amid a reduction in risk appetite.   
► The AUD and NZD were among the worst G10 performers, with AUD/USD falling by -0.36% to 0.7011 
and NZD/USD by -0.39% to 0.5651.  
Equities 
► A pullback in equities, with the Stoxx 600 down 0.55%. Tech and industrial stocks underperformed, 
particularly hardware (-3.4%). Energy and consumer staples were the only sectors in positive 
territory.  
► Implied volatilities are rising (VStoxx +1.2 pts) but remain very low, particularly compared to MOVE 
(bond volatility). A slight pullback in equities, with the Stoxx 600 down 0.55%. Tech and industrial 
stocks underperformed, particularly hardware (-3.4%). Energy and consumer staples are the only 
sectors in positive territory.   
► SPX and Nasdaq were flat at the close yesterday. The Magnificent 7 outperformed (+0.7% 
yesterday). 
► Nikkei is up 1.3% this morning and European futures are up.

Real Estate  
► 
Office in London City - According to CoStar, trading giant Jane Street is doubling its London 
footprint by pre-letting 465,000 sq ft at JP Morgan AM’s One Spitalfields for 2029. As the City’s 
largest office deal in over a decade, this landmark transaction highlights the intense competition 
for premium workspace. Facing a severe shortage of prime supply, major occupiers are now 
forced to secure their future locations years in advance. 
► 
European commercial real estate trends: H1 2026 - According to BPCE’s study, the European 
commercial real estate market is slowing down due to geopolitical tensions, particularly in 
the UK and Germany, whereas Spain, the Netherlands and Poland are showing progress. A 
further correction in values is expected as a result of rising interest rates. In the office sector, 
Southern Europe (Milan, Madrid, Barcelona) is holding up well with moderate corrections and 
prime rents rising across 29 metropolitan areas. High street retail remains stable, while logistics 
experienced a yield correction in Q2-26. 
► 
AI growth in office Real Estate - According to a Colliers study, the rise of AI is reshaping the 
European office market, driven by the leading trio of London, Paris, and Berlin. This fast-
growing sector is generating highly capitalized occupiers in search of flexibility. For investors, the 
key challenge is to deliver connected, adaptable buildings in prime metropolitan locations, where 
the essential talent pools and data infrastructures are concentrated. 
► 
US CRE pricing - According to MSCI, in August 2026, US commercial property prices edged up 
+0.1% monthly and annually, amid tightening Federal Reserve monetary policy. Sector 
performance was highly fragmented: CBD office prices rose fastest (+9.4% YOY), while suburban

Transportation & Mobility  
► 
AUTO & AIR - The Automotive and Airline industries had an eventful summer. In our post-
summer edition of Transportation & Mobility – What’s New and What’s Next, we focus on 
where restructuring can genuinely defend margins, how far rating risk has really receded, and 
which names across autos and airlines still offer mispriced spread potential.  
► 
AUTO – European new car registrations rose 5% YoY in August but remained 22% below August 
2019 levels. The UK (+13% YoY), Spain (+12% YoY) and France (+7% YoY) outperformed the market, 
while Italy (+3% YoY) and Germany (+3% YoY) underperformed. In this recovering market, 
electrified vehicles continued to outpace the overall sector, with BEV penetration reaching a new 
high, supported by elevated fuel prices and ongoing subsidies. In August, BEVs accounted for 
29.2% of EU + UK + EFTA registrations (+9pp YoY; 23.2% YTD). By OEM, Mercedes-Benz was the 
only major European player to keep pace with the market (+8% YoY), whereas Renault Group 
recorded the weakest performance (-4% YoY). Tesla’s earlier strong momentum slowed sharply 
(+4% YoY vs. +43% YTD), while Chinese OEMs again posted robust growth, with the combined 
market share of BYD, SAIC (MG), Chery Group and Leapmotor now exceeding 9%.
