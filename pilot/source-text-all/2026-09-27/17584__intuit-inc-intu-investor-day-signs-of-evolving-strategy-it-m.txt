Key takeaways:  
 
 
AI: Progress on the feature roadmap; pace of innovation key. The latest updates to 
Intuit Intelligence (first rolled out October 2025) feature wider integration into QBO 
Advanced and IES, and incremental offerings like voice-enabled mobile Payroll, 
conversational Multi-Entity accounting, and additional integrations with the frontier 
models (quote-to-cash, deeper payroll queries, QB Capital lending insights). Intuit 
noted: 1) query routing across 60+ LLMs; 2) monthly touchpoints between Intuit’s CTO 
and the AI labs; and 3) improved coding velocity and ROI on the company’s $110mn in 
FY26 token spend. Some features (e.g. customizable agents and skills) appear to still be 
in beta and adoption is a work-in-progress: AI conversations doubled QoQ, likely off of a 
low base. Meanwhile, 2.5mm customers engage with Intuit Intelligence automations 
monthly - presumably out of the ~8mm GBS online customer base, a 31% adoption rate 
vs. the 77% of US SMBs who say they regularly use AI per Intuit. As is the case with 
changing knowledge worker behavior broadly; we think training, enablement and time 
will be needed to close this gap; particularly because of the mixed reviews the AI rollout 
has garnered online (from what we have seen, a portion of the customer base is either 
indifferent to the AI features, or is unhappy at the associated price increases). 
Barbell strategy and the Big Bets: This strategy continues to be key, especially for 
QuickBooks where Intuit did not raise price at the low end this year, and introduced a 
free tier at the lower end. The company continues to bet on Assisted Tax/Mid-Market 
business solutions as two prongs of their Big Bets. These are driving outsized growth, 
albeit off of a small base. In Tax, Assisted share gains are offsetting DIY losses (Exhibit 4), 
which is accretive to revenues; but >75% of Assisted gains come from DIY trade-up, and 
DIY continues to lose customers who leave the TT system, a trend which accelerated in 
FY26 (see Exhibit 5). Furthermore, we continue to see the risk that AI commoditizes 
Assisted, not just DIY. In GBS, there are two counterbalancing factors: Agent 
Studio/Accountants/broadening reach in the mid-market are TAM expansionary; vs. 
higher penetration in core (~20% of the customer TAM) and moderating pricing 
increases. The mid-market TAM is competitive, and on Intuit’s disclosures, with similar to 
slightly higher levels of penetration (Exhibit 7). On the lower end, QuickBooks free 
should drive customer acquisition for price-sensitive customers who can then trade up 
(20K already have in the first six months), and we look to gauge the success of this 
strategy through disclosures on cohort upgrades.

Consumer: Broadening the touchpoints and solving what they can solve. We have 
written about the ways AI is changing the tax industry, and part of the challenge for 
Intuit is that the cost to do a tax return 100% accurately with human-level IQ and 
minimal customer effort is trending down to the cost of compute (a few cents). We think 
this is a difficult hurdle for incumbents to innovate against. That said, we did see signs 
that Intuit is willing to reinvent its consumer business the way it has been able to do 
historically. If Intuit can leverage its: 1) its vast customer database (37.4M monetized 
customers); 2) wealth of data on consumers (60K attributes per customer) and 
businesses that sell to consumers (580K per SMB); 3) growing physical store presence to 
build out a broader array of services, the company has a higher chance of offsetting 
pressure in the traditional tax business. Could Intuit in 2031 be offering brokerage, 
financial advisory, agentic commerce, and more? The key will be to what extent this 
consumer vision takes shape against a backdrop of new consumer agent offerings like 
Muse, and there is a scenario where Intuit’s data/assets/brands/customer relationships 
could be leveraged into new areas of business not currently contemplated by investors.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
