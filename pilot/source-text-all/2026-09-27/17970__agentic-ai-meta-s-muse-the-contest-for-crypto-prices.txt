Agentic AI, Meta's Muse, & The Contest For Crypto 
Prices 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, SEP 25, 2026 - 03:20 AM 
Two weeks after Meta shipped Muse, the outrageous performance of the company's stock this 
week has morphed the agentic-AI economy from sounding like tech gobbledygook to looking 
like a consumer product.  
On September 8, 2026, Meta rolled out a personal agent that does not merely answer 
questions, it books travel, sends email, haggles a bill, fills forms, shops, and then pays for it all 
through Stripe Link, running on a dedicated cloud virtual machine with its own browser. 
Zuckerberg framed it as software that works "24/7 on your behalf." Ten days later it was the top 
free iPhone app in the US. Wall Street began treating Muse as a possible new revenue engine, 
not another chatbot experiment. 
While not the first - and definitely not the last - that launch matters more for the narrative of an 
agentic economy than for any single booking Muse has completed.  
For two years, 'agents' have lived in developer terminals, enterprise pilots, and on-chain 
experiments whose transaction counts looked impressive until you asked who was actually 
paying. Muse is the first time a company that already reaches billions of people has put a 
goal-seeking agent in a chat thread next to Instagram, WhatsApp, and your favorite clothes 
retailer, and told 'average joes' to hand it their inbox and their credit card. The story is no longer 
that agents could act, it is that a mass-market firm is betting they will. 
While the world is focused on how this new 'agentic' economy will drive new revenue sources at 
the back-end (and greatly disrupt other real-world intermediaries, such as banks this week) in 
the 'inference economy' (as Goldman's Rich Privorotsky highlighted earlier in the week), we see 
the timing of a recent concomitant surge in crypto as perhaps more interesting and significant 
longer-term .  
Muse spends through Stripe, with one-time payments and purchase warranties, not through a 
wallet. If the first widely used consumer agent settles like a human shopper with a credit card, 
the agentic-economy thesis does not automatically become a bitcoin thesis. 


[Page 2]
 
What Muse reinvigorates is demand for software that transacts. Whether those transactions stay 
on card-rails or migrate onto programmable money is the argument that follows, inspired by a 
discussion by infamous former hedge fund manager Hugh Hendry recently. 
 
From answers to actors 
Agentic AI is the shift from systems that produce an output and stop to systems that pursue a 
goal. A generative model writes an itinerary; an agent books the flights, watches the fare, and 
then pings you only when it needs approval. Researchers now treat agentic systems as 
coordinated loops - perceive, plan, act, check, adapt - that can run for hours or days with limited 
oversight. Muse has packaged that loop into a cute furry animal and simple to understand 
consumer interface: it keeps working after the app is closed, surfaces unsolicited suggestions, 
and treats a saved recipe reel as a grocery list waiting to be executed (we know, our wife was 
prompted to do this). 
The full potential of this new 'friend' is not a better assistant, it is scale (hence the $200 
billion-plus addition to META's market cap in the last few days). 


[Page 3]
 
 
If agents mediate a large slice of digital commerce - some projections put agent-mediated 
consumer activity in the multi-trillion-dollar range by 2030 - the number of counterparties that 
never sleep will dwarf the number of human shoppers online at any hour. 
Muse is an early test of whether that scale arrives through a handful of platforms or through a 
messier market of agents that can hire, pay, and settle with each other amd strangers. 
Why crypto is still in the picture 
Muse proves that people will let software spend, at least inside a trusted brand with a warranty. 
It does not prove that every future agent will have Meta's distribution, Stripe's risk control, or a 
human legally attached to every charge. 
However, the payment problem for agents at industrial scale is not 'move a thousand dollars 
once'. It is 'pay a million times for data, inference, APIs, compute, and other agents', often at 
sizes card networks were never designed to clear. 
Stablecoins on public chains settle continuously, across borders, at costs measured in fractions 
of a cent. 
Protocols such as Coinbase's x402 turn HTTP's old 402 'Payment Required' status into a 
machine-readable paywall. 
Google, Stripe's Tempo work, AWS Bedrock payments, and Circle's agent-marketplace 
experiments all treat on-chain settlement as a first-class option for machine commerce. 


[Page 4]
 
Early volumes are still small, and much of the first wave of "agent payments" was scripts and 
speculation rather than autonomous shoppers. Muse does not immediately prove or disprove 
that idea, but it does raise the potential ceiling (and move the ball forward): if hundreds of 
millions of people get used to an agent that acts, the next question is what those agents use 
when they leave Meta's walled-garden. 
 
Crypto was, almost accidentally, built for entities that are addresses rather than citizens.  
Agents that cannot open a bank account can still hold a wallet. 
Identity registries, session-limited spend, and marketplaces where one agent discovers and 
hires another are being shipped because the old web assumed a human at the keyboard. 
Muse assumes a human behind the agent. 
The full agentic economy will include software that is not anyone's 
personal concierge... 
Just this week, we saw BlackRock write about "The Machine-Native Economy" 


[Page 5]
 
 
Their conclusion... 
these developments position AI as a structural catalyst for digital asset adoption and digital 
assets as a potential facilitator of the AI economy: AI interprets information and directs action, 
while blockchains provide machine-readable assets and programmable settlement.  
This relationship remains underappreciated and could expand the role of digital assets as core 
infrastructure for an increasingly autonomous digital economy. 
...fits with our broad thesis... 
How that could lift crypto prices... 
Muse-style commerce can explode on cards and stablecoins while bitcoin and ether sit still if 
agents treat chains as pipes and dollars remain the asset. 
The bull case for prices has distinct channels. 
●​ First is fee demand. If agents pay for block space in native tokens - ether, SOL, and 
other gas assets - then sustained machine traffic is a bid for working capital, not just a 
narrative. Agentic micropayments are starting to be considered a candidate 'killer app' 
for settlement networks that are fast and cheap. 
●​ Second is collateral. Agents that hold treasuries, make markets, rebalance yield, or post 
bonds need liquid, programmable collateral. That tightens float in assets they actually 
use. 


[Page 6]
 
●​ Third is adjacent infrastructure: decentralized compute, data, storage, and verification. 
Agents that buy inference by the request expand networks that track those resources 
on-chain. 
●​ Fourth is capital formation. Once agents earn, they can reinvest. 'Zero-human 
companies' remain a research thesis, but Muse makes the first step - software that 
completes paid work - feel less hypothetical. 
●​ Fifth is the market's familiar loop: narrative plus flows. Muse broke open the 
agentic-economy narrative (and unleashed some flows). The 2024-25 wave of 'AI agent 
tokens' showed how quickly prices detach from product. Full potential would invert that. 
Liquidity would follow the rails that agents actually choose, which so far has meant 
high-throughput chains and dollar stablecoins more than meme tickers. 
 
...But, full potential is not automatic 
Muse itself is a reminder that the first-order winner of consumer agents may be traditional 
finance platforms & card networks, and stablecoin issuers - not every large-cap crypto coin. 
TradFi is competing hard for the human-authorized slice of agent spend with regulatory bodies 
eventually deciding which agents may hold assets and who is liable when one spends badly.  


[Page 7]
 
As Goldman's Privorotsky highlighted earlier this week, Agentic AI can be enormously valuable 
while remaining inside corporate clouds, billed on invoices, and settled in bank money. Muse is 
that path at consumer scale. 
The thought experiment we are suggesting here is that crypto wins the price argument when/if a 
meaningful share of activity prefers permissionless rails - because they are cheaper at the 
margin, because some agents lack legal personhood at banks, and/or because open discovery 
of services beats a single company's catalog. 
The surge, if it comes 
Muse did not confirm any thesis that the 'agentic economy' will make crypto prices rise, it proved 
that the public will accept an agent that acts, pays, and keeps going. 
That is the first (until now, missing) psychological step in the agentic-economy story. 
At full potential, millions of processes that cannot sign a card agreement will still need to pay for 
work, prove identity, post collateral, and coordinate with strangers.  
 
Public blockchains already offer this path. 
The interesting question after Muse is not whether AI will be important. It is whether the next 
hundred million agents settle like customers of Meta - or like addresses. If they settle like 
addresses, crypto prices will not merely react to a revived narrative, they will be part of the 
operating system those agents run on.
