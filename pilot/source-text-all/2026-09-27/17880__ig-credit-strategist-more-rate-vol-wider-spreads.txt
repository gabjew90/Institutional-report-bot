More rate vol = wider spreads 
The jump in rate vol has already pushed liquid IG spreads +5bps wider this week. We 
expect spreads to remain weak as the rate volatility is unlikely to abate soon. While IG 
supply should slow due to higher borrowing costs and higher vol, the primary market will 
need to restart as soon as rates stabilize given the still sizable pipeline of M&A and 
potentially AI deals as well. Moreover, the Fed needs to tighten financial conditions, 
which should make it harder for stocks to rally. The big loss on bonds and lackluster 
equity returns should both weigh on inflows to bond funds and ETFs, which have already 
slowed in September. 
How to position in credit? 
On the curve, we overweight 5yr and 30yr tenors, and underweight 10yr tenor on 1) a 
flatter Treasury yield curve, 2) a drop in 30yr supply and 3) weaker HG fund flows. We 
like non-cyclical sectors including Utilities, REITs, and Energy over the cyclical sectors 
such as Autos and European Yankee banks. We move our BBB positioning to neutral to 
take down the beta of the portfolio in the environment of weaker spreads. 
Mini rate shock 
Wednesday's big interest rate selloff is concerning as it illustrates the demand for bonds 
remains weak. The strong US economy, highlighted by the PMIs, should not be a huge 
surprise. Instead, the price action likely highlights that investors are not yet comfortable 
owning bonds. 
French Yankee banks vs French sovereign 
French government bond spreads to bunds have widened to over 100bps on concerns on 
budget deficits & the upcoming elections. Still, USD Yankee French bank spreads appear 
rich vs. US banks and the French bank spreads in EUR. We screen for French Yankee 
bank bonds trading at the tightest spread percentiles YtD. 
More yield, less duration 
As IG bond yields hit YtD highs, the duration of IG supply dropped to YtD lows so far in 
September. As a result, IG 10s30s spread curve continued to flatten by 7bps from the 
recent steep levels in late July. We expect IG 30yr spreads to continue to outperform. 
What did insurance co’s buy in 2Q? 
Insurance companies net purchased $54.2bn of corporate credit securities in Q2 2026.

Key views 
• 
US IG spread view. Investor demand for bonds should be weaker in the 
environment of elevated interest rate volatility. Supply volumes should slow down, 
but only temporarily. Spreads are biased wider in the near term. We hedge the Iran 
escalation risks by being long US over European Yankee issuers, and less cyclical 
sectors. We hedge M&A supply risks by being underweight Health Care and Food & 
Bev. Our outlook for 2026: IG spreads should grind tighter and remain range bound. 
Low recession risk / robust growth, and a strong credit quality should all remain 
supportive. Supply is seasonally slower in 2H, which should offset weaker demand. 
We look for less bank and hyperscale issuance in 2H, but heavier M&A supply (IG 
Credit Strategist: Supply matters). 
• 
Quality. We move our BBB positioning to neutral to take down the beta of the 
portfolio in the environment of weaker spreads. Excluding AI, single-A / BBB spreads 
are about as compressed as it gets. 
• 
Sectors. We seek to outperform with an overweight view on sectors with more 
spread (Pipelines). We hedge Iran risks with preference for less cyclical sectors with 
spread (Insurance, Utilities) and Energy. We are underweight tight sectors with 
elevated re-leveraging and M&A risks (Consumer Products, Health Care). For more 
details, see IG Credit Strategist: Sector outlook.  
• 
Curve. The 10s30s spread curve should remain flat, supported by higher yields and 
less back-end supply. 5s10s should steepen on a more hawkish Fed outlook and a 
flatter US Treasury yield curve. 10yr spreads could be negatively impacted by 
weaker HG fund / ETF flows.

More rate vol = wider spreads 
The jump in rate vol has already pushed liquid IG spreads +5bps wider this week. We 
expect spreads to remain weak as the rate volatility is unlikely to abate soon. While IG 
supply should now slow due to higher borrowing costs and higher vol, the primary 
market will need to restart as soon as rates stabilize given the still sizable pipeline of 
M&A and potentially AI deals as well. Moreover, the Fed needs to tighten financial 
conditions, which should make it harder for stocks to rally. The big loss on bonds and 
lackluster equity returns should both weigh on inflows to bond funds and ETFs, which 
have already slowed in September.  
How to position in credit?  
A bigger Fed hiking cycle means a flatter Treasury yield curve and a steeper IG spread 
curve in the front-end (5s10s). The back end should also remain supported due to 
shorter duration of supply. That means barbell positioning of overweight the front end 
and 30yr, underweight 10yr. Spreads for the 10yr tenor also tend to be the most 
vulnerable to fund / ETF outflows. 10yr spreads have already widened vs. the wings, but 
there is more room to go. 
Given the start of the hiking cycle and the lack of resolution in Iran, the cyclical sectors 
are beginning to underperform in IG credit. That will likely continue. We reiterate our 
preference for Utilities, REITs, and Energy over the cyclical sectors like Autos and 
European Yankee banks. We also move to overweight the less cyclical Telecom tactically 
while the rate vol remains elevated. 
Finally, while BBBs are currently near the max-compressed levels, over the past four 
years the BBB/A spread ratio did not move much during prior episodes of either wider 
spreads or higher yields. Still, we move our BBB positioning to neutral to take down the 
beta of the portfolio in the environment of weaker spreads.

Strong AI economy = more rate vol 
The AI investment cycle is supporting a strong US economy due to 1) strong capex 
spending on the business side and 2) wealth effect on the consumer side. The Fed hiking 
cycle is unlikely to disturb either one in a big way. As a result, the Fed may need to hike 
more than expected to overcome the positive tailwinds of AI. Our rates strategists see 
further room for rates to sell off as a result (see Global Rates Weekly: Running from 
rates).

Supply disruption is temporary 
The recent big jump in borrowing costs should weigh on supply volumes over the next 
few weeks. 10yr Treasury yields sold off by +40bps or more on seven occasions since 
2021. During five of them, supply volumes dropped below the seasonal trend, often by a 
big margin. The two exceptions were Mar-2022, when risk-on following the initial shock 
of the Russian invasion of Ukraine led to heavier volumes, and Mar-2023, when issuers 
were front-running the expected Fed hikes (Exhibit 5). 
The analysis in Exhibit 5 tracks the weekly share of annual supply. Big jumps in 
borrowing costs lowered issuance by 0.5% to 1% per week, often for a few weeks. Given 
our $2.1tr full year 2026 supply forecast, that translates to weekly supply running $10 to 
$20bn below the seasonal trend. 
However, the big drivers of IG supply are currently the funding needs for AI and M&A. 
That supply is unlikely to be cancelled due to higher borrowing costs. That means the 
selloff in rates is simply delaying the issuance into November or potentially 2027.

The current environment of rising yields and potentially lower equity prices as the Fed 
hikes rates is bad for inflows to bond mutual funds and ETFs. In fact, inflows have 
already slowed so far in September (Exhibit 6).

First, bond investors tend to chase returns. As a result, a big drop in bond prices often 
triggers outflows. For example, during the previous hiking cycle in 2022, the selloff in 
rates triggered large outflows that peaked in May 2022 (Exhibit 7). 
Second, higher stock prices trigger rebalancing out of equity and into fixed income. The 
Fed that is trying to tighten financial conditions would not be consistent with another 
big leg up in equities. That should also weigh on inflows to high grade bond funds and 
ETFs (Exhibit 8).

Spread curves: weak 10yr tenor 
A hawkish Fed and a flatter Treasury yield curve support spreads in the front end (see 
Situation Room: September FOMC: A spread curve steepener). Higher yields and the 
resulting drop in 30yr supply support spreads in the back end (see Situation Room: More 
yield, less duration). That means 10yr is the most vulnerable point on the IG spread curve 
in the current environment. Moreover, 10yr spreads also tend to underperform on the 
back of outflows from HG funds and ETFs (Exhibit 9). As a result, we expect the wings (5 
and 30yr) to continue to outperform the belly (10yr) on the IG spread curve.

Situation Room: Mini rate shock 23 September 2026 
 
Mini rate shock 
Wednesday’s big interest rate selloff is concerning as it illustrates the demand for bonds 
remains weak. Treasury yields jumped across the curve, such as +17bps for 5yr (Exhibit 
17). That was the largest move in almost two years (since Oct 2024). That was on the 
back of strong US (and European) PMI reports for September (Exhibit 15), a weak 5yr 
Treasury auction (3.1bps tail), and a +4% jump in Brent oil price.  
How surprising is the strong US economy? 
The strong US economy, highlighted by the PMIs, should not be a huge surprise. Chair 
Warsh repeatedly emphasized the US economic strength during the FOMC meeting last 
week (see report: US Watch: Sep FOMC review: Hike first, ask questions later). Instead, 
the price action likely highlights that investors are not yet comfortable owning bonds in 
the environment of a Fed restarting the hiking cycle, continued conflict in the Middle 
East, and a resilient US economy and consumer.  
Bad for demand 
Not surprisingly, the outsized move in yields triggered a sizeable jump in implied rate vol 
(Exhibit 14). Realized rate vol also remains well above the lows in 1H-2026 (Exhibit 18). 
The elevated rate vol is bad for demand and offsets the bullish impact on spreads from 
the relatively less heavy IG supply volumes in September.

Situation Room: French Yankee banks vs French sovereign 21 
September 2026 
 
French Yankee banks vs French sovereign 
French government bond spreads to bunds have widened to over 100bps on concerns 
about budget deficits and the upcoming elections (see Liquid Insight: French bonds 
under the radar). That’s the widest level since the European sovereign crisis in 2012 
(Exhibit 20). Not surprisingly, liquid 10yr French Yankee banks have cheapened as well, 
by about 10bps vs. the big six US banks since early July (Exhibit 21). 
Rich French bank Yankee spreads 
Still, Yankee French bank spreads appear rich based on two metrics. First, while the 
French sovereign spread is at 14-year wides, the spread ratio of Yankee French banks to 
the US big six banks remains still near the typical levels over the past two years (Exhibit 
19).  
Second, USD French bank spreads have outperformed EUR French bank spreads by 
about 5bps so far in September. As a result, USD spreads are currently at the richest 
levels relative to EUR YtD (Exhibit 22, Exhibit 23). Our recent European Credit Investor 
Survey shows 70% of European investors are currently negative on French Corporates 
(see Chart 20 here: Credit Investor Survey: Out of gas, into cash).

Inside, we screen for French Yankee bank bonds trading at the tightest spread 
percentiles YtD (Exhibit 25).

Situation Room: More yield, less duration 22 September 2026 
 
More yield, less duration 
As IG bond yields hit YtD highs, the duration of IG supply dropped to YtD lows so far in 
September (Exhibit 27). As a result, IG 10s30s spread curve continues to flatten, by 3bps 
so far in September and by 7bps from the recent steep levels in late July (Exhibit 28). We 
expect 30yr spreads to continue to outperform.  
First, our interest rate strategists expect 30yr Treasury yields to remain near the current 
levels into the year-end (5.25%, see Global Rates Weekly: From Jackson to action). That 
should support strong yield sensitive investor demand on the backend. Second, the 
duration of supply naturally falls when borrowing costs go up (Exhibit 30). That’s what is 
likely happening in September (Exhibit 26).  
Jumbo deals, such as hyperscalers and large M&A trades, provide an offset as they need 
to issue across the full curve (Exhibit 29). We look for slower hyperscaler supply for the 
remainder of the year (see Situation Room: Hyperscaler supply shock is fading), although 
M&A supply pipeline remains elevated (see Situation Room: Less M&A activity in August, 
funding pipeline still big). Still, on net, the duration of supply should be lower going 
forward, keeping the flattening pressure on the 10s30s IG spread curve.

Flows 
Stocks weak, loans strong, IG is ok 
IG ETFs reported inflows along with loans, while stocks reported outflows this past week 
ending Sep 23rd.  
Inflows accelerated for US HG bond funds and ETFs, with a +$7.15bn inflow up from a 
+$0.42bn inflow in the prior week. Since IG corporates account for about a third of the 
high grade universe AUM, that translates into ~$2.3bn demand for IG. The inflow was 
entirely from ETFs (to +$7.42bn from +$2.87bn), as flows were roughly flat for HG 
Funds (to -$0.27bn from -$2.45bn). Flows turned positive for short-term HG (to 
+$2.36bn from -$0.45bn), while inflows accelerated ex. short-term (to +$4.79bn from 
+$0.87bn). 
Appetite for floaters 
Stocks reported outflows this past week (to -$15.75bn, the biggest outflow in 3 months, 
from +$62.14bn), while flows turned positive for HY (to +$0.14bn from -$1.91bn). 
Inflows notably improved for loans (to +$0.38bn, the biggest inflow in 5 months, from 
+$0.09bn) and were steady for munis (to +$1.09bn from +$0.74bn). On the other hand, 
outflows continued from global EM bonds (to -$0.34bn from -$0.47bn). Finally, money 
markets reported a +$20.80bn inflow this past week, following a -$59.50bn outflow a 
week earlier.

Appendix: defining high grade 
We define our high grade flows metric as a combination of “bond” and “corporate bond” 
fixed income funds and ETFs domiciled in the US (and investing in any currency, 
although the majority is invested in USD). We also exclude funds primarily focused on 
Government, HY, EM and MBS from the sample. The “bond” category refers to the broad 
high grade market, which includes Treasuries, mortgages and agencies in addition to 
corporate bonds. This category accounts for the majority of AUM and flows. On the 
other hand, the dedicated corporate bond funds and ETFs are much smaller in terms of 
assets under management. We also include the “total return” bond category in our 
tracking of high grade flows. Finally, note that “short-term” maturity refers to duration 
of 0 to 4 years. On average, IG corporate bonds account for about ~32% of the total 
AUM of our high grade bond fund / ETF universe.  
 
Supply 
US IG gross issuance was $36.5bn this week, below our $50-$55bn estimate likely due 
to the increase in rates and rate vol. This week’s supply ending Sep-25 consisted of 
$16.6bn financials, $3.3bn high-quality industrials and $16.6bn BBB industrials. 
Given $1683.4bn of gross issuance, $760.6bn of maturities and $156.2bn of additional 
redemptions, net issuance is tracking $766.6bn YTD. We expect issuance to remain 
heavy next week in the $45-$55bn range led by potential M&A issuance, as suggested 
by news reports. We also note that the jump in yields and interest rate volatility could 
continue to weigh on any opportunistic issuance. For the month of September, we 
expect $190bn supply (see Situation Room: IG September supply: $190bn).  
New issue performance improved this week compared with the week of September 14 
as the average new issue concession declined to 3bps from 4bps last week, while the 
average break performance worsened at -2bps tighter this week versus -3bps tighter 
last week. As a result, the overall new issue performance, which we measure as new 
issue concession plus break performance, widened to 1bps this week from 1bps last 
week. This week’s new issues are trading 1bps tighter on average from pricing.
