Polymarket: Leading global prediction market operator 
Polymarket is a global prediction market operator with separate US and international 
platforms. In the US, Polymarket operates a CFTC-regulated exchange. Internationally, it 
operates a crypto-based exchange. Shayne Coplan founded Polymarket in 2020 as a DeFi 
platform, but in early 2022, the company agreed to stop offering contracts to US users 
following regulatory action by the CFTC. In 2025, federal investigations were closed, 
enabling the return to the US. Combined weekly volume across its US and international 
platforms has increased from ~$200M a year ago to more than $4B in July 2026. 
International: Leading crypto-based prediction market 
Polymarket gained mainstream recognition during the 2024 US presidential election. 
While polling produced mixed signals, Polymarket garnered attention for correctly 
forecasting President Trump's victory. The election helped popularize founder Shayne 
Coplan’s view that requiring participants to put money behind an outcome can produce 
more informative forecasts than polling alone. Today, Polymarket remains the leading 
DeFi prediction market, with ~99% of measured wallet activity, according to Allium.   
US: Gaining share in a competitive market  
Polymarket officially re-launched in the US in early 2026 and recently started brand 
campaigns for the football season. Despite heavy marketing investments from peers, 
Polymarket’s message is resonating – Polymarket has led app downloads in Sept. and 
gained 4pts of share, bringing US share to 12%, according to regulatory filings. 
Polymarket’s taker fees are largely in-line with peers, while Polymarket offers market 
maker rebates to support liquidity.  
Financials: Fee-based model, recently valued at $21B  
Polymarket primarily generates revenue through transaction fees charged for matching 
buyers and sellers. In the US, taker fees peak at ~1.74% of notional volume on a contract 
priced at $0.50, while market makers receive rebates that peak at ~31bps. Polymarket 
was most recently valued at $21B in a $1B fundraising round led by 1789 Capital, 
implying a ~17.5x annualized run rate revenue multiple compared with similar public 
comps at 13x.   
Growth areas: Non-sports, institutional, & global liquidity 
For prediction markets, potential areas of expansion include: 1) continued growth in 
sports, particularly combos; 2) non-sports markets, including short-duration crypto, 
political, and economic contracts; 3) institutional adoption for hedging and direct 
trading; and 4) data monetization. Polymarket has also discussed unifying its US. and 
international platforms, which could increase the liquidity available through a single 
order book.  
Risks: Regulation, market integrity and volume quality 
Sports event contracts face legal and regulatory challenges from states, tribes, gaming 
regulators, and federal lawmakers. Federal circuit courts are currently split on key legal 
questions, increasing the possibility of future Supreme Court review. Prediction markets 
also face scrutiny related to insider trading, market manipulation and wash trading.

Company background 
Polymarket was founded in 2020 by Shayne Coplan. Polymarket is an information 
markets platform, allowing users to put their money behind their views, rather than 
relying on traditional news platforms and social media for information. Polymarket 
started as a crypto based decentralized exchange, and Polymarket was banned in the US 
from January 2022 after regulatory scrutiny from the CFTC. Polymarket continued to 
operate offshore and gained mainstream popularity during the 2024 Presidential 
Election for correctly predicting the election outcome. In 2025, the DOJ and CFTC closed 
investigations, which enabled Polymarket’s return to the US. Polymarket acquired QCEX, 
which held registrations as a CFTC-designated contract market (DCM) and derivatives 
clearing organization (DCO), for $112 million. Today, Polymarket continues to grow its 
decentralized exchange outside of the US, and they are rapidly growing volume in the 
US.  
Timeline: How did we get here? 
Prediction markets (PMs) have existed for decades, with the CFTC granting its first 
prediction market license in 2004. While the concept is not new, Polymarket was the 
first prediction market platform to achieve broad retail adoption and meaningful 
consumer engagement. Its popularity grew offshore, particularly during the 2024 US. 
presidential election cycle, which helped bring PMs into the mainstream. At the same 
time, CFTC-regulated exchanges were gaining traction in the US., supported by two key

Decentralized crypto-based exchange 
Shayne Coplan built Polymarket as a decentralized exchange in 2020. Polymarket was 
built on the Polygon chain, with transactions denominated  in the USDC stablecoin. 
Polymarket emerged as a leader in crypto-based prediction markets, driven by its fast 
settlement and low-cost trades. In early 2022, Polymarket agreed to cease offering 
noncompliant event-contract markets to US users following CFTC enforcement action. 
The platform continued operating outside the United States.  
Decentralized exchange vs. centralized exchange 
Relative to a regulated US. prediction market exchange, Polymarket's offshore, 
decentralized exchange differs in several important ways, including how customer funds 
are held, how trades are matched, and how markets are resolved. Rather than depositing 
funds with a centralized operator, users maintain control of assets through blockchain-
based wallets and interact directly with smart contracts that facilitate trading and 
settlement. Trades are settled on chain and market activity is publicly visible, providing a 
level of transparency that is generally not available in traditional financial or betting 
markets. Users of decentralized exchanges are often attracted to this model because of 
its accessibility, transparency, global reach, and the ability to settle transactions nearly 
instantaneously without relying on a central intermediary.

weeks surrounding the US. presidential election, volume exceeded $4.5 billion. The 
election served as a catalyst that exposed millions of users to PMs for the first time, 
transforming Polymarket from a niche product into a mainstream source of real-time 
probability forecasting. 
- 
The French Whale: One of the election’s most widely publicized stories 
involved the so-called “French Whale,” a trader reportedly holding more than 
$70 million of exposure to a Donald Trump victory. The position attracted 
extensive media and social-media attention, further raising Polymarket’s profile.

Perpetual futures  
Polymarket recently launched perpetual futures, or “perps,” on its international platform. 
Perps are derivative contracts that allow traders to take leveraged directional positions 
in an asset without owning the underlying asset. Unlike traditional futures, perps have no 
expiration date. Venues use periodic funding payments to keep contract prices aligned 
with the underlying spot market. Perps appeal to retail traders because they provide 
capital-efficient exposure and leverage (Polymarket offers up to 50x).

Potential for an Airdrop 
Another factor that has likely contributed to Polymarket's international popularity is 
speculation surrounding a potential future token launch and associated airdrop. The 
speculation gained momentum after Shayne Coplan, founder of Polymarket, posted on 
social media in late 2025: “$BTC, $ETH, $BNB, $SOL, $POLY ৡৢৣ
৤”. While the company 
has never formally announced a token or airdrop, many users interpreted the post as a 
signal that a native "POLY" token could eventually be launched. In crypto markets, 
airdrops are commonly used to distribute new tokens to early adopters, with allocations 
often tied to a user's historical activity on a platform. As a result, the possibility of a 
future token reward could create a incentive for users to trade, provide liquidity, and 
remain active on the platform. 
Relaunching in the US: Polymarket is back  
In 2025, the US Department of Justice dropped charges against Polymarket, and 
Polymarket acquired DCM and DCO licenses from QCEX for $112M in July 2025. 
Polymarket soft launched in November 2025, opened up to the public in early 2026. This 
NFL season marks the first material marketing campaign we have seen for Polymarket – 
with a campaign featuring LeBron, Derek Jeter, among others.  
US Strategy  
Polymarket operates as a financial exchange, matching buyers and sellers through a 
centralized order book, similar to traditional exchanges such as CME Group and Cboe 
Global Markets. One of the unique aspects of prediction markets is that they can 
function as both the exchange and the consumer-facing brand. Historically, exchanges 
relied on third-party brokers for customer acquisition and order flow, but Polymarket has 
driven growth primarily through its own brand, social media presence, and consumer-
facing app/website. While some PM platforms have emphasized broker partnerships, 
Polymarket’s success has largely been driven by direct user engagement with relatively 
limited reliance on third-party distribution.

Business model and unit economics 
Revenue: The primary source of revenue for exchanges is trading fees from matching 
buyers and sellers. Polymarket earns a fee for every contract on its exchanges, but the 
US and international platforms have different fee structures. 
- 
US: In the US, Polymarket charges a market taker fee of 0.0695 x # of 
contracts x probability x (1 – probability). On a 50/50 contract, the taker is 
paying ~1.74% of the notional volume. Polymarket separately offers makers a 
rebate that peaks at 31bps of notional at a 50% probability. Both taker fees and 
maker rebates decline toward the probability tails.  Assuming a normal 
distribution of trades, Polymarket’s revenue is 1.4% of trading volume.  
- 
International: On the DeFi platform, Polymarket varies fees by market type. 
Crypto markets have the highest fees at 1.75% of notional volume for a 50/50 
contract. Sports/Economics/Culture peak at 1.25% of notional volume and 
finance/politics/tech markets have the lower fees at 1% of notional volume. 
Based on Polymarket’s volume mix year-to-date, taker fees are ~1.3% of 
volume. Polymarket offers market makers rebates of 15-25%.

Growth opportunities  
US prediction market volume Is heavily concentrated on sports, with more than 80% of 
volume for the industry coming from sports event contracts. When Polymarket launched 
in the US in late 2025, they only launched with sports contracts, but they have added 
contracts on politics, crypto, culture, and economics.  
Combo feature 
Combos, or parlays, have become an important driver of reported PM volume. Over the 
past month, combos represented ~50% of industry volume. Because combos tend to 
have lower odds, they require less customer capital than an even money contract and 
can cause notional volume to increase faster than customer dollars wagered. For 
example, a customer may pay $0.10 for a contract that settles at $1 if all legs win, 
compared with approximately $0.50 for a 50/50 contract. Increasing its combo mix could 
therefore help Polymarket narrow the notional volume gap with peers.

Appendix 
What is an event contract?  
Event futures contracts are derivative contracts with payoffs based on the occurrence of 
a specified event, like an election, the closing value of a stock index, or a sports contest 
or championship. Event contracts can be used to hedge the risk of a specified event (like 
a hurricane), but they can also be a tool for speculation. They are often framed as a “yes” 
or “no” question where the total denomination is $1: A contract pays $1 if an event 
happens and $0 if it does not. Traders can buy event contracts at any price between $0 
and $1 until the event or market is resolved. 
What is a prediction market?  
Prediction markets are exchanges on which participants trade contracts tied to future 
events. Most use a continuous double-auction model: buyers submit bids, sellers submit 
offers, and trades occur when prices match, similar to a stock exchange. Market makers 
may provide additional liquidity by continuously posting bids and offers.
