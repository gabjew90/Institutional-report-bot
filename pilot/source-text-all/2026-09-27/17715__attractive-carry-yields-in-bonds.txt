Central bank pricing is driven by inflation fear and robust economic growth
Source: UBS CIO GWM, Bloomberg, as of 25 September 2026, 11:30pm CET.
Bond market pricing of future central bank rates
CIO perspective: Re-accelerating energy end-product prices, possible El Niño-related food price inflation, and stronger-than-expected 
US and European economic growth are delaying a decline in inflation and prompting central banks to be more restrictive. However, 
the market pricing of hikes looks excessive. High carry yields make positive total returns over 12 months very likely, in our view.
Top preference: Bonds are attractive. Prefer short-to-medium tenors in corporate bonds and up to 10-years in high quality bonds.

Corporate bonds: In a tight market, selectivity is everything
Corporate bonds continue to offer an attractive combination of income and 
resilience. With yields still near multi-year highs, balance sheets largely 
sound, and yield curves comparatively steep, we expect annual mid to 
high single-digit returns over the medium term.
Selectivity remains key, however. Uncertainty around growth, inflation and 
geopolitics is likely to persist, while M&A activity, bond supply, and issuer 
specific risks should continue to drive dispersion at the single-name level.
In IG, we continue to favor bonds with mid term maturities issued by 
quality A/BBB rated names. We also like exposure to national 
champion banks.
Within high yield, we favor higher-quality issuers with resilient business 
models, solid free cash flow generation, and manageable refinancing 
profiles. 
As an alternative to traditional high-yield exposure, we also continue to see 
value in selected hybrids issued by quality financial and non-financial 
companies. 
See Conviction calls to put cash to work for specific investment ideas.

French sovereign outlook further strained by higher cost of debt (report link)
Absence of a public consensus on reforms to achieve fiscal consolidation makes major deficit-reduction measures unlikely even after the presidential election. 
Higher bond yields complicate future deficit reduction, as the net cost of debt service is on a steep upward path.
We maintain a Deteriorating issuer credit outlook and expect the first agency to cut France to the “mid A” level by 2027. Current ratings are Moody’s: Aa3, 
negative; S&P and Fitch: A+, stable. At the current pace of fundamental deterioration, France should remain in the A category through 2030.
French bonds trade wider than BBB-rated Italian ones across the curve, suggesting the fiscal concerns are priced in. But heavy supply and reduced buying by 
Asian investors make valuations sensitive. There may be an upper limit to the France-Italy spread, so that bad French news may instead pull Italy wider.
A steep spread curve makes the mid-part of the sovereign and agencies’ curves most appealing. Pre-election spread spikes may offer attractive entry levels.

Sovereign debt affordability strained by higher yields (report link)
If bond yields stay higher for longer, fiscal pressures would rise globally, with the greatest strain on countries already facing deteriorating credit outlooks.
Higher term premia and real yields could amplify the economic impact of energy supply constraints, creating a negative feedback loop between yields and fiscal 
dynamics that would need to be broken by central banks.
Japan and the US appear most exposed to funding stress and most likely to respond with policy measures; such actions would likely have positive spillovers for 
global bond markets.
Investors positioning for potential interventions via long duration bonds should consider the risk of delayed or constrained policy responses, as well as cross-asset 
implications, particularly for currencies.
