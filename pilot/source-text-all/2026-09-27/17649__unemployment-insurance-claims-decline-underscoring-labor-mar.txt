Unemployment Insurance Claims Decline, 
Underscoring Labor Market Stability 
Headlines 
 
Initial unemployment insurance claims fell by 1,000 in the week ending 
September 19. 
 
 
The four-week moving average of initial claims moved closer to 200,000, 
a level that has provided a floor for claims over the past two years. 
 
 
Seasonally adjusted continuing claims fell to 1.719 million in the week 
ending September 12. 
Details
The Unemployment Insurance (UI) Claims report for 
the week of September 19, 2026 continued to point 
to a stable U.S. labor market. UI claims remain well 
below averages seen prior to the COVID-19 
pandemic and are again approaching a four-week 
moving average of 200,000, a level claims have 
struggled to move below during the past two years 
(see Figure 1). Payroll employment growth has 
averaged roughly 80,000 jobs per month in 2026, 
modestly above the pace needed to absorb new 
entrants to the labor force. The breadth of hiring has 
also expanded in recent months to include 
manufacturing and other heavy-industry positions. 
Low initial and continuing claims are consistent with 
labor market data showing continued job growth 
and limited layoffs. 
 
Continuing UI claims declined in the week of 
September 12, 2026 and now sit more than 10% lower 
than year-ago levels (see Figure 2). Continuing 
claims remain exceptionally low by historical 
standards and have trended modestly lower in 
recent months. If sustained, they could approach 
their pre-pandemic lows. 
The Federal Reserve recently began raising interest 
rates, which PNC expects will continue over the 
coming quarters, to slow inflation that remains 
above its 2% target. Although job growth has 
remained steady despite policy uncertainty and 
rapid technological change, tighter monetary 
policy 
could 
weigh 
on 
hiring 
and 
business 
investment. Initial and continuing unemployment 
insurance claims provide a timely measure of labor 
market conditions and will be closely watched for 
signs that higher interest rates are affecting hiring 
and layoffs. 
 
At the regional level, the manufacturing-heavy 
upper Midwest region saw a small collective rise in 
UI claims in the week of September 19 after similarly 
small gains in the prior week. Employment in the 
Manufacturing sector, as well as in Transportation & 
Utilities, began rising in the middle of this year after 
several years of outright declines. Continued hiring 
in these sectors could help support labor market 
conditions in states such as Michigan, Indiana, and 
Ohio. Hiring in manufacturing and transportation is
