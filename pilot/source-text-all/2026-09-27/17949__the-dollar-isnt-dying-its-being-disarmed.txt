Forget de-dollarization
The market has spent years asking what will replace the dollar. That may be the wrong question.
TS Lombard argues that the real BRICS objective is increasingly dollar diversification, not dollar
replacement. China, in particular, appears focused on building enough financial autonomy to survive a
future US sanctions campaign rather than replacing the dollar with the RMB as the world's reserve
currency.
The distinction matters. The dollar can remain king while becoming harder to weaponize.
King dollar isn't dead
The numbers don't support the dramatic de-dollarization narrative.
Only around 2.5% of Indian imports were settled in rupees in FY26, while RMB invoicing accounted for
just 1.36% of Brazilian imports in 2025. Deep dollar liquidity, huge US capital markets and
convertibility remain extremely difficult to replicate.
TS Lombard expects the dollar to reign into the 2030s.
This isn't about dethroning the dollar. It is about building an escape route.
TS Lombard
Russia was the live experiment
Russia provided the world's first real stress test of what happens when a large economy gets hit with
the full force of the dollar weapon.
The lesson was two-sided. Russia found ways around SWIFT, and China-Russia trade moved
overwhelmingly into rubles and RMB. But alternative messaging systems did not eliminate the bigger
problem: correspondent banking, liquidity and convertibility remain powerful US-controlled choke
points.
For Beijing, the lesson looks obvious:
Don't wait to be sanctioned before building the insurance.
TS Lombard
China is buying insurance
China has spent years building the plumbing.
CIPS has expanded from 217 participant banks to 1,829, while CIPS together with Hong Kong's CHATS
clears around RMB3 trillion of payments per day. Beijing is simultaneously pushing RMB trade
settlement, swap lines, offshore RMB assets and yuan-denominated commodity contracts.

None of this makes the RMB the new dollar.
It does something more immediately useful: it raises China's ability to keep transacting if Washington
ever reaches for the financial bazooka.
TS Lombard
Watch 2027–28
This isn't only a China story.
India's UPI, Brazil's Pix, Europe's TIPS, Project Nexus and Africa's PAPSS are gradually creating a web of
instant-payment systems outside the traditional correspondent-banking architecture.
TS Lombard sees 2027–28 as the important window, as more of these rails begin linking together. The
volumes remain small, but the plumbing is arriving before the flows.
That's the non-obvious point:
You don't need a BRICS currency. You need interoperable rails.
TS Lombard
The great dollar irony
There is one major problem with the de-dollarization story: technology is also making dollars easier to
own.
Dollar-backed stablecoins are producing what TS Lombard calls “accidental dollarization” across parts
of EM. Brazil is a striking example: stablecoins account for more than 90% of crypto flows even as
policymakers try to reduce dependence on the dollar.
Governments are building infrastructure to escape the dollar while their citizens increasingly access
digital dollars outside the traditional banking system.
The dollar's next distribution network may therefore be crypto.
Don't fight the tape
None of this automatically gives you a bearish dollar trade.
In fact, that's arguably the most important market distinction. Structural efforts to neutralize US
sanctions power can accelerate while the dollar itself remains dominant and trades strongly.
The long-term DXY trend continues to hold remarkably well.
The geopolitical story and the FX trade are not the same thing. De-weaponization does not mean de-
dollarization.
