Growth in Key Verticals Excl. Data Centers Continues While Billings Grow 
Sequentially: The Dodge Momentum Index was relatively flat sequentially in August 
compared to +4% in July led by a moderation in data centers while planning across 
other key sectors accelerates. Conversely, the ABI improved sequentially to 47.2 with 
positive billings in the commercial/industrial sector despite others remaining in 
negative territory. Geographically, the improvement was led by the Midwest followed 
by the West that moved closer to the 50-mark. Although we are encouraged by the 
recent expansion, macro uncertainty and higher rates remain an overhang to a 
broader, more consistent recovery. In the meantime, we look for relative 
outperformance in healthcare, transportation, and education given industry-specific 
dynamics. Given these sectors represent ~60% of AWI’s sales, along with greater 
exposure to R&R and company-specific initiatives, we maintain our positive view on 
the stock. We also believe CSL is well positioned led by the re-roof cycle along with a 
focus on innovation and cost improvements, allowing it to better leverage the shift 
towards more energy efficient buildings. 
Office Utilization Mixed Across Top 10 Metros: Utilization across metros was 53% 
as of September 2, up 180bps YOY but down 120bps sequentially and 370bps below 
the post pandemic high in March, per Kastle card swipe data. Utilization was mixed 
sequentially with declines in 6 out of 10 metros led by New York down 9%, followed 
by Austin off 4% and Houston, Los Angeles, Philadelphia down 1% each while Dallas 
rose 3%. However, utilization remains above early 2025 levels as employees return to 
the office. 
Non-Res Exposure in our Coverage Led by Armstrong & Carlisle (on CL): With 
~95% of AWI’s revenues from this end market, we look for it to realize the greatest 
impact from the initiatives and operating environment described above, followed by 
CSL at ~80%. Others within our coverage could also see implications, though to a 
lesser extent, including Owens Corning, A.O. Smith, and Mohawk, all of which derive 
20+% of sales from non-res construction.

Key Macro Factors 
 
 
Architecture Billings Index 
The August Architecture Billings Index (ABI) improved to 47.2 from 46.6 in August as 
commercial rose sequentially to 50.4 from 46.7 in July while all other sectors moderated. 
Mixed-use deteriorated the most vs July at 42.5 from 43.2 followed by residential and 
institutional declining to 47.8 and 47.0, respectively, from 48.4 and 47.4 last month. We 
note billings have been negative for 20+ months, one of the longest sustained downturns 
in the industry as architecture firms deal with inflation and elevated borrowing costs. 
This is in addition to labor constraints that are weighing on activity in select verticals. We 
note the ABI is a leading indicator of spending for non-residential construction activity, 
with an average lead time of 9-12 months. 
 
Billings in the Midwest moved into positive territory at 50.7 from 46.7 last month while 
the other three regions stayed negative. That said, the West was close to expansion at 
49.8 vs 47.8 in July, while the Northeast and South moderated to 40.7 and 46.1, 
respectively, from 44.8 and 48.7 last month.

Dodge Momentum Index  
The index came in at 282.0 in August off 0.4% from the downwardly-revised July reading 
of 283.0 but up 4.2% YOY. The sequential move reflects moderation in data centers 
offset by acceleration in most other key sectors. We note 31 projects valued at $100+mn 
entered the planning stage this month including four data centers at $350+mn each. 
Specifically, commercial fell 3.0% sequentially but was up 2.3% YOY, well above 2019 
levels given a surge in data center planning in late-2025. Excluding this vertical, the 
sector was relatively flat with July as retail and warehouses slowed while office buildings, 
parking garages, and hotels strengthened. Institutional planning gained 7.8% YOY and 
was up 4.9% vs July led by an acceleration in education, recreational, and public building 
projects while healthcare moderated slightly after four months of consecutive growth. 
Despite the slowdown this month, we expect data centers to continue to support 
activity into late 2027. That said, we will keep an eye on headwinds that could delay 
projects including inflation, labor, and supply chain disruptions as well as the broader 
macro. The Dodge Momentum Index is a 12-18 month leading indicator of 
non-residential construction spending as it measures initial reports for projects.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
