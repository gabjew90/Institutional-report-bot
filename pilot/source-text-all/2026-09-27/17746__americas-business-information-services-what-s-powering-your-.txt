with our TMT and Consumer Business Unit colleagues on the long-term transition 
toward agentic commerce. We believe AI agents will increasingly shape discovery, 
comparison, purchasing and payments over the next 3-5+ years, shifting value 
toward platforms that control consumer intent and the infrastructure needed to 
convert it into trusted transactions. Our positively skewed names include EFX, TRU

resilience scores based on our framework have a statistically significant relationship

Americas Technology: Entering the Agentic AI Era for the Commerce Landscape. 
Beginning with the launch of ChatGPT in November 2022 and the subsequent scaling of 
large language models (LLMs), and continuing more recently with conversational agentic 
canvases being increasingly adopted by 1b+ global consumers, AI is increasingly 
impacting and reshaping the consumer shopping journey. While adoption today is 
concentrated in product discovery, research, and recommendations, we expect steady, 
measured progress toward end-to-end agentic shopping, where AI can increasingly act 
on behalf of consumers across discovery, comparison, purchase, and payment. We view 
this as a long-duration transition (think over the next 3-5+ years), analogous to the 
development of eCommerce itself, rather than an overnight disruption (most notably 
since consumer computing habits change slower than the frontier of technological 
enablement). Our central conclusion is that value should increasingly follow consumer 
intent. As AI agents become a more important starting point for shopping, the strategic

Americas Business & Information Services: Written Off but Still Winning Part III: 
Testing AI Resilience Against Organic Growth. In Part III of our Written Off but Still 
Winning series, we test whether structural AI resilience is visible in fundamentals across 
Information Services. Part I introduced our segment-level framework for scoring 
companies’ structural AI resilience across proprietary data, regulatory moat, workflow 
entrenchment and output accountability, and Part II compared those scores with stock 
performance. We now evaluate resilience against eight quarters of organic revenue 
growth, examining growth level, variability and recent momentum. We find that 
companies with higher AI resilience have delivered stronger organic growth: Tier 1 
companies generated average growth of 11.2%, compared with 7.6% for Tier 2 and 
2.7% for Tier 3. Statistical testing supports this relationship, though AI resilience does 
not correspond with lower growth variability because pricing actions, transaction 
volumes and end-market cycles remain important drivers of reported results. Evidence 
from our subsequent AI research, earnings and the 2026 Communacopia + Technology 
Conference reinforces our published scores while sharpening the importance of 
workflow control and monetization. We combine these findings with valuation to 
identify Buy-rated MCO, SPGI and TRI as best positioned. In contrast, we remain 
cautious on Sell-rated FDS since it carries the lowest resilience score in our Info Services 
universe, and improving near-term execution and AI monetization do not eliminate 
longer-term substitution risk across replicable financial-data workflows, in our view. Link 
to our 9/22 note here.

Chart of the Week 
Agentic Commerce Expands Identity and Risk Decisioning. Agentic commerce 
requires payment controls to incorporate new agent, identity and transaction context. 
Emerging commerce and payment protocols provide much of the technical 
infrastructure required for agents to interact with merchants, transmit consumer 
consent and initiate transactions. UCP standardizes agent-to-merchant communication 
across discovery, checkout and post-purchase workflows, and Visa Trusted Agent 
Protocol, Mastercard Agent Pay and AP2 provide mechanisms for agent registration, 
user-authentication claims, credential binding and transaction-level consent. Existing 
payment credentials, issuer authorization, merchant onboarding and network fraud 
controls will remain central to validating participants and protecting transactions in 
agentic commerce. That said, selected higher-risk events may require additional context 
to assess account-takeover or synthetic-identity risk, changes in the relationship 
between the consumer and payment account, unfamiliar merchant counterparties or 
financing requests subject to lender policies. Agentic commerce may therefore increase 
the value of identity verification, counterparty intelligence and real-time fraud and 
credit decisioning as complementary inputs to existing payment controls. EFX and TRU 
provide consumer, account, device and merchant-risk inputs that could support these 
assessments, with FICO providing fraud and credit decisioning infrastructure around 
events such as new account linkages, unfamiliar merchants and financing requests.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
