Production outlook in Venezuela 
 
 
On the back of the Chevron and ENI announcements in Venezuela on September 2, we 
update our Top Projects modelling for Venezuela in this note. In addition, the 
US-Venezuela oil agreement signed by Secretary of State, Marco Rubio, and Secretary of 
War, Pete Hegseth, on 2 September implies further upside to our current modelled 
numbers. 
The deal gave the US majority control of >65 bn bbl of Venezuelan reserves, granting the 
US government governance rights, economic ownership and guaranteed low-cost 
off-take from a new private Venezuelan oil champion poised to be the second-largest 
private oil company by reserves in the world. Under the agreement, Venezuelan interim 
authorities granted North American Blue Energy Partners (NABEP), a privately held 
operator and the second-largest private Venezuelan oil producer, 100-year concessions 
on 17 oil fields holding c.65 bn bbl of proven reserves. In connection with this 
agreement, the US Department of War’s Office of Strategic Capital was granted a 35% 
equity stake in NABEP’s parent, and the US Department of State secured the right to 
purchase 20% of NABEP’s off-take at production cost, with right of first refusal on the 
remaining 80%. NABEP has flagged intent to invest up to $100bn over 25 years, with 
output routed through US refineries. 
As a result, we see Venezuelan crude reaching c.1.9 mboed by 2030 (+945 kboed vs 
2025) and c.2.3 mboed by 2035 with upside to those numbers if more companies come 
on-stream, on the private side or public side such as the recent Continental Resources 
announcement. 
On Oil, the biggest growth contributions come from Chevron and ENI. Chevron is 
targeting c.600 kboed by 2031 from 280 kboed under a $7bn 5-year plan across its 
Petropiar, Petroindependencia and Petroboscan JVs. ENI has secured a 25-year licence 
over c.35 bn bbl of certified oil in place at Junin-5 and guided to a ramp >200 kboed. 
Repsol plans to triple oil production by 2028 from c.45 kboed today, mainly from the 
Petroquiriquire field, and GeoPark (Not Covered) aims to ramp Bare from c.10 kboed to 
c.90 kboed by 2038 following its latest transaction. 
On gas, we see c.140 mboed added on Dragon (Shell) and Loran (BP/XRG) with potential 
FID in 2027E/28E, feeding the existing Trinidad Atlantic LNG plant and further upside 
from Perla, where Repsol and ENI have flagged FLNG export ambitions that we have not 
yet captured in our numbers.

Economics  
 
 
On the economics side, the new fiscal regime (c.56% government take under LOH 2026) 
has materially improved project-level economics with an oil breakeven of c.$58/bbl, 
placing Venezuela in the 2nd quartile of the cost curve (below the 50th percentile at 
$64.5/bbl). Venezuela’s gas breakeven of c.$3.8/mcf (peak-weighted across Perla, 
Dragon and Loran) places it in the 2nd quartile of the gas cost curve (below the 50th 
percentile at $4.3/mcf). 
The transformation in Venezuelan project economics runs entirely through the new fiscal 
package under the Ley Organica de Hidrocarburos (LOH). Under the reformed LOH 
2026, the fiscal framework is built around two new contract types. The CPPH (Contrato 
de Produccion y Participacion de Hidrocarburos) applies to ENI at Junin 5 - a 25-year 
contract with 25% royalty and 35% income tax, with ENI as exclusive operator. Chevron’s 
revised JV agreements at Petroindependencia, Petropiar and Petroboscan carry 
enhanced fiscal, commercial and legal terms which management describes as 
supporting total costs below $20/bbl, a c.$35/bbl breakeven and a >50% IRR at $70 
Brent.  
On an NPV-at-15% basis, we see the largest value in Venezuelan assets for Chevron 
($6.4bn), ENI ($4.8bn) and Repsol ($1.9bn), with potential Dragon/Loran FID derisking 
further value for Shell ($0.3bn) and BP ($0.1bn).

Oil Services 
 
 
Tenaris (TENR.MI, Neutral): Tenaris has been present in Venezuela for many years, and 
currently supplies Chevron OCTG needs in the country. Chevron has confirmed business 
as usual with all current orders. Tenaris has long-term relationships both with current 
players like Chevron, ENI and Repsol, and US majors that could potentially increase its 
presence in the country (XOM and COP). In addition, Tenaris owns a local office (service 
center) in Venezuela, and TuboCaribe facility in Colombia which includes 105kt of 
welded pipe manufacturing capacity and a 250kt seamless pipe finishing plant. This 
might provide a logistical advantage to Tenaris to expand business in Venezuela. 
Vallourec (VLLP.PA, Buy): Vallourec delivered OCTG to Venezuela in the 1990s and 
currently does not have presence in the country. The company used to supply premium 
OCTG solutions from a technical standpoint (sour service casings/13Cr or CRA tubings 
for corrosive/high temperature/high pressure conditions). These pipes were previously 
supplied from Vallourec’s European mills. Today, with production concentrated in Brazil 
rather than Germany, Vallourec could benefit from a significantly more competitive cost 
base and logistics chain.  
Venezuela OCTG market would require complex higher-premium OCTG solutions. 
The majority of Venezuela’s 300 bn barrels of proven oil reserves are located in the 
Orinoco Oil Belt, where primarily onshore crude is high-sulfur and heavy. Orinoco 
crude has API gravity of 9.5–12, and sulfur content of 4%–5%, making it comparable to 
Canada’s oil sands bitumen (link). The Orinoco Belt’s heavy crude oil is extracted by 
horizontal multilateral wells (link). This technique is called cold heavy oil production 
(CHOPs). This is when sand is injected into a perforated hole, resulting channels allow 
crude oil to flow to the surface, either by itself or via secondary recovery techniques. For 
multilateral wells, OCTG requirements focus on high-performance connections, robust 
materials (like corrosion resistant alloys or CRAs), and connection testing protocols that 
simulate complex downhole conditions (pressure, temperature, high doglegs, 
mechanical loads, fatigue, and corrosion) to ensure well integrity, especially for 
production casing that supports multiple branches and stimulation cycles.  
We estimate Venezuela OCTG demand could reach 140-240kt by 2030; and $0.6-1bn 
market size. Given an absence of reliable historical OCTG consumption data, we look at 
Venezuelas’ historical Rig & Well count data to estimate potential OCTG market size 
opportunity. 
Rig count. The 2007-16 average rig count in Venezuela amounted to 70 rigs, according 
to Baker Hughes, at 2.4 mbpd oil production. Based on the historical relationship 
between production and rig count, 2 mbpd oil production (which our commodities team 
uses to estimate downside risk to their 2030 oil price forecast) would require 60 active 
rigs. At the same time, given efficiency improvements for newer rigs, we assume a 40-50 
active rig count range by 2030. 
Wells per rig. Assuming 12 wells drilled per rig per year in Venezuela, we estimate 
480-600 new wells could be drilled annually by 2030. 
OCTG per well. Per-well OCTG pipe consumption ranges from more than 1,000 tons in 
some U.S. offshore wells to 500-700 tons in horizontal wells in onshore U.S. resource 
plays, to a little more than 100 tons in vertical wells in conventional fields (link).

Operating practice in Venezuela has settled around horizontal wells with extensions of 
1,000-2,000 meters as the preferred procedure as of 2021 (link), which is compared to 
>3,000 meters in the US shale (10,000 feet). We therefore assume 300-400 tons OCTG 
per well, 40% lower than in US shale horizontal wells. 
Using the assumptions above, we arrive at 140-240 kt of OCTG demand in Venezuela 
by 2030. With premium OCTG solutions ranging from $3,000 to 7,000/t, we assume a 
$4,000/t OCTG price and arrive at $0.6-1bn total OCTG market size in Venezuela by 
2030.  
The current addressable OCTG market size for Tenaris and Vallourec is 5.7 mn t and 
c.$18bn, on our estimates, which implies the Venezuela market could add 3-4% to 
their addressable demand in volume terms and 3-5% in dollar terms given 
potentially more premium pricing (necessary due to the primarily high-sulfur and 
heavy crude in the country).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.

Ratings, coverage universe and related definitions 
Buy (B), Neutral (N), Sell (S) Analysts recommend stocks as Buys or Sells for inclusion on various regional Investment Lists. Being assigned a Buy or Sell 
on an Investment List is determined by a stock’s total return potential relative to its coverage universe. Any stock not assigned as a Buy or a Sell on an 
Investment List with an active rating (i.e., a  stock that is not Rating Suspended, Not Rated, Early-Stage Biotech, Coverage Suspended or Not Covered), is 
deemed Neutral. Each region manages Regional Conviction Lists, which are selected from Buy rated stocks on the respective region’s Investment Lists 
and represent investment recommendations focused on the size of the total return potential and/or the likelihood of the realization of the return across 
their respective areas of coverage.  The addition or removal of stocks from such Conviction Lists are managed by the Investment Review Committee or 
other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts’ investment rating for such stocks.    
Total return potential represents the upside or downside differential between the current share price and the price target, including all paid or 
anticipated dividends, expected during the time horizon associated with the price target. Price targets are required for all covered stocks. The total 
return potential, price target and associated time horizon are stated in each report adding or reiterating an Investment List membership.
