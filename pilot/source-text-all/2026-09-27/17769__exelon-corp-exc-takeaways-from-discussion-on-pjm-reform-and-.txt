On 9/24, we hosted a discussion with Mr. Will Sauer, Exelon’s Vice 
President of Federal Regulatory Affairs, to discuss the company’s 
federal regulatory and power market reform priorities. The company 
remains focused on working to accommodate large load growth 
while protecting existing customers through TSAs, supporting grid 
reliability, and providing new customers with optionality. Ultimately, 
EXC does not believe the current capacity market construct in PJM is 
adequate to incentivize new supply, preferring more of a prompt 
capacity procurement process that would give states and customers 
a larger roll in resource planning and reorient the PJM auction to a 
residual one. In addition, the company sees potential for heightened 
scrutiny around data centers and bill inflation to persist beyond 
November, with opportunities for more trust to be built between 
consumers and data center developers.  
We continue to see policy and regulatory uncertainty ahead of 
November as a key headwind to our coverage which is currently 
trading at an average 15.4x P/E (ex. CA) on our 2027 estimates, 
relative to over 17x on average historically. With EXC operating in 
jurisdictions where bill inflation has been elevated and data center 
policy has been uncertain, we continue to monitor headlines and 
regulatory outcomes that could impact the company’s capital plan 
or earnings growth outlook, which we currently see at 6% on 
average through 2030.  
PJM’s current market structure has proven inadequate as a 
useful supply tool, but is undergoing several constructive 
reforms. EXC addressed PJM’s recent capacity auctions which have 
yielded record-high prices and have not delivered adequate new 
generation. The company views the Reliability Backstop 
Procurement (RBP) and the Interim Resource Adequacy Service 
(IRAS) as important steps but ultimately stopgap measures that are 
not expected to impact EXC’s capital plan. Broader reform is still

purpose of the capacity auction as a residual resource. EXC sees merit in shifting to a 
prompt capacity procurement process that is closer to the delivery timeline and 
removes the reliance on long-term, uncertain load forecasts. It would also allow states 
and large customers to take a larger role in resource planning, in EXC’s view. While EXC 
does not expect PJM to completely move away from the capacity market, the current 
three-year framework is clearly not optimal.  
Protecting existing ratepayers is key priority while integrating large-load growth. 
Two main ways this has manifested is through EXC’s advocacy around colocation and 
implementation of Transmission Security Agreements (TSAs). On colocation, EXC 
recognizes the benefits of colocation like time to market, and is focused on protecting 
existing customers, grid reliability, and giving large load customers optionality moving 
forward. The company expects clarity from the FERC on colocation in 1H27, though 
notes there is still more debate on the effective data of the new transmission service. To 
address those focus areas, EXC is focused on its TSAs to protect customers in the event a 
project does not materialize. TSAs require data centers to post financial collateral where 
~40% of Exelon’s 11 GW high-probability pipeline is already TSA-backed, representing 
$1bn in posted collateral. These agreements solidify the transmission revenue 
requirement, filter out speculative pipeline projects, and provide protections for existing 
ratepayers if projects fail to materialize. 
Heightened scrutiny around data centers and bill inflation into the midterm 
elections. Affordability and data center-related load growth are in focus at all levels of 
government, including the federal policy level and there is heightened political scrutiny 
on these topics in campaigns. EXC noted that data center opposition is bipartisan and 
sees potential for legislation to move forward around the rate payer protection pledge, 
or perhaps around permitting reform, which could include siting and cost allocation 
among other provisions. While the current headlines have weighed on stocks to a higher 
degree recently with the midterms coming up, EXC generally views affordability as a 
more sticky issue, particularly given the shift post midterms into the set up for the 2028 
presidential election. EXC sees an opportunity for both utilities and data center 
developers/customers to help articulate the value of data center development to 
customers and foster more trust with consumers. 
Valuation and key risks 
 
 
We are Sell-rated on EXC, with a 12-month $45 price target based on an unchanged 
14.75x Q5-Q8 P/E multiple. Key risks for EXC include: 1) regulatory outcomes, 2) cost 
management, 3) balance sheet cushion relative to downgrade threshold appealing to 
investors seeking a defensive name, and 4) incremental capex opportunities beyond the 
base level T&D spend.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
