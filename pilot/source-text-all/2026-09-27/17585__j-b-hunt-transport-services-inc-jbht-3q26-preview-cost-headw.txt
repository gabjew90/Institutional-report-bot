Bottom Line: JBHT indicated that, due to various cost-related 
pressures (cumulative $45m; details below), the 3Q seasonal 
progression would be adversely impacted with EBIT showing a 
decline in the range of 5%-10% - which compares to our prior 
forecast for an ~11% increase QOQ.   
JBHT announced during a recent conference that 3Q would have 
headwinds of $10m from higher fuel costs, $10m from higher claims 
cost (largely cargo claims), and $25m from higher driver pay through 
sign-on bonuses, safety bonuses, and stay-on bonuses. It is our 
impression that majority of these costs rest in Dedicated given the 
larger population of drivers and tractors; however,some of the costs 
are felt in intermodal as well. The challenge for 3Q and in the near to 
medium term is understanding how long these headwinds persist or 
if they fall away after 3Q. That said, what is encouraging is that JBHT 
has reiterated strength in demand for their intermodal and 
dedicated product that is requiring them to be prudent in what type 
of freight they accept in order to manage revenue quality and cost. 
What will be key as we move into 2027 is if JBHT can translate that 
demand into revenue per load growth and begin to narrow the gap 
between intermodal and TL pricing to cover their cost to serve and 
improve margin.  
While some of the costs could prove transitory around fuel and 
claims, there is some uncertainty as to how quickly driver related 
cost pressures around retention bonuses, recruiting cost, etc. will 
linger in the coming quarter or two.  Given the uncertain timing, 
we think it prudent to reset our margin level lower as we exit 
2026, which impacts the trajectory for margin level in the out 
quarters (and lowers our estimates in both years; details below), 
although we note that we assume a return to normal to somewhat 
better-than-normal sequential margin outlook beginning in 2027, as 
well as a profit per load estimate between $0.28-$0.30 which stacks 
up better than the pre-Covid norm of closer to $0.25 (in thousands).

read-through perspective – we think other than potential fuel drag on some operating 
ratios we are not hearing of other related cost induced pressure on 3Q EPS. 
We introduce greater detail into our revenue per load estimate in intermodal in our latest 
model. Previously, we estimated revenue per load including fuel but did not estimate 
what fuel or revenue per load ex-fuel could be. Using the YoY change in historical 
revenue per load including fuel, we calculate estimated fuel surcharge change YoY by 
subtracting the YoY change in reported revenue per load excluding fuel. Using the 
historical relationship of the fuel surcharge change YoY with diesel prices YoY, we can 
project what we believe the surcharge could be based on diesel futures. This allows us to 
consider how fuel prices influence overall revenue per load based on what we believe 
pricing excluding fuel could be (Exhibit 1). 
 
We lower our 3Q26 EPS estimate from $2.15 to $1.80 on the lower EBIT trajectory from 
2Q to 3Q due to larger than expected headwinds. Our 2026 EPS estimate declines from 
$7.75 to $7.15 as we reset our margins lower given the uncertain duration of these 
headwinds. We lower our 2027 EPS estimate from $9.50 to $9.00 and our 2028 EPS 
estimate from $10.60 to $10.10 on the lower margin reset but note that we anticipate a 
return to normal to better than normal seasonality in 2027.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
