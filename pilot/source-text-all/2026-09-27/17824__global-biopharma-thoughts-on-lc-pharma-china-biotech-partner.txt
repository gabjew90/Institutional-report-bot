Global Pharma Is Increasingly Leveraging Chinese Biopharma To Build 
Pipelines
In recent years there has been rapid growth in China’s biotech sector, as well as a marked increase in US/EU Pharma 
companies turning to China to augment internal R&D programs. Overall, we expect China biotechs will remain a 
meaningful oppty for US/EU pharma going forward and would not be surprised to see the recent uptick in biz dev 
continue.
◼Pursuing biz dev in China has a number of advantages for US/EU pharma. 
◼Biz dev in China can help US/EU pharmas more effectively build out pipelines, often with less investment required vs 
US/EU biz dev. And with many US/EU names approaching patent cliffs, we expect biz dev will remain a focus for the 
group. 
◼In addition, running early-stage trials in China is easier/faster vs in the US/EU, allowing companies to accelerate early 
development of attractive assets. Companies can then leverage this data to better inform global studies.
◼At the same time, we acknowledge this increased oppty is somewhat offset by the increased competition from 
China. 
◼Increased innovation from China could lead to increased competition going forward. 
◼This likely results in some companies altering disclosure practices (i.e. no longer disclosing ph1 mechanisms) to preserve 
first mover advantages/somewhat delay fast-followers. 
◼And while some Chinese biotechs will likely look to build global infrastructure over time, we see the US/EU pharmas well 
positioned in terms of financial capability, experience, and talent.
◼Partnerships thus far have concentrated on a handful of modalities/therapeutic indications but we expect this focus 
will broaden over time. 
◼To date, deals have focused on incretins given the unprecedented size of the obesity market oppty as well as PD-1/VEGF 
bispecs and ADCs given the remaining unmet need across various oncology indications.
Source: JP Morgan Research.

Key Questions Remain on The Oppty for China Biotech Deals
Source: JP Morgan Research. 
◼There has been debate around how well data generated in China-only populations will translate to global trials. 
◼Concerns have been more pronounced in indications where the populations differ meaningfully across geographies (e.g., 
age, gender), and where the SOC varies. 
◼Going forward, we expect the level of concern to remain indication-specific with obesity and similar indications being less 
controversial, while oncology is seen as having more translatability risk. 
◼We should gain additional clarity on the translatability of these trials over the next 6-12 months as additional global trials 
read out.
◼We see limited risk from proposed US policy changes aimed at restricting the use of clinical data generated in China 
and limiting US pharma collaboration with Chinese biotechs. 
◼The House has proposed prohibiting the use of data from certain countries, including China, to support US IND 
applications and has also proposed a bill that would add biotechnology to the COINS act, increasing government scrutiny 
of US/China biotech collaborations. 
◼We ultimately do not expect either of these bills to be passed in their current forms, and do not anticipate a meaningful 
impact on the US pharma industry’s clinical development strategies or the industry’s interest in China biotech partnerships.
◼We expect these partnerships will remain a key component of global drug development for the foreseeable future. 
◼We expect that many Chinese companies’ near-to-mid term (3-5 years) ex-China strategies will continue to focus on out-
licensing and partnership deals similar to the ones that are currently gaining traction. 
◼We continue to see these partnerships as win/win for the global pharma names, which benefit from increased pipeline 
breadth/new product flow, as well as for Chinese biopharmas, which are able to capture attractive global drug economics 
without having to build significant ex-China infrastructure.
◼Net-net, we see increased partnerships between US/EU pharmas and China biotechs as ultimately positive for both 
parties. On the US/EU side, these partnerships provide access to relatively affordable/de-risked BD oppties, allowing 
the companies to more effectively build pipelines & offset LOEs. And for the China biotechs, companies will be able 
to gain access to global markets more quickly/effectively. We ultimately would expect the number/size of 
partnerships to continue growing and to expand to new therapeutic areas/modalities over time.

Translatability of China Data to Global Studies Remains a Key Debate
One of the key questions for each of the China partnered programs is how well data generated from China-only trials 
will translate to a global population. While we see this largely depending on the indication, we expect that in general 
China data will roughly translate to global trials. We further expect that US/EU pharma companies will continue to 
leverage these China biotech partnerships to rapidly run a broad set of China-only studies that are then used to inform 
more selective/de-risked global registrational programs. 
◼Where we have less concern:
◼We see obesity and similar indications as less controversial. For the incretins, the meaningful body of data generated from 
LLY and Novo’s global programs clearly support translatability of China-only trials investigating similar mechanisms to 
global trials. 
◼We also have less concern for China trials that have demonstrated HRs that imply efficacy well beyond current SOC, 
especially when the SOC/control arms/background therapies are similar across geographies. 
◼Where we have more concern:
◼Indications where there are different SOCs/background therapies for different geographies will be harder to gauge 
translatability between China-only and global trials given the trials may have different control arms/combo regimens.
◼We would also see more risk around translatability if there are notable differences in the patient populations by geography 
(e.g. differences in ages or genders affected). 
◼Historically there have also been some trials where tolerability is better for China-only populations compared to a global 
population. There could be risk that tolerability is worse for a global population, which would in turn increase patient drop-
outs/impact the trial’s probability of having a significant result. 
◼Finally, we would have more questions around the potential translatability to a global trial if the China-only trial 
demonstrated a more borderline clinical signal given a global population is likely to introduce more variability in responses. 
Source: JP Morgan Research.

Limited Risk With US Proposed Regulatory Changes
We are watching two recently proposed regulatory changes from some US Congress members: 1) calls to ban the use of 
clinical trial data generated in China (and select other geographies), and 2) limit US Pharma investment in Chinese 
biotechs. While we will continue to monitor these motions, we ultimately think these headlines are more noise than a 
material risk to US-China biotech collaboration.
◼We see little risk of the US banning Chinese clinical trial data. The House recently passed the 2027 FDA appropriations 
bill with language that would prohibit using data from certain countries, including China, to support US investigational new drug 
applications. 
◼Supporters of the motion are concerned by the number of ph1/2 trials being run in China given limited ability of the FDA to 
inspect clinical trial sites/verify safety standards and the transfer of US tech/expertise to China. 
◼The House has also recently proposed a bill that would add biotechnology to the COINS act, making US deals with China 
subject to review (Biotech Investment National Security Act, BINSA). In addition, the Treasury department in its new rules 
under COINS act might add “biotechnology” as a restricted technology.
◼While these bills could potentially restrict the ability of US pharma companies to partner with China for drug 
development, we do not see a high likelihood of these bills ultimately being passed by both the House and the Senate 
in their current forms. 
◼We also would not expect a meaningful impact to the US pharma industry or for US pharma companies to drastically 
alter their clinical development strategies. 
◼Importantly, our recent conversations with companies in our coverage have continued to highlight the importance of 
China biotech partnerships, with many pointing towards an increased presence in the region. 
Source: congress.gov; house.gov; JP Morgan Research.

Transactions Thus Far Have Centered On Select Therapeutic Areas
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
US/EU pharmas have acquired China biotech assets with a wide range of targets/treatment modalities. That said, the 
three most common areas of focus have been 1) PD-1/VEGF bispecs, 2) ADCs and 3) incretins.
◼PD-1/VEGF bispecs from China biotechs have had promising data in a number of solid tumors and have been key 
assets of interest for US pharma with a string of deals over the past 2-3 years. We’re watching: 
◼Ivonesimab: Summit’s PD-1/VEGF bispec from Akeso
◼Pumitamig: BMY’s PD-L1/VEGF-A bispec partnered with BioNtech (acquired Biotheus)
◼PF-4404: PFE’s PD-1/VEGF bispec from 3SBio
◼Incretins from China biotechs are attractive biz dev oppties for both pharmas with an established presence in the 
field, as well as those looking to enter the space given the unprecedented market size. Here we are most focused 
on: 
◼Elecoglipron (AZD5004/ECC5004): AstraZeneca’s oral GLP-1 from Eccogene
◼UBT251: Novo Nordisk’s GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon triple receptor agonist from The United Laboratories
◼Ribupatide: Kailera’s GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist with oral and injectable formulations from Hengrui Pharma
◼Finally, ADCs represent sizeable oppties across a range of indications, with China biotechs developing assets 
across numerous targets, as well as next-gen capabilities such as bispec ADCs. Notable assets include:
◼Sac-TMT: MRK’s TROP2 ADC from Kelun-Biotech
◼HS-20089 and HS-20093: GSK’s B7-H4 and B7-H3 ADCs, respectively, from Hansoh
◼Going forward, we expect these will remain areas of interest for US/EU pharma. As interest in China biotechs 
continues to grow, we anticipate new therapeutic focuses will emerge as well.

Where Partnerships with Chinese Biopharmas Could Go From Here
Source: JP Morgan Research. 
Another debate on the future of the global pharma/China partnerships is how these will evolve over time. Specifically, 
will Chinese biotechs continue to see these partnerships as advantageous or will some of these companies look to 
globally develop and eventually commercialize assets on their own. 
◼Based on our recent conversations with many Chinese biotech/pharma management teams, we expect that many Chinese 
companies’ near-to-mid term (3-5 years) ex-China strategies will continue to focus on out-licensing and partnership deals 
similar to the ones that are currently gaining traction. That said, there could be some outliers that eventually attempt global 
commercialization on their own.
◼Along these lines, we do continue to see these partnerships as win/win for the global pharma names as well as Chinese 
biotechs with the multinational names benefitting from increased pipeline breadth/new product flow and local Chinese 
companies able to capture attractive global drug economics without having to build significant ex-China infrastructure.

PD-1/VEGF Bispecs Emerging As Next-Gen Immunotherapies 
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼There has been increased interest in PD-1/VEGF bispecifics given both the size of the potential oppties (particularly 
in NSCLC) and that this is one of the first therapeutic categories where all of the lead assets are from China. 
◼By targeting both PD-1/PD-L1 and VEGF these bispecifics can reverse T-cell immunosuppression and normalize tumor 
vasculature, potentially improving solid tumor treatment outcomes vs monotherapy treatment with a PD-1/PD-L1 (e.g. 
Keytruda, Opdivo) or a VEGF (e.g. Avastin). 
◼Given the immense size of the Keytruda market, there is a large degree of interest from both pharmas and investors 
in new modalities that could replace PD-1s over time, particularly in the number of tumor types where PD-1/PD-L1s 
are the SOC.  
◼Based on the data to date, including historical data for PD-1 and VEGF antibodies, we see NSCLC as the largest/most de-
risked oppty in the space.

What We’re Watching Next – HARMONi-3
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼Following ASCO we are expecting HARMONi-3 1L NSCLC global data for ivonescimab later in the year with more 
incremental updates expected for the other assets in development. 
◼Summit’s HARMONI-3 trial is the global Ph3 trial looking at Ivonescimab + chemo vs Keytruda + chemo in NSCLC (with 
squamous and non-squamous cohorts that are evaluated separately). Here, we note that Summit recently passed the 
interim PFS analysis for the squamous cohort without reaching statistical significance, although the independent data 
board recommended the study continue. We will also be closely watching for additional updates on the non-squamous 
cohort (the larger of the two indications) where final PFS data is anticipated in 1H27.
◼Beyond HARMONi-3, BMY and PFE are both running large ph3 programs. While these will take time to read out, we will 
be watching for additional ph2 data across indications, as well as any potential interim data from the ph3 trials ahead of full 
data later in the decade. 
◼We will also be watching for earlier-stage datasets from MRK and ABBV’s PD-1xVEGFs and would expect both 
companies to pursue rapid development plans once POC is established (with ABBV noting the potential to move directly to 
ph3 and MRK potentially exploring a PD1xVEGF / sac-TMT combo).

Key Remaining Questions For PD-1/VEGF Bispecs
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼One of the key debates for the PD-1/VEGF class is how well Chinese-only trials will translate to a global population. 
◼Questions have been raised around how meaningful a role these assets could have across different geographies where 
the patient populations may vary (e.g. age, gender), and where the SOC may be different (i.e. use of therapies only 
approved in certain countries). 
◼The field further has questions around the best development path forward for PD-1/VEGFs. 
◼We would not be surprised to see a range of different approaches, including potentially looking at novel-novel combos 
(e.g., PD-1/VEGFs combined with TROP2 ADCs). 
◼From here, there are also debates on which indications beyond lung cancer hold the most promise. 
◼Given the range of indications where PD-1s have demonstrated a benefit we expect companies will pursue broad 
development programs. However, we expect that each indication will need to be de-risked separately given the differences 
in the tumor microenvironment.

Data Thus Far Points to Benefit vs PD-1 in 1L Squamous NSCLC
Source: company websites and presentations; Brahmer, ASCO (2026); JP Morgan Research. 
◼As a reminder, at ASCO earlier this year the HARMONi-6 OS presentation demonstrated ivonescimab + chemo has a 
clear benefit across subgroups vs a PD-1 + chemo in a 1L squamous NSCLC Chinese population. 
◼Compared to PD-1 + chemo, ivonescimab + chemo showed a stat sig improvement on PFS (11.1 months vs 6.9, 
HR=0.60) as well as a meaningful improvement in OS (27.89mo vs 23.69mo, HR=0.66). 
◼Importantly, there was a clinically meaningful PFS benefit across subgroups, with HRs ranging from 0.64-0.68 across 
different PD-L1 TPS subgroups. 
◼However, we expect ivonescimab, or any of the PD-1/VEGF bispecs, will need to show an OS benefit in a global trial 
in order to meaningfully dislodge share from Keytruda/the PD-1 class given the controversies with the HARMONi-6 
data. 
◼Concerns were raised by the ASCO discussant on how relevant the selected patient population would be for a global 
market. This trial excluded patients with age >75 (where more tox would be expected) and has a HR of 0.93 in patients 
age 65 or older while the median age for lung cancer patients in the US is ~70.
◼There are also questions around how to think about the applicability of the data to the larger non-squamous NSCLC 
opportunity.

Incretin Market Expected to Exceed $200bn
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
We see the incretin market growing to >$200bn over time, much of which will largely be served by incumbents LLY and 
Novo. However, even a small segment of the market can translate to a meaningful oppty for later entrants given the 
unprecedented size of the market. 
◼As we think about the GLP-1 market over time, we expect LLY and Novo will maintain a duopoly in the incretin 
market given: 1) significant time-to-market advantages (US rebate walls, brand awareness in DTC, etc), 2) thus far limited 
differentiation for potential new entrants, and 3) the companies’ broad portfolios/pipelines with products addressing the full 
spectrum of patient needs.
◼Obesity pricing will continue to decrease over time as cash pay increases. The size of the cash-pay market has 
significantly exceeded expectations thus far and we see substantial room for further uptake in both the overweight population 
& in obese pts w/out commercial coverage. That said, we see volume growth more than offsetting pricing declines given the 
still single digit % penetration of incretins in the obesity space and high level of price elasticity we have seen thus far, further 
highlighting the potential for small market shares to translate to sizeable assets for additional entrants. 
◼Given the size of the market oppty, multiple competitors aim to compete over time, even if only to capture a small 
patient segment. Along these lines we’ve seen a number of US/EU pharma companies pursue China incretin partnership 
oppties. These partnerships may be for next-gen assets with the potential to improve on efficacy and/or tolerability, assets 
with different dosing modalities (e.g. oral), or may bring an incretin in-house for potential combination approaches (e.g. Astra 
and REGN).

Elecoglipron Brings AstraZeneca Into the Obesity Space
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼While data to date for Astra’s oral GLP-1, elecoglipron (licenced from Eccogene), does not suggest meaningful 
differentiation as a monotherapy in obesity or diabetes, Astra is looking at a combo strategy that could allow the 
company to play a larger role in the incretin field than is currently expected. 
◼Numerous ph2 datasets for elecoglipron were presented at ADA 2026:
◼In obesity (VISTA), at week 36 the 75mg dose (titrated weekly) elecoglipron arm showed an 11.8% per-protocol weight 
loss from baseline, with vomiting rates of 27%. Weight loss was slightly below that of Foundayo 36 week ph2 weight loss 
of 13.5% and Oral Wegovy 36 week ph3 weight loss of 14%. 
◼In type 2 diabetes (SOLSTICE), elecoglipron 75mg (with a Q2W dose escalation phase including 8 weeks of 4 step up 
doses) showed a per-protocol change in HbA1c from baseline of 1.9% at 26 weeks and a mean weight loss of 7.7%, with 
vomiting rates of 11%.
◼Net-net in obesity we see elecoglipron VISTA data depicting a broadly similar profile to other oral obesity products 
Foundayo and oral Wegovy, and as such do not currently see the elecoglipron as a monotherapy being particularly 
differentiated. However, we see a more meaningful oppty as a potential combination partner with Farxiga (SGLT-2) 
in diabetes. From here we will be watching for elecoglipron Phase III trial initiations across obesity, T2D and other 
indications in H2’26 (readouts likely in 2029-30).

While Early, UBT251 Adds GGG to Novo’s Pipeline
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼Novo Nordisk has obtained the global rights to The United Laboratories’ GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple agonist, UBT251. 
Initial China-only data for the asset has been encouraging, however, we expect global data will be needed before

Other GLP-1 Assets We Are Watching In China
Source: company websites and presentations; JP Morgan Research. 
◼Corxel Pharmaceuticals/ Vincentage Pharma’s CX11 (oral small molecule GLP-1 RA): Phase 3 China-Only trial 
showed weight loss of ~12% (vs ~1% from pbo) at 52 weeks, which is in line with Lilly’s Foundayo’s global phase 3 
results at 72 weeks. Vincentage has not disclosed tolerability data from the trial, however it stated that it is in line with the 
GLP-1 class. Vincentage is planning to submit an NDA for the drug in obesity to China’s NMPA in the “near term”. 
◼Corxel is running the global program for CX11, having initiated the U.S. ph2 trial in mid-2025 and data expected this year 
(we think mid-2026). The trial is testing a higher dose than that tested in the China phase 3 (200mg vs 160mg). 
◼JPM View: CX11 is an asset to watch with a potentially competitive profile. However, we think the asset will have a 
significant time to market disadvantage (we think ~2029) vs LLY’s Foundayo, and will likely also have other competitors in 
the market by that time including Structure’s alieniglipron and AZN’s AZD5004. In this context, we think CX11’s global 
trials will need to show more differentiation on weight loss/tolerability, both of which should be a challenge. On tolerability, 
we think Foundayo could have a sustainable advantage with its pharmacokinetic (PK) profile: half-life of ~30-50 hours (vs 
CX11’s ~5-6 hours) and more moderate peak-trough ratio. And finally, we note that CX11 has more of a danuglipron-like 
scaffold (other similar assets have not shown encouraging data, especially on tolerability/safety).
◼Ascletis Pharma’s ASC30 (oral small molecule GLP-1 RA) and ASC39 (oral small molecule amylin-selective RA). 
The U.S. phase 2 study of ASC30 (GLP-1) in obese patients w/o T2D showed pbo-adj weight loss of ~7.7% at 13 weeks, 
which seems competitive with Foundayo. GI tolerability in the study is at comparable levels with Foundayo (20-30% vomit 
rate). The company plans to initiate global phase 3 program in 3Q26. Ascletis is also developing ASC39 (preclinical) 
which could be the only known oral small molecule amylin SARA to enter the clinic (file IND in 3Q26), both as a 
monotherapy and in combination with its GLP-1. 
◼JPM View: This seems like the more interesting asset(s) to watch given competitiveness on both efficacy and tolerability 
as well as the asset’s PK profile. The tolerability profile should improve further in the global phase 3 which will utilize 4-
week titration steps (vs 1-week titration steps in phase 2). And unlike CX11, ASC30 has more of an orforglipron-like 
scaffold and a PK profile more similar to Foundayo (half-life is 35-40 hours, Cmax to peak to trough ratio less than 2:1). 
The asset will face a similar time to market disadvantage as Corxel, but we see some more room for differentiation. And 
the company’s oral small molecule GLP-1/amylin combo could be even more differentiated.

ADCs Have a Sizeable Oppty Across a Range of Indications
◼ADCs have become a key treatment modality of interest over the last decade, with broad applicability across 
oncology. In particular, there is an oppty to improve SOC for the numerous cancers where chemo is still the treatment 
backbone despite toxicity limitations. 
◼Numerous ADCs have been approved with multi-bn $ potential over time. Some of the more recent major ADC 
launches including Astra’s Enhertu and PFE’s Padcev have had strong sales ramps with potential for meaningful growth 
over time as additional indications are added to the label. 
◼As US/EU pharmas look to build on the success of approved ADCs/further add to their oncology pipelines, China 
will remain an area of focus. We’ve seen a number of US/EU and China ADC partnerships emerge in recent years and we 
expect ADCs will continue to be a key partnership oppty as the technology continues to expand to new targets, new 
payloads, new modalities (dual target ADCs), and indications beyond oncology (e.g. immunology). 
◼Within our coverage universes, TROP2 is the most significant ADC target with interest in the potential 
differentiation of Kelun/MRK’s sac-TMT from competitor TROP2 ADCs.
