Kontoor Brands Inc. (KTB): Helly Hansen Investor Day Takeaways
2 September 2026 | 3:08PM MDT
We attended Helly Hansen’s Investor Day hosted by KTB in Oslo, Norway, on 
September 1st-2nd, 2026. At the event, KTB provided a comprehensive update on 
the path for the Helly Hansen brand to grow to >$1.1bn by 2030 through US 
acceleration, Technical Outdoor category expansion, and Workwear while also 
expanding brand segment margins to a mid-teens level. Helly Hansen was framed as 
KTB’s primary growth engine, while Wrangler remains the company’s more balanced 
grower, mandating mid-single digit-revenue growth, gross margin expansion, 
increased brand investment, and durable cash generation.  
Key messages from the event included: (1) KTB introduced long-term targets for 
Helly Hansen of >$1.1bn revenue (10% CAGR from FY25 pro forma revenue of 
$675mn), mid-to-high 50%s gross margins, mid-teens operating margins, and 
>$500mn of cumulative cash generation through 2030; (2) The US is the brand’s key 
growth acceleration engine, where management is targeting expansion to at least 
$500mn of US revenue by 2030 and for the country to generate 60% of the 
forecasted brand growth during the period; (3) Technical Outdoor is a key area of 
expansion opportunity for the brand beyond Ski and Sail - here management 
expects growth will accelerate and become the largest incremental Sport business 
driver in the 2H of the 5-yr plan fueled by market share opportunities in hiking and 
other outdoor sports; (4) Management expects the ~$175mn Workwear business to 
double by 2030, supported by significant US growth and investments; (5) Revenue is 
expected to accelerate in the back half of the plan, with growth accelerating in FY27 
and stepping up further in FY28 as the benefits of earlier investments scale; (6) 
Brand awareness remains a key opportunity, with management planning to double 
marketing investment dollars and shift toward a more brand-led approach while 
shifting towards brand marketing and away from performance spend; and (7) 
Management expects profitability to improve first, creating investment capacity that 
supports revenue acceleration as growth initiatives mature.  
Our view: We step away from the event encouraged by the significant growth and 
margin opportunity ahead for Helly Hansen, particularly in the US and in workwear. 
Our two days in Oslo helped give us incremental conviction that KTB has several of 
the right leaders in place to drive the next phase of growth, and we look forward to 
seeing the initial contributions from the newly hired NA GM (Sport). We also are 
encouraged by the broad-based planned contribution to growth across several 
dimensions (US wholesale/DTC, current core and emerging categories, and 
sport/work). Net, we believe the brand is in the early stages of growth across several

Looking ahead, execution remains key. We believe investor reaction to the targets was 
somewhat muted given: (1) The targets suggest an acceleration in growth vs. the current 
run rate, with management targeting a 10% 5-yr CAGR vs. the MSD% underlying growth 
rate the brand is currently delivering in FY26. (2) The company is embedding an 
acceleration in growth in several areas of the business in the 2H of the plan, including the 
competitive technical outdoor and work segments. (3) The contribution from 
non-synergy margin drivers such as mix, scale, and leverage will also increase 
throughout the plan. 
Key takeaways: 
Helly Hansen long-term targets: Management introduced 2030 targets of >$1.1bn 
n
revenue (10% CAGR from FY25 pro forma revenue of $675mn), mid-to-high-50% 
gross margins, mid-teens operating margins, and >$500mn of cumulative cash 
generation through 2030. The strategy is anchored around three pillars: (1) 
Supercharge the US, where ~60% of growth is expected to originate; (2) Expand in 
Technical Outdoor, which management expects to become the largest Sport growth 
driver in the back half of the plan; and (3) Power Workwear. Importantly, the 
>$1.1bn revenue target excludes China JV revenue, which management views as an 
additional source of upside. Management also reiterated that acquisitions in core 
categories remain part of KTB’s longer-term strategy, although the return hurdle 
remains high given the magnitude of the organic opportunity. 
Cadencing of growth and margin expansion: Management outlined a growth 
n
algorithm where the first half of the plan is more margin-led, while revenue 
acceleration occurs in the back half as investments mature. Near-term margin 
expansion is expected to create investment capacity that will be reinvested into 
marketing, talent, product development, and distribution. Within Sport, core ski and 
sailing categories are expected to drive the majority of growth through FY28, while 
Technical Outdoor becomes a larger contributor thereafter. Workwear is expected 
to double by 2030, with growth accelerating as US investments scale.  
Brand awareness and marketing: A key focus of the plan is increasing awareness in 
n
North America. Helly Hansen’s US aided awareness currently sits at ~30%, below 
larger outdoor peers despite strong ratings on technical performance, trust, warmth, 
and weather protection. Management plans to double marketing investment 
through 2030, shifting from a historically performance-led approach (~70% of 
spend) toward upper-funnel brand building, category-led storytelling, and 
dedicated Sport and Workwear marketing teams. The investment ramp began in 
2H26 and should increase around Helly Hansen’s 150th anniversary in 2027.

n

growth is expected to remain balanced between wholesale and DTC, with 45%-50% 
of incremental growth coming from wholesale and 50%-55% from DTC. Within 
wholesale, management emphasized that the opportunity is less about adding new

relationship reverses from FY28-FY30, when Technical Outdoor contributes ~60% of 
growth. Management emphasized that Helly Hansen’s competitive advantage stems 
from its professional-driven innovation model, leveraging relationships with ski 
patrol, mountain guides, search-and-rescue teams, sailing professionals, and more 
than 55,000 professionals globally. Innovation remains anchored by proprietary 
technologies including Helly Tech and LIFA, with management describing Helly as a 
multi-category specialist rather than a category generalist. Management highlighted 
the size of the opportunity, noting that a 100bp share gain in Technical Outdoor 
alone would represent ~$440mn of incremental revenue vs. $140mn in Wintersports 
and $50mn in Sailing.

We are Buy rated on KTB. Our $103, 12-month price target is based on 10.5x Q5-Q8

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
