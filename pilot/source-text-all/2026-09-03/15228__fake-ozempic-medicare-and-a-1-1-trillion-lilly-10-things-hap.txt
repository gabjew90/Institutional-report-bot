Health is the true wealth
While the tape argues about AI, the yen and whatever macro crisis comes next, healthcare has quietly
become interesting again. Biotech is within spitting distance of its early-2021 highs, Lilly is getting
comfortable in the trillion-dollar club, and European pharma is beginning to attract flows after a
miserable run.
There isn't one grand thesis here. Instead, consider this our latest collection of observations from
across pharma and biotech — some bullish, some less so, but all worth having on the radar.

The cross-party trade

Healthcare stands out: historically one of the strongest sectors in the second half of midterm years,
with defensive characteristics if the macro backdrop deteriorates. The one sector that doesn't
care who wins.

And finally, the (obese) elephant in the
healthcare room...
No collection of pharma observations would be complete without GLP-1s. Given the sheer importance
of the theme — both to healthcare and increasingly to the global consumer economy — we wanted to
finish with a few observations that go beyond individual stocks and show just how differently the
revolution is playing out around the world.
Mounjaro magic
The FDA last week approved Lilly's Mounjaro (tirzepatide) to reduce cardiovascular risk in adults with
type 2 diabetes. And now we are all waiting patiently for the arrival of retatrutide.
Fifteen years, almost 40x your money, $1.1 trillion market cap, now 15th in global market cap. The
only company on the top 15 list that actually saves lives and makes us live longer.

Google Finance
Enter Medicare
The GLP1 story is, of course, far from over. Early Medicare GLP-1 access is already driving uptake, with
prescriptions among adults 65+ jumping from 5.6% to 6.7% between June and July, led by obesity use
and Zepbound. Lilly is pointing to real-world data suggesting Zepbound may lower broader healthcare
spending, up to $607 lower costs at 12 months excluding the drug's own cost. The demand curve
just got a federal buyer.
Brazil fake GLP-1s
Brazil’s GLP-1 boom is creating a sizeable black market and growing safety risk with penetration has
reached 5.5% vs 3.7% globally, while smuggled and compounded products could account for ~51% of
the ~$7bn market and illegal seizures have surged ~100x. With 83 deaths and 3,600+ complications
under investigation, regulators are accelerating cheaper approved alternatives—including sub-$100
semaglutide—which could increasingly pull demand back into regulated channels and pressure
branded pricing. 
Indian obesity
Indian generic weight-loss drug sales have reached only ~20% of forecasts through July. The lesson:
price isn't the only barrier. Cultural stigma, socioeconomic factors and distrust of generics are proving
surprisingly powerful obstacles to GLP-1 adoption.
