Key stories 
Aroundtown: Weaker interest coverage but stable 
portfolio valuations 
UBS credit view: Stable 
Agency ratings: n.a. / BBB, stable 
 
European real estate company Aroundtown reported mixed 
2Q26 results. Like-for-like rental income growth of 2.7% 
reflected solid re-letting and indexation, led by hotels 
(+4.4%) and residential (+3.5%), while offices lagged 
(+0.9%). Disposal activity continued, led by hotels, which 
generated EUR 350mn in proceeds. The company deployed 
the proceeds toward share buybacks and residential 
property acquisitions. Portfolio revaluations (+0.1%) were 
relatively flat and the overall portfolio vacancy rate was 
stable at 7.6%. Loan-to-value moderately weakened to 
43% (FY25: 41%) and interest coverage fell to 3.3x (FY25: 
3.9x) as the issuer’s weighted cost of debt crept up to 
2.4%. Total cash and liquid assets of EUR 3.9bn remain 
sound, in our view. In addition, the company has EUR 1.0bn 
of undrawn credit facilities. Ongoing liability management 
has helped limit significant debt maturities until 2027.FY26 
guidance was maintained. 
We maintain our Stable issuer credit outlook for 
Aroundtown. As with most European real estate issuers, 
weakening interest coverage ratios remain a key area to 
monitor amid elevated government bond yields and 
upcoming debt maturities. In the case of Aroundtown, the 
refinancing of low-coupon debt over the next 24 months 
will likely place further pressure on interest coverage, 
although we expect it to remain manageable. As such, we 
do not expect any near-term rating actions, as the issuer 
should maintain headroom compared to S&P’s downgrade 
trigger of 1.8x. As for its portfolio fundamentals, despite 
acceleration in German growth, demand for Aroundtown’s 
offices remains subdued (vacancy rose 40bps sequentially 
to 13.6%). Management highlighted potential upside to 
office assets with progress on conversions to serviced 
apartments and data centers. We do not believe this, or 
other external growth opportunities, will bring about 
significant capex expansion, but in any case, the issuer has 
a further EUR 400mn of properties classified as held for sale 
(with a vacancy rate of 33%) with potential proceeds to 
support development and investment needs (and also 
shareholder returns). 
Aroundtown’s bonds remain among the widest trading in 
our investment grade coverage with a particularly steep 
bond curve which we believe fairly reflects its portfolio risks. 
However, we find Aroundtown’s short-dated bonds such 
as its EUR 1.45% 2028 appealing for buy-and-hold 
investors given the yield pickup over comparable BBB-rated 
bonds in our coverage and our comfort with the issuer’s 
financial liquidity position. For longer-dated bond 
positioning, we prefer more defensive carry in the bonds of 
higher-rated German residential landlord Vonovia. 
Geoff Wan, CFA, Analyst 
Coty: Margins remain under pressure 
UBS credit view: Deteriorating 
Agency ratings: Ba1, neg / BB, watch- 
 
Coty, the global beauty products group, reported mixed 
4Q26 results (quarter ended 30 June). While results were 
ahead of expectations at the top line, they reflected 
ongoing margin attrition due to lower volumes and US 
tariffs. Revenues increased 1% (-1% at constant FX) to USD 
1.3bn, with sales in the Americas (44% of total) up 9% 
(+6% like-for-like), helping to mitigate weaker EMEA sales 
(42% of total), which fell 8% (-10% at constant FX). Asia 
Pacific sales also increased 11% (+7% at constant FX). 
Gross margins fell 140bps y/y to 61%, reflecting lower 
volumes, stock obsolescence, and tariffs, in line with the 
full-year trend. Adjusted EBITDA fell 26% to USD 93.6mn 
(7.4% margin, -230bps), largely reflecting the decline in 
gross margins, while adjusted operating income dropped 
42% to USD 40mn, with margins contracting by 230bps to 
3.1%. Free cash flow was stronger at USD 73mn (4Q25: 
USD 35mn), lifting full-year free cash flow to USD 348mn 
(FY25: USD 278mn). Net debt fell USD 47mn q/q to USD 
2.9bn and declined by USD 839mn over the full year, 
largely helped by USD 750mn of Wella proceeds. However, 
markedly lower LTM earnings meant that net leverage 
declined by just 0.1x to 3.4x. Liquidity remained adequate 
at USD 1.8bn, including USD 176mn in cash, with no major 
maturities until 2028 beyond the USD 500mn notes due in 
2027. 
FY27 
guidance 
remains 
withdrawn, 
with 
management framing the year ahead as a “transition” year, 
although it is targeting FY27 free cash flow at similar levels 
to FY26. Near-term guidance was provided, with 1Q27 
revenues expected to decline by “a low- to mid-single-digit 
percentage.” Gross margins are expected by management 
to decline by 50-100bps, reflecting recent trends, but 
should be partially offset by productivity initiatives and 
procurement actions. The company expects adjusted 
EBITDA to decline by a "low-teens percentage,” which 
would represent a sequential improvement from the more 
significant declines in 2H26. Management expects free 
cash flow to be above USD 300mn in 1H27, reflecting the 
seasonally strongest second quarter. 
We are revising our issuer credit outlook to Deteriorating 
from Stable, reflecting ongoing margin pressure and a lack 
of deleveraging progress. Against a more challenging 
consumer backdrop, the sale of the company’s remaining 
Wella stake should have represented 0.5x of deleveraging 
when originally announced at the end of last year. The All-
In-To-Win cost savings program is coming to an end, having 
contributed USD 250mn in savings over the past year and 
more than USD 1.0bn over the past six years. Management 
has said that a successor program is being finalized, 
targeting “several hundred million dollars in productivity 
savings” over the next three years. An ongoing fixed-cost 
savings program is also underway. The next major 
challenge for Coty will be addressing the Gucci Beauty 
license exit in FY28 (around USD 700mn in revenue and 
USD 140mn in EBITDA), meaning that cost-cutting remains 
high on the agenda. Capital allocation prioritizes debt 
reduction, with common dividends suspended since 2020 
until net leverage approaches 2.0x. The company is due to 
receive USD 150mn from Kering in relation to Gucci, with 
USD 250mn already received, and management is still

Estée Lauder: Credit profile reaches an inflection 
point 
UBS credit view: Stable 
Agency ratings: A3, neg / A-, neg 
 
Estée Lauder's 4Q26 and FY26 results (ended 30 June 
2026) provide the strongest evidence yet that the 
company's turnaround is gaining traction. Organic sales 
rose 5% y/y in 4Q26, ahead of expectations, with growth 
across all regions. Encouragingly, areas that have weighed 
on performance in recent years also improved during FY26, 
with Travel Retail returning to growth and Mainland China 
delivering positive organic sales growth and market share 
gains. While North America and the Makeup segment still 
need to demonstrate a sustained recovery, recent results 
suggest the business is moving in the right direction. The 
stronger top-line performance translated into improved 
profitability and cash generation, prompting management 
to raise FY27 adjusted operating margin guidance to 
12.7%–13.5% from 12.5%–13%. 
We are revising our issuer credit outlook to Stable from 
Deteriorating, reflecting our increased confidence that the 
company's credit profile has reached an inflection point. 
Estée Lauder has faced significant rating pressure over the 
past three years as earnings weakened, leverage increased, 
and softness in Travel Retail and China persisted. While the 
rating agencies continue to maintain negative outlooks and 
leverage remains above downgrade thresholds, recent 
leverage, cash flow, and profitability trends have developed 
more favorably than expected. Moody's expected adjusted 
gross leverage to peak above 5x in 4Q24, but it ultimately 
reached approximately 4.3x, only modestly above the prior 
quarter and well below its forecast, though still elevated 
relative to the 3.5x downgrade threshold. We also view 
management's indication of a more conservative capital 
allocation 
approach, 
including 
setting 
aside 
transformational M&A ambitions, as supportive of credit 
quality. This includes planned deleveraging through 
repayment of a USD 500mn bond maturing in March 2027. 
Combined with ongoing restructuring benefits, we are 
comfortable adopting a more favorable view of Estée 
Lauder's credit trajectory over the near to medium term. 
Estée Lauder USD bonds trade broadly in line with lower A-
rated peers and consumer staples peers despite having a 
less defensive business profile and the negative outlooks 
maintained by both rating agencies. Continued operational 
improvement and a stronger credit trajectory could provide 
modest support for spreads; however, in our view, current 
valuations already reflect much of the recovery story. As a 
result, we view risk-reward for bondholders as broadly 
balanced. 
Helen Habegger, Analyst 
Lockheed Martin: Strong 2Q26 results, stronger 
outlook 
UBS valuation view: Core 
UBS credit view: Improving 
Agency ratings: A2, stable / A-, pos 
 
Lockheed Martin (LMT), the world's largest defense 
contractor, reported stronger-than-expected 2Q26 results. 
EBITDA rose 7% y/y to USD 2.9bn, FCF jumped to USD 
2.9bn from USD -150m in 2Q25 (albeit benefitting from 
the timing of customer receipts and tax payments), while 
net debt and net leverage declined to multi-year lows of 
USD 16.7bn and 1.5x, respectively. The company also 
raised its FY26 EBIT and FCF guidance, supported by 
strength across all business segments. 
We are revising our issuer credit outlook to Improving from 
Stable. LMT's credit profile is underpinned by its scale, the 
breadth and sophistication of its product portfolio, a strong 
balance sheet, and a long track record of conservative 
financial management. Demand for LMT's key products—
including missiles, missile defense systems, and F-35 fighter 
jets—is strengthening and becoming more broad-based, as 
evidenced by 2Q26's surge in order intake, which drove 
backlog to a record USD 230bn at quarter-end. We 
therefore expect a multi-year period of revenue and 
earnings growth, coupled with healthy cash generation, 
leading to further improvements in credit metrics. 
LMT's bonds trade at the tight end of the single-A 
spectrum, reflecting the company's healthy fundamentals, 
favorable outlook, and investors' increasingly positive view 
of defense contractors. We believe this valuation is 
appropriate and keep LMT on our Core list, with a 
preference for the short end of the curve, including the USD 
1.85% 2030s. 
Peter Prek, CFA, Analyst 
NVIDIA: Record results offset by shareholder returns 
and increased commitments 
UBS valuation view: Core 
UBS credit view: Stable 
Agency ratings: Aa1, pos / AA, stable 
 
Leading chipmaker NVIDIA reported record 2Q27 results 
(quarter ended 26 July) but these were offset by record 
shareholder returns and increased guarantees and 
commitments. As a result, we revise our issuer credit 
outlook to Stable from Improving. NVIDIA, the pre-eminent 
developer of chips, networking equipment, and software 
underpinning the AI buildout, reported revenue more than 
doubling (+106% y/y) to a record USD 96.2bn (+18% q/q). 
Growth was driven by compute demand in its core Data 
Center business across hyperscale and AI cloud, industrial,

and enterprise (ACIE) end-markets. Edge Computing, 
which serves neoclouds, enterprise customers, and 
sovereigns, also delivered healthy growth, with revenue up 
27% y/y (+18% q/q). Management reiterated that demand 
remains supply-constrained and, for the first time, provided 
year-ahead guidance, forecasting 70% revenue growth for 
FY28. Gross margins rose 320bps y/y but were flat q/q at 
76% and are expected to decline to 71-72% due to 
“extreme pricing conditions in memory.” Guidance is for 
3Q27 revenue of USD 108bn and gross margin of 74% 
supported by the next-generation Vera Rubin release—
expected to be the “fastest product ramp in NVIDIA’s 
history.“ 
We revise our issuer credit outlook to Stable from 
Improving. Despite record results and an upbeat outlook, 
further credit upside appears limited by higher shareholder 
returns, 
increased 
commitments, 
and 
guarantees. 
Shareholder returns were a record USD 26bn year-to-date, 
representing 60% of free cash flow (FCF), above the 50% 
guidance, which may well prove to be a floor. For 2Q27, 
FCF was USD 21.5bn, down by half q/q due to higher 
working capital, reflecting inventory build-up ahead of the 
Vera Rubin launch and extended payment terms for 
“certain investment-grade customers.” Customer financing 
is becoming more prominent, with NVIDIA announcing 
USD 105bn of guarantees for a 20-year lease by OpenAI 
(not covered by CIO). It also announced a sharp increase in 
commitments, up USD 160bn q/q to USD 279bn, largely for 
memory procurement. That said, the balance sheet remains  
exceptionally strong, with a liquidity  position of USD 57bn 
(including USD 22bn of cash) versus gross debt of USD 
33bn, resulting in negligible gross leverage. 
We move NVIDIA from our Preferred List and put on Core 
List. The company recently issued USD 25bn of new 
bonds—its first transaction in five years—in a surprise move 
with all tranches now around 10-20bps wider. We believe 
this may reflect market indigestion following a wave of 
similarly rated hyperscaler issuance, as well as high issuer 
correlation, which may be creating concentration concerns 
for investors. That said, we view select notes as attractive. 
Mustaq F Rahaman, Analyst 
Standard Chartered: Outlook lifted to Improving on 
consistently stronger results 
UBS valuation view: Core 
UBS credit view: Improving 
Agency ratings: A3, stable / BBB+, pos 
 
Standard Chartered (SC) reported sound 2Q26 results, with 
profit before tax of USD 2.3bn, up 2% y/y. This was 
primarily driven by higher revenues, particularly NII, which 
rose 7% y/y. Non-interest income, accounting for half of 
the bank’s revenues, was flat q/q. From a segment 
perspective, Wealth & Retail Banking showed the strongest 
performance, with revenues increasing 18% y/y, driven by 
strong business momentum in the Investment Products 
segment. With a RoTE of 17.4% in 1H26, the bank 
continued to demonstrate strong profitability, well above 
its target of more than 12% for FY26. Asset quality 
continued to improve, with the NPL ratio at 1.9% (down 
from 2% in 1Q26). The loan-loss ratio of 20bps was down 
12bps q/q but included another USD 44mn overlay related 
to tensions in the Middle East (Middle East exposure 
accounted for 6% of loans). The bank maintained its 
through-the-cycle guidance of 30-35bps. The CET1 ratio 
improved to 14.2%, above the regulatory requirement of 
10.3%. A newly announced USD 1bn share buyback is 
expected by management to reduce the ratio by 37bps. For 
FY26, SC upgraded its revenue-growth guidance to the 
midpoint of the 5-7% range, with NII up by low single digits 
(from previous guidance of flat) and statutory RoTE of more 
than 12%. In the medium term, the bank targets RoTE of 
more than 15% in 2028 and 18% in 2030, with an 
unchanged CET1 ratio range of 13-14%. 
We are changing our issuer credit outlook from Stable to 
Improving following sound 1H26 results. This change is 
based on sustainably improved profitability and a solid 
balance sheet, which we believe positions the bank well for 
a rating upgrade in the short to medium term. Its strategic 
focus on organizational simplification and lower-risk assets 
supports a consistently stronger credit profile, in our view. 
S&P recently changed the outlook on its BBB+ rating from 
stable to positive, citing SC’s improved comparability with 
higher-rated G-SIB peers in terms of profitability, franchise, 
and balance sheet strength. While SC’s credit profile has 
improved, in our view, its footprint and business profile 
remain subject to higher volatility, given its larger 
proportion of capital-market-related businesses and its 
footprint in China (5% of revenues) and the Middle East 
(6% of revenues). 
We are adding SC to our Core list. SC’s senior bonds trade 
moderately wider than those of fully single-A rated peers. 
While we view SC’s bonds as fairly valued, we think they 
offer a degree of value for hold-to-maturity investors, given 
the issuer’s higher carry. At current levels, senior bonds in 
EUR and USD allow investors to lock in appealing yields, in 
our view. SC has already achieved its full-year MREL 
funding target of USD 9bn (USD 7.3bn HoldCo senior and 
USD 1.6bn AT1). While prefunding could occur in the 
second half of the year, this should limit potential supply 
pressure. 
Sebastian Petrich, Analyst

Friedrichshafen (ZF Group) reported stronger-than-guided 
1H26 results, marking an improvement across key credit

Revenues declined 2.0% y/y to EUR 19.3bn (+0.5% at

business momentum extends into 2H26.

into 
fee 
and 
commission-generating 
businesses should continue under several banks’ strategic

exposures.

and

UBS CIO risk views
Credit risk flags
CIO attaches a credit risk flag to the instruments under its coverage. Credit risk is assessed based on the remaining tenor and / 
or instrument type. The flag indicates the likelihood that a holder of the instrument will not receive a coupon or principal 
payment when it comes due. For subordinated and hybrid instruments, which are usually callable and have a remote or no 
fixed maturity date, we apply one uniform credit risk flag per issuer and instrument type. The idea is to reflect the possibility 
of contractual trigger events or regulatory intervention occurring. Either can impose losses on bondholders regardless of the 
remaining term of the instrument or a specific issuer default event. Credit risk flags only indicate our view of the riskiness of 
a particular instrument. Credit risk flags should not be seen as recommendations to buy, hold or sell. In fact, any combination 
of risk flags and relative value recommendations is possible.
Very low credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is very low (cumulative 
probability of less than 2%).
Medium credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is low to medium 
(cumulative probability of non-payment between 2% and less than 20%).
High credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is at least one in five 
cumulatively.
Issuer credit outlook
We complement the instrument-specific risk information of the credit risk flags by indicating our outlook for the credit quality 
of an issuer over the next 12 months. Depending on instrument pricing, all combinations of an issuer credit outlook and 
relative valuation recommendations are possible.
Improving: We expect the credit profile of the issuer to improve, to an extent that may result in upgrades by rating agencies.
Stable: We do not expect the credit profile of the issuer to change meaningfully.
Deteriorating: We expect the credit profile of the issuer to deteriorate, to an extent that may result in downgrades by 
rating agencies.
UBS CIO valuation views
Relative value bond recommendations
Our relative value bond recommendations are based on an average investment horizon of six to 12 months. They reflect 
our assessment of a bond's attractiveness relative to comparable instruments under CIO coverage. Comparable instruments 
typically exhibit similar credit quality, are denominated in the same currency, and belong to the same segment of the bond 
market.
Views on a particular instrument can change within the six- to 12-month time frame, and those that apply to one instrument 
do not necessarily apply to others of the same issuer. Views on a particular instrument may be withdrawn if it does not have 
a sizeable basket of comparable instruments under CIO coverage.
Attractive
Bonds seen as “attractive” are expected to generate a total return exceeding the average return of comparable 
instruments. Our recommendation can stem from a positive view on the issuer’s credit profile not fully reflected 
in the price, unduly high risk premiums, our take on an instrument's call probability, the risk of coupon 
deferrals, and external factors including regulatory intervention.
Fair
Bonds seen as "fair" are expected to produce a total return broadly in line with the average return of 
comparable instruments.
Expensive
Bonds seen as "expensive" are expected to earn a total return that is less than the average return of 
comparable instruments. Our recommendation can stem from a negative view on the issuer’s credit profile

Sell recommendations
Sell
A Sell recommendation is assigned when the risk of an adverse outcome for an instrument exceeds what is 
reflected in its current valuation. Such situations can include those in which the instrument appears likely to 
post negative total returns until redemption, either due to a highly negative yield to maturity or an imminent 
call at a price below market valuations.
Issuer valuation views
In addition to the relative value bond recommendations, CIO provides issuer valuation views for selected issuers from 
developed countries. Large, frequent issuers often provide a relatively consistent bond curve in their main issuing currencies. 
A general valuation view on them provides useful guidance when constructing a bond portfolio or assessing new issues. 
Issuer valuation views cannot simply be broken down to the instrument level. Any combination of our credit risk flags and 
issuer valuation views is generally possible. Issuer views do not restrict CIO from having different valuation views on individual 
bonds.
Preferred list: Bonds of issuers on our Preferred list are generally expected to offer a more attractive relative valuation than 
those of similarly rated peers.
Core list: Bonds of issuers on our Core list are generally expected to generate total returns in line with those of similarly 
rated peers. Core issuers offer relatively liquid bond curves and comparatively stable credit profiles.
Avoid list: Bonds of issuers on our Avoid list are generally expected to offer a less attractive relative valuation than those of 
similarly rated peers. The decision to include an issuer on the Avoid list reflects, in most cases, relative value considerations, 
which can but do not have to be based on an expected deterioration in credit quality. As long as we have not issued a Sell 
recommendation existing positions may be held.
