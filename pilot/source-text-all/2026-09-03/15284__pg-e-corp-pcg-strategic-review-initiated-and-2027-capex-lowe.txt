developments in California and the implications to its broader 
strategic plan. As a result of the recent events, there is continued 
uncertainty around whether structural reform will be introduced in 
the near term, but we believe the option remains a possibility. On 
today’s conference call, PCG lowered its 2027 capital spending plans 
by $2 bn and initiated EPS guidance of $1.78-$1.82 for 2027. 
Management also announced that the board of directors authorized 
a strategic review to consider options and engage with the state on 
paving a path forward. We reflect the updated company guidance in 
our financial model, and still expect PCG to grow EPS at a ~9% CAGR 
through 2030. While we anticipated a more comprehensive update 
on today’s call, we recognize that discussions regarding potential 
wildfire liability reform remain fluid.  
As we wrote previously, we see potential for the discount to narrow 
for PCG (which currently trades at a 55% discount to the group on a 
P/E basis), if a special session is called and a more comprehensive 
package is agreed upon (we take no view on the likelihood of any 
outcome). 
Key takeaways

developments and implications to its capital allocation, EPS

takeaways below:

Per the company, this change in PCG’s capital plan is meant to benefit customers 
through lower financing costs, while not sacrificing investment in safety as well as its 
Wildfire Mitigation Plan and Safety Certification requirements. The company 
outlined work at risk of being deferred due to the lower 2027 capex of connecting 
new housing projects, interconnecting new renewables projects, technology 
upgrades, and large load beyond the initial 1.8 GWs. 
Guidance: The company reaffirmed full-year 2026 EPS guidance of $1.64–$1.66, 
n
initiated 2027 EPS guidance of $1.78–$1.82 (reflecting ~9% growth at the midpoint), 
and suspended multi-year capex, rate base, and long-term EPS growth guidance 
beyond 2027 pending the strategic review. Given the legislative uncertainty, 
management commented on the potential to revisit the 2027 capital plan if policy 
reform is sufficient.  
Strategic Review: PCG announced that the board of directors has authorized a 
n
comprehensive strategic review to evaluate all regulatory, financial, corporate 
structure, and capital allocation alternatives to unlock corporate value and achieve 
investment-grade credit ratings, subject to guardrails that recognize safety 
commitments, Wildfire Fund obligations, and labor/pension agreements. The 
strategic review will be led by a committee made up of four independent directors, 
and will involve engagement with state regulators, policymakers, and stakeholders to 
pave a sustainable path forward. While the timing of the strategic review is unknown, 
management commented on other reviews taking 12-18 months, where we should 
expect updates on the quarterly earnings calls. 
Our view 
Ultimately, PCG’s investor call provided less comprehensive updates than anticipated, 
though 2027 EPS guidance was broadly in line with our published estimates. While we 
expected more clarity on capital allocation shifts, a special session in California remains 
possible based on commentary from the Governor and Assembly Speaker regarding 
ongoing work on comprehensive reform. Given the timeline for Governor Newsom’s 
upcoming exit from office in January 2027, there is potential that the special session 
could be called sooner rather than later. 
That said, we recognize a special session is not guaranteed and that PCG could continue 
to trade at a 50%-55% discount to the peer group in the absence of comprehensive 
reform. As written previously, we await final clarity on the legislative process to assess 
full impacts on the CA IOUs. We also look to credit rating agency updates, where 
management noted S&P’s signals of maintaining the current rating, while pointing to 
ongoing concerns over the limited progress of credit-supportive legislation.  
Model Updates 
We reflect the changes to the capital plan including lower spending in 2027 by $2bn and 
the initiated 2027 guidance of $1.78-$1.82. These changes maintain our 2026 EPS of 
$1.66 and lower our 2027-2029 EPS estimates from $1.81/$1.98/$2.16 to 
$1.80/$1.97/$2.15. We also lower our P/E multiple slightly from 12.5x to 12x to reflect 
continued uncertainty, which bring our price target down from $24 to $23.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
