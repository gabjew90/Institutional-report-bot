DUNELM  (DNLM) - Good Things in Store; Upgrading to Buy; Hold to Buy, Tgt 850p to 1050p. Last Close 
832p. 
Benjamin Yokyong-Zoega: We upgrade Dunelm to Buy ahead of its Strategy Update as we believe management 
will outline a revamped digital offer and increased store investment to drive accelerating earnings growth as 
Dunelm progresses towards 10% market share. Profit warnings this year have raised doubts over mid-term 
margins and the consistency of market share gains but we like the underlying business model. Cash conversion 
is strong at c.70% with a c.9% FCF yield in Cal-27E. Trading at a 10.3x P/E, we think little credit is being given to

LEGRAND  (LEGD.PA) - Strong foundations, constructive outlook; Hold to Buy. Tgt EUR150 to EUR160. 
Last Close EUR134.55. 
Gael de-Bray: Legrand's historical 20% P/E premium to the sector average has vanished, which appears 
unjustified given we believe the company's organic growth dynamics are stronger than ever, even with 70% of 
revenues (exposed to buildings) not yet contributing. We like the quality of Legrand's positioning (a "giant in 
niches"), its defensive attributes (strong pricing power, high and steady margins, best-in-class FCF generation) 
and its ability to leverage the trend towards mass electrification, the growing demand for energy efficiency in 
buildings and the AI infrastructure build-out. We expect the stock to re-rate as the EU residential market should 
gradually recover over the next 12 months, boosting investor confidence in Legrand's sustained growth 
trajectory even if datacenter demand eventually moderates. We upgrade our rating to BUY with a new TP of 
€160. 
  
ITALIAN FINANCIALS - After summer wrap-up; Recommendation and Target Price Changes: Azimut - 
Hold to Buy, tgt EUR35 to EUR46, Banco BPM - Buy, tgt EUR16.2 to EUR18.1, Credem - Hold, tgt EUR17.1 to 
EUR20, BPER Banca S.p.A. - Hold, tgt EUR13.4 to EUR14.4, Fineco - Hold, tgt EUR20.7 to EUR23.4. 
Giovanni Razzoli: Italian financials delivered strong 2Q results, with both banks and asset gatherers exceeding 
our bottom-line expectations by 9% and 13% respectively. Our 2026-29E bottom-line forecasts remain broadly 
unchanged for banks but are revised up by 5% on average for asset gatherers, primarily driven by Azimut's 
M&A activity. We maintain a "BUY" on BancoBPM despite strong performance (+36% YTD) due to its M&A 
optionality, which has increased following the announcement of the tender offer by MPS . Relatively weak price 
action since then (-5% vs. the SX7E), in our view is unjustified and represents an attractive entry point. Banca 
Generali remains our top pick among Asset Gatherers (BUY - TP EUR 76.4) due to strong company-specific 
initiatives. Here, the uncertainty generated by the tender offer by MPS may generate a slowdown of business 
origination short term and weaker market performance (e.g. last year during the tender offer launched by 
Mediobanca, BGN underperformed domestic peers by an average of 30%).

CAIRN HOMES PLC  (CRN.L) - Upward RoE trajectory continues; Tgt 245p. Last Close 234p. Buy. 
Jonathan Coubrough: Cairn has reported an excellent first half, growing EPS 82%. The €50m buyback 
contributes to a DB 7% total distribution yield this year. This is funded by a positive inflection in H1 cash flow, 
reflecting higher profits and more moderate land + wip investment following significant investment last year. 
Management say they are confident in continuing the sustained ROE trajectory, now guiding to c.17.0% in FY26, 
which we forecast will increase by another c.70bps in FY27. We believe this high ROE trajectory warrants the 
1.9x NTAV multiple. 
  
FERRARI N.V  (RACE.MI) - Driving the Next Cycle; Tgt EUR460. Last Close EUR357.05. Buy. 
Nicolai Kempf: We expect Ferrari to unveil two new models in the Range segment this year, replacing the 296 
GTB and GTS. These successors are likely to retain a hybrid powertrain and be positioned below the 849 
Testarossa in the pricing hierarchy. Beyond this, we expect a facelift of the Purosangue around 2027, alongside 
what is likely to be Ferrari's most anticipated upcoming launch, the F80 Aperta. While the F80 Aperta will likely 
be produced in significantly lower volumes than the F80 coupé, its substantially higher pricing will support 
revenues as soon as the F80 starts being phased out. Overall, we believe Ferrari's strong product pipeline, 
continued mix enrichment, pricing power and greater exposure to UHNW customers support its premium 
valuation relative to Hermès. 
  
MOTORPOINT GROUP PLC  (MOTR.L) - Motoring ahead of expectations; Tgt 170p. Last Close 130p. 
Buy. 
Benjamin Yokyong-Zoega: Motorpoint have raised FY profit guidance in an ad-hoc statement this morning, 
ahead of their 1H update scheduled for early October. FY27 PBT is now expected to be in the range of £9.8m-
£10.8m. This is c.7% ahead of current consensus at £9.1- £10.0m and implies 30-44% yoy growth. Trading 
volumes have been stronger than anticipated and the raise has been driven by margin improvements yielding 
better unit profitability while supply dynamics remain favourable. Following the recent opening in Leeds, 
Motorpoint have secured a further store to open in Spring 2027. Overall this is a positive update, which points 
to progress on the site pipeline and continued margin benefits following investment in technology, data and 
AI. We lift our FY27E PBT forecast 12% from £9.1m to £10.2m (+36% yoy). On our updated forecasts, the stock 
trades on 15.2x Cal-26E P/E, falling to 12.0x in Cal-27E. Buy.

JD SPORTS FASHION PLC  (JD) - Lack of spending from the Kids of America - retain Hold; Tgt 95p. Last 
Close 82p. Hold. 
Adam Cochrane: JD Sports is facing a tough athleisure industry backdrop with deteriorating trends in the US, 
its largest market, more than offsetting any operational improvements. The lowered guidance range prudently 
reflects the tougher market conditions. Whilst JD is "controlling the controllables" and focussing on areas of 
relative growth, it is not able to offset the weaker industry growth, especially in the US. Following commentary 
from adidas, Puma, Zalando, On Running and Dick's about margin pressure from promotional intensity, excess 
inventory, limited excitement in new releases and weakness in the EMEA consumer the outlook is not 
compelling.
