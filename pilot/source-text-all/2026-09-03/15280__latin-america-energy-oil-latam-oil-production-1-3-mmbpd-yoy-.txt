Latin America Energy: Oil: LatAm oil production +1.3 mmbpd YoY in 
2Q26
2 September 2026 | 5:56PM BRT
In this note, we summarize high frequency oil production data in Latin America and 
leverage our coverage of main oil producers in those countries to assess future 
growth potential. We also list stocks which would benefit from a strong production 
growth scenario in LatAm. 
In 2Q26, average oil production in the region increased by 14% YoY 
(c.+1.3mmbpd) to 10.8 mmbpd, mainly boosted by increased output in Brazil 
(c.+680 kbpd YoY), followed by Venezuela (c.+270kbpd), Guyana (c.+240kbpd) and 
Argentina (c.+140kbpd). Oil output in Mexico, Colombia and other LatAm countries 
were mostly stable on a YoY basis. On a sequential basis, we estimate average 
output increased by 5% QoQ (c.+470kbpd), mainly supported by Brazil 
(associated with the ramp-up of recently inaugurated FPSOs, Exhibit 5) and a gradual 
recovery and ramp-up of production in Venezuela. See country by country 
chartbook inside.

As previously noted, we expect average oil output in the region (ex-Venezuela) to

shale Vaca Muerta, which should more than offset some decline in production in

12% YoY in 1H26 excluding Venezuela. As such, we remain comfortable with our

considering i) a potential acceleration in drillings in Argentina’s Vaca Muerta in 2H26, ii) 
Some of Petrobras’ recently inaugurated platforms are still ramping up and iii) an 
additional platform (FPSO Errea Wittu, +250kbpd of capacity) is scheduled to have first 
oil in Guyana in 4Q26.

We are Buy rated on Valero Energy (VLO), where we highlight the company’s 
o
leverage to elevated refining margins, low-cost operations, and a track record 
of execution. In addition, we expect that as incremental heavy oil production 
from the region enters the global market, this should result in more favorable 
light-heavy crude differentials and ultimately benefit refiners with Gulf Coast 
exposure. For VLO, given the company’s size and scale in the Gulf Coast, we 
anticipate the stock to be a key beneficiary given its feedstock sourcing ability, 
premium asset portfolio, and commercial optimization efforts. 
We additionally are Buy rated on Marathon Petroleum Corp. (MPC), where 
o
we highlight the company’s balance sheet strength, compelling capital returns 
profile, midstream distributions, and attractive West Coast/Gulf Coast 
exposure. We also note management has discussed running more than double 
Venezuelan crudes in 2Q26 (versus 1Q26) on its recent earnings call, 
ultimately reducing exposure to Brent-based crudes, improving capture rates, 
and enhancing cash flow generation. 
Although we maintain a Neutral rating on Phillips 66 (PSX), we highlight the 
o
company’s leverage to widening Western Canadian Select (WCS) differentials, 
which we anticipate should become more favorable on the back of increased 
heavy oil supply from incremental Venezuelan production. In addition, we note 
the company is now the third largest buyer of Venezuelan crude worldwide (as 
per management commentary on the 2Q earnings call), which we expect 
should benefit capture rates and earnings going forward.

o

including Guyana. The company’s offshore portfolio now includes a ~30%

over 1.7 mbd of gross capacity by 2030. Beyond Guyana, management now 
outlines plans to invest above $7 bn gross in Venezuela over the next five years 
alongside three joint ventures. We expect gross production to more than 
double, from ~280 kbd today to ~600 kbd by 2031. On a net basis, we 
estimate production increases to ~180 kbd, adjusted for royalties, with capital 
spend of ~$3 bn. In Argentina, the company produces around ~80 kbd and 
expects output to more than triple by 2035.

o

industry-leading cost discipline. The company recently reported record gross 
Guyana production of ~900 kbd, with Uaru on-track for first oil later this year. 
Longtail is progressing toward final investment decision, with management 
also evaluating potential for a ninth FPSO. While management guides to a 
~100 kbd decrease in net entitlement in 3Q26 as capital recovery accelerates, 
we note the asset is positioned to double free cash flow generation by 2030 
compared to 2025.

We are Buy rated on SLB (SLB), where we see earnings driven by strong 
o
international exposure, particularly to Latin America countries such as Brazil 
and Argentina, as the company remains one of the names with the highest 
international exposure in oilfield services. SLB continues to see growth from 
Latin America, notably from unconventional work in Argentina as well as 
offshore and deepwater developments in Brazil. We also see the potential for 
SLB to see incremental earnings from Venezuela over the long-term, as the 
company is working to potentially scale up the operational capabilities in the 
country as operators look to increase production over time. As a result, we 
remain constructive on SLB as one of the largest OFS companies in the space, 
who we see as well-positioned to benefit from increasing activity in Latin 
America. We also note SLB’s position as one of the larger OFS players in the 
Middle East as an area of potential earnings growth for the stock, as we see 
the anticipated ramp up of production post conflict in the region driving 
higher earnings revisions over the long-term. 
We are Buy rated on Halliburton (HAL), where we see earnings driven 
o
primarily by increasing international activity, particularly in Latin America with 
the company’s work for YPF providing unconventional completion services. 
HAL has seen higher stimulation activity in the country also driving earnings, 
and continues to plan to utilize its ZEUS electric frac services in the region, 
which we anticipate will position the company to see potential similar future 
contracts with their existing customers in other regions who are looking for 
similar solutions. We additionally highlight HAL’s exposure to other 
international regions such as the Middle East, particularly the UAE, where we 
see anticipated production ramps after the re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz 
driving earnings power for the company over the long-term as one of the key 
players in the region. 
We are Buy rated on TechnipFMC (FTI) which continues to have a strong 
o
customer base for orders, particularly with PBR for subsea equipment and 
services. We continue to see growth for FTI, as well as margin expansion as the 
company sees the potential for an order cadence higher than the historical 
annual run-rate of $10 billion, as well as growth from iEPCI 2.0. With the 
company industrializing the remaining portions of the subsea ecosystem 
through the implementation of iEPCI 2.0, we estimate EBITDA margin 
expansion over the long-term. As a result, we remain constructive on FTI as we 
estimate margin expansion and strong order inbound over the next several 
years.

We are Buy rated on Petrobras with price targets based on a 12m-fwd EV/EBITDA of 
3.5x. Our 12-month price targets for PETR3.SA/PETR4.SA/PBR/PBR__A are BRL 
60.80/BRL 56.80/USD 23.90/USD 22.40. Key risks to our investment thesis include 
lower-than-expected Brent prices; BRL appreciation; lower-than-expected production; 
potential government intervention. 
We are Buy rated on Vista with a 12-month price target of USD 77.10. Our price target 
is based on a 2028E EV/EBITDA multiple of 3.8x. Key risks to our rating are: i) 
lower-than-expected realized oil and gas prices; ii) weaker-than-expected production 
contribution from new drilling; iii) government intervention; iv) higher-than-expected 
lifting costs; and v) CAPEX overruns. 
We are Buy-rated on CVX, and our 12-month EV/DACF, FCF Yield, and P/E-based price 
target is $225. Key risks relate to commodity prices, refining margins, and operational 
execution. 
We are Neutral-rated on XOM, and our 12-month EV/DACF, FCF Yield, and P/E-based 
price target is $168. Key risks relate to commodity prices, refining margins, and 
operational execution. 
We are Buy rated on HAL, We value the shares on the basis of an average of two 
methods between a target 7.5% FCF yield and a 7.5x target EV/EBITDA, on a 
through-cycle basis, between 2027-31. We are maintaining our 12-month target price 
of $42. Key risks: 1) Weaker NAM pricing power, 2) lower NAM activity, and 3) 
lower-than-expected margin uplift from int’l growth. 
We are Buy rated on FTI. Our 12-month price target is $80 and is based on an average 
of the valuations implied by our target through-cycle (‘27-’31) EV/EBITDA multiple of 
10.0x and target through-cycle (‘27-’31) FCF yield of 7.0%. Key Risks: 1) weaker 
commodity prices, 2) loss of contracts, 3) slower adoption of new technologies, 4) 
supply chain issues. 
We are Buy rated on Shell, supported by its superior balance sheet and its attractive 
pipeline of projects to 2028. Brazil represents c.19% of Shell’s 2026E upstream portfolio 
and is a material contributor to near-term growth. Shell’s Brazilian production is 
supported by the near-term growth of Mero. Beyond 2028, Shell-operated Gato do 
Mato provides an incremental leg of growth that partially offsets natural decline at the 
mature pre-salt hubs. We view Shell’s valuation as attractive relative to its history, with 
the shares trading at 4.4x 2026E EV/DACF (c.-32% vs. the 20y average), despite a high 
FCF yield of 16.3%, above the EU Big Oils average of 14.5%. 
We are Buy rated on Galp owing to its differentiated upstream growth and reserve life. 
Brazil represents 100% of Galp’s 2026E production in our field-by-field build, and we 
expect production to grow by c.12% in 2028E vs. 2026E, driven by the ramp-up of 
Bacalhau. Galp also stands out as having the highest reserve life among EU Oils. Its Brazil 
position is underpinned by at Tupi, Iracema, Sépia, Atapu and Bacalhau, together with its 
Namibia position, in our view justifies Galp’s c.22% premium to the EU Big Oils coverage 
average on 2026E EV/DACF.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
