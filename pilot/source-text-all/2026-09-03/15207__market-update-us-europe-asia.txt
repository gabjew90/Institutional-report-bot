U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.71% | Nasdaq -1.03% | Dow -0.79%
Synopsis

US equities finished lower in Tuesday trading, ending not far off their worst levels amid upward pressure on rates and oil and renewed
weakness in the AI trade. Stocks saw a defensive rotation, with growth and cyclicals lower while secular sectors held up better. Momentum
was weaker with both semis and memory down, software saw a big decline and Mag 7 names were mostly lower. Other laggards included
networking and communications, transports, building materials, multis, machinery, regional banks, private equity, homebuilders, travel
and tourism, media, and China tech. Outperformers included energy, pharma and biotech, managed care, exchanges, steel, agricultural
chemicals, staples retailers, dollar stores and telecom. Treasuries were weaker with some curve flattening and yields up 3-6 bp, the dollar
index was up 0.3%, gold finished down 1.9%, silver was off 2.4% and Bitcoin futures were off 2.2%, while WTI crude settled up 5.2% near
best levels on more US-Iran kinetic activity. On the single-stock side, Anthropic reportedly agreed to a $35B computing deal with Lambda,
a cloud provider backed by NVDA-US, with infrastructure company HUT-US developing the Texas data center involved in the project; AAPL-
US claimed in a new filing that OpenAI is destroying key evidence in a trade secrets case; MDT-US organic growth was better with

Macro

US confirms fresh strikes on Iran: Centcom confirmed that US forces carried out fresh strikes on Iranian targets on Tuesday, in response
to Iran's attempted attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and on US servicemembers in the Middle East. The recent
ramp in kinetic activity has put upward pressure on oil, with WTI crude near $90 a barrel, its highest level in roughly a month. On potential
spillover effects for global growth, there has been even more scrutiny on the strength in refined products, particularly diesel: prices have
jumped to their highest level in more than four months and are up over 50% from the middle of June. Treasury Secretary Bessent said the
US is preparing to announce further corporate sanctions as part of its economic campaign against Iran.

Global bond yield backup: The backup in global bond yields was the dominant story, with 10-year JGBs hitting 3% for the first time since
1996, 10-year Gilts at their highest since 2008, 10-year Bunds highest since 2011, and the 30-year Treasury bond above 5% for 55 days
this year, the most since 2006. The US 30-year yield has risen in seven of the past nine sessions following brief August buyback relief.
Structural forces continue to get outsized attention, particularly the US deficit and $40T-plus debt load, corporate supply pressure from
the AI capex boom, Japan's fiscal expansion with the BoJ behind the curve, and geopolitics, where recent focus has been on oil and refined
products.

Data misses without shifting the macro narrative: August ISM Manufacturing posted a surprise decline to 54.6, missing consensus of
55.8 and down from July's 55.6, though still the second-highest print year to date; new orders fell three points to 53.7, the lowest since
March, the prices index was unchanged at 71.1 and the employment index fell 1.6 points to 51.2, remaining in expansion for a second
straight month. Report commentary leaned negative, centred on pricing volatility, the Iran war, increasing lead times and tariffs. July
JOLTS job openings came in at 7,271K against consensus 7,313K, above June's downwardly revised 7,182K, with the quits rate ticking
down to 1.9% from 2.0%. The final S&P Global Manufacturing PMI was marked up 0.7 points to 53.9. Fed Governor Barr said the FOMC
should act decisively to raise rates if inflation does not appear to be moderating sufficiently, though it can be patient if upcoming data
show progress toward the 2% target. Attention now turns to Friday's August employment report, where the Street looks for a 55K increase
in nonfarm payrolls following a 23K July decline, an unchanged 4.1% unemployment rate and average hourly earnings up 0.3% m/m.

Breadth dips to multi-month lows as defensive trend confirms: Breadth was negative for a fourth straight day, tied with mid-July for the
longest negative streak since November 2025. Just 45.4% of S&P 500 stocks sit above their 50-day moving average, the lowest since 15
May, while 62% are above their 200-day, the lowest since 1 July. Fourteen S&P names hit fresh 52-week lows, the most in a session since
23 July, though nine hit fresh highs, an improvement on Monday's five. Defensives, measured by the average daily return of XLP, XLU and
XLV, turned positive against cyclicals on a 21-day basis for the first time since late July, having underperformed by over 430 bp as recently
as 27 August. Software trailed semis on the day but continues to outperform on a 21-day rolling basis for a 37th consecutive session, the
longest streak since August 2015. Despite the Treasury headlines, the MOVE index remains rangebound, averaging 73.3 in August against
73.0 since 1 April.

A bearish spin to start September: September is historically the worst month of the year for stocks, with the S&P 500 and Dow tending to
decline 1.1% and posting positive returns just 44.5% and 42.6% of the time respectively; Nasdaq data back to 1971 show an average
0.8% decline. JPMorgan notes that across the last nine midterm election years since 1990 the S&P has averaged a 2.2% Q3 decline
against a -0.1% 30-year average, though post-midterm performance has historically been strong. Beyond seasonality, Citadel Securities
flagged a fading earnings tailwind with Nvidia out, a waning corporate bid as the month advances, depressed volatility with little room left
to compress, redeployment of systematic capacity from the July momentum unwind, quarter-end pension rebalancing, quarterly options
expiration and a two-sided macro calendar.

EU Market Recap
Germany -1.1% | France -0.4% | UK -0.3%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished weaker on Tuesday, with the FTSE 100 back online after Monday's UK bank holiday. Travel and Leisure,
Technology, Industrial Goods and Services and Basic Resources were the worst performing sectors, while Oil and Gas, Healthcare and
Chemicals gained. Bonds were lower: Gilts rose 7 bp to 5.22% and the Bund was up 1 bp to 3.35%. The dollar was firmer across the board,
oil traded above August highs with WTI crude up 2.8% near $88/bbl, gold fell over 1.5%, industrial metals were mixed and Bitcoin was
lower. On the macro front, eurozone inflation accelerated in August to 3.3% y/y as expected and unemployment increased in July, final
eurozone manufacturing PMIs were slightly softer than flash while the UK update was revised a touch higher, German retail sales came
in much weaker than forecast in July, UK shop price inflation hit a two-year high in August, and UK housing updates from the BoE and
Nationwide remained soft. PM Burnham was due to make his first statement to MPs in Parliament, focused on public control of essential
services and decentralisation of power. On the corporate front, NOVN-CH reported positive Phase III MS results from remibrutinib, activist
Elliott built a stake in AI-FR and is pressing management to improve profitability, UCB-BE reached an MFN drug pricing deal with the US
government, PGHN-CH cut FY performance-fee growth to 20-25% from 25-40% prior, and WPP-GB is considering cutting an additional
1,000 jobs by year-end on top of the roughly 11,000 since 2025.
Macro

Eurozone inflation at a 2023 high but core benign: Eurozone August flash HICP printed 3.3% y/y, in line with consensus and up from 2.9%
prior, the highest since September 2023. Almost the entire increase was due to energy, at 14.3% from 10.3% prior, as oil, gas and refining
margins moved with the latest Iran flare-up. Inflation in Spain, Italy, Germany and France accelerated but still missed heightened
expectations. Beyond energy the details were more benign: core eased to 2.4% from 2.5%, back to pre-conflict levels, services slowed to
3.0% from 3.3%, food was unchanged at 1.2% and goods ticked up to 1.2% from 0.9%, a first modest sign of broadening with no wage-
price spiral evident. The details support a 10 September ECB hike, though the path from October is data-dependent. Bank of Finland's
Rehn said markets are correct to discount a 25 bp hike on 10 September but that later steps will be meeting-by-meeting, adding that the
ECB must prepare for the impact of a protracted Middle East war on energy prices; he sees 2026 eurozone growth resilient near 1.5-2%
and no wage-price spiral yet. Markets still discount another 25 bp hike by February 2027, though economists broadly expect no further
moves.

Downing Street forced to reiterate fiscal rules after gilt selloff: Downing Street was forced to restate the importance of its fiscal rules after
Gilts played catch-up on returning from the bank holiday weekend. The 10-year yield hit its highest level since 2008 at 5.25% and the 20-
year its highest since 1998 at 5.83%, the latter the benchmark the OBR uses for budget assumptions. Panmure Liberum's chief economist
estimated that sustained higher yields would add £6B to government debt interest costs, with a significant bearing on the tone of the
October budget; OBR estimates in March showed the annual cost of servicing debt rising from £130B this year to more than £160B by
the end of the decade. Deutsche Bank anticipates a more benign fiscal event, at its lightest still implying £10B-£20B in gross spending,
while Aberdeen sees tax rises as inevitable and both Jefferies and Pantheon see fiscal headroom reduced by £10B-£12B because of the
Iran conflict. Separately, Chancellor Healey vowed to set out a clear path on defence spending to reach NATO's 3% of GDP target, which
on OBR calculations would cost an additional £17.3B by 2030.

UK consumer prices firm while housing stays soft: The BRC's UK shop price inflation measure hit a two-year high in August at 1.5% y/y
against consensus 0.9% and 0.9% prior, with food inflation at 2.8% from 2.2% and non-food at 0.9% from 0.2%. The BRC highlighted
higher energy, input and commodity costs filtering through, particularly for imported and processed ambient foods, while in non-food
electrical prices rose as the AI boom forces up the price of memory chips and storage; BoE Agents found supermarkets expect food
inflation to peak at 4-5% this year. Housing remained soft: BoE mortgage approvals came in at 56.05K in July against consensus 59.0K
and a prior 58.22K, well below the 60.80K six-month average, while the Nationwide house price index rose just 0.2% in August, taking
the annual increase to 1.6% from 2.1%. Business sentiment was the brighter spot, with the IoD Economic Confidence Index improving to
-49 in August from -63 in July, though respondents cited tax uncertainty (58%) and general policy uncertainty (55%) as the biggest
constraints on growth and investment.

European gas near three-year highs with storage at a decade low: European gas prices softened slightly but remain close to three-year
highs near EUR69/MWh as renewed Gulf hostilities threaten to delay the LNG restock Europe still requires, with concerns that Qatari and
broader Gulf export flows stay constrained through winter. The bigger story remains storage: EU inventories were around 63% full in late
August against a five-year norm near 80%, the weakest seasonal reading in over a decade, and analysts flag that continued sluggish
injections could leave the bloc entering winter roughly 20% below average. Country-level dispersion is notable, with Italy and Poland
tracking above 80% full while Germany, Europe's largest storage market, sits at around 51%, alongside the Netherlands at 45% and
Belgium at 51%. Absent a Hormuz reopening, Goldman Sachs sees TTF gas above EUR100/MWh, with LNG competition against Asian
buyers the key swing factor into Q4.

Le Pen pitches a 3% rule and EUR125B of cuts: At last week's Medef debate, Le Pen positioned herself as the fiscal conservative in the
field, floating a German-style golden rule to keep the deficit under 3% of GDP and a EUR125B savings plan within five years targeting
migration-related spending, 'useless' agencies and France's contribution to the EU budget, while doubling down on rolling back Macron's
pension reform to allow retirement at 62. The tension is fiscal: the debt stock stands at 117.5% of GDP, the deficit remains above the EU
cap, the 10-year is at over 15-year highs and the finance ministry sees interest costs at 2.8% of GDP in 2027, leaving very little room for

Asian Markets
Japan -0.15% | Hong Kong -0.93% | China -0.16%
Synopsis

Asian equities ended mostly lower on Tuesday in another directionless session, with declines for China's mainland benchmarks, Hong
Kong and Singapore, Australia's ASX and South Korea's Kospi either side of the flatline, gains for Taiwan on MediaTek's surge, and India
paring early losses. The won was stronger again with little movement elsewhere in FX. JGB yields moved higher with the 10-year above
3%, precious metals were flat, base metals mixed and cryptocurrencies caught a bid. Asia equities were in a holding pattern as focus
steered back to the bond market. In regional developments, August PMIs were positive again although there were early signs of fatigue,
with expansion in South Korea and Taiwan appearing constrained by supply-chain constraints, material shortages and little growth in
employment, while most of Southeast Asia, Japan and China expanded again; India's fiscal Q1 GDP beat expectations largely on a surge
in investment, and South Korea's August exports rose almost 70% to nearly $100B as chip demand continued while imports were less
than expected. On the corporate front, Huawei posted a steep H1 profit decline, blaming rising memory costs and capex spend; LG Energy
Solution signed a lithium supply agreement with Arkansas-based Smackover; Nvidia is to invest $3.5B in Taiwan's MediaTek; unionised
workers at Hyundai Motor accepted a new pay deal, averting potential strike action; Hanwha Aerospace's bid for Austal may be in doubt
after the US government gave mixed messages over the deal; and Shein's private backers saw a 73% valuation decline on its Hong Kong
listing, with shares closing lower on their first day of trading although off their trough.
Macro

JGB 10-year breaches 3% for the first time in 30 years: Bonds were under pressure across the region with yield curves steepening. The
benchmark JGB 10-year yield touched a fresh 30-year high of 3.00%, breaching a widely feared psychological level, with the policy-
sensitive 2-year also notably higher. The 10-year auction (JPY2.6T, re-open #383, 2.7% coupon) resulted in a highest accepted price of
JPY97.64, below the QUICK consensus range of JPY97.70-79, with a highest yield of 3.011% versus 2.900% prior and the highest since
August 1996, though a shorter tail mitigated the negative reaction. Fiscal discipline concerns came into sharper focus after Nikkei
reported FY27 budget requests ballooned to a record ~JPY143T ($895B), above the government's tentative JPY140.6T target, with several
unquantified line items that stand to increase the total further. Elsewhere, the Australian 10-year yield hit its highest since 2008 as
September RBA rate hike expectations continue to build, with some economists predicting a further increase by year-end, while South
Korean yields edged higher with sources noting the government is planning near-record bond issuance for 2027.

Chip demand lifts Asian factories and South Korean exports: Robust demand for AI-related exports underpinned Asian economic activity
in August. S&P Global PMIs showed output and production continuing to expand throughout the region but with evidence of deceleration:
South Korea's index declined to 52.3 from 53.1 as supply-chain constraints and materials shortages weighed on output, though export
orders expanded at their fastest pace in almost six years; Taiwan eased to 54.7 from 55.1 on cost pressures, slow employment growth
and supply chain issues; Japan saw a marked upturn in output and new orders, taking the headline to 54.9 from 54.5 with new business
growth at its highest since early 2018. China's RatingDog index improved to 51.5 from 50.9 as new orders, new export orders and output
all improved, following the official PMI's return to expansion. Southeast Asian readings also improved, including substantial growth in the
Philippines. South Korean export growth accelerated further with semiconductor shipments up more than 200% in August. Japan's MOF
corporate survey capex ex-software rose 3.6% y/y in Q2, above consensus 1.6% and following a 1.4% decline, with ordinary profits
expanding a sharp 24.6% from 14.6% in Q1 and margins improving for a third straight quarter to 11.3%.

Katayama and Bessent agree an orderly yen is essential: Japan Finance Minister Katayama met US Treasury Secretary Bessent at the
North Carolina G20, their first meeting since the joint intervention operation a month ago. Katayama told reporters they reaffirmed the
consensus that an orderly yen exchange rate is essential for global financial market stability, and that ongoing coordinated bilateral efforts
serve a common purpose. He avoided commenting on BOJ policy when asked about Bessent's latest remarks, denying they discussed the
need for rate hikes. The exchange follows notable attention on a Bessent interview with CNBC in which he reiterated he cannot affect the
natural equilibrium though can send a signal, noted he possesses information the market does not have, and said he believes the
Japanese government and the BOJ will do the things that lead to a stronger yen. He also suggested the Abenomics reflation era has
probably ended.

India Q1 GDP beats but raises RBI hike risk: India's Q1 GDP grew 7.8% y/y versus consensus of 6.9% and the RBI's 7.0% forecast, with
Q4 (January-March) growth revised higher to 8.6% y/y from 7.8% despite higher energy prices and global trade disruption from the Iran
conflict. Private consumption, the largest share of GDP, grew 7.1% y/y helped partly by GST cuts, manufacturing grew 9.2%, financial
services 12.1% and fixed capital investment 11.9% against 5.8% a year earlier; gross value added rose 8.2% y/y. Economists said the
higher-than-expected growth and the sharp revision to Q4 could push the RBI to hike sooner rather than later, with one noting the key
risk is more about the persistence of high oil prices, rupee weakness and tighter global financial conditions than domestic growth. Focus
now turns to August inflation after July's 4.5% headline print. Separately, a weaker and uneven monsoon left a 14% rainfall deficit over
the summer, which may push food prices and inflation higher.

Shein lags in Hong Kong debut while AI names find bids: Shein fell as much as 10% in its Hong Kong debut, with slowing growth,
competition and valuation concerns weighing on sentiment; the retail tranche was oversubscribed 5.6x, far short of recent listings by
Unitree and CXMT. There were pockets of strength in the AI space, with MediaTek leading Taiwan semis after Nvidia agreed to invest
$3.5B, China foundational models rallying with Z.AI at a two-week high following Monday's earnings release featuring a 50% m/m increase
in August ARR, and Kuaishou a standout after announcing a China state-backed investment fund will invest in Kling AI.
