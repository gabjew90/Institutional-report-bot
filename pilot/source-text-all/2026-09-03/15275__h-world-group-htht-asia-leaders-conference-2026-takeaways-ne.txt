H World Group (HTHT): Asia Leaders Conference 2026 Takeaways: New 
signings and hotel adds on track. Supply chain the key focus but hard to 
2 September 2026 | 11:06PM HKT
 
We hosted H World management at our Asia Leaders Conference 2026 in Hong Kong 
on Sept 1-2. 
Key takeaways: 
(1) Resilient leisure but sluggish business travel demand. Manage hotel ADRs 
across region to minimize cannibalization and competition - Similar to Atour’s 
comment, H World’s management also noted resilient leisure but sluggish business 
travel demand ytd. B2B corporate customers now account for low-teens % of its 
total room nights booked. Given ongoing new hotel supply in the market, its more 
mature hotels generally face more intense competition with same-hotel RevPar -3% 
yoy in 2Q26. To optimize ADR and maintain occupancy, they are actively rationalizing 
regional pricing strategy (i.e., better manage the ADR gap between newly opened 
and mature hotels within the same regions to avoid cannibalization) and expanding it 
from Shanghai to broader Eastern China region. They are also taking various 
initiatives to capture inbound travel demand by, for example, enabling overseas 
registration of H World mobile app, listing H World’s hotels on overseas OTA 
platforms, establishing alliance with Accor on reciprocal membership benefits, etc.  
(2) Steady network expansion and closure with quality upgrade and focus - 
Management noted that its new signing momentum remains largely stable ytd at 
~2.8-2.9k annually. While franchisees are becoming more cautious, the top-tier 
chain hotel brands including H World managed to keep their new signing momentum 
by continuing to gain shares. While gross hotel addition tracked slightly behind in 
1H26, it has picked up in Jul-Aug - they still feel comfortable with its full-year gross 
opening target of 2.2-2.3k hotels. They also expect hotel closure to remain 
unchanged at 600-700 per annum (vs. 668/729 in FY24/25) as they continue to 
phase out and upgrade the older products (e.g., Hanting 3.5 and below which 
accounts for 45% of its Hanting brand hotels). Payback periods for new signings are 
strictly controlled at 3-5 years which they believe could be achieved by H World’s 
pricing premium (ADR generally Rmb20 above comparable brands nearby), booking 
volume secured from its own central reservation systems with 60-65% in direct 
booking, supply chain management, better operational efficiency, etc.

(3) Difficult to provide guidance on potential upside from supply chain - H World has 
opened up its centralized procurement platform for its franchisees and other hotels. 
Construction materials and fresh/frozen foods are intentionally excluded from its 
platform to avoid the complexity in handling localized logistics functions and cold chain 
management. The group has not provided target upside in its take rate or other revenue 
from its supply chains, but it reiterates that its focus is to support franchisees 
operationally rather than purely for its own profitability or monetization. In a way, it is 
taking advantage of H World’s large scale with 14.3k hotels in its network generating 
over Rmb100bn hotel GMV and ~Rmb30bn GMV from consumable products and 
construction materials per annum to negotiate lower pricing or procurement costs from 
its suppliers or logistic companies. This in turn would allow its franchisees to incur cost 
savings in both pre-opening capex and operating expenses, thereby shortening their 
payback period.  
(4) Asset-light overseas expansion steadily progress - H World is gradually expanding 
into Southeast Asia markets by replacing its asset-light franchisee model, targeting to 
add a few hotels overseas per annum. Over the past few years, the group has started to 
make such attempts by helping their domestic franchisees diversifying and seeking hotel 
investments overseas. They only formalize and segregate this division and lump it under 
H World International or HWI in 1Q26. These franchise hotels in ASEAN market generally 
achieve ADRs comparable to tier-1/2 cities in China while its opex is tracking behind 
more like hotels in tier-3/4 cities in China, hence allowing them to achieve better 
profitability. We believe it will take a while before these segment would contribute more 
meaningfully to H World’s profitability in longer run.   
Price Target Risks and Methodology - H World Group 
We are Buy rated on H World Group with ADR/H-share 12-month target prices of 
US$65.4/HK$51.0. Our TPs are SOTP-based, based on a 14x FY26E EV/EBITDA on its 
core hotel operations business and other investments at reported book value. We view 
our valuation as warranted given we expect H World to also be a key beneficiary of 1) 
value-focused consumption trends; 2) industry consolidation amid rising chain 
penetration; 3) pricing power on quality product offerings. 
Key Risks: (1) weaker-than-expected macro leading to slower-than-expected RevPAR 
growth; (2) weaker-than-expected funding channels in China leading to 
fewer-than-expected franchisee additions; (3) slower-than-expected recovery of 
consumer/travel demand in China; (4) dilutive M&A; and (5) weaker-than-expected 
operating performance and cash drag from Deutsche Hospitality. 
Investment Thesis - H World Group 
H World is one of the leading chain hotel operators in China. It also generates 20+% of 
revenue from Deutsche Hospitality (DH), a premium hotel brand based in Germany. We 
are Buy rated on H World shares (HTHT/1179.HK), given (1) RevPar upside on 
demand/supply imbalance and continued RevPar outperformance on solid execution; 
(2) reacceleration of market consolidation with better market liquidity fulfilling 
franchisees’ appetite to add hotels. We see the current valuation as undemanding, with 
FY26E EV/EBITDA trading below mid-cycle levels.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
