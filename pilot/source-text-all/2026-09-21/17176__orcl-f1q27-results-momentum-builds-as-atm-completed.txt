ORCL F1Q27 Results
Momentum Builds as ATM
Completed
The $20bn equity raise was fully executed, another $26bn of
"good" backlog was added, management downplayed
project delays, and alluded to having capex levers. We remain
positive despite recent rally.
Trade idea: Buy ORCL 6.70% 2056s
Buyside sentiment was fairly positive into results and ORCL cleared what we believe
was a high bar. Funding and capex guidance/messaging was reiterated and
management leaned slightly further into their ability to add backlog without growing
capital needs. Margins stepped down, but as expected with large assets going online
in the quarter and this year.
Our expectation is for sentiment to strengthen post results, with ORCL having now
strung together three consecutive credit-positive quarters. While we are cognizant
that ORCL is at the local tights, it is still a fair amount wider since 4Q26 results while
the credit story continues to strengthen. We see the October investor day as a
possible catalyst and remain positive on spreads.
Barclays Rating: Overweight
Moody's/S&P/Fitch: Baa2(N)/BBB-(S)/BBB(S)

Credit Highlights and Relative Value
• RPO rises without incremental funding needs. RPO increased from $638bn to $664bn q/q
(+$209bn y/y), while noting that there is no incremental funding for the business due to the
structuring of those contracts. This is consistent with the company's recent messaging that
customer prepayments will play a growing role in the business (see more below), and
management noted that the new RPO will not impact capex or revenues until FY28 or
beyond. Given that the initial $300bn of contracts with OpenAI has likely begun to burn off to
some extent, OAI now likely represents at most ~45% of the total backlog.
• $20bn ATM program completed; leverage moves lower. Oracle confirmed it completed its
full $20bn ATM equity program during the quarter. Cash from ops was a record $23.1bn and
gross capex was $28.5bn, while net "cash" capex was $18bn, after adjusting for $11.4bn of
customer prepayments and an $830mn outflow tied to the timing of working capital
financing. Net of the $1.6bn dividend, FCF was a $7.0bn burn ($7.8bn including working
capital financing). Management reiterated FY27 capex guidance of $90–95bn gross and no
more than $70bn net, consistent with its RPO commentary. We estimate gross and net
leverage fell from 4.2x and 3.4x to 3.9x and 3.1x, respectively, driven by EBITDAR growth, a
higher cash balance, and $4.2bn of debt paid down, slightly more than their $3bn July
maturity.
• Top-line ahead; software remains subdued. Revenue in the quarter was slightly ahead
($19.3bn vs. $19.1bn est), increasing sequentially between 4Q and 1Q for the first time, while
Cloud Infrastructure revenue printed ahead of estimates as well ($7.4bn vs. $7.2bn est).
Software fell sequentially, and was down 3% y/y, largely attributed to the Support sub-
segment. Gross margins were lower, as expected, in the quarter, near 60% due to an
acceleration in data center capacity coming online. F2Q27 revenue is expected to grow
between 30% and 34% in USD terms, while the company guided total revenue for FY27 of at
least $90bn.
• Capacity deliveries and site updates. Oracle delivered 850MW of capacity in 1Q containing
more than 300,000 GPUs, nearly 3x total 4Q deliveries and 73% of all capacity delivered last
fiscal year. Abilene remains the anchor, with 131,000 GPUs delivered in the quarter, and six of
eight buildings now handed over, representing 618MW and 75% of site capacity, while
management noted that OpenAI's new GPT-6 Astra model was trained at the site. Shackelford
is progressing well, with first Vera Rubin systems due to customers in 2Q. Notably, none of
Shackelford, Santa Teresa (NM), Port Washington (WI) or Saline (MI) contributed to 1Q
deliveries, which management framed as evidence of a broad, diversified pipeline rather than
concentration in a few marquee sites. Construction is on track at both New Mexico and
Wisconsin, with the open items on the power side, an air permit for on-site Bloom fuel cells in
Dona Ana County, and grid design and delivery coordination, though management was
explicit that potential delays at the two sites would not affect prior FY27 revenue or earnings
guidance. Recall that ORCL's various sites are already funded via a combination of data center
bonds (RDMICH) and construction/bank loans, and we continue to expect any refinancing of
these structures to wait until closer to completion and stabilization.
Relative Value: Maintain OW
At the risk of sounding like a broken record, we believe the credit narrative at ORCL is
strengthening. The company is steadily building a credibility around its backlog strategy while
continuing to prioritize the balance sheet. Management again emphasized the levers available
to minimize funding needs (customer prepays, bring your own hardware, vendor relationships,
etc), but stopped short of answering the question about when it could turn FCF positive. One
area that remains less comfortable is the timeline for its data center projects, particularly for

in its various projects, but we still consider this be the "show me" part of the ORCL story.
ORCL spreads were ~10bp tighter post results and cumulatively ~40bp off of the wides reached
during the July selloff. That said, spreads are still ~30bp wider since the June earnings call.
While part of this can be explained by the market and the S&P downgrade, we still see room for
ORCL to compress, especially if the funding picture for 2027 turns out to be more diversified
than some investors expect (ie, additional equity/converts, other currencies, chip
financing, etc). We view the October investor day as a possible catalyst and look for
management to provide more detail on the outlook, possibly adding color on the phasing of
capex and FCF over the next couple years.
Although there is a lot of 30y paper in the structure, we still view the long end as the best part of
the curve to express our positive bias, especially at current yields. We recommend buying the
ORCL 6.70% 2056s; although 9-10bp tighter post results, we see room for the long end to grind
another 5-10bp tighter in the near term and for the curve to flatten somewhat. However, given
the rally over the past two months, we suspect that a more substantial rally may be on hold
until the October investor day. 
We note that ORCL's aggregate short base continued to rise steadily over the summer,
particularly in the 2031s, 2036s, and 2056s, with its total ticker short base now exceeding $5bn.
We suspect this may begin to come down as ORCL continues to string together strong quarters
of execution and capacity ramps, but we still highlight that a number of the tranches remain
fairly technical within the structure. Meanwhile, ORCL 5Y CDS is still a popular hedge for broader
OpenAI and ecosystem exposures, and remain correlated to the equity, likely requiring a more
meaningful rally in the share price to see a larger move to the downside for the contract beyond
modest tightening.
