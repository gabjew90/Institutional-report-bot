YETI Holdings (YETI): Investor Day Takeaways
20 September 2026 | 4:06PM MDT
We attended the YETI Investor Day in Austin, Texas, on September 17th, 2026. At the 
event, management introduced a long-term financial framework through FY30 
calling for MSD-HSD% annual sales growth, HSD-LDD% adjusted operating income 
growth, LDD-high teens% adjusted EPS growth, and $1.2bn-$1.4bn of cumulative 
FCF. Underpinning this growth is a plan to grow international revenue to >$1bn, scale 
Bags & Soft Coolers into a $1bn platform, and drive productivity through Project 
Upcycle, a newly introduced cost savings program targeting $100mn in savings. 
Management also reiterated its FY26 outlook.   
Key messages from the event included: (1) YETI is materially accelerating its growth 
in bags and soft coolers. The category was described as YETI’s major growth engine, 
expecting the business to double by 2030. (2) International is now a core pillar of the 
growth algorithm, targeting >$1bn of international revenue vs. $394mn in 2025. This 
is led by Europe and APAC and management is now formally targeting several new 
geographic launches including Japan, Korea, and China. (3) US growth is now driven 
by commercialization. Management is increasingly leaning into commercialization 
opportunities in the US marketplace to drive stronger productivity of existing 
accounts, additional shelf space, new doors, sports, consumers, and marketplaces. 
Marketplaces were a key incremental focus of the presentation. (4) Project Upcycle 
was unveiled and identified ~$100mn of productivity opportunities intended to fund 
investment and support margin expansion. (5) Management provided scenarios of 
the market states that could drive the high end or low end of its growth algorithm, 
noting that the low end of the range embeds flat US drinkware growth. Here, YETI 
indicated they would be significantly disappointed if the low-end scenario (flat US 
DW) came to pass. (6) Importantly, YETI highlighted several opportunities that could 
drive growth beyond the current targeted growth algorithm, including travel, 
apparel, cookware, international expansion, and experiences beyond the current 
framework. 
Our view: We step away from the event incrementally more constructive on YETI’s 
long-term growth opportunity and the path for the stock from here. Management’s 
clear articulation of the broad-based drivers of growth provides greater visibility into 
brand growth ahead. Stepping back, we believe the event marked a clear inflection 
point in management’s strategic vision for the brand as it transitions from a durable 
hard goods brand to a premium lifestyle brand across broader segments and use 
cases, evidenced by the company’s formal decision to introduce technical apparel.

Building blocks at the low end: The MSD% sales scenario assumes no growth 
o

International growth assumes a continuation of momentum in the UK and the 
rest of the company’s current international business, with international 
contributing more than half of total incremental growth. Under these 
assumptions, YETI expects HSD% adjusted operating income growth and 
LDD% adjusted EPS growth, supported by productivity, margin expansion, and 
share repurchases. Management noted the lower-end case to demonstrates 
that the growth model can support earnings growth even at MSD% sales 
growth. 
Building blocks at the high end: The HSD% sales scenario assumes LSD% US 
o

sales productivity, expansion of the domestic distribution footprint, faster 
growth in Bags & Soft Coolers, product extensions adjacent to existing 
categories, and additional growth in underpenetrated European markets.

LDD% adjusted operating income growth and high-teens% adjusted EPS 
growth. Management noted that the category, channel, and international 
targets presented during the event collectively build to the high end of the 
revenue range.

Valuation and key risks 
We are Buy rated on YETI with a 12-month price target of $60 based on a 10.5x Q5-Q8 
EV/EBITDA valuation methodology. Key downside risks include: (1) 
slower-than-expected growth in the core US market; (2) increased competitive intensity 
within drinkware and adjacent categories, resulting in market share pressure, higher 
promotional activity, or reduced pricing power; (3) weaker adoption of newer product 
categories, including bags, packs, and other innovation platforms, limiting YETI’s ability 
to diversify growth beyond Drinkware; (4) slower international expansion; and (5) 
persistent tariff, freight, commodity, or supply chain pressures that limit margin 
expansion and earnings growth.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
