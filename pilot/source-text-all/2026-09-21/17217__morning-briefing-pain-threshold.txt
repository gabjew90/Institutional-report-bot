OUTLOOK 
A wild start to the week yesterday leaves bond markets reeling and US 10s perched above 5% 
after the latest ambush by oil and gas prices. Knives keep falling overnight and at the open this 
morning, with investors standing by the wayside. Outside of the benchmark debt auctions, the 
buying has effectively dried up. Aussie 10y yields leapt 8bp to 5.41% after the melt-up in the US 
above 5%. Treasuries have been stretched since yields crossed the 4.90% level last week. For the 
Bund, the equivalent was 3.30%. Yields are up another whopping 20bp since then. The Fed 
seemingly has little choice but to respond tomorrow and perhaps  beyond with tighter policy but 
without knowing the answer about the neutral rate, or when policy is appropriately tight. Levels 
are stretched and mean-reversion is overdue, but this could depend on energy prices, or a wobble 
in risk assets reviving safe-haven demand. Spillovers have tentatively emerged in stocks and in 
credit, cue the pullback in S&P and European stocks, and the widening in iTraxx credit indices. In 
FX, the rebound in USD/JPY dovetails with UST/JGB spreads and tells us that the adjustment in 
positions has run its course. This was confirmed in the CFTC data yesterday. Shorts have been 
flushed out. With the Fed ready to tighten and the policy spread widening vs the BoJ, some are 
still hesitating to go outright long Yen. CHF/JPY may be a safer alternative if the drawdown in risk 
assets gathers momentum. Japan has reportedly committed to raise defense spending to 3.5% 
of GDP.  The intra-day gyrations in EUR/CHF yesterday from 0.9510 to 0.9440 did not escape our 
attention and may indicate that we are near the limit where the blowout in 10y OAT/Bunds is 
fuelling safe-haven demand. The de-correlation of the Swissie was associated with the repricing 
of the European vs Swiss rates but with the latest ECB rate increase out of the way, French budget 
discussions taking shape and risk assets struggling, dialling back short CHF positions could make 
tactical sense. The French government lowered its growth forecast to 0.5% for this year, forcing 
it down the avenue of new savings worth €30bn to keep the deficit below 5.1%. Our central 
scenario (40% probability, slidepack p6) is that the budget will pass using art. 49.3 with small 
concessions.  
In EM, China activity data reaffirmed the familiar narrative of weak domestic demand and 
resilient exports, with little sign that policymakers are prepared to intervene to rebalance growth. 
Retail sales slowing to 0.4% yoy in August, FAI YTD worsened to -7.2% yoy. Car sales plummeted 
18.5% yoy, highlighting weak domestic consumption. Industrial production bucked the trend 
with growth accelerating to 5.2% yoy, supported by strength in tech and AI sectors. Property 
investment is down 19.9% ytd, worsening from 19.2% decline in first seven months of 2026. Prices 
of both the new and used home prices fell last month. Elsewhere in Asia, the INR skidded to the 
weakest level in nearly two months, leading State-run banks to offer dollars, most likely on behalf 
of the RBI. Headline CPI accelerated to 4.82% yoy in August and WPI ticked up to 9.92% yoy. The 
10y IGB yield climbs 7bp this morning to 7.035%, the highest sin four months.
