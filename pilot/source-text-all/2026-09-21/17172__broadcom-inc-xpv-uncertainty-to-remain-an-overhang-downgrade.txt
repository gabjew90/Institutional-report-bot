Downgrade to Marketweight on XPV uncertainty 
We are downgrading our rating on Broadcom (issuer & bonds) to Marketweight from 
Overweight due to uncertainties around the XPV platform established with Blackstone 
and Apollo. In light of the uncertainties with this platform, we view the bonds as fairly 
valued at +30-45 bp wide to non-XPU A-rated semis which have lagged semis by ~20-30 
bp since early June. AVGO has managed its balance sheet conservatively (1.3x gross 
leverage) with substantial growth potential (revenue & FCF +3-4x from FY25 to FY28). 
However, we believe credit spreads will continue to face headwinds as the XPV platform 
grows, partially from perceived credit risk (we explore below) but mainly from 
unfavorable spread technicals as investors potentially look through XPV financings to 
AVGO on ultimate parent risk and/or hedge via CDS. Key uncertainties are end-customer 
concentration, the amount and features (i.e. RVG) of future financings and the extent and 
pace of growth in the XPV platform. However, given FCF growth potential, likely cash 
build assuming modest stock buybacks, and debt tenders in June, it is unclear if AVGO 
could reduce balance sheet debt more aggressively to manage its strong credit profile, 
which would be a positive for bonds. We expect more clarity with results in early Sept. 
We are raising 2026 revenue and EBITDA estimates by 10% and 13%, respectively, to 
reflect earnings and guidance. 
Modeling out the XPV platform: risk likely manageable 
The XPV platform jointly announced by Broadcom, Blackstone and Apollo began with a 
1GW+ tranche valued at $35B, with XPUs leased by Anthropic, and plans to grow the 
platform to 20GW through ’28. Broadcom is providing a backstop/residual value 
guarantee on a majority of the debt financing (but not providing direct seller-financing) 
and estimates the senior debt on the initial $35B XPU tranche (~1.1GW est) has a 
maximum loss exposure of $29B (immediate 100% default at 0% recovery on full 
funding). To be more precise on loss exposure (and slightly less unrealistic), we have 
staged the funding ratably over 16 monthly rounds, assuming 5-year debt amortization, 
20% price declines/5-year chip life, and a 25% price shock at default at each point over 
the life of the lease. We estimate maximum RVG exposure peaks at $26B in Sep ’27 and 
max loss exposure (collateral value less RVG) of $2.9B. If we add subsequent 2GW deals 
on a quarterly basis (since its through ‘28), max RVG across the 20GW (at ~$32B/GW) 
reaches $370B in mid-‘29, with max loss exposure of $42B at 100% default and $10.5B 
at 25% default rate assumption. Relative to potential cash build and AVGO’s non-AI 
earnings, we believe this level of loss would be manageable for AVGO from a credit 
perspective, with gross leverage rising to 2.2x at YE’28 (excluding est. AI EBITDA). This 
assumes a buyer of chips at the shocked price. Details on pages 2-5. 
Being a credit donor requires strong credit ratings 
AVGO’s strong credit ratings (A3/A-/A-) enable its participation in the XPV platform 
(chips + RVG), in our view. However, it is unclear how the ratings agencies will adjust 
credit metrics for the RVG exposure. Initially, S&P intends to treat the RVG as debt-like 
but as the platform grows, it’s unclear if methodologies could evolve to become 
scenario-based. We expect the agencies’ three positive outlooks to be at risk.

Background on the XPV platform 
In June, Broadcom, Blackstone and Apollo established the XPV platform to lease XPUs to 
end-customers (specifically TPUs leased to Anthropic with a 5-year lease in the first 
deal), funded by secured debt (mix of loans and debt) with an amortization period that 
matches the lease term of the chips. The partnership intends to scale the platform to 
20GW per the initial press release. Our understanding of the structure is aggregated 
from company filings, press releases, press reports, rating agency reports, and Big Sky 
Funding LLC / APCHIP bond details posted on Bloomberg. Broadcom is providing a 
backstop / residual value guarantee on the majority of the debt ($29B), although we 
understand there are multiple steps/mechanics in place in the event of a default prior to 
AVGO providing the RVG, such as AVGO assuming the lease (per AVGO).  
One concern is the lack of diversification of both the product (XPUs/TPUs with no 
secondary market currently) and the end-customer/lessee concentration (Anthropic and 
OpenAI cited in the press release, with the others to-be-determined). It is unclear how 
much of the 20GW planned size for the XPV platform could overlap with the compute 
agreements that Broadcom has previously announced with OpenAI (10GW) and 
Anthropic (5GW+) or to the extent included in revenue guidance if TPUs.   
While AVGO is providing a backstop (i.e. not providing direct funding) via its participation 
in the XPV platform, seller-financing-styled arrangements have grabbed much attention. 
However, we note large seller-financing dynamics are commonplace in other large 
markets. For example, auto manufacturers/OEMs are active participants in financing 
purchases of new vehicles, with their captive fincos having approximately 54% market 
share of new car loans in the U.S. (per Experian). Should underwriting turn out to be 
strong (i.e. no/low defaults), then we view the XPV platform as a way for Broadcom to 
deploy the strength of its balance sheet (via backstops) to increase competitiveness with 
Nvidia and AMD.  
Estimating RVG exposure 
Broadcom disclosed the first XPU XPV deal resulted in $29B of maximum exposure (i.e. 
senior debt) relative to the $35B total value of the “more than” 1GW of XPUs. However, 
as the deal is not fully funded currently, maximum loss today is likely significantly lower. 
This also assumes 100% default and 0% collateral value, which is not realistic, in our 
view.  
We provide a framework to estimate a range of risk exposures related to the RVG based 
on various public documents and our own assumptions on the current $35B deal as well 
as model out the growth in risk should the program achieve the “more than” 20GW cited 
in the press release.  
We estimate peak maximum exposure in the initial deal reaches $26B with peak loss 
exposure of $2.9B, assuming 5-year XPU life (-20% decline in price/year) and additional 
25% price shock if a default event were to occur. Included in our assumptions is a 
monthly funding cadence for the remainder of the $35B/1GW+ tranche and 5-year debt 
amortization across the capital structure (loans, bond) as indicated by the schedule on 
Bloomberg.

Stress testing leverage 
To translate RVG and loss exposure to adjusted credit metrics, we make multiple 
assumptions. We calculate the potential loss on the XPV platform based on a 100% 
default rate for the purpose of stress testing, apply the 25% price shock as described 
earlier and add this loss to debt. This assumes that another party would buy the chips at 
that price (below the RVG exposure) and that AVGO would issue debt with which it would 
use to retire the existing XPV senior debt.  
In the event of material defaults, we assume the quality of earnings from AVGO’s AI 
revenues would be put into question. Therefore, we recalculate EBITDA excluding est. AI 
(all AI, not just AI compute) at an estimated incremental margin (full gross margin minus 
½ opex), to be conservative. This leaves approximately $35-40B of remaining EBITDA on 
an annual basis going forward to support AVGO’s existing capital structure. This is overly 
punitive as it excludes AI revenue from non-XPV related customers as well but we 
believe is helpful to illustrate the strength of AVGO’s balance sheet. 
Combining these adjustments we see adjusted (gross) leverage on Adj. EBITDA excluding 
AI at ~2x in 2027 and rising to 2.2x for 2028/2029 as the RVG would have grown. This 
does not include any cash build up from FCF generation until that point. We expect FCF 
after dividends of $85B in 2027, which alone could absorb the $42B potential loss

Relative Value 
Overall, AVGO spreads are 35-45 bp wider since early June after retracing 13-20 bp from 
recent wides in late July. By comparison, semis are about 15 bp wider on average vs. 
early June, with the underperformance in AVGO explained by the XPV platform, in our 
view.  
With AVGO 4.95% ‘36 and 5.7% ‘56 at 105 bp and 118 bp (on G), we believe spreads are 
fairly valued relative to non-AI single-A semis (TXN, QCOM) at +30-45 bp back. In 
addition, relative value also looks fair with 10Y G spreads flat to behind BBB-rated semi 
comps, a not-uncommon predicament between higher rated issuers with supply 
technicals vs. BBB-rated issuers without such technicals.

Valuation & risk 
Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) 
We rate Broadcom at Marketweight as we believe spreads fairly reflect uncertainty 
related to the XPV platform, which we expect will result in persistent unfavorable 
technical in spreads, despite Broadcom's success in growing AI revenues and cash flows 
and managing its balance sheet conservatively, following the deleveraging after the 
VMWare acquisition. 
 
Upside risks include favorable outcome or slower growth with its XPV platform, 
continued strong revenue and FCF growth, continued focus on managing the balance 
sheet conservatively, including potential further deleveraging. Downside risks include 
unfavorable outcome with the XPV platform from a counterparty risk and/or technical 
headwinds in credit spreads, a less conservative approach to managing the balance 
sheet with more aggressive shareholder returns and/or large acquisitions.
