Everybody hates it. We don't care.
We will probably lose 90% of you when we say this article is about Europe. The remaining 10% will
presumably disappear when we reveal it is about European tech. European tech? Does that even exist?
Bear with us. Because beneath the jokes sits a setup that is at least becoming interesting: European
software has bounced only slightly off the lows and positioning remains near the bottom of its historical
range. Valuations are still depressed. And after more than a year of negative revisions, there may, just
may, be signs of a pulse. In other words: hated, cheap and perhaps getting slightly less terrible.
Nobody wants it
European funds have taken software exposure toward the bottom of the historical range. That is quite
the contrast to the seemingly permanent love affair with US tech. Sometimes crowded trades work.
Sometimes the interesting setup is what nobody wants to own.

Services: same story

FactSet
Is that a pulse?
Revenue revisions have skewed negative since Q1 2025. Lately, however, the line has stopped looking
quite so terminal.
No boom. No European AI miracle. Just the possibility that less bad becomes good enough when
expectations are already buried.
FactSet
SAP: From European king to fallen angel
As recently as spring 2025, SAP was Europe's largest company. Today it is down roughly 20% over 12
months and needs a 50% rally just to reclaim its high.
The underperformance versus US software has been brutal. Which is precisely what makes it
interesting.


[Page 3]
And you're not paying US prices
Global software trades at around 1.5x PEG. SAP sits closer to 1.0x — roughly a one-third discount.
Nobody owns it. Nobody likes it. Expectations are buried. And you’re not paying US prices. For once,
being European might actually be the feature, not the bug.
