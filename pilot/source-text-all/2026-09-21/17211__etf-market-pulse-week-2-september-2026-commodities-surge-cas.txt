ETF Market Pulse 
Week 2 – September 2026: Commodities Surge, Cash Builds and AI Innovation Accelerates 
 
 
ETF Market Pulse 2.0: We are enhancing our weekly ETF Market Pulse with a broader 
and more comprehensive set of market indicators. In addition to tracking weekly 
performance across all unleveraged ETFs and ETCs domiciled in the US and Europe, 
providing detailed insights into the best and worst performing market segments and the 
drivers behind their moves, we are now incorporating primary market flows across all 
US and European ETFs and ETCs, covering all major asset classes. The publication will 
also feature a summary of new product launches globally. This expanded framework 
aims to provide a more complete view of investor positioning and product innovation 
across the ETF ecosystem. 
 
Energy shock reshapes the ETF leaderboard. ETF performance was driven by the 
energy and geopolitical shock, with commodities, Japan equities and short-term rates 
leading gains. Investors favoured exposures directly linked to energy scarcity and 
supply-chain disruptions, boosting commodities, tanker shipping and European natural 
gas. In contrast, rising yields and a risk-off backdrop weighed on long-duration and high-
beta segments, with uranium miners, biotech equities and palladium among the 
weakest performers. Overall, markets rewarded immediate beneficiaries of tighter 
energy markets while penalising more economically sensitive and financing-dependent 
exposures. 
 
ETF flows remained resilient at $25bn last week, with equities capturing 70% of 
inflows, but strong demand for money-market ETFs highlights a cautious investor 
mindset. Investors continued to selectively add risk through US small caps and 
Treasuries while reducing exposure to crowded trades such as US credit, 
semiconductors and Korea, equities. On a relative basis, inflows favored defensive and 
quality-oriented segments including Health Care, AI, Spain, overnight rates and REITs. 
Overall, flows might point to a selective “buy-the-dip” environment, with investors 
positioning for a potential macro turning point while maintaining a preference for 
liquidity and short-duration assets. 
 
ETF launches remained globally diverse, but three themes stood out this week: the 
expansion of leveraged products, the broadening of thematic exposures, and growing 
adoption of active fixed income ETFs. Asia focused on more traditional equity and 
semiconductor strategies, with a notable South Korean launch combining the S&P 500 
and short-duration bonds. Europe saw continued momentum in active fixed income 
alongside new leveraged exposures to major equity indices. Meanwhile, the Americas 
remained the most innovative region, led by a diverse mix of leveraged, thematic and 
active fixed income launches, including highly specialized US strategies and an AI-
focused equity ETF in Brazil.

ETF Primary Market Flows: selective dip-buying, but 
liquidity remains king 
ETF flows stayed strong at $25bn last week, 70% into equities, but the 2.4% of AUM flowing 
into money-market ETFs is the clearest signal: investors remain willing to deploy capital, but are 
keeping liquidity high amid rising yields and renewed inflation concerns.  
The strongest absolute inflows into Russell 2000, 20+ Year Treasuries and 0–3 Month 
Treasuries point to selective dip-buying and early positioning for an eventual reversal in rates, 
rather than outright risk-on. Meanwhile, outflows from US IG/HY credit, semiconductors and 
Korea suggest investors are reducing exposure to crowded or rate-sensitive trades.  
On an AUM basis, flows towards Health Care, AI, Spain, overnight rates and developed-market 
REITs, alongside limited appetite for materials, BB high yield and Brent, reinforce the message of 
rotation rather than capitulation.  
Overall, flows suggest “buy the dip, but selectively”—with investors positioning ahead of a 
potential macro turning point while still preferring the safety and yield of short-duration assets.

In Asia, product development was relatively traditional, with new launches focused on domestic 
equity exposure and regional semiconductor trends. The most notable innovation came from 
South Korea, where a new ETF combines a 50/50 allocation to the S&P 500 and short-duration 
fixed income, reflecting demand for balanced risk-return profiles. 
In Europe, activity was split between active fixed income ETFs and leveraged ETPs. The launch of 
euro-denominated active corporate and sovereign bond ETFs highlights the ongoing adoption of 
active management in fixed income, while the introduction of leveraged exposures to the S&P 
500, Nasdaq 100 and DAX underscores persistent retail appetite for tactical trading tools. 
The Americas remained the most innovative region. In the United States, new products ranged 
from leveraged ETFs, including a single-stock leveraged ETF on Hyperliquid Strategies, to highly
