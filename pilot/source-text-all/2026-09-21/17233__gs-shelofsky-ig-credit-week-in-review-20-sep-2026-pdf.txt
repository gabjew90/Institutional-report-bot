GS Shelofsky - IG Credit Week in Review 20 Sep 2026
Brad Shelofsky  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Managing Director, IG Credit Trading
Sun 20 Sep 2026, 6:48pm ET
Executive Summary:
IG100 closed -3.5bps tighter, outperforming periods of macro weakness as all-in-yield levels, defensive
setup, and the composition of the well-anticipated supply remains supportive. A hawkish Fed hike, crude
oil persistently above $100 per barrel, and heightened concerns around the AI ecosystem have all
coincided with a puzzling and frustrating grind to a sub 15 VIX. While a Long End Corp Index a ~6.3%
screens extremely supportive, we have started to see demand shift down the curve to the belly given the
flattening of the rate curve. AI vs. AI at Risk posted its worst day since DeepSeek on Monday (-2.2
standard deviations) following Dario’s call for a more measured pace. Credit markets were largely
unfazed, and Monday through Wednesday marked the second-best three-day stretch of Debt/Equity
outperformance since the basket’s launch. Primary market remains robust in volume but only 5% of
supply was in the long end as duration remains allusive. 640mm of IG outflows this week. With IG total
return negative YTD (-1.4%) there will understandable focus on whether that’s a one off data point or start
of a larger trend. YTD flows +197bn vs +97bn YTD ’25.
Following the unanimous Fed decision to hike and hawkish tilt to the day, the rate curve continued to twist
flatten. 10s30s now sits at a one-year low and we have seen the effects emerge in a few forms within the
IG world. Frist, we are seeing a shift down the curve from the all-in-yield buyers. The 7-12y bucket was
the most net bought on tflow last week with 1.8bn of secondary buying and our PT business saw 1.7:1
better buying in the 7-15y bucket, our largest skew. The second dynamic has been a touch more nuanced;
Given the flattening of the rate curve, there was a notable increase in demand for 7-9y paper and 12-15y
paper given the improved relative value on a G-Spread basis.

The primary market themes seen throughout September persisted this week: high issuance volumes with
limited market impact. Total supply reached $55bn across 22 deals, modestly above the $50bn forecast,
though only 5% was issued in the long end of the curve. The week was highlighted by Citigroup’s $12bn
four-part offering, the largest transaction in the bank’s history. Demand remained strong, with average
oversubscription of approximately 4x, marking the fourth-highest weekly average YTD. Month-to-date
issuance now stands at $137bn, with risks increasingly skewed to the downside of our $230bn monthly
forecast.
Debt/Equity correlation across the AI complex remains extremely low. The space continues to trade more
or less exclusively on supply/demand technicals rather than fundamental drivers. Monday delivered a 2+
standard deviation move in equities following Dario's call for pacing. Against that backdrop, our
hyperscaler basket widened a mild 1.8bps on the day, then rallied more than 7bps tighter across Tuesday
and Wednesday as favorable positioning, short covering, and a brief pause in supply proved dominant.

10 year lookback versus IG corps and UST. Rate vol has suppressed the retail bid and record supply has

Global Credit Trader: An Update on AI-Related Debt Issuance: Amanda Lynam and team are increasing
their 2027 estimates for global Hyperscaler issuance $400bn to $420bn, a reflection of some modest
increased in CapEx forecasts. That number would represent a ~60% increase vs 2026 estimates of $250bn
($229bn YTD). A reminder that Hyperscalers account for only ~40% of YTD AI related issuance, and
given the expectations for accelerated project and chip financing, the total numbers for AI next year are
almost hard to comprehend.
