Sun 8 Nov 2026, 3:21:17 PM ET
Democrats took control of Congress and a majority of governor’s mansions following Tuesday’s midterm
elections, resulting in a large midweek reversal and unsettled market conditions. Investors digested the
shift in Congressional control, acutely focusing on concerns about data center development and the
potential for more oversight of the broader AI build-out. While markets reacted positively Wednesday,
$2.5tn in market value was erased from the Nasdaq on Thursday as our Vol Panic Index surged to a 9.57
out of 10 on the session, representing the third largest daily jump ever 8,9 .
The scoreboard in credit looked different . Despite the pick-up in vol and the S&P’s 2.7% weekly loss, IG
closed just 1bp wider, HY was 4bps tighter, while loans logged their best weekly gain since April, showing
how credit continues to march to the beat of a different drummer…at least for now 12 .
1. CREDIT: the side story, again
Wednesday’s rally across risk assets resulted in the best single day for HY in roughly seven months, as
election uncertainty was removed and hopes for a de-escalation in geopolitical tensions and a potential
tariff pullback fueled sentiment.
Thursday’s equity decline – which registered as the largest one-day drop for the S&P 500 since April 2025
– was felt across credit, but in a meaningfully different way. Cash spreads more than gave back
Wednesday’s gains, but CDX HY moved just 0.75pt lower against the 1.45pt move the S&P implied – a
2.7 standard-deviation outlier .
There have been 10 days since 2018 where the S&P 500 moved as much as it did on Thursday. CDX HY’s
median move on those days was -2.25pts – three times the magnitude of Thursday’s loss – and it has never
lost less (h/t Reid Zhou).

2. GENERICS: boring is beautiful
This outperformance allowed generic secondary levels to remain supported and catalyzed the largest HY
issuance total in five weeks 13 . Not only was the calendar active, but it performed well, too. The 8 issuers
that approached the market saw their debt trade up an average of 1.1pts with all 10 tranches logging gains
in the aftermarket 13 .
While the HY calendar was centered around ‘generic’ risk, the biggest deals of the week came from the
other end of the quality spectrum and continued to blur the lines between IG and HY.
As our GIR colleagues highlighted , over the last six business days there have been five separate bond
deals among the hyperscaler universe totaling $67bn in aggregate value, taking the hyperscalers’ collective
YTD global debt issuance up to $296bn – meaningfully above our full-year 2026 forecast for $250bn.
Oracle’s $10bn, five-part deal on Monday was part of that $67bn, and its new 5y tranche (ORCL 7.45 ’31)
closed Friday with a 7.5% yield – higher than 83.8% of the 989 bonds in the HY double-B index, where
the median yield is 6.42% 15 .
That could mean double-Bs are rich or that AI supply is cheap (or perhaps neither!?). What we do know is
that the spread gap between double-Bs and IG says it’s the latter: at 114bps, it sits in the 92 nd percentile
since April 2025 and is 46bps wider than its late-August tights. The market is now pricing some IG AI
supply as though it is high yield .
Even so, the technical bid for higher-quality HY paper has been overwhelming and is likely to continue for
non-AI, non-energy, and non-triple-C risk…

3. AI AND ENERGY: two pockets of pain
The calm in generics masked weakness at opposite ends of the macro story. Data-center debt sold off as
investors weighed more oversight of the AI build-out while energy traded lower as the Iran war looked
closer to ending. Of the two, AI did more damage.
To put this into perspective – looking back at last week 15 :
Only 20% of energy bonds rose in price vs. 68% across HY, leading to a -0.28% return from the sector;
HY energy widened 24bps in a week when the broader index tightened 4bps and HY ex-Energy returned
+0.49%;
Energy makes up 11.4% of the HY universe ($168bn across 230 bonds) and 14.3% of BBs. The sector
trades 174bps tighter than the rest of HY – in the 5th percentile since April 2025 (before the war, the sector
traded 3-5bps wider) – i.e. with every 100bps of energy widening, it costs the HY index about 0.38pt and
BBs about 0.51pt;
However, while data-center bonds are less than half the size of energy in the double-B index (6.7% vs
14.3% of market value), they cost the index twice as much last week (-0.10pt vs -0.05pt);
Of the 29 HY AI bonds traded at GS, the average note fell 1.67pts while the rest of HY rose 0.36pt. Within
double-Bs, the average AI/data-center bond fell 1.52pts – about 4.5x the 0.34pt decline in BB energy.

TAKEAWAY
Credit held up far better than its equity beta implied following Tuesday’s midterm elections . Plenty of last
week’s data was encouraging, but much of what moved under the surface still warrants concern. Single-
name dispersion remains near its highs, and several sectors are working through micro cycles of their own,
notably software and building products. The maturity wall is the slower-burning problem: HY bonds due
in 2028 carry a 5.9% average coupon, against a 6.9% median yield across the index today, and 12.9% for
triple-Cs 17 . Most issuers can absorb that repricing when they come back to market. The weakest credits
– which accounted for 11 of last week’s 13 five-point decliners – may face a harder road ahead. The index
has taken its beta blockers; the left tail has not
