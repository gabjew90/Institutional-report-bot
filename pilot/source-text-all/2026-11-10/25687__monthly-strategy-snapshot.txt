November 2026
Jobless Claims Drop To Lowest In 57 Years
Higher Rates Deepen Housing Weakness
•
High-frequency jobs data signal a stable labor market. Weekly initial claims for 
unemployment insurance fell to 194k, their lowest since July, driving the four-week moving 
average—which smooths out weekly variability—to its lowest level since 1969. Continuing 
claims remain near 2023 lows, reinforcing signs of labor-market health.
•
September’s CPI exceeded consensus expectations, with headline inflation rising 0.6% 
MoM (3.7% YoY) while core CPI increased 0.3% MoM (2.6% YoY). Gasoline drove about half 
of the headline uptick. Higher costs for shelter, services, transportation, and medical care 
suggest that inflation pressures extend beyond energy, complicating the Fed’s rate path.
•
Higher rates continue to weigh on housing. With 30-year mortgage rates near a three-year 
high of 7.24%, pending home sales, housing starts, and single-family permits (a gauge of 
future construction) have fallen sharply. Builder confidence sank to a 14-year low, while 43% 
of builders are cutting prices, the highest share since at least 2020.
•
China's economy grew 4.5% YoY in Q3, beating expectations and easing pressure for 
more aggressive stimulus as strong exports offset weak consumer demand. Meanwhile, the 
euro zone's flash composite PMI fell to 51.9, its lowest since emerging from contraction in 
July as French services slipped back below 50 amid strikes and political unrest.
•
The US economy is on solid footing despite headwinds from fuel prices and borrowing 
costs near multi-year highs. A healthy labor market and rising household wealth have kept 
spending stronger than sentiment suggests, while AI capital investment provides a powerful 
growth engine.  We expect growth of 2.4% in 2026 before moderating to 2.3% in 2027.
•
The oil price shock and AI-related capex have kept inflation above the Fed’s 2% target, 
but we expect cost pressures to subside. Moderating fuel prices should cool inflation, while 
AI-driven productivity gains should help offset some of the inflationary pressure from the AI 
buildout. Together, these should keep PCE inflation on a downward path. 
•
The labor market remains healthy, with monthly job gains settling into a slower yet 
sustainable pace. We expect payroll growth to average ~70k/month, enough to hold the 
unemployment rate near 4.2% through 2027. AI may reshape some roles, but it should mainly 
lift productivity and shift workers toward new opportunities rather than drive broad losses.
•
While the US economic cycle is maturing, growth is poised to outpace its developed 
market peers. Weaker domestic demand, less exposure to AI-driven capex, and low 
productivity growth remain headwinds across other developed markets. EM growth remains 
stronger, albeit with wide-ranging regional differences.

Strength Beneath The Surface Rewards Diversification
Tech Sector’s Free Cash Flow Remains Positive
•
The S&P 500 fell 0.9% in October as concerns over AI demand and financing drove a sell-
off in Tech (-6.8% MoM). Lower Treasury yields supported a rotation into cyclical and rate-
sensitive sectors, with the equal weight S&P 500 outperforming the cap-weighted index by 
5.9%, its widest margin since April 2009 as investors question the durability of the AI buildout. 
•
With nearly two-thirds of the S&P 500 having reported, Q3 earnings look upbeat. 81% of 
firms have beaten earnings estimates, led by the Energy sector (100% beat rate), while 3Q26 
EPS growth is tracking ~30% YoY—marking the third straight quarter of 25+% growth. 
Excluding the Tech sector, Q3 earnings are still growing 20% YoY. 
•
Tech failed to receive a boost during 3Q earnings despite robust AI-driven cloud demand. 
Tech EPS are growing ~65% YoY, while consensus estimates point to FCF rising 52% in 2026 
and 46% in 2027, outpacing the rest of the S&P 500. Following the pullback, Tech is trading at 
the narrowest premium on a NTM P/E basis relative to the S&P 500 (4%) since 2020.
•
Brazilian stocks fell 12% MoM following the surprise presidential election outcome, 
weighing on EM LatAm (-7.6%), where Brazil is 59% of the index. European stocks ticked 
down 0.2% as the lower Tech weighting helped offset French political concerns, allowing 
MSCI Europe ex-UK to outperform the S&P 500 by 1.1%. Still, the S&P 500 leads by 5.8% YTD.
•
The US bull market stumbled as it entered its fifth year in October, but strong 
fundamentals remain a tailwind. Corporate earnings are unusually robust for this stage of 
the cycle, while history offers additional support. For example, fifth-year bull markets and the 
12 months following midterm elections have typically produced positive returns.
•
Near-term risks are building, but earnings remain the market’s strongest support. 
Despite Fed hawkishness, Treasury yields near multi-decade highs, and geopolitical risks, we 
expect the S&P 500 to reach 8,450 over the next 12 months ($385 EPS/~22x P/E). Robust 
earnings growth and improved valuations should provide a favorable backdrop for equities.
•
Tech, Industrials, and Consumer Discretionary are our preferred sectors. Strong earnings 
and historically moderate valuations support Tech, while the AI infrastructure buildout, 
power generation, and defense spending favor Industrials. Steady job gains and income 
growth should help Consumer Discretionary recover from this year’s underperformance. 
•
We continue to favor US equities, supported by stronger economic growth, superior 
earnings momentum, and leadership in AI. Internationally, we favor EM Asia, one of the key 
beneficiaries of AI capital investment, and Japan for its solid earnings and tech exposure. 
Meanwhile, lower oil prices could weigh on LatAm next year.

•
The Fed held rates at 3.75%–4.00%, with two FOMC voting members dissenting in favor 
of a 25 bp hike. The statement upgraded its growth assessment from “solid” to “strong” and 
reiterated that incoming data will guide future decisions. Chair Warsh noted that financial

•
The 10-year Treasury yield fell 35 bps to 4.94%, its sharpest monthly decline since 
February 2025, while the Bloomberg Treasury Index gained 2.4%. Yields retreated from an 
October intra-day peak of 5.37% as AI-related concerns weighed on equities and fueled safe-
haven demand for Treasuries, underscoring the diversification value of bonds.
•
Falling Treasury yields supported longer duration IG credit, offsetting modest spread 
widening. The Bloomberg US Corporate IG Index gained 2.2% MoM despite spreads widening 
10 bps to 90 bps, led by weakness in Tech and other AI-related issuers. IG outperformed HY 
(+0.3% MoM). CCC-rated spreads widened to 1,116 bps, just shy of their 2022 peak. 
•
Following their worst month since 2008, Munis rebounded 3.9% in October, their best 
gain since November 2023. Falling Treasury yields, amplified by the muni index’s longer 
duration, drove the recovery as September’s sell-off restored relative value. The 10-year AAA 
muni-to-Treasury ratio fell below 70%, near its 3-year average, from above 80% in September. 
•
The Fed is set to deliver one more hike in 2026 and then hold policy steady. With the labor 
market healthy, inflation set to gradually cool, and recent tightening beginning to restrain 
financial conditions, policymakers should have room to remain patient. A patient approach 
can make the economy more durable while still moving toward stable prices. 
•
Moderating inflation and less Fed tightening than markets expect should pull the 10-
year Treasury yield toward 4.50%–4.75% over the next year. A sustained move above 5% 
would likely require renewed inflation, stronger than expected growth, or a prolonged hiking 
cycle. Healthy Treasury demand should also help keep yields contained. 
•
Bonds offer a meaningful source of income for diversified portfolios. Compared to the 
previous decade, today’s higher starting yields provide solid income, greater resilience to 
rising rates, and attractive total return potential if economic growth slows. Importantly, 
investors can earn generous yields while potentially reducing credit risk. 
•
We continue to favor staying up in quality, preferring Treasuries, short-duration 
corporates, and longer-dated munis. Although IG credit spreads remain relatively tight, 
attractive all-in yields, healthy growth, and solid balance sheets are supportive. High yield 
corporates are more vulnerable to rising volatility as the cycle continues to mature.

Oil Prices Continue To March Higher
Silver Extends Decline From January’s Record High
•
The Bloomberg Commodity Index slipped 0.4% MoM, cooling from a 14-year high as 
weakness in grains (-9% MoM) and metals outweighed higher oil. Soybean prices fell on 
record expectations for the upcoming US harvest. Aluminum production is recovering faster 
than expected at smelters that were damaged early in the US-Iran conflict.
•
WTI crude rose ~15% in October to ~$104/bbl, marking its fourth consecutive monthly 
gain. Concerns that hostilities could resume after the US midterms, alongside periodic 
Iranian attacks on Persian Gulf shipping, kept export volumes constrained. WTI is now up 
~50% since bottoming in early July, during the Strait of Hormuz’s brief reopening.
•
Lower Treasury yields and the geopolitical risk backdrop failed to provide support for 
sentiment-driven precious metals, pushing gold down 6.9% and silver down 18.6% MoM. 
Gold closed below $4,000/oz for the first time since July and its lowest close since October 
2025. Meanwhile silver has fallen nearly 60% from its January record high.
•
Alongside higher oil prices, the US dollar rose 0.4% MoM to its highest level since March 
2025. Much of the advance reflected euro weakness, as political unrest and mounting fiscal 
concerns in France pushed the currency 1.7% lower against the dollar. The dollar pared some 
gains late in the month as risk-off sentiment pulled Treasury yields lower.
•
The path to a US-Iran diplomatic off-ramp remains challenging, with the post-midterm 
period raising the risk of renewed escalation. As a result, energy commodities are  
vulnerable to disruptions and higher for longer prices. Even so, we expect the US economy 
and financial markets to absorb the impact without derailing the broader expansion.
•
Without a durable US-Iran deal, Persian Gulf oil exports are unlikely to fully recover, 
keeping prices significantly above pre-war levels. Higher prices are leading to demand 
destruction and encouraging more global supply. This should gradually rebalance the market 
and pull WTI toward our $65 target, though volatility along the way may be inevitable.
•
Global bond yields near multi-decade highs remain a headwind for precious metals by 
increasing the opportunity cost of holding non-income-producing assets. Industrial metals, 
by contrast, can continue to benefit from structural demand tied to the AI infrastructure 
buildout, the electrification theme, and global defense spending. 
•
After its recent rally, further dollar upside appears limited, though stronger US growth, 
the Fed’s hawkish stance, and favorable rate differentials reduce the likelihood of 
revisiting the USD’s 2026 lows. We expect EUR/USD to trade between 1.10 and 1.20 over the 
coming year, while the threat of intervention limits further weakness in the Japanese yen.
