Eyes on earnings
Insights from the Fundamental Equities Team
A Q2 2026 corporate earnings review from BlackRock Fundamental Equities
A European earnings revival. A positive earnings season for Europe builds on recent 
earnings improvement, and earnings strength has translated into performance and 
flows. Within Europe, we focus on the areas that have the potential for consistent, 
long-term earnings growth – both AI beneficiaries and beyond.
The second quarter earnings season was positive for Europe. Earnings grew 23% 
from the same period a year earlier, just behind U.S. growth of 25%.1 After a decade 
of stagnation after the Global Financial Crisis, Europe once again has exciting and 
consistent earnings growth, as the chart below shows.
Earnings drive markets and, since the start of 2025, Europe has outperformed US 
equities.2 Investors have taken note, with BlackRock data showing that flows into 
European equities have picked up in recent months.
Some of the earnings improvement can be explained by higher energy prices, which 
boost the large energy companies in the European index. This growth may fade if 
there is a lasting resolution to the Middle East conflict and energy prices subside. 
But Europe also has several large, world-class companies that are the beneficiaries 
of long-term trends and growth drivers, and we believe that a portfolio that 
concentrates on these companies is well positioned to see lasting earnings growth.
We also believe that European equities come with attractive valuations – the region 
currently trades at a 25% discount to U.S. equities – and offers investors a sensible 
source of regional and sectoral diversification.3 Europe has companies that benefit 
from AI, companies that we think are set to grow for many years without much direct 
exposure to AI, and also a selection of quality companies that are currently available 
at steep discounts to their history. We explore these three areas on the next page.

Why European momentum can continue
Europe’s AI champions
We believe Europe offers differentiated exposure to the 
upcoming wave of spending on AI, which we estimate will 
reach $10 trillion by 2030. From semiconductors to 
electrification and gas turbines, various European 
industries are benefiting from AI infrastructure 
investment and the associated surge in power demand. 
Examples from the recent earnings season include chip 
toolmaker ASML, which saw a 20% upgrade to 
consensus expectations after it raised guidance for this 
year’s earnings.4 The company also announced 
substantial capacity expansion plans. Siemens Energy, 
which provides gas turbines and grid infrastructure, 
reported a 62% year-on-year increase in gas-services 
orders, with a record backlog and production lead times 
extending several years into the future.
The winners elsewhere
We continue to see attractive opportunities in the civil 
aerospace industry, where there is growing demand, high 
barriers to entry, double-digit earnings growth and 
strong capital returns. The recent earnings season 
marked another strong quarter, with many of the 
aftermarket companies – involved in maintenance and 
servicing – beating expectations. Fears over jet-fuel 
prices have led to volatility in this area, but aftermarket 
names are still benefitting from ageing fleets and under-
supply, driving high demand for spare parts. Safran was 
the standout name in the quarter, comfortably beating 
expectations and raising full-year guidance on stronger 
aftermarket demand. Airbus, Europe's largest aircraft 
manufacturer, highlighted improved execution and 
progress towards its delivery and production targets.
Earnings across the European banks continued to 
develop positively over the last quarter. Net-interest 
income remains strong in a favourable interest-rate 
environment, while balance-sheet growth has returned in 
certain regions. Overall earnings were strong, with 
revenues generally ahead of expectations, resilient asset 
quality and encouraging cost discipline. The competitive 
backdrop also remains favourable, reflecting the 
significant improvement in industry structure over the 
past decade through consolidation and balance-sheet 
repair. We remain selective, favouring banks with 
stronger structural growth or meaningful cost 
opportunities that can drive further earnings growth over 
the coming years.
The moment for quality to shine?
Quality is a common attribute across many of the 
companies we invest in. We seek businesses with secular 
growth prospects and attractive economics, supported by 
dominant competitive positions.
Many of Europe’s high-quality companies have 
underperformed over the past five years amid a period of 
rising interest rates. The result is that quality can now be 
found at attractive valuations, as the chart below shows. 
Europe remains home to many world-class franchises, 
companies owning core technologies that make them the 
enablers of some of the large transformational changes 
going on around us. As the quality fundamentals of these 
businesses reassert themselves, we expect a greater 
dispersion between the best companies and the rest.
