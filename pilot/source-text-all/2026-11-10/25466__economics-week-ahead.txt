Economics Week Ahead
 
Next week, attention turns to Tuesday’s CPI report, with no year-over-year readings 
available because of last October’s shutdown-related data gap. We expect headline and 
core inflation to clock 0.28% and 0.21% month-over-month, respectively. Softer core 
inflation would offer some relief after September’s upside surprise but should still leave 
our call for a December rate hike intact. Housing remains under pressure, with existing 
home sales expected to fall 5.0% in October.
Abroad, UK Q3 GDP should post another firm reading, supported by business investment 
and AI-related spending. Inflation takes center stage in emerging markets, with prices 
expected to accelerate in Mexico, Brazil, and India. Persistent pressures should favor holds 
from Banxico and Brazil, while we have brought forward our expected Reserve Bank of 
India hike to December.

U.S. Week Ahead
CPI • Tuesday
This month’s CPI report will be unique. Since the BLS did not collect 
price data in October 2025 amid the government shutdown, it will 
not publish year-over-year inflation rates this month, which will put 
an even bigger spotlight on the month-over-month changes. We 
estimate the headline CPI rose 0.28% in October. Gasoline inflation 
slowed following September’s sharp increase, while food inflation 
likely picked up as higher commodity and transportation costs 
gradually make their way into the grocery store.
We expect the core CPI to have risen 0.21%. Some of September’s 
more pronounced goods price increases, including in apparel and 
toys, are unlikely to have been repeated in October. This should 
drive a cooling in core goods inflation from 0.25% in September to 
0.11% in October, the latter being roughly in line with its average 
monthly pace this year.
Within services, we look for primary shelter inflation to return to 
trend following September’s strong increase. Medical care services 
inflation should hold steady at last month's pace, but we will be 
watching the health insurance line carefully since October is one 
of the semiannual periods in which the underlying source data is 
updated. Elsewhere, the recent strength in travel-related services 
is likely to fade after sharp increases in airfares and hotel prices 
in recent months. All told, we expect core services inflation to 
moderate to 0.24% in October.
A softer core reading would be welcome after September’s upside 
surprise, but it would do little to alter the broader picture of 
2026 being a lost year for bringing core inflation back to 2%. We 
expect the report to reinforce the slow and uneven nature of the 
disinflationary process, underpinning our expectation for the FOMC 
to hike once more in December.

Existing Home Sales • Thursday
Affordability remains a formidable headwind for the housing 
market. Existing home sales rose modestly in September but 
remained weak at a 3.99 million annualized rate. We estimate 
that resales fell by 5.0% in October, pulling the pace down to 
3.79 million. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate reached 
7.0% by the end of September, causing pending home sales to 
fall to the lowest level over the past 25 years. Supply growth has 
simultaneously slowed, spurring ongoing price increases on top of a 
cumulative 57% price hike since the pandemic.
Looking ahead, slightly lower economic growth expectations should 
put some downward pressure on mortgage rates, but the housing 
market is unlikely to break out of its anemic state any time soon.

Advanced Economies Week Ahead
U.K. GDP • Thursday
The UK economy looks set for another firm quarter despite higher 
energy costs and tighter financial conditions. Next week’s Q3 GDP 
release should show growth of 0.7% quarter-over-quarter and 1.9% 
year-over-year, with business services and investment spending 
providing much of the support. The breakdown and September’s 
monthly figures should offer a clearer picture of momentum 
heading into Q4. Survey data point to continued expansion through 
Q3, though momentum softened in September. Technology 
services and AI-related investment supported growth, while higher 
energy bills and mortgage rates weighed on consumption and 
consumer-facing services.
Resilient activity leaves policymakers less room to wait as inflation 
risks build. The BoE raised Bank Rate to 4.00% this week despite 
limited evidence of second-round effects, and now projects 
inflation to reach around 5% by mid-2027 as energy costs feed 
through. We continue to expect another hike in Q1 2027 to bring 
Bank Rate to 4.25%.

EM Week Ahead
Mexico CPI • Monday
Mexico’s inflation looks set to edge higher again in October. We 
expect headline CPI to rise 3.69% year-over-year, with core inflation 
up 3.84%. Both annual rates would exceed their first-half readings 
of 3.62% and 3.78%, respectively. The expiration of summer 
electricity subsidies should drive much of the non-core increase. 
Electricity prices jumped 18.51% month-over-month in the first 
half of October as subsidies ended in 18 cities. Within core, services 
should provide the largest contribution, with higher airfares and 
late-October tourism demand likely adding pressure.
The renewed pickup in inflation should limit Banxico’s room to ease 
after yesterday’s 25 bps cut to 6.25%. We expect rates to remain 
unchanged through the rest of 2026 and 2027. While the board 
could become more dovish after Jonathan Heath’s term ends in 
December, inflation pressures persisting into next year could keep 
further cuts off the table.

Brazil CPI • Thursday
We expect Brazil’s October headline inflation to rise to 4.81% year-
over-year, with food prices outweighing some relief from electricity 
tariffs. The extended IPCA reading through mid-October showed 
that September’s electricity-driven pressures had faded as the 
tariff flag shifted from yellow to green, removing surcharges from 
electricity bills. Food consumed at home still pushed inflation 
higher, largely due to supply pressures. More broadly, prices rose 
across eight of nine major CPI groups.
Even with inflation picking up, the Brazilian Central Bank (BCB) 
still has room to cut from a Selic Rate of 13.75%. Restrictive 
policy is weighing on activity, though uncertainty over the Lula 
administration’s fiscal plans favors patience for now. We expect 
rates to remain unchanged through year-end before cuts resume in 
Q1 2027. Higher inflation could slow the pace of easing, but we still 
see room for the cycle to continue.

India CPI • Thursday
We expect India’s October headline inflation to rise to around 6% 
year-over-year, at the top of the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) target 
range. High vegetable prices should drive much of the increase, 
while base effects could lift the annual rate to that level even if 
monthly inflation slows to ~0.4% from September’s 0.69%. Food 
pressures remain a concern. Weak rainfall and El Niño threaten 
agricultural output, while higher energy and transport costs could 
limit relief from government measures. September wholesale 
price growth of 11.39% year-over-year also points to broader price 
pressures. Meanwhile, high-frequency indicators suggest growth 
remains firm, which gives the RBI less reason to delay tightening.
We have brought forward our expected hike from Q1 2027 to 
December, which would take the policy rate to 5.75% by year-end. 
Resilient growth and inflation near the upper band strengthen the 
case for an earlier move. Beyond December, we expect a more data-
dependent approach. Further hikes in 2027 remain an upside risk 
rather than our base case.
