With hyperscaler capex now exceeding cash flow from operations and the midterms 
results introducing the potential for new constraints on development, investor focus 
has increasingly shifted to how much runway remains in the US data center 
construction cycle. In this report, we address this question by segmenting the 
planned US data center capacity pipeline into three distinct categories: (1) under 
construction, (2) high probability announcements (per Aterio’s definition), and (3) 
remaining medium and low probability announcements (per Aterio’s definition). We 
then layer each category into a construction spending trajectory. Given the 
uncertainty around timing, we also size the total construction opportunity 
embedded in the pipeline, regardless of when it is spent. The goal of our analysis in 
this report is to show how much of the cycle is already visible and what would need 
to be seen from here, rather than to call when AI infrastructure investment peaks. 
Bottom line: We see good visibility into continued US data center construction 
spending growth through 2028 from projects already under construction and 
announced projects that Aterio views as high probability. While the midterms results 
introduce additional risk to project timelines, a potential outcome is extending the 
duration of the cycle, rather than significantly impacting the TAM. Projects already 
under construction support strong spending growth through 2027 and underpin just 
above half of our 2028 spending forecasts, with subsequent project starts layering 
on top. Visibility becomes more limited beyond 2028, with the 2029-2030 outlook 
increasingly dependent on announced projects converting to construction. We are 
tracking a robust pipeline that could support continued growth, but we don’t have 
the visibility to underwrite the trajectory today.

How long is the data center construction cycle? 
 
 
In this note, we leverage Aterio’s project level data center tracking (as of October 
end) to assess the visibility of the US data center construction cycle by separating 
planned capacity into three buckets: (1) projects already under construction, (2) 
high probability announced projects (per Aterio’s definition) that have not yet 
started construction, and (3) remaining medium and low probability announced 
projects (per Aterio’s definition). We then translate each bucket into an expected 
construction spending trajectory to answer a simple question: if no additional data 
centers were announced from this point forward, what would the spending trajectory 
look like based solely on the existing pipeline? In reality, the construction spending cycle 
could peak well beyond the horizon of our analysis as new projects continue to be 
announced and project timelines are highly likely to extend. Importantly, this illustrative 
exercise assumes projects come online according to their current targeted completion 
dates. Continued new project announcements and/or delays to existing project timelines 
could extend the construction spending cycle beyond the horizon of our analysis, with 
delays likely pushing spending further to the right and reducing the capital intensity of 
individual years.

Revising our data center construction spending forecasts  
We raise our data center construction spending forecasts for 2026/2027 to $80 
bn/$100 bn from $67 bn/$87 bn previously, and introduce our 2028 data center 
construction spending forecast of $125 bn. We understand the optics of raising our 
data center forecasts following the midterms, which introduced the prospect of delays, 
but the impulses driving our higher forecasts were set in motion over the last few 
months. Data center spending accelerated from 55% yoy in June to 90% in September, 
while construction starts in 3Q were up meaningfully yoy. Importantly, data center 
construction spending is distributed across a project’s construction cycle, so our in-year 
spending forecasts are tied to expectations for GW of capacity coming online in that 
current year and the following two calendar years. For example, calendar year 2026 
construction spend is driven by projects energizing across 2026, 2027, and 2028. Our 
forecasts are based on the current expected completion cadence of projects under 
construction expected to be energized in 2026-2028, and then we layer in what we view 
is a conservative estimate of spending associated with high probability announced 
projects expected to come online in 2028-2030. We include no speculative or even 
medium probability announcements in our forecasts. 
What data are watching from here? 
(i) Project starts. Data centers take ~2 years to construct, so projects that start 
construction in 2027 could support spending through 2029. We estimate project starts 
lead bookings for our MEP coverage by ~ six months, also providing visibility into the 
near-term orders trajectory.  
(ii) Incremental announcements. Data centers take ~1 year to start construction 
following announcement, so announcements in 2027 should continue to build spending 
visibility through 2030. Project announcements pending construction are up 55% yoy. 
We will also be watching the conversion of announced projects into Aterio’s 
high-probability category. Today, there are ~464 GW of announced projects, with ~171 
GW categorized as high probability. 
How large is the opportunity?

complementary way to frame the opportunity is to quantify the construction spending 
value embedded in the existing project pipeline, regardless of when it will be spent. The 
current US data center project pipeline represents ~$1.9 tn of potential construction 
spending by our estimates, although importantly, this is a measure of total pipeline value 
rather than an estimate of what will ultimately be spent. This includes projects under 
construction that are highly likely to be energized, as well as low-probability announced

Methodology. We calculate the value of the data center pipeline for each of our three

construction and high probability pipelines. At $3.5 bn per GW, this implies an 
incremental $1 tn+ of spending.

indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
