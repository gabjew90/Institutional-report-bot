We provide midterm thoughts on the "time to 
power" stocks (Powered Shell Providers and 
Behind the Meter power generation players) as a 
result of the midterm election results. 
By far, the most important election result, from the 
perspective of data center development, was the 
Texas gubernatorial election. Democrat Gina Hinojosa's victory in the  
gubernatorial race in Texas could bring greater policy uncertainty and constraint 
on future supply, increasing the relative value and pricing power of existing, 
power-secured facilities. In August, Texas paused portions of its data center 
interconnection process after Gov. Greg Abbott ordered an audit of all data center 
projects in ERCOT’s queue. Interconnection requests had reached approximately 474 
GW, more than 5x ERCOT’s record peak electricity demand, with data centers 
representing roughly 90% of new power requests. Gov. Abbott warned that 
development on this scale could threaten grid reliability and stability, prompting 
ERCOT to delay its Batch Zero transmission planning study for the first group of 
projects entering Texas’ new large load interconnection process. The audit was 
intended in part to distinguish credible projects from speculative requests that may 
be inflating Texas’ demand outlook. In terms of actions a Texas Governor  can take:

The election of Democrat Hinojosa in the Texas gubernatorial race in our view 
increases risk of data center delays and cancellations.
Stocks with largest Texas data center and Behind the Meter power exposure: 
CIFR, MARA, GLXY, SEI, VST, CEG, AEP, SRE, CNP and NRG, power equipment 
stocks.
Relative "time to power" winners: WMB, EQIX, DLR, WULF, APLD, TLN, BE, HUT in

IPPs: 
We expect knee-jerk underperformance across the group, but continue to see 
valuation support from cash flows on current assets, contracts, and forward 
curves before considering data center upside. We think underperformance initially 
would be led by VST and followed by NRG and CEG. TLN and PEG do not have 
Texas exposure - we would expect pressure on sentiment to impact TLN and PEG 
shares but would expect relative outperformance. In our view, the stocks currently 
price in very little if any valuation credit for data center contracting activity or 
power price upside from tightening markets as a result of data center power 
demand. We calculate the value of VST's current assets before upside opportunities 
to be ~$180/share, well above yesterday's $140 level. For CEG we estimate that 
value to be $249, slightly below the stock's $263 level but that would exclude all 
potential PJM data center contracting upside, which we still see as significant and 
supporting our price target. For NRG we estimate that value at $129/share, well 
above the current $96 price, and for TLN we estimate $430 vs. the $357 stock price 
We would expect modest downside in ERCOT forwards. ERCOT power prices are 
in modest contango through 2029 but absolute power price levels only reach the 
low-$50s per MWh by that time. This is far too low to incentivize meaningful new 
fossil plant supply, and battery storage economics have become much less attractive 
in the market this year. We don't think the curve is pricing in meaningful load growth 
to begin with, so would only expect a modest near-term decline in the curve  
We would expect relative outperformance in the more heavily PJM exposed IPPs, 
particularly TLN and CEG, and going forward would expect investors to focus on the 
potential for nuclear contracts (CEG and VST) and TLN's data center contracting 
efforts. 
Power equipment and on-site power companies 
We would expect underperformance across the group due to the risk of slower data 
center industry growth rather than Texas-specific exposure, with engine suppliers 
potentially most sensitive (INIO). Equipment suppliers GEV, INIO, and BE have 
growth outlooks that are heavily driven by data center power demand and Texas is 
clearly a large and important market for data center industry growth. Of these 
players, GEV has the most diversified customer base and regional exposure and we 
would expect limited impacts to earnings forecasts (given visibility to 2030+ with 
significant contracted volumes) but downside in multiples across the group. 
Investors have debated the supply-demand balance for engine manufacturers like 
INIO, and Texas has been a meaningful market for onsite power with engines so INIO 
could potentially be exposed to slower bookings activity in 2029+. We don't see 
strong exposure for Bloom to Texas specifically though there are several large 
projects (Hedgehog, Yellowbox) seeking air permits that investors may discount 
more heavily.

We would call out Solaris as having attractive valuation support based on its 
current contracts. The company's four key long-term contracts with hyperscalers 
and major data center operators support an $81 valuation level assuming no future

Utilities 
Texas exposed utilities: CNP most sensitive to downside in our view, while other 
TX utilities already trade at cheaper relative valuations. Potential implications 
could be both a slowdown in data center activity, along with the possibility that new 
regulatory commissioners get appointed as terms expire (two in 2027), with the 
prospects of utility treatment that is less favorable to investors. CNP has the 
highest exposure to Texas, trades at an ~11% premium, and is well-owned so could 
be most exposed to a downside correction. SRE, AEP, XEL, and ETR all have Texas 
exposure as well, and we would expect underperformance on potential earnings 
revisions modestly downward along with multiple compression 
For gas utilities we don't see a direct impact since the Texas Railroad Commission 
(which oversees the group) is elected and data center activity has not meaningfully 
impacted companies like OGS and ATO. 
We would expect investors to shift into more defensive utilities like AEE, ED, DUK, 
and remain in ETR (given Louisiana exposure)
EQIX should be well positioned in this environment given its nine operating Texas 
facilities, although the company-level benefit may be limited by the modest size of 
its Texas footprint (~3% of 280 global data centers) and the availability of capacity 
within those facilities. DLR has a more measurable Texas exposure - Dallas metro 
represented 4.2% of total annualized rent as of 3Q26. DLR’s 80 MW, $300 million 
Digital Garland project remains exposed to the incoming administration’s treatment 
of ERCOT’s verification process, but a slower or more restrictive approval 
environment could increase the scarcity value of DLR’s stabilized, power-secured 
assets. Shares for both operators have exhibited a higher beta to broader AI-related 
headline volatility, creating risk of short-term price volatility - however we remain 
positive on colocation data centers and believe incumbent operators should see 
further pricing power. 
WULF is insulated against Texas data center regulatory uncertainty, and its likely 
next data center project is, in our view, in Kentucky, where we see significant 
support for WULF's data center development in the state. WULF navigated a 
moratorium at Lake Mariner and a potential moratorium at Justified and neither 
event materially affected site delivery or overall scheduling. WULF’s experience 
building power projects and managing moratoriums could favor the company and 
reinforce our view that WULF’s management is one of the strongest in the sector. 
APLD is also a relative beneficiary, given lack of exposure in Texas and a focus on 
growth in states that we view as politically favorable.
We view Bloom Energy (BE) as well positioned in an environment in which 
Democrats may increasingly push back against the environmental and noise impacts 
from natural gas turbines. Given Bloom Energy's lack of harmful pollutants such as

We continue to have a high level of conviction that WMB is materially mispriced 
relative to BTM data center power growth that is likely to become more clearly 
visible over the next 12-24 months. As a provider of BTM power solutions that 
leverage its natural gas pipeline footprint, WMB offers geographic flexibility and gas 
turbine access through large pre-orders that allow it to provide gas-fired power to 
hyperscalers inside of 18-24 months of contract signing. We see an attractive 
asymmetry at current levels, with significant upside if further data center projects 
materialize. Subsequent positive growth capex and EBITDA estimate revisions, 
should they materialize, both (1) lower forward consensus multiples and (2) justify 
further re-rating to account for widening above-sector growth and out-year 
sustainability, in our view. 
We expect terminal value concerns with WMB's power projects to diminish as 
the competitive benefits and long-term optionality of WMB's power 
infrastructure become clearer. We continue to believe the most likely post-
contract outcome for these power generation assets will be customer renewal, with 
decade+ operational histories at that point to inform the renewal decision, 
complexities with grid access that are not easily resolved and revenue share 
agreements that can co-exist with grid access (sale of excess power to the grid) all 
supporting factors. Longer duration PPAs (15+ years) and use of combined cycle 
turbines in future site designs should further improve asset longevity. To the extent 
not renewed, WMB has options in what likely will be power short markets and 
could then look to sell/contract the power with other offtake, divest the assets, or 
repurpose and rate base the turbines (60-70% of project capex) in interstate 
pipeline compressor stations. Within the first contract, WMB will have earned full 
return of and on capital.
