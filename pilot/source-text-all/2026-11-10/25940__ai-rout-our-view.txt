AI rout: Our view
Recent volatility across AI and technology markets has created a more challenging
backdrop. While uncertainty around regulation, financing and the pace of AI
investment could keep markets volatile in the near term, the longer-term structural
case remains compelling as we enter the next phase of AI adoption.
PRO
Matthew Quaife 06/11/2026
What’s happened?
Global equity markets have come under sharp pressure, led by AI, semiconductor and data-centre-
related companies. The technically driven sell-off followed sweeping Democratic gains in the US
midterm elections, which raised concerns that greater regulatory scrutiny on data-centre development
could slow the pace of AI infrastructure investment. The scale of the move also appears to have been
amplified by crowded positioning, leveraged investors and options-related hedging activity.
In particular, Democratic victories in key states such as Texas, Ohio and Georgia have raised concerns
over greater restrictions on data centre development, including tighter permitting requirements and
potential construction delays. Given the importance of these states to planned US AI infrastructure
investment, this has raised questions over the pace of the capital expenditure cycle. Historically,
technical selling of this magnitude can leave markets oversold, potentially creating opportunities to add
exposure.
Unlike previous cycles over the past few decades, the current AI investment cycle is driven primarily by
capital expenditure rather than consumer spending. The nature of this cycle means it requires financing
to sustain the same pace of investment, and therefore a technically driven sell-off can have an impact.
AI remains transformative, with the potential to reshape how we work and live, while driving meaningful
change across industries. It is also a key driver of global growth, not just from the direct investment
demand but also from the indirect wealth effects from equity market rallies that have supported
consumer demand. As such, any slowdown in the capital expenditure cycle could weigh on headline
GDP growth, potentially supporting a further rally in bonds, which have already moved higher amid
hopes of a calmer Middle East.

What does this mean for markets and investors?
In the near-term, the financing needs of the capital expenditure cycle, combined with technical factors,
mean that market volatility could remain elevated, and this needs to be accounted for in portfolio
construction.
Over the medium to long term, AI will continue to transform industries and meaningfully influence
corporate earnings. More broadly, earnings growth remains a key driver of medium-term returns, and
we continue to expect healthy earnings growth over the next two years, supported by a global economy
that remains in expansion. The recent rise in interest rates and market volatility has also led to valuation
multiple compression, leaving some higher-quality companies trading at attractive valuations.
How are we adapting to the changing AI opportunity?
Within AI, we believe the next phase of the investment cycle will increasingly favour higher-quality
companies with easier access to financing, while opportunities broaden towards companies adopting
AI rather than those focussed purely on capital expenditure.
We have reduced and adjusted the composition of our AI and technology positioning in recent months.
We are keeping these positions smaller, assessing markets from a technical perspective and looking for
opportunities to add exposure to higher-quality companies, taking these factors into account.
Broader market performance
Away from AI stocks themselves, it is worth noting that minimum volatility stocks have rallied
significantly. Since the beginning of October 2026, European minimum volatility indices are up more
than 12 percent, while broad global indices are down more than 2 percent over the equivalent period.
A number of stocks in areas such as consumer staples are up more than 20 percent. This suggests that
recent market moves reflect a rotation within equities rather than a broad withdrawal from equity
markets, with hedge fund liquidations likely contributing to the volatility. Some of these areas are also
looking overbought in the short term, while Europe may face additional headwinds in 2027.
Diversifiers such as gold have held up relatively well. If the AI investment cycle slows, we could see a
reversal of the recent downtrend in gold, given the broader market backdrop, developments in
government bonds and the AI trade, and positioning that has already become lighter.
Looking ahead
We reduced our overall risk exposure from overweight to neutral in September and remain neutral
today. While we do not see indicators pointing to the end of the economic cycle, and the broader
macroeconomic backdrop remains supportive, uncertainty around interest rates and developments in
the Middle East means that market volatility could persist.
We continue to look for opportunities to add risk when supported by our models and assessments. In
the current volatile environment, we believe it is increasingly important to remain focused on
fundamentals and actively identify opportunities to generate additional returns. At the same time, we
remain mindful of the financing required to sustain the AI capital expenditure cycle and the potential for
market volatility to become self-reinforcing.
