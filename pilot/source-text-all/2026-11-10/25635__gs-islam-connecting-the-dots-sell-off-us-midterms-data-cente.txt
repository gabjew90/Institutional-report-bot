Connecting the Dots: Sell-off, US Midterms, Data centers
and Conviction List plus key research
6 November 2026 | 9:02PM GMT | Research | Equity
Sell-off. The initial US sell-off post the blue tsunami at Midterms spread beyond the broad
data center-related names (with owners of current compute seen as the relative beneficiaries
in a DC slowdown on lower supply) to the index level.
Data centers. We are revisiting this expert call with Aterio and the charts which show the
scale of Texas in the US pipeline (26% of 2026-2030 net additions) and how most of that
pipeline is announced and not under construction. Michael Ng frames the risks to his US DC
forecasts here and thinks that his forecasts are conservative. It is worth noting that he
highlights Aterio’s definition of under construction includes site works (e.g., land clearing)
which developers typically do ahead of getting an electricity services agreement or
environmental & air permitting (i.e., the moratorium can affect under construction DCs too).
Investors are debating whether a slowdown in US data centers would be positive for
Europe – Alberto Gandolfi has long been arguing that Europe does have a significant DC
opportunity too (as recent deals have shown, including Drax this week). In his view, DC
investments should be looked at in an idiosyncratic way given the execution risk, and
Solaria is a potential data center beneficiary in Europe with an upcoming catalyst
(CMD 19 Nov at GS office in London, Buy rated and on the Conviction List).
Coming back to idiosyncratic ideas in volatile times: see our November Conviction
List. One of the things we look for is upside to consensus earnings (see Exhibit 1). This
month, we added Carlsberg (solid topline growth underpinned by strong soft drink trends and
progress on deleveraging) and Deutsche Bank (improving revenue momentum in the
Corporate and Private bank, well-placed for German momentum) while removed Haleon,
HSBC and CTP from the list. Read the full report here and listen to the podcast version
here.
