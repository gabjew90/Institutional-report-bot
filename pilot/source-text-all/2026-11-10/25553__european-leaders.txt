Positioning and key trends
European Leaders Europe is entering a phase of renewal. After years of
underperformance, the region now combines cyclical with powerful structural

drivers. European earnings growth is set to accelerate, supported by fiscal
expansion, policy reforms, and structural drivers such as artificial intelligence,
power and resources, and longevity, as well as Europe’s own ambitious reform
agenda. We see attractive opportunities for European companies across five key
drivers.
Industry 4.0 and Automation The Fourth Industrial Revolution (Industry 4.0) is
underway and, in our view, is set to transform manufacturing through smart
automation. Advances in AI, humanoid robotics, and industrial software are
rapidly expanding the capabilities of automation equipment. We believe market
demand is at a turning point, with manufacturing recovery gaining momentum.
Recent PMI surveys show conditions have steadily improved, with the index
reaching 50 for the first time in three years—a key signal of expansion. Looking
ahead, Europe’s next industrial cycle should be supported by fiscal stimulus and
the continued shift toward smart manufacturing.
Defense and Infrastructure After decades of stagnant defense spending and
declining capabilities, Europe is entering a major investment cycle in defense and
infrastructure. This shift is driven by the Russia-Ukraine conflict and ambitious
NATO spending targets, including the goal to reach 5% of GDP by 2035. National
modernization plans signal the most significant security and infrastructure build-up
in recent history. Europe now faces the urgent challenge of upgrading outdated
military assets—many dating back to the 1990s—by investing in new equipment,
adopting advanced technologies, and expanding inventories. We believe the
European defense market—including cyber security—is set to become one of the
most attractive end-markets, offering high visibility and reduced cyclicality thanks
to strong structural growth drivers. In addition, we expect significant infrastructure
investments like airfields, bridges, ports, railways, pipelines, LNG terminals, and
the power grid.
Decarbonization Europe’s decarbonization strategy and energy policy are among
the most ambitious globally. With clear targets, robust policy frameworks, and
substantial investment, the EU is determined to become the world’s first climate-
neutral continent by 2050. This transformation demands ongoing innovation, but
it also unlocks significant opportunities for economic growth, job creation, and
global leadership in clean energy technologies. Renewables already made up 47%
of Europe’s power generation mix in 2024, and this share is set to rise further.
Additionally, the shift toward electrification, renewable energy, and greater energy
efficiency is poised to benefit companies across the European industrial sector. In
parallel, the expansion of smart and green mobility is opening new growth
avenues for automobile manufacturers.
Demographics and Consumers Europe is at the forefront of global demographic
change, with rising life expectancy and persistently low fertility rates making it the
region with the world’s oldest population. Nearly 20% of Europeans are now over
the age of 65, compared to just 10% globally. This demographic shift is fueling
sustained demand in health care, consumer health, and luxury sectors. Health care
spending per capita for those over 65 is three times higher than for working-age
individuals, providing strong tailwinds for pharmaceutical innovators and medical
device manufacturers. Consumer companies also benefit from wealth
concentration and increasing demand for health-related and luxury products.
Financing structural reforms Europe’s transformation depends on its ability to
secure adequate financing. According to the Draghi report, delivering the EU’s
structural reform agenda will require annual investment of around 5% of GDP
through 2030—well beyond the capacity of public budgets alone. The solution lies
in mobilizing Europe’s substantial private savings and channeling them into
productive, long-term investments. This is the core objective of the proposed
Savings and Investment Union (SIU), which aims to deepen capital markets,
harmonize regulation, and revive securitization—unlocking new lending and
investment opportunities.

Company
Airbus
NL0000235190
CIO Selected
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is CIO Selected
Airbus looks attractive on an NTM EV/EBIT basis, in our view. The
company is benefiting from a multi-year backlog and increasing
production rates, which we believe should support earnings growth
at an attractive pace above the European industrials sector average
through the end of the decade.
Company profile
Airbus Group has three divisions: (1) Airbus Commercial, currently
one of two manufacturers of 120-plus-seat civil aircraft and military
transport aircraft; (2) Airbus Helicopters, a leading helicopter
company, previously named Eurocopter; and (3) Airbus Defence &
Space, which develops and manufactures a range of defence and
security platforms and products, including fighter aircraft, missiles (via
its 37.5% stake in MBDA), UAVs, defence electronics, space
transportation, and satellites and satellite services. Airbus also owns
50% of ATR, a turboprop aircraft manufacturer.
What drives our opinion
The investment case for Airbus is built on several key strengths. The
company benefits from a robust order backlog, reflecting strong and
sustained global demand for its commercial aircraft, particularly the
A320neo and A350 families. This demand is driven by airline fleet
renewal and expansion as passenger traffic from a growing middle
class and airlines seek more fuel-efficient models. Airbus is gradually
ramping up production, especially for narrowbody jets, and UBS
expects delivery volumes to increase in the next years as supply chain
challenges ease. This should support both revenue and margin
growth. The company is also focused on expanding operating
margins through cost discipline, pricing power, and a favorable
product mix. Strong free cash flow generation supports shareholder
returns through dividends and potential buybacks. The key downside
risks to our Airbus investment case include travel restrictions similar to
those experienced during the COVID-19 period or fuel shortages,
uncertainty around near- and medium-term aircraft deliveries due to
ramp-up delays, and unfavorable FX developments, particularly euro
appreciation versus the US dollar.
Lead strategist: Alexander Stiehler, CFA, CIO Head of Longer Term
Investment Themes, UBS Switzerland AG

Alcon Inc
CH0432492467
CIO Selected
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is CIO Selected
Alcon deserves a P/E valuation premium relative to the overall market,
in our view, given its attractive and improving margin as well as its
fast profit growth. We believe its current 12-month-forward
consensus P/E estimate stands at an unjustified discount to the
median of just over 32x since its listing in 2019.
Company profile
Alcon designs, manufactures and distributes a full suite of eyecare
products within two businesses: Surgical (57% of sales) consists of
implantable products (intra-ocular lenses), consumables (surgical kits)
and equipment (lasers). Vision Care (43% of sales) includes contact
lenses and ocular health (over-the-counter eye drugs). Alcon is the
number one and number two global player in the surgical and vision
care markets, respectively. Alcon is headquartered in Geneva,
Switzerland.
What drives our opinion
Alcon holds leading positions in two attractive ophthalmology
markets, both of which should enjoy mid-single-digit percentage
demand growth over the next few years. It dominates the global
market for intra-ocular lenses, which are used to treat cataracts, and
is a major player in contact lenses. After its investments peaked in
2018—around the time of the spin-off out from Novartis—the
company is now benefiting from a series of product launches, which
should help to gain market share and steadily boost its operating
margin. Key recently launched new products include Vivity intraocular
lenses in China, its Unity vitreoretinal cataract system, and Precision7
soft contact lenses. This has also been followed by launches of the
Belkin Vision glaucoma laser device as well as the Tryptyr for dry eye.
Also, Alcon has low financial gearing.
Key risks include failure of pipeline products; new or tougher
competition; legal threats including product liability cases; access,
reimbursement and pricing challenges; a shifting political and
regulatory environment; currency and interest rates. Alcon-specific
risks include inability to trade patients up to multifocal intra-ocular
lenses and further intangible asset impairments.

Company
ASM International
NL0000334118
CIO Selected
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is CIO Selected
We expect strong top-line growth given the transition to gate-all-
around (GAA) transistors and ASM International’s defensive position.
Also, we see growth in FY26 accelerating into FY27 as the 2nm ramp
drives ALD and epitaxy adoption, with a significant scale up in
capacity for 2nm over time. Over the medium term, we see
innovations such as molybdenum, which is replacing some copper
and tungsten metal layers, as the driver of top-line growth. We
expect continued results in leading-edge semi-caps given robust fab
investments and tight memory capacity.
Company profile
ASM is a leading supplier of semiconductor process equipment for
wafer processing. In recent years, it brought Atomic Layer Deposition
(ALD) and Plasma Enhanced Atomic Layer Deposition (PEALD) from
R&D right through to mainstream production at advanced
manufacturers sites.
What drives our opinion
ASM International (ASM I) is a key enabler of the shift in transistor
architecture from fin field-effect transistors (FinFET) to gate-all-around
(GAA) designs. As transistor sizes continue to shrink to boost
computing capabilities, FinFET transistors are reaching their limits in
terms of performance and power efficiency. At increasingly smaller
nodes, FinFET designs encounter issues such as short-channel effects
and power leakage, which impact the reliability of leading integrated
circuits (ICs), ultimately reducing yield and increasing costs. GAA
transistors offer greater control over the channel, as the gate
surrounds it entirely, allowing for improved current flow between the
source and drain. This enables the production of faster, smaller, and
more efficient chips, supporting the continued progress of Moore’s
Law. ASM International is well positioned for this transition due to its
expertise in atomic layer deposition (ALD) and epitaxy, in our view.
Both technologies are critical and experience a significant increase in
layer intensity during the shift to GAA transistors. We believe ASM I is
likely to maintain or grow its market share over time, as its
established presence in existing fabs and broad chemistry know-how
—developed over decades—provide a strong advantage in
distribution, product offering, and partnerships. ASM International is
particularly geared toward the most advanced technology nodes, as
foundries are scaling their first generation GAA capacity. Key risks
include the possibility of a smaller 2nm node than expected, which
could limit equipment demand; competitive pressures that may erode
ASM International’s market share; and, since equipment stocks are
sensitive to the economic cycle, any macroeconomic slowdown could
weaken end-market demand and ultimately impact equipment sales.
Adverse FX risks could also impact operating performance.

ASML
NL0010273215
CIO Selected
Changes
There are no changes to selection and/or weight.
Why it is CIO Selected
ASML has one of the strongest business positions in our coverage, in
our view. We think its strong leadership position on its technology
and irreplaceable supply chain make it truly defensive. We believe
market participants are over-discounting cyclical fears at the expense
of structural growth drivers and thus, we view the current valuation
as an attractive entry point. We expect the significant tightness in
memory supply/demand and elevated memory spot prices to sustain
amid the strong structural growth of AI, and continued conversion of
traditional DRAM capacity to AI High Bandwidth Memory capacity.
Therefore, we see an attractive multiyear prospect of elevated
capacity additions. Having said that, semiconductor equipment stocks
tend to be very sensitive to macroeconomics as any deterioration in
end-markets such as consumer semiconductors and memory for AI
semiconductors can lead to order push-outs or cancellation of
equipment due to the lower need for capacity. We thus recommend
investors monitor consumer macroeconomic data points.
Company profile
ASML is the leading supplier of lithography equipment for the
manufacture of semiconductor devices. A Philips and ASMI spin-off
established in 1984, ASML has progressively gained market share to
become the leading supplier of lithography equipment to the
semiconductor industry, with few able to compete at the leading
edge. The company’s products are critical to the ongoing advances in
semiconductor technology. ASML supplies all major semiconductor
manufacturers, including Samsung, TSMC, Intel and Hynix.
Competition primarily comes from Nikon and Canon.
What drives our opinion
ASML is a leading supplier of semiconductor lithography, a critical
part of the semiconductor manufacturing process that has been the
driving force behind continued transistor shrinkage. We believe
spending on lithography as part of semiconductor capex should at
least maintain its intensity over the medium term, which should
enable ASML to benefit from healthy topline growth. Given its strong
visibility, we view ASML’s long-term (2030) target as plausible, which
implies quite an attractive valuation for such a defensive business.
However, we note that the revenue recovery may be uneven;
therefore, investors should stay attentive to this risk. While ASML
doesn’t have the most attractive revenue growth profile in the semi-
cap landscape, we believe it has a strong gross profit positioning
through its pricing power and thus we envision healthy bottom line
EPS growth. ASML’s core moat is centered around ﻿technological
differentiation leading to a monopoly position in EUV lithology.
However, often underappreciated, ASML’s role as a systems
integrator and thus a strong supply chain partner increases its
defensive qualities. Therefore, attempting to disrupt ASML requires
exceptional execution on many hard-to-do tasks. Risks include a
structural decline in lithography spending and significant execution
issues across major customers resulting in delay in capital spending
plans. In the near term, a lack of recovery in consumer semiconductor
end-markets could also limit wafer demand. ASML will need to scale
capacity significantly over time, the complexity of which creates
execution risk, which may lead to disappointing financial results in
any given quarter. Adverse FX risks could also impact operating
performance.

Why it is CIO Selected
We view Barclays as attractive given inexpensive valuations (based on

Company profile
Barclays is a UK-headquartered banking group. It is engaged in UK

What drives our opinion
Barclays has a diversified business model across both businesses and
geographies, which we believe should enhance its resilience in
volatile markets. However, the bank remains heavily exposed to
investment banking and has further cost optimization potential in the
UK. Normalizing interest rates would be supportive for the UK
business as well as its US credit card business, which should benefit
from both improved margins and higher volumes. The bank is well
capitalized, with a CET1 ratio of 14.3%. Major risks include
macroeconomic uncertainty, a worse-than-expected credit cycle,
lower market revenues, more challenging regulation, an adverse
outcome from the FCA motor finance review, litigation risks, and
increased competition.

Why it is CIO Selected
We like Deutsche Bank given its strong positioning in global capital
markets and its exposure to an improving investment banking cycle.
The bank should also benefit from a more supportive German macro
backdrop and enhanced capital flexibility, underpinned by ongoing
operational improvements. In our view, current valuation is
undemanding.
Company profile
Deutsche Bank is a leading international financial services provider
and the largest bank in Germany. The group consists of four
operating business units: the Corporate Bank, which includes
Commercial Banking and Global Transaction Banking, the Investment
Bank, the Private Bank (retail banking, private banking), and Asset
Management (DWS).
What drives our opinion
We see Deutsche Bank entering a new phase of earnings growth,
driven by accelerating revenue momentum across its Investment
Bank, Corporate Bank and Private Bank franchises, alongside positive
operating leverage. Management targets revenue growth of more
than 5% annually through 2028 while limiting cost growth to around
2%, supported by digitalization, cloud migration and ongoing
efficiency initiatives. This should help lift ROTE from around 10% to
above 13% from 2028 onwards, in our view. In addition, a stronger
German macroeconomic backdrop provides a further tailwind. The
bank is also set to combine improving operational performance with
significantly greater capital flexibility, facilitating increasing share
buybacks. Major risks include weaker-than-expected revenue growth,
deteriorating credit quality, adverse market conditions, exchange rate
and interest rate volatility, and regulatory or tax changes.

Why it is CIO Selected
We like Erste’s leading franchise in CEE, which we believe will enable
the bank to achieve mid single-digit loan growth, surpassing the
average sector growth in 2026. Furthermore, we see the Santander
Polska acquisition as a positive catalyst for the stock, adding to the
share of growth markets. This is not reflected in today’s valuation
(based on a 2026 price-to-earnings multiple), in our view.
Company profile
Erste Bank is a universal bank that offers retail, corporate and
investment banking services. The bank has subsidiaries in Austria, the
Czech Republic, Romania, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia and Serbia.
Erste’s portfolio is characterized by strong retail gearing, a top five
position in most market, and significant emphasis on digital channels.
Its major shareholders include Erste Foundation, savings banks and
savings banks foundations, and Caixa Bank (9.9%), as well as other
parties to the shareholder agreement, while the free float stands at
c70%.
What drives our opinion
Erste Bank has leading franchises in Central and Eastern Europe
(CEE). With the region’s continued strong macro prospects, we
believe Erste Bank is set to continue to benefit from a convergence in
retail demand towards Western European standards. Given the
combination of leading market shares, a de-risked loan book, and a
solid capital position, we expect the bank to be well positioned for
sustainable growth. Erste Bank has successfully completed the
acquisition of a 49% controlling stake in Santander Polska, which
adds another growth engine to the group (at attractive margins).
Poland also offers Erste broader access to intra-regional trade flows
and potentially the opportunity to roll out its online bank ‘George’ in
the country. We see Erste Bank’s digital franchise as a strong long-
term differentiator in the region and its current valuation as
attractive. Major risks include the volatility of interest rates, exchange
rate developments, credit and market risks, and regulatory changes.

Ferrari NV

CIO Selected
Changes

Why it is CIO Selected
Ferrari’s 3Q26 results were once again good, with a further upgrade
of the full-year revenue guidance across all metrics, proving once
again the strength of the brand and its business model. We believe
Ferrari will continue to deliver on margins in 2026 and beyond. We
particularly like Ferrari within the automobile sector as we believe
that thanks to its unique positioning, Ferrari is not affected by the
issue of Chinese competition like other brands. As a side note, its
plug-in hybrid models are in high demand, reaching 42% of total
shipments in FY25 (down to 33% in 3Q26), and we expect this to
evolve over time. Its electrification strategy—aiming for 40% hybrids
and 20% pure-EVs by 2030—looks reasonable to maintain the
unique character of the Ferrari brand, in our view, but more
emotional EV products might be needed.
Company profile
Ferrari is among the world’s leading luxury brands, focused on
design, engineering, production, and sales of the world’s most
recognizable luxury performance sports cars. The company’s name
and history are closely associated with its Formula 1 racing team,
Scuderia Ferrari, the most successful team in Formula 1 history. The
company operates in four segments: Cars and Spare Parts; Engines;
Sponsorship, Commercial and Brand; and Other. The company is
headquartered in Maranello, Italy.
What drives our opinion
Ferrari is one of the world’s most prestigious brands, with a unique
and superior business model. Ferrari’s aim is to keep supply tight to
preserve its brand value and pricing power. Volume growth will likely
not serve as a major driver of the stock. However, a strong product
portfolio, an increasing level of vehicle customization, and more
limited editions are keys to success, in our view. The introduction of
the brand-new 849 Testarossa and the recently presented 12Cilindri,
296 Speciale, Amalfi, and F80 all confirm the success of this strategy
and ensure continuing earnings momentum, in our view. On its
capital market day (CMD) on 9 October 2025, Ferrari presented its
new 2030 strategy and financial guidance. While it disappointed
those who had hoped for aggressive growth forecasts, we think it
confirmed the ongoing successful strategy of focusing on the brand’s
scarcity value. At the end of May, the first fully electric Ferrari model
was finally presented—a very atypical design for Ferrari, and priced at
EUR 550k, which is well above the company’s current average selling
price. While the progressive design surprised brand enthusiasts and
left the impression of “limit interest by design”, it looks like it appeals
to another customer group and the order book seems to be covered
for 2027. The valuation (in P/E terms) is at a substantial premium to
its auto peers, trading closer to those in the luxury sector. Ferrari
enjoys decent visibility on its sales, with long lead times and an order
book that extends into 2028, resulting in relatively stable and visible
earnings and cash flows, which is highly atypical for an auto
company. The main risks are substantial wealth destruction that
impacts demand from Ferrari’s high-end clientele; the length of the
current geopolitical situation and its effect on demand in the Middle
East; societal or regulatory pressure to address wealth inequality in
certain markets; unfavorable emission-related regulations; very high
trade tariffs with the inability to pass them on via higher prices
(mitigated by Ferrari immediately announcing up to 10% price
increases on the initial US tariff announcements); a substantial
strengthening of the euro; supply chain disruptions; any dent to the
brand, including the market reception of the just introduced EV, and
any major disappointment in financial results, as well as way too high
(growth) expectations by investors as seen on various times in recent
past, which would pressure its still-high valuation multiples.
Lead strategist: Rolf Ganter, CFA, CIO Head of Europe Equities, UBS
Switzerland AG

Galderma Group AG

Changes

Why it is CIO Selected
We believe Galderma stands out as a compelling stock due to its
strong commercial execution and clear path to margin expansion. The
stock is trading at around 48x on a 12-month forward P/E multiple, a
significant premium compared to European mid-cap pharma peers at
21x, reflecting its superior earnings growth outlook (26% CAGR for
2026-29 versus 12% for peers). Nevertheless, we believe an even
higher valuation premium is justified, given the sustainability of
Galderma’s franchise.
Company profile

Why it is CIO Selected
Siemens Energy trades at a significant discount to its US peers on a
range of valuation multiples, making it an attractive investment
opportunity, in our view. The company benefits from strong,

Company profile
Siemens Energy AG is a power equipment manufacturer with
offerings across the energy value chain. The company offers a range
of products and solutions, including gas turbines (large and
industrial/aeroderivative gas turbines), compressors, transmission
products (transformers, switchgears), and wind turbines. It has four
reporting segments: Gas Services (forming c32% of sales), Grid
Technologies (22% of sales), Transformation of Industries (14% of
sales), and Siemens-Gamesa (32% of sales). Siemens Energy was
spun off from Siemens Group and listed as an independent company
on 28 September 2020.
What drives our opinion
Siemens Energy is positioned to benefit from long-term
electrification, grid modernization, and rising power demand, which
should support sustained investment in transmission, transformers,
and gas turbine infrastructure. A still-tight gas-turbine market into
2030 underpins order growth, while the expanding installed base
should steadily increase higher-margin aftermarket revenues over
time. At the same time, Grid Technologies gives the group direct
exposure to decarbonization and network upgrade spending,
improving earnings visibility and quality. Together, grid and
aftermarket businesses should account for the majority of EBITA by
FY30, supporting durable cash flow growth and stronger shareholder
returns. Risks: weaker-than-expected grid spending, lower-than-
expected data center capex spending, which would have a negative
impact on additional electricity demand and potentially result in lower
the demand for gas turbines, project execution risks, the turnaround
in the wind business stalling, and supply-chain disruptions.

Company profile
Sika is a diversified group, geared to construction (construction
division: c80% of sales) and transportation (industry division: c20%,
mainly automotive). Sika generates c40% of its construction turnover
in infrastructure and c50% in renovation/modernisation (c30%
residential, c70% non-residential). Within construction, the concrete
industry is Sika’s most important end market (c25%), followed by
roofing. The industry division’s main activities include the bonding of
car windows, and, increasingly, the replacement of mechanical
fastening techniques by bonding. Auto OEM sales account for c10%
of group sales.
What drives our opinion
Sika has an above-average exposure to structural growth trends in
green construction, including energy-efficient buildings,
decarbonization, and building refurbishing needs. While global
construction markets were cyclically weak from 2023-25, we see
signs of it bottoming out in 2026. While Chinese sales trends remain
mixed, India is strongly supported by robust infrastructure
investments. While Sika now benefits from rising synergies after a
large acquisition in 2023 (MBCC), its debt leverage is quickly
moderating, supported by robust free cash flow generation. Overall,
we expect mid-single-digit organic sales growth and a rising
operating margin supported by scale and product mix effects.
Chemicals companies are cyclical and face risks relating to supply-

and interest rates, as well as potential environmental, safety, and
regulatory liabilities. Company-specific risks include a lack of recovery
of the construction market and auto production volumes, and its
ability to execute well on business strategy and M&A.

What drives our opinion

program in its investment banking and French retail divisions,
reallocating capital to higher-growth businesses. The bank’s
profitability trajectory has improved in recent quarters, supported by
cost controls and a rebound in revenues. Major risks include the
macroeconomic backdrop, asset quality, weaker-than-expected
market revenues, execution risks around the ongoing restructuring
program—such as failure to reduce costs in the French retail and CIB
businesses—as well as regulatory, litigation, and political risks.
