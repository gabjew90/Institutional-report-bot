Q3’26 results so far: not kind to most share prices 
Q3’26 reporting in the North American precious metals space picked up last week with 
five companies and is set to pick-up further this week with eight (four precious metal 
producers, three royalty & streaming (R&S) companies and one primary silver producer). 
To date, it’s been tough, with 5/6 share prices underperforming the S&P/TSX Global 
Gold Index following publication, despite (mostly) ok results. The weak gold price hasn’t 
helped. The majority have pointed to emerging cost pressures (diesel, consumables, 
drilling, parts, etc.) but also expressed an ability to partly mitigate these. 
Key focus items for Q3’26 precious metals results season 
In Q3’26, gold averaged $4,263 per ounce (/oz) down 5.4% quarter-over-quarter (QoQ) 
and up 23.3% year-over-year (YoY). Our North American precious metals coverage is 
forecast to generate free cash flow (FCF) of around $6.1 billion (bn) in Q3’26E (and 
$29.2bn in 2026E). While gold (and silver) prices were down QoQ, with sector free cash 
flow still robust, capital allocation and shareholder returns should be a key focus. Expect 
those that can surprise up on capital return while still investing in growth (and meeting 
or beating guidance), should see their share prices perform best post Q3’26 results. 
Expect a focus on early outlooks for 2027 (production, cost, and reserve updates).

Gold falls 3.0% due to Fed uncertainty  
Gold began the week at $3,998/oz on Monday (26-Oct-26) and rose to $4,017/oz as 
traders weighed the outlook for Federal Reserve policy. Prices advanced further to 
$4,060/oz on Tuesday (27-Oct-26), supported by bargain hunting after the metal fell 
below a key technical level ahead of the Fed’s rate decision. Gold edged higher to 
$4,067/oz on Wednesday (28-Oct-26) after the Fed left interest rates unchanged and 
reiterated its commitment to containing inflation. The metal reversed course on 
Thursday (29-Oct-26), falling to ~$4,022/oz as the Fed offered limited guidance on its 
next move, leaving open the possibility of a rate hike before year-end. Selling 
accelerated on Friday (30-Oct-26), with gold opening at $4,042/oz before falling to 
$3,875/oz, as rising oil prices fuelled inflation concerns and a global tech selloff 
prompted investors to liquidate bullion to cover losses elsewhere. Gold ended the week 
down 3.0%. Gold equities also weakened, with the S&P/TSX Global Gold Index, 
Philadelphia Gold and Silver Index (XAU), and NYSE Arca Gold BUGS Index (HUI) declining 
4.1%, 3.9%, and 3.9%, respectively.

Model updates 
Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM): reiterating our Buy rating 
We update AEM our model post Q3’26 results that included adjusted EBITDA of $2.74 
billion (bn), that beat BofAe and Bloomberg/Visible Alpha consensus. Total capital return 
to shareholders was $455m, or around 53% of FCF. While the 2027 budget has not been 
completed, management noted that the 50% payout rate (vs. 40% previously) is a 
reasonable estimate for next year; we maintain our estimate of $2.4b in capital returns 
for 2027, approximately 41% of our FCF estimate, leaving room for this to move higher. 
We update our model for management commentary on Q4 operations, including planned 
maintenance shutdowns, that will reduce throughput at Detour and Kittila in Q4’26E. We 
model Q4’26E gold production of 809koz with 2026 gold production of 3.31Moz at the 
low end of the 3.30-3.50Moz guidance range, in line with company expectations. We 
raise our Q426E total cash costs to $1,175 per ounce (/oz), bringing our 2026E total 
cash costs to $1,114/oz, now in line with AEM guidance for full year total cash costs to 
be at the high-end of the $1,020-$1,120/oz guidance range. We increase our 2026E EPS 
to $11.04 from $10.89. We modestly lower our 2027E/2028E EPS from $12.22/$9.87 to 
$12.19/$9.83. Our net asset value (NAV) estimate increases slightly from $109.55 per 
share to $109.80 per share. Our NAV-based PO is unchanged at $233/C$331 per share, 
given rounding and the small adjustment to NAV. AEM is our 2026 top precious metals 
pick for its portfolio of top tier gold mining assets in top jurisdictions, a well-established 
track-record of meeting or exceeding targets, a suite of attractive growth projects, and 
substantial exploration upside potential. Reiterate Buy rating. 
Alamos Gold (AGI): lowering PO to $34.0/C$48.0 per share 
AGI reported Q3’26 adjusted EBITDA that was 5% below BofAe on 3% lower gold sales 
and 4% higher costs. However, Q3 EPS was in-line as lower EBITDA was offset by lower 
depreciation and share comp. AGI maintained its 2026 guidance, with Q4 production 
expected to increase 18-34% QoQ to 140-160koz on higher Island Gold District (IGD) 
underground rates and grades. However, the Phase 3+ shaft was delayed one quarter, 
capex increased 5%, and PDA H2'27 production is expected to be below plan, increasing 
downside risk to 2027/28 guidance. We update our model for Q3’26 results, higher 
Q4’26 production (150koz vs 142koz prior), lower 2027E production (608koz vs 656koz 
prior) and higher costs. As a result, we now forecast EPS of $2.12 for 2026E (+2% vs 
$2.07 prior) and $2.64 for 2027E (-9% vs $2.89 prior). The impact of lower production in 
2027E is partly offset by reduced revenue loss following the elimination of 34koz of 
Argonaut hedges. Our 1.0x (multiple unchanged) NAV based PO decreases to $34.0 
(C$48.0) from $36.0 (C$51.0) per share. Reiterate Neutral rating pending greater clarity 
with February 2027 guidance. 
Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF): increasing PO to $15 per share 
Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) reported Q3'26 adjusted EBITDA that was above BofAe and 
consensus. The beat vs BofAe was mainly driven by lower costs (see our note: Q3’26 
EBITDA beat; 2027 EBITDA potential raised; 19-Oct-26). For Q4'26, CLF guided to an 
adjusted EBITDA of $715m, below prior BofAe of $793m, as benefit of improved pricing 
is partly offset by increased maintenance outages, higher input costs and incentive 
comp. For 2027, CLF sees EBITDA potential of ~$4.5 billion (bn), driven by lower 
discounts ($350m), fixed price contract resets ($850m), and higher spot pricing ($1.1bn) 
partly offset by higher costs ($250m) vs. Q3'26A annualized, assuming spot pricing 
holds. We update our model for lower discounts and higher costs, and now see 2026E/ 
2027E/2028E EBITDA of $1.73bn/$2.58bn/$2.26bn (from $1.76bn/$2.47bn/$2.13bn) 
and EPS of $0.15/$1.38/$1.05 (from $0.18/$1.27/$0.90). We increase our PO from 
$14.0 to $15.0 per share, based on 7.0x 2026E EV/EBITDA, 6.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA and 
1.0x net asset value (valuation multiples unchanged). We reiterate our Neutral rating, 
which reflects our cautious near-term view on US flat-rolled steel pricing, balanced by 
CLF's reasonable valuation, and path to balance sheet improvement.

Kinross Gold (KGC): we remain cautious 
We update our model after KGC reported Q3’26 results, with EBITDA in-line to BofAe 
but 2-4% below consensus. KGC noted that it is on track to meet its revised 2026 
guidance for attributable gold equivalent ounce (GEO) production of 1.84 – 1.86Moz 
(BofAe at 1.842Moz attributable). We increased our Q4’26 production estimate from 
428.9 thousand ounces (koz) to 438.5koz (in line with KGC expectation of 430-450koz). 
Round Mountain operations have stabilized, with grades and recoveries both up 
significantly QoQ. At Bald Mountain, Q3 production was higher than our expectation on 
significantly higher tonnes leached, despite recoveries below our estimate. We increase 
Q4’26 tons leached at Bald Mountain while lowering recovery expectations. Our 2026E 
EPS increases from $2.32 to $2.39. Our NAV and NAV-based PO are unchanged. 
Reiterate Underperform rating given KGC’s less compelling production/cost outlook 
relative to peers, heading into 2027E, with production expected to be relatively flat year-
over-year (yoy), and cash costs higher yoy (vs. peer production up, and unit costs down).  
Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM): updates as results roll-in 
As the operators of several of WPM’s portfolio assets have reported Q3’26 results since 
our last model update, we update our WPM model for these and reduce 2026E EPS to 
$4.36 from $4.52 per share. Our positive view of WPM is based on WPM’s peer-leading 
long-term attributable production growth, long average reserve life, and world-class 
asset portfolio. Reiterate Buy rating.

Price objective basis & risk 
Agnico Eagle Mines (AEM) 
Our $233 per share price objective is based on Agnico Eagle trading at 2.00x times our 
estimated NAV (current net debt position plus a mining asset DCF utilizing a 5% WACC). 
Historically, North American gold producers have traded between 1 and 2 times NAV, 
with 1.75-2.0 times being accorded to the organically growth-oriented gold producers 
such as AEM. 
 
Risks of this price objective not being attained are commodity price weakness, the 
inability to secure financing for expansion or development projects, unforeseen 
operating problems, and political risk in the countries the company operates in. Upside 
risks are commodity price strength, better-than-forecast operating performance and 
favorable changes in the political environment in the regions where it operates. 
Alamos Gold (YAGI / AGI) 
Our price objective for Alamos Gold (AGI/YAGI) is US$34.00/C$48.00, based on the stock 
trading at 1.00x our estimated net asset value (NAV) and a 1.42 CADUSD FX rate. 
Historically, North American precious metal producers have traded between 1x and 3x 
NAV but more recently between 1x and 2x, with unhedged, growth-oriented producers 
occupying the upper end of the range and Junior and Mid-tier producers trading 
historically at a discount to the senior producers. 
 
Upside risks to our PO are a higher-than-forecast gold price, a lower-than-forecast 
CADUSD FX rate, exploration success at Island, Mulatos and Young-Davidson, better-
than-expected synergies at Magino, advantageous changes to the regulatory and/or tax 
regimes in the jurisdictions in which they operate, and the potential for AGI to be 
acquired at a premium valuation. 
 
Downside risks are a lower-than-forecast gold price and higher-than-forecast CADUSD 
FX rate, risks of the Argonaut transaction not receiving regulatory approval, operational 
challenges with the Magino mine, risks associated with undertaken M&A (such as with 
respect to integration of assets), and unforeseen operating and development issues at 
other assets. 
Centerra Gold (YCG / CGAU) 
Our price objective for Centerra Gold is C$22.75 per share and US$16.00 per share, 
based on the stock trading at 0.75x our estimated net asset value (NAV). Historically 
North American precious metal stocks have traded between 1 and 3 times NAV, with a 
median of 1.50 times over the past nine years (and 2.00 times longer-term), with 
unhedged, growth-oriented producers occupying the upper end of the range. 
 
Downside risks to our price objective for Centerra Gold are commodity price weakness, 
delays in the development of its growth projects and the potential for additional 
operating issues at Öksüt. Upside risks to our price objective are gold and molybdenum 
prices rising, unexpected exploration success at any of the assets, and a favorable 
renegotiation of its gold/copper stream on Mt. Milligan. 
Cleveland-Cliffs (CLF) 
Our $15.00 PO is based on 7.0x 2026E EV/EBITDA, 6.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA and 1.0x net 
asset value, with each value equally weighted. The 7.0x 2026E EV/EBITDA target 
multiple is higher than its historical trading average of 7.4x and is more in line with that 
of its key steel peers given an emerging recovery in US auto production and CLF's 
substantial automotive exposure relative to public peers. 
 
Downside risks: 1) new capacities pressuring steel prices & margins, 2) obsolescence of 
its capacity relative to newer supply forcing closures, 3) higher-than-expected capex or 
interrupted operations to address older mills or environmental liabilities, 4) union

Eldorado Gold (EGO / YELD) 
Our price objective of $51.00 (C$72.00) is based on the stock trading at 1.25x times its 
estimated net asset value (NAV). Historically, North American precious metal stocks have 
traded between 1 and 3 times NAV over the last five years, with a median of 1.50 times 
(and 2.00 times longer-term) and with unhedged, growth-oriented producers occupying 
the upper end of the range. We value EGO below the lower end of the valuation range 
due to higher political risk for operating in Greece and Türkiye. 
 
Downside risks to our price objective for Eldorado Gold are commodity price weakness, 
the inability to secure financing for expansion or development projects, unforeseen 
operating problems, political, legal or permitting challenges in the regions in which the 
company operates, rising capital and operating costs and delays in the development of 
its growth projects. 
 
Upside risks are a higher-than-expected gold price, a more hospitable regulatory 
environment in the jurisdictions where the company operates (in particular, Greece), 
more favorable foreign exchange rates than now projected, and better-than-expected 
operational results. 
Kinross Gold (KGC) 
Our price objective for Kinross Gold is $24.75 per share, and is based on the stock 
trading at 1.25 times our net asset value (NAV) estimate, reflecting production growth 
that is forecast to be slightly lower than the senior/mid-tier average. Historically, North 
American precious metal stocks have traded between 1 and 3 times NAV, with 
companies with declining production at the lower end of the range. 
 
Risks to our price objective for Kinross Gold are commodity price weakness, the inability 
to secure financing for expansion or development projects, unforeseen operating 
problems, political or legal challenges in the regions in which the company operates, 
rising capital and operating costs and delays in the development of its growth projects 
or current mine expansions. On the upside, risks are stronger than expected commodity 
prices and an operating turnaround that exceeds our expectations in terms of timing and 
magnitude, and exploration success. 
Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM) 
Our price objective for Wheaton is $169.00 per share, and is based on the stock trading 
at 3.25x times our estimated net asset value (NAV). Our NAV per share is based on a 5 
percent discount rate. Historically, North American precious metal stocks have traded 
between 1 and 3 times NAV, with a median of 2.00 times longer-term (1.50 times in the 
last three years), and with royalty/streaming companies occupying the upper end of the 
range. 
 
Risks to our price objective for Wheaton are continued commodity price weakness, 
unforeseen operating problems at the mines on which its silver streams are based, 
financing challenges, political or legal challenges in the regions in which the mines 
operate, and delays in the development of projects for which the company silver 
streams.
