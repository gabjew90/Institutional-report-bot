Economy | Consumer Jitters Fail To ‘Haunt’ Economic Growth
• The preliminary reading of 3Q26 GDP (+3.4% QoQ 
annualized) increased in line with our expectations. A 
surge in private investments (+19.4%) driven by the AI 
buildout supported the economy. In addition, 
consumer spending (+3.8%) was robust due to growth 
in services categories (transportation, food, etc.).
• Tensions between US/Iran remained elevated as 
the White House’s attention shifted to the midterm 
elections. The Strait of Hormuz remained blockaded 
by the US, and Houthi militant strikes on Saudi 
refineries caused crude oil to climb back above 
$100/bbl. Gas prices remained firmly above $4/gallon.
• Real-time indicators—including department store 
sales, hotel occupancy, and restaurant bookings—
remained strong, highlighting consumer resiliency. 
• The Federal Reserve maintained a hawkish tone at the 
October FOMC meeting, although kept interest rates 
unchanged at 3.75% - 4.00%. Both Lisa Cook and Beth 
Hammack dissented in favor of raising rates by 25bps. 
Chairman Warsh suggested the committee needed to 
“accumulate more information” before determining 
the correct stance of policy and cited uncertainty was 
heightened due to geopolitical issues.
• October ISM Manufacturing PMI (55.5) remained 
resilient as production rose at the fastest pace since 
November 2021, and new orders continued to grow.
• The unemployment rate increased marginally to 
4.2% (from 4.1%) as the US economy added only 29k 
jobs in September. 
• Initial jobless claims (~194k) have remained at or below 
200k for 7 straight weeks—the longest streak since 1969.
• September headline CPI inflation (+3.7% YoY) rose at the 
fastest pace since May. Approximately 50% of the 
increase was attributed to surging fuel prices, which 
increased at the fastest pace since March. Meanwhile, 
Core CPI (+2.6% YoY), rose faster than expected (+2.5%). 
• Consumer Confidence (91.7) rose 7.5 points in 
October—the fastest monthly increase since May 2025. 
The ‘Present Situation’ subindex (120.4) improved due to 
rising optimism about current business conditions. 
However, consumers are planning to spend less on 
services and durable goods over the next six months, 
reflecting sticky inflation eroding purchasing power. 
• The Retail Sales Control Group (+0.1% MoM) eased 
considerably, while the headline reading rose 0.8% MoM. 
The headline increase was driven primarily by higher gas 
prices rather than broader consumer demand.
• Housing data for September was soft. While existing 
home sales (+1.5% MoM) increased for the first time in 
four months, housing starts (-5.2% MoM), building 
permits (-0.4% MoM), and new home sales (-0.6% MoM) 
all declined. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate 
reached its highest level (~7.5%) since April 2024.
• China Manufacturing PMI (49.3) fell into contraction 
territory due to a pullback in overseas orders and softer 
consumer demand following the National Day Holiday. 
• Euro Zone Manufacturing PMI  (52.4) eased as 
employment declined for the first time in three months.

Fixed Income
•
The Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index (+2.4% 
MoM) posted its largest monthly increase since 
December 
2023. 
Despite 
building 
inflationary 
pressures, Treasury yields decreased on fears of 
reduced AI investment. Additionally, the market now 
expects just two rate hikes over the next 12 months, 
down from four hikes a month ago. 
•
Municipals (+3.9% MoM) rallied for the first time in 
four months, ending their longest losing streak since 
May 2024. Attractive valuations and yields following 
the worst month since 2008 produced the best 
October return for munis since at least 1980.
•
Treasuries (+2.4% MoM) posted their strongest 
performance since December 2023 as Treasury yields 
decreased across the curve. After climbing to multi-
decade highs, the 2-, 10, and 30-year Treasury yields 
each tumbled by ~30bps. 
•
US investment-grade bonds (+2.2% MoM) rose for 
the second time in three months. While elevated 
corporate bond issuance caused IG spreads to widen 
~10bps to ~90bps, declining bond yields supported 
the longer-duration posture of the overall index as 
well. All four major IG sectors (tech, industrials, 
utilities, and financials) ended the month positive. 
•
Emerging market bonds (+1.3% MoM) rose for the 
second time in three months. Spreads widened 
~26bps to ~196bps but remained well below their 20-
year average of ~320bps. 
•
International 
sovereign bonds 
(+0.8% 
MoM) 
increased for the first time in six months. Despite 
persistent inflationary pressures, hawkish global 
central bank rhetoric, and a stronger dollar, the 
sharp drop in global bond yields supported the rally.
•
High yield bonds (+0.3% MoM) rose marginally. 
Spreads continued to widen at the lower rungs of 
credit quality (CCC spreads +110bps) pushing high 
yield spreads higher by ~28bps to ~340bps. 
•
The Bloomberg Commodity Index (-0.4% MoM) 
declined for the first time in four months. Weakness 
in precious metals and grains offset strong gains in 
crude oil  due to continued global supply constraints 
stemming from the US/Iran conflict.
•
The US Dollar Index (+0.4% MoM) rose for the 
second consecutive month to its highest level since 
April 2025. Resilient US economic growth and Fed 
hawkishness supported the strength. 
•
The Bloomberg Grains Index (-9.1% MoM) declined 
at the fastest pace since June 2024 as larger than 
expected US crop forecasts and rising inventories 
eased supply concerns. Wheat (-10.7%) fell as weak 
export demand outweighed concerns about tight 
global supplies. Soybeans (-4.5%) also declined for 
the first time in four months. 
•
The Bloomberg Precious Metals Index (-9.0% MoM) 
fell for the 7 th time in the last 8 months as elevated 
bond yields, a stronger US dollar, and a hawkish Fed 
outlook decreased demand for non-yielding metals. 
Gold (-6.9%) finished the month under $4,000/oz. for 
the first time since October 2025 and silver (-18.6%) 
declined for the fourth time in five months.  
•
The Bloomberg Industrial Metals Index (-2.0% 
MoM) fell as global growth concerns and weak 
Chinese demand outweighed longer-term AI-driven 
demand trends. Copper (-2.1%) fell on overall weaker 
demand from China. 
•
The Bloomberg Energy Index (+7.6% MoM) rallied 
for the fourth consecutive month, the longest stretch 
of consecutive gains since March 2025. Crude oil 
(+15.2% MoM) ended the month above $100/bbl for 
the first time since April driven by supply constraints 
and geopolitical risks. Despite a late-month bounce 
driven by increased demand expectations ahead of 
the winter season, natural gas (-4.2%) declined on 
weaker demand trends in October.
•
The MSCI AC World Index (-1.6% USD MoM) fell for 
the second straight month—the first time the index 
has experienced consecutive monthly losses since 
March 2025. Developed markets outperformed on a 
relative basis given their reduced tech exposure. 
•
EM equities (MSCI EM -8.0% USD MoM) posted their 
steepest monthly decline since March, pressured by 
the tech selloff. As a result, EM underperformed the 
developed markets (MSCI EAFE -0.7% USD MoM) by 
the widest margin since October 2022. 
•
EM Asia (MSCI Asia ex Japan -8.2% USD MoM) 
underperformed EM Latin America (MSCI LATAM         
-7.6% USD MoM) for the first time in three months.
•
Japanese equities (MSCI Japan -4.2% USD MoM) 
declined for the first time in four months and lagged 
global equities by the widest margin since March. 
BOJ tightening concerns and global semiconductor 
fears were key drivers of the decline in October. 
•
US Large-Cap equities (S&P 500 -0.9% MoM) 
declined for the fourth time in five months as AI 
demand/financing concerns drove tech weakness. As 
a result, large-cap value outperformed large-cap 
growth by the widest margin since February 2001.
•
Despite the headline decline, nine of 11 S&P 500 
sectors ended the month positive. Energy (+14.0% 
MoM) and C. Staples (+11.6% MoM) were the two best 
performing sectors, while Comm. Services (-15.1%) 
and Tech (-6.8%) lagged the broader index. 
•
European equities (MSCI Europe ex UK –0.2% USD 
MoM) posted back-to-back monthly declines for the 
first time since Dec-2024. The region outperformed 
on a relative basis due to its limited tech exposure 
while rising energy prices weighed on performance.
•
US Small-Cap equities (Russell 2000 +0.6% MoM) 
outperformed large caps for the first time in four 
months as declining bond yields were supportive to 
small caps due to their greater sensitivity to rates.
