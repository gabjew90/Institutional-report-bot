In the US, Treasury’s ongoing reliance on bills, larger buybacks, and higher coupon 
redemptions ought to see net coupon supply available to the public decline from about 
$1.2tn this year to $1.1tn next year. Our baseline is that Treasury will begin to increase 
auction sizes in 2-to-10y maturities starting in August (keeping long-end supply stable), 
with the ramp up taking place over several quarters. Inclusive of bills, net supply 
available to the public should be little changed, reflecting slightly higher issuance needs 
coupled with modestly higher net bill purchases by the Fed to stabilize reserves as a 
share of bank assets. Unless there is preemptive action to raise the debt ceiling, 
constraints on Treasury’s borrowing may mean that bill issuance is backloaded in the 
year as Treasury manages under the headroom afforded by extraordinary measures and 
cash on hand until Congress raises or suspends the borrowing limit (see here for 
discussion on these mechanics from the prior debt limit episode). 
We project Euro Area net supply to the public will fall modestly in 2027, driven by a mix 
of lower issuance needs and decelerating QT. We expect increased German supply as 
fiscal support continues to build. But the end of the NGEU program will essentially halve 
EU net funding requirements, and we forecast Spain will remain on a fiscally conservative 
path despite upcoming elections. ECB QT is poised to become modestly friendlier thanks 
to the maturity profile of ECB holdings. Versus our baseline, risks skew towards a further 
deceleration in QT given the combination of excess liquidity increasingly moving within 
the plausible equilibrium range for bank reserves demands and the emergence of 
sovereign stress in the second half of 2026.

Our Japan economists expect increased new issuance in FY27 based on the MoF’s latest

Relative to GDP, our estimates for net supply available to the public imply issuance on 
the order of 5% in the US, Germany and the UK, with Japan the outlier closer to 10%. 
Meanwhile, free float—the stock of debt outstanding ex-central bank holdings as a 
share of GDP—is likely to grow by around 1-to-2pp in the US and UK, 4pp in Germany 
and 6-to-7pp in Japan (Exhibit 2). 
In the US, the broader issuance picture should see total net issuance net of the change in 
Fed holdings across USTs, agency MBS, and USD IG continue to drift higher on the back 
of increasing IG supply (Exhibit 3). However, net supply as a percent of nominal GDP is 
likely to remain relatively steady (the same is likely to be true when looking at issuance in 
duration-weighted terms). 
 
The likely skew of risks around swap spreads across markets should hinge in part on the 
supply environment, though in some cases, especially Europe, other considerations are 
likely to play a role. On balance, we lean constructive on spreads in the US (mainly from a 
carry standpoint) and the UK, and see risks skewed towards tighter swap spreads in 
Germany and Japan. 
For the US, valuations are fair in front-end spreads, while longer maturities sit on the

sustaining wider levels, as do Treasury’s demand-sensitive approach to issuance and

We think the backdrop in Europe should see renewed Bund cheapening versus swaps on 
two key considerations. First, staggered relief in sovereign credit is likely to reduce the 
safe-haven value of Bunds over time and undo at least a portion of the year-to-date 
outperformance versus swaps. Second, a further increase in Germany free-float is 
directionally consistent with cheapening pressure as well, though we would note that 
prior to sovereign stress materializing, 10y Bund spreads had been comparatively stable

large share of the increase in free-float compared to the US and UK (Exhibit 6), a balance 
sheet policy adjustment would support Bund spreads. 
We think lower duration supply and a friendlier QT profile in the UK should offset the 
small headwind from rising free float, supporting modest further widening in 10y and 
30y Gilt swap spreads. Our fair-value model1 points to incremental valuation support at 
10y, while 30y spreads screen broadly fair but poised to benefit from their greater 
sensitivity to reduced duration supply (Exhibit 7). Although spreads already price some
