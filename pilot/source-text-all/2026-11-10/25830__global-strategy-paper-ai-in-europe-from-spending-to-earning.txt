GLOBAL STRATEGY PAPER - AI in Europe: From Spending to Earning
 
• Europe captured less of the AI upside, but that is becoming an advantage as markets shift from spending to
returns. AI Infrastructure represents c.15% of the STOXX 600 and only 2pp of its expected 9% earnings growth in
2027, versus c.40% of the S&P 500 and 9pp of its expected 13% growth.
• AI Phase 3, Revenue generation: An attractive contrarian opportunity. European Software and IT Services have
de-rated despite resilient earnings and margins. As extreme long Hardware versus short Software positioning
begins to reverse, we highlight companies with evidence of AI monetisation.
• AI Phase 4, Productivity: The largest, but least proven, opportunity. Our screen of long-term AI beneficiaries
combines high labour intensity with substantial exposure to AI automation, implying c.45% potential EPS upside
 
EUROPE WEEKLY KICKSTART - 3Q26 Earnings Tracker: When Earnings and the Economy Diverge
 
• Earnings continue to deliver despite the high hurdle. Roughly 60% of the STOXX 600 companies have reported 3Q
results, representing c.70% of market cap. Consensus now expects EPS growth of 23% y/y, or 13% excluding
Commodities, while median EPS growth is expected at 11% (Exhibit 1).
• Earnings are beating expectations by 3.4% on a market-cap-weighted basis (Exhibit 9) and 2.9% on an equal-
weighted basis (Exhibit 8), slightly above historical norms. Price reactions remain asymmetric, with misses being
penalised more heavily than beats are rewarded. Energy, Telecoms, Healthcare and Technology have led the
beats
 
US WEEKLY KICKSTART - The equity index impact of SpaceX inclusion so far
 
• As its float has increased, so has the weight of SpaceX as a constituent of several major equity indices and as a
holding of passive funds. Passive ETFs owned roughly $30 billion of SpaceX at the end of October, with roughly
$20 billion of that total attributable to funds benchmarked to the Nasdaq-100. We expect upcoming rebalances to
drive modest additional increases in index weights for the stock. 
• SpaceX is not currently an S&P 500 constituent, and current index rules point to potential inclusion in H2 2027
unless a corporate action accelerates the process.
 
EUROPE MULTI-INDUSTRY - Thoughts on European datacenter exposed names post US Midterm elections
 
• Prysmian and Legrand are the most exposed, but have also derated significantly. 
• Prysmian is likely the stock in our coverage with the highest exposure to Texas, given its Encore Wire and
Channell acquisitions as well as expansion plans for 8 new medium voltage cable manufacturing lines to be built
over the coming years in the state. 

• Legrand has the largest datacenter business in our coverage (32% sales) and is significantly more weighted
towards the US than others, however it has also derated significantly, while also displaying one of the most
depressed non-datacenter businesses in our coverage due to heavy exposure to European resi. It is also one of
the most ROIC resilient names in our sector over history. We delve into these points in our recent upgrade to Buy
here.
EUROPEAN UTILITIES - Bold strategic pivots can pay off...
 
• Drawing together our prior work, we identify four corporates where we see scope for structural expansion in
multiples: 
(1) Enel: rotating out of South America and deploying its €20-25 bn B/S headroom towards US renewables (or
a vertically integrated utility) would re-rate the stock, we believe; 
(2) RWE: increasing its stake in Amprion would diversify growth and lower the risk profile of the income
statement. We believe this would structurally re-rate the stock; 
(3) Naturgy enjoys up to €12 bn B/S headroom and has a large emerging market portfolio; a shift towards
developed-market electricity could support higher-quality earnings and multiple expansion; and 
(4) Centrica: acquisitions of regulated revenue streams could continue to have a meaningful impact on the
portfolio mix and valuation.
 
Fresenius Medical Care (FMEG.DE) - Reflecting on FY27 headwinds and management transition; cutting estimates
and remain Neutral post Q3
 
• Cutting estimates; expect EBIT to decline in FY27e. We have updated our estimates to reflect the management's
updated commentary on headwinds into Q4 26 and FY27. For FY27 specifically, we now assume €325m TDAPA
headwinds and have also lowered our assumption for overall reimbursement uplift in US care delivery to 1%
(which is considerably below the level of inflation we assume for the cost base). 
• Primarily driven by those changes, we have cut our FY26/27/28e estimates for adjusted EBIT by -1/-9/-9%.
 
Lancashire Holdings Ltd (LRE.L) - Attractive dividend yield of 12% sustainably supported by underappreciated
earnings resilience; reiterate Buy
 
• As the commercial/reinsurance cycle softens, the key investor pushback on Lancashire has centered on its
exposure to weakening property catastrophe conditions and the historical shrinking of its portfolio in softer markets.
We think this overlooks how the business has evolved since the last soft cycle: Its underwriting portfolio is broader
while its earnings stream is less volatile. We therefore expect less contraction than in the previous soft cycle.
 
Dassault Systemes (DAST.PA) - Core momentum intact and Life Sciences returns to growth; remain Neutral
 
• Looking forward, we expect investor debate to centre on the 4Q26 exit rate and FY27 guidance, ARR dynamics,
the sustainability of the improvement in Life Sciences, AI roadmap, product adoption and monetisation, and the
ability to demonstrate sustained cash conversion, all of which we expect the company to expand on at its Capital
Markets Day on November 17 in Paris.
