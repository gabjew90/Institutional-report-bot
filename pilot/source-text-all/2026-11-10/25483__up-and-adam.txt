Friday, November 6, 2026
The 10-year Treasury yield has fallen 53 bps from its 5.37% intraday peak one month ago, its highest level since 2002. Lower oil prices 
and concerns that a data center slowdown could weigh on economic growth are boosting demand for bonds. However, Citi’s Economic 
Surprise Index is near a three-year high, while Treasury technicals suggest the downward momentum in yields is stretched. A near-term 
bounce would not be surprising, but we expect the 10-year to drift lower toward 4.50%–4.75% over the next 12 months.

Chart of the Day: Yields Reverse Course
With Treasury yields cooling, 30-year mortgage rates have eased to 7.17% after reaching a three-year high of 7.61% in early October. 
Meanwhile, new home sale prices fell 3.9% YoY as builders offered concessions to support demand. While the moderation in mortgage 
rates is encouraging, they remain ~120 bps higher since the start of 2026. Next week’s existing home sales report will provide a broader 
read on market conditions, but the bigger picture is unchanged: housing affordability challenges aren’t going away anytime soon.
Tech was the worst performer, but every sector declined, with the S&P 500 suffering its sharpest one-day drop since the tariff turmoil of 
April 2025. The index is now 6% below its recent high. While volatility can be unsettling, the S&P 500 historically averages 3.3 declines of 
more than 5% annually. So far in 2026, there have been just two, with none exceeding 10%. Given our positive earnings outlook, we 
remain constructive on equities and view weakness as an opportunity for long-term investors.
