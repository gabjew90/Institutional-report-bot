The anti-AI trade
India's relative performance is finally turning after a historic period of underperformance. JPM
highlights that India's rolling 12-month performance relative to emerging markets recently reached its
weakest level in two decades, lagging by roughly 60 percentage points. The recovery has only just
begun, but the relative-performance dislocation remains extreme.

The $115 billion positioning opportunity
Global investors remain heavily underweight India. JPM highlights that EM funds are running significant
underweights relative to benchmark allocations, a dramatic reversal from the large overweights seen
in the mid-2010s. A simple return to benchmark-neutral positioning by foreign long-only funds could
generate approximately $115 billion in inflows. The potential positioning reversal is enormous.

India is getting cheap
India's valuation reset is becoming increasingly interesting. JPM highlights that MSCI India now trades
at 18.5x forward earnings, a 13.4% discount to its 10-year average of 21.4x. Banks trade at a 19%
discount, while real estate is almost 40% below its historical average. The catch is beneath the surface:
small caps still command a 31% premium to their long-term average. The valuation opportunity is far
from uniform.

India's earnings comeback
India's earnings recovery is gaining momentum. JPM highlights that 71% of MSCI India companies have
beaten expectations this reporting season, while just 16% have missed, the strongest beat/miss ratio in
more than two years. With roughly two-thirds of companies having reported, Q2 FY27 earnings growth
is tracking above 20% YoY, double the pace seen just two quarters ago. Earnings revisions are now
turning higher, adding another layer of support to the recovery.
JPM
The capex revival
India's private investment cycle is finally showing signs of a meaningful revival. After a decade of weak
spending, private-sector project announcements have climbed from roughly 10% of GDP in FY23 to
around 16% in FY26, according to JPM. Investment is picking up across power, renewables,
semiconductors, EVs, metals and real estate. The power grid sits at the centre of this expansion, with
peak electricity demand projected to rise from 271GW to 459GW by 2035–2036. This is increasingly
becoming a broader domestic investment story, not just an equity-market rebound.

The oil relief trade
Falling oil prices could become a powerful catalyst for Indian equities. India imports 85–90% of its
crude, making lower prices a direct tailwind for inflation, the rupee and corporate margins. During the
previous oil-relief rally, real estate surged 40%, consumer discretionary gained 22% and industrials
rallied 19%, while IT and energy lagged. The interesting part is that lower oil initially drove the
recovery, but the subsequent rotation away from AI trades helped India outperform the broader
emerging-market complex.

India's AI paradox
India may be emerging as a hedge against the global AI trade, but AI itself poses a significant threat to
parts of the domestic economy. JPM highlights that 80% of the current Indian IT services revenue pool
faces potential contraction from AI, while only 20% has expansionary potential. The risk goes beyond
technology stocks. Slower hiring and job losses could eventually weigh on household incomes and
urban consumption. India may benefit from the global AI rotation, but it is far from immune to AI
disruption.
