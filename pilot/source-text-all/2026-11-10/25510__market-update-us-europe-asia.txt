U.S. Markets Recap
US -4.36% | Nasdaq -5.52% | Dow -3.44%
Synopsis

US equities ended sharply lower on Thursday, near the worst levels of the day. The major indices more than erased Wednesday's big post-
election gains, and the S&P 500, Nasdaq and Russell 2000 all had their worst sessions since April 2025. Breadth was negative, though the
equal-weight S&P held up nearly 150 bp better than the cap-weighted index. The AI trade was the main drag, with double-digit declines
widespread across semiconductors and semicap, memory and neoclouds (the SOX posted its worst day since March 2020), while TSLA-US
and NVDA-US were the notable Mag 7 decliners. The move was a broad risk-off trade tied to capacity concerns coming out of the midterms,
with no cushion from the broadening-out trade.

Treasuries were firmer with the curve flattening and short-end yields down 10-11 bp, while the dollar index was flat and gold finished up
1%. WTI crude rose 2.7% but remains sharply lower this week. Initial jobless claims printed at 189K versus 196K consensus, and preliminary
Q3 productivity of 3.6% SAAR beat the 2.8% consensus. Cleveland's Hammack and Richmond's Barkin both favoured gradual rate hikes.

Laggards included cybersecurity, networking, banks and airlines, while staples, integrated energy, E&Ps, pharma and managed care out-

Macro

AI trade drives broad risk-off as oil bounce adds to unease
Broad risk-off trade with AI/momentum under heightened pressure after a big selloff yesterday and spilling over to other pockets of the
market. Recent weakness a function of capacity concerns coming out of the midterms. However, no cushion from the broadening out trade,
as had been the case as of late (rotation > risk off). While still down big for the week, today's oil bounce another negative for sentiment
given some of the lingering skepticism about a credible near-term diplomatic offramp. Market's inability to take advantage of strong earn-
ings growth (Q3 running at nearly +32%) another high-profile overhang as of late. Partly chalked up to scrutiny surrounding peak growth
(and peak capex) dynamics, with that narrative also supported by potential for a slower AI buildout.

Oil stabilizes as market waits for clarity on the Mideast path
Oil stabilizing today after WTI's 15%+ three-day drop driven by diplomatic hopes and thoughts Trump could seek a post-election settlement.
While the drop has helped diesel crack spreads move near their lowest levels since August, WTI futures still don't see crude dropping back
below $80/barrel until June. Analyses about Trump's prosecution of the Iran war largely speculation at this point. VP Vance yesterday
agreed the war was a major factor in the midterm result; Trump stressed he would do it again to prevent Iran obtaining a nuclear weapon
(Washington Post). Axios yesterday discussed Trump's choice between renewed kinetic action and negotiation. Meanwhile, Hormuz inci-
dents continue (Reuters) and higher flow of energy products through the strait remains dependent on an extensive, expensive military
deployment.

Initial jobless claims at 189K, lowest since 1969
Initial jobless claims came in at 189K vs 196K consensus and prior week's upwardly revised 195K (from 194K), now back at lowest level
since 1969. Continuing claims came in at 1718K vs 1719K consensus and prior week's downwardly revised 1695K (was 1701K). Elsewhere,
October's Challenger Layoffs report showed job cuts fell 71% y/y to their lowest October level since 2023. Technology again led all industries
with 171,501 announced cuts. Market/economic conditions remained the leading driver for a second straight month, accounting for 37%
of October layoffs. AI-related cuts fell to a 2026 low of 1,265, though AI still accounted for ~20% of YTD layoffs. Meanwhile, planned hiring
rose 57% y/y to its highest October level since at least 2009.

October payrolls on Friday: consensus +65K, unemployment seen at 4.2%
October nonfarm payrolls report out Friday (6-Nov, 8:30 ET). Consensus for +65K headline print, up from September's +29K. Private
payrolls consensus of +65K, up from prior +46K. Unemployment rate expected to hold at 4.2%. AHE expected to tick up 0.2 pp to 0.3%
m/m, though annualized AHE seen down 0.1 pp to 2.9% y/y. Average workweek forecast to hold at 34.4. Previews noted potential headwinds
from new UAW strike, revocation of Haitian TPS status, though upside risks from construction and manufacturing (AI data center tailwinds)
and leisure/hospitality (seasonal adjustments). Print unlikely to have much of a Fed impact given focus on inflation, view that labor market
supporting spending but not inflationary.

Fedspeak stays hawkish as Hammack and Barkin back rate hikes
Cleveland's Hammack (voter) reiterated her hawkish stance, saying that she expects monetary policy to need to move to a more restrictive
position to bring inflation back to the 2% target (Reuters). Hammack also said she prefers frequent, smaller rate adjustments to minimize
market disruption while preventing an inflationary mindset. Richmond's Barkin (non-voter) told CNBC he sees interest rates rising further,
though not sure by how much or at what pace. However, he also said policy is close to neutral though still a bit accommodative, also specu-
lated on the possibility of hidden risks in intermediaries helping finance AI buildout.

European Markets Recap
Germany -0.4% | France +0.3% | UK -0.5%
Synopsis

European equities finished mostly lower, erasing earlier gains late in the session as the US selloff deepened. The Stoxx 600 remained close
to August's record closing high. Tech, industrials and telecom were the biggest decliners, while chemicals, retail and defensive sectors such
as insurance and food and beverage were the best gainers. European government bonds were broadly firmer, led by German Bunds. French
OAT yields fell 3.0 bp, modestly underperforming Bunds, with the OAT-Bund spread widening to 154.6 bp from 153.5 bp, while UK Gilts
were broadly flat.

Central banks were in focus. The BoE raised its key rate by 25 bps to 4%, with an 8-1 vote split, while Norges Bank held as expected but said
it was ready to raise rates if needed. The Dutch government formally backed Knot's nomination in the ECB presidency race. On data, Euro-
zone PPI surprised on the upside, German factory orders rebounded after a big drop in the prior month, French industrial production
missed expectations and Italian retail sales beat, while construction PMIs in the Eurozone and the UK remained weak.

HFG-DE rose 13% on a Q3 beat, GN-DK rose 11.6% on a Q3 profit beat and a plan to distribute at least DKK 4B to shareholders by end-
2027, and LXS-DE rose 5.7% on stronger free cash flow. RHM-DE fell 16.1% after cutting FY sales guidance for the second time in three
months, NEM-DE fell 9.7% on softer Manage and Media segment guidance, and KTN-DE fell 7.4% on weaker revenue and undelivered
products linked to the memory chip crisis. MAERSK.B-DK fell 6% on Q3 misses despite raising FY EBITDA guidance to $14-14.5B.
Macro

BoE hikes key rate by 25 bps to 4% in hawkish 8-1 vote
BoE hiked key interest rate by 25 bps to 4% in a hawkish 8-1 vote split, citing degree of inflationary pressure in the pipeline and stronger
than expected activity data. Minutes showed hawkish MPC member Mann backed 50-bps move, while Governor Bailey highlighted without
news that softens inflation risks there is an argument for further increases. Pill also backed further tightening. However, Breeden, Ramsden
and Lombardelli noted downside risks to inflation outlook, suggesting follow up hike in December not certain. Monetary Policy Report
showed central projection with inflation peak at 4.9% in Q2 2027, which is more aggressive than sell-side expectations. BoE expects inflation
back at target by late 2028. Revised up 2026 GDP forecast to 1.6% versus prior 1.1%, 2027 at 1.3% versus 1.1% and 2028 at 1.2% from
1.7%.

Norges Bank holds at 4.50% and reiterates high-for-longer stance
Norges Bank unanimously left policy rate unchanged at 4.50% at today's decision, in line with expectations. No new forecasts or rate path
published. Committee reaffirmed Sep guidance, saying outlook suggests probable need to keep policy rate elevated, and bank remains pre-
pared to raise rates further if warranted (Bloomberg, e24). Governor Bache underscored inflation remains too high. Underlying CPI-ATE
stuck near 3% for extended period, slightly above Sep projection in latest reading, while capacity utilisation assessed as slightly below
normal and labour market remains tight. SEB, Nordea, DNB and others continue to see 4.50% as probable peak in this cycle, with rate cuts
not expected until late 2027 at the earliest.

German factory orders rebound 4.4% in September but miss forecasts
German factory orders rose 4.4% m/m in Sep versus 4.8% gain expected and only partially reversed downwardly revised 10.1% plunge in
Aug (Reuters, Bloomberg). Was fastest growth since Mar, driven primarily by 10.5% jump in automotive orders after two weak months,
alongside 8.3% gain in machinery and equipment. Orders for other transport equipment fell 10.4%. Domestic orders rose 6.6%, outpacing
a 2.8% increase in foreign demand. On less volatile three-month basis, however, orders only 0.2% higher in the Q3 versus Q2. Overall, data
indicates bounce from artificially depressed Aug rather than decisive acceleration in demand, with signal more tentative stabilization head-
ing into Q4, not broad-based industrial recovery.

UK construction PMI falls to 43.6 as housing market weighs
S&P Global UK construction PMI came in at 43.6 in October versus consensus 46.1 and prior 46.1, as weak housing sector weighed on
headline. Was the 22nd month running that sector remained in contraction and steepest pace since June. Residential housing index rec-
orded lowest reading since December 2025 at 35.1. Civil engineering work at 45.9 was the slowest pace of contraction since May 2025, and
commercial activity at 46.3 was slightly easier compared with September's 16-month high. Total new work fell by the sharpest pace since
June. Input cost inflation accelerated sharply from September's seven-month low, with purchasing prices the steepest since June.

EU leaders aim to appoint Lagarde's successor at December summit
Reuters cited a senior Eurozone official, who said Wednesday that EU leaders aim to appoint ECB President Lagarde's successor at mid-
December summit. Eurozone governments have to put forward candidates by Friday and Eurozone finance ministers will plan to confirm
nominations and process on Monday. Dutch government nominated former Dutch central bank chief Knot on Wednesday (DutchNews.nl).
Spain expected to nominate BIS chief and former Spanish central bank governor de Cos, though has already received backing from Spanish
PM Sanchez (eutoday). Germany's Nagel reported to be keen on the role and there could still be surprise nominations. Eurozone official
said if formal steps take longer, new ECB chief could be appointed in January.

Synopsis

Asian equities were volatile for a second day on Thursday and ended mixed. The MSCI Asia Pacific ex Japan index fell around 1.1%, off its
lows, with steep falls in South Korea and Taiwan offset by a sharp gain in Hong Kong. Japan's Nikkei 225 closed lower but the Topix ended
higher. Mainland China stocks rallied, Australia led sharply higher by miners, New Zealand closed at a record high and India outperformed
again, near a six-month high and up 6.0% this week. The yen was notably stronger though off its highs, JGB and other sovereign bond yields
were lower, and gold and other precious metals rose sharply.

Central bank and data news was mixed. The September BOJ meeting minutes showed support for continuing rate hikes, Japan's final ser-
vices PMI was softer than the flash reading, and Japan's 10Y bond auction drew weaker demand than last month's. Malaysia's central bank
held its policy rate as expected, and Indonesia's Q3 GDP expanded by a higher-than-expected 5.7% despite a sharp decline in government
spending.

Tech was the weak spot as AI-buildout worries hit memory and chip names: Kioxia (285A-JP) fell more than 13%, SK Hynix (000660-KR)
9.5% and Samsung Electronics (005930-KR) 7.6%, taking the Taiex to a three-month low. In contrast, Hong Kong's IT and consumer stocks
surged in a rotation trade. Toyota Motor (7203-JP) rose 16.7% after raising its FY profit forecast by 15% on strong hybrid demand, while
SoftBank (9984-JP) fell 6.8% after China reportedly told it the ABB robotics purchase would not be allowed, and NextDC (NXT-AU) rose
sharply on reports that Goodman and Digital Realty were building stakes.
Macro

September BOJ minutes show support for continued rate hikes
Minutes from BOJ's September meeting noted decision to raise interest rates underpinned by assessment that economic activity and prices
were tracking baseline projections, financial conditions remained accommodative with low real interest rates, and underlying inflation had
been fairly close to 2% recently. Members backed continuing rate hikes with one arguing need to accelerate pace if signs of upward deviation
in prices were observed. One said appropriate to bring rate closer to approximate policy goal soon. Underlying inflation approaching 2%
with B2B pricing pressures spilling into prices of consumer goods and wage-price pass-through continuing. Global AI-related demand and
yen depreciation also contributing to rise in price of durable goods. Members noted Japan's economic growth being underpinned by gov-
ernment measures, accommodative financial conditions, AI-related demand, and a waning of the adverse effects of high oil prices.

Bank Negara Malaysia keeps policy rate on hold at 2.75%
Bank Negara Malaysia kept its overnight policy rate on hold at 2.75% as widely if not universally expected with handful of economists
expecting 25 bps tightening. Bank said it expects higher energy costs to push consumer inflation higher and for it to remain elevated with
ongoing pass-through costs to downstream consumer prices. BNM expects FY26 GDP growth above 5.0%, at top end of government's fore-
cast, as technology upcycle, investment and tourism continue to be major growth drivers. Said it expects upward pressure on inflation next
year from geopolitical developments, high commodity prices, adverse weather; added will remain 'vigilant' on prices. Bloomberg cited sev-
eral economists had expected BNM to flag rate hike early next year. Bank continued to omit 'appropriate' description from commentary,
hinting it still wanted room to hike.

China blocks SoftBank's $5.4B purchase of ABB robotics unit
China has blocked SoftBank's (9984-JP) $5.4B acquisition of ABB's robotics division in a surprise move that could weaken the company's
ambitions of developing physical AI. Separately, China-based chipmaker YMTC (0HCR9G-E) has paused its IPO amid recent market weak-
ness, according to a Reuters report.

China solar, renewables and tech platforms strengthen
China solar and renewable stocks surging with Democrats in support of green energy (Bloomberg). Democrats already promising to increase
scrutiny over White House efforts to wind back emission rules and may attempt to revive solar and wind subsidies phased out under 2025
tax law (Reuters). China tech platforms strengthening amid thoughts they could benefit from any slowdown in US data center buildout
(SCMP). Any constraints on computing capacity could also see China retain or widen its advantage on costs.
