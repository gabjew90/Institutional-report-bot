Energy Futures: When Managed Money Brent Oil Selling Tops $6bn

• As the US and Iran signaled more willingness to reach a diplomatic solution, the notional amount of
Managed Money Brent oil selling topped $6bn for only the 7th time in the past 5 years according to
Commitment of Traders. Managed Money sold $6.6bn from October 27th - November 3rd alongside a 5.3% drop
in price. Liquidation ($4.9bn) was the main driver. Recall this time period included President Trump reportedly
choosing negotiations over strikes and Supreme Leader Khamenei granting the Iranian President authorities for
dialogue (link).
• Recent history suggests that flows will remain challenged. Dating back to Liberation Day, there were 3 similar
instances of heavy Managed Money Brent Oil selling. Each time, negative activity persisted for the following 2
weeks, most notably post the initial Iran-US MOU in June of 2026.
• However, a longer lookback shows 1 example of a swift reversal. Over May 28th - June 4th, 2024, Managed
Money sold $7.9bn due to OPEC's scheduled ending of so-called "voluntary" output curbs. After OPEC ministers
pushed back against the market reaction, Managed Money net length was fully restored.
• This time, the midterm elections initially prompted more long terminations and threatened positive
trend. On November 4th, the Democratic sweep induced narratives of upcoming constraints on President Trump's
agenda, particularly funding overseas military operations and other foreign policy commitments. Brent nosedived
8.7% and open interest shed $2.7bn. According to GS Futures Strategists' CTA framework, short-term momentum
turned negative for the first time since August, unlocking some additional sales.
• Though interestingly, price eventually stabilized and the option surface revealed some bullish
posturing. During November 5th and 6th, Brent recouped 3.9%. Moreover, 3 month implied volatility richened and
normalized 25 delta put-call skew cheapened. Calls were repurchased.
