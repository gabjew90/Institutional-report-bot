and ~5 GW is at risk of delay in '28: We size the megawatt exposure in the US

in place a set of requirements that new data center projects must meet which may 
delay, rather than reduce, data center volumes.

What's at risk?  We leave our current total capacity estimates unchanged, and look 
to simply size the exposure.  We believe these exposures represent an upper bound 
of capacity that may eventually be affected (delayed, cancelled, etc.). In that vein, 
we clarify this estimate is an upper bound because two factors - partisan alignment 
and constraints on authority - will limit the implementation of certain campaign 
proposals. Specifically, a Texas Governor can: (1) issue an executive order to 
temporarily pause new projects until the legislature can put real guidelines in place 
(but if a Republican-led legislature acts quickly, this could be a number of months), 
(2) make appointments to the power regulatory body (the PUCT - these are 6-year 
terms that come up in 2027), (3) provide policy direction to various state agencies 
on permitting (ie halting permits that an agency hasn’t issued yet). Governor-elect 
Hinojosa has advocated for a a moratorium until laws are passed (applying to new 
permits, not for those already under construction). What would require legislation 
(which is currently controlled by Republicans in TX): (1) more durable changes, 
including tax changes including ending tax breaks (sales-tax exemption), (2) giving 
localities power to reject projects (Governor-elect Hinojosa has planned to 
empower local officials to negotiate with developers, which requires a change in the 
law), (3) codifying ratepayer protections, and (4) union hiring requirements all will 
require consent from the state legislature. 
What's in the pipeline? As discussed in the Methodology section of our previous 
note, our data center capacity estimates reflect a probability-weighted expected 
value of operational capacity. This methodology results in conservatism vs. total 
announced pipeline capacity due to 1) historical execution rates vs. announced 
pipeline, and 2) explicit probability haircut of 30% in base case estimates. In 
addition, we note that based on the underlying 451 Research data set, some 
announced projects (e.g. certain ERCOT Batch Zero projects) may not be included.  
For these reasons, the exposure in our capacity model will be lower than the 
broader pipeline of announced projects.
