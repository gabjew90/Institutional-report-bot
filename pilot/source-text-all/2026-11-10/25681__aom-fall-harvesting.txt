 
 
 
Alger on the Money 
Fall Harvesting     
Many investors believe selling stocks with embedded losses is a good tax strategy. While the S&P 500 has 
delivered strong returns year-to-date (YTD) as of October 2026, a meaningful number of stocks within the 
index have posted negative YTD returns. With many investors following this tax strategy, could battered-down 
stocks provide an attractive entry point to prudent investors just as others are selling?  
 
 
Historical Outperformance of Tax Loss Candidates 
 
 
 
 
  
 
• Investors often harvest equity losses for tax purposes at the end of the year. Mutual funds typically sell 
before October 31 (the deadline for most to realize capital gains) and retail investors by December 31. As 
the graphic shows, stocks that are tax loss candidates (TLCs), or those down more than 10% in a calendar 
year through October, have historically outperformed the stock market from November through January by 
an average of 1.7% (from January 1986 through January 2026, according to analysis from Bank of 
America).  
• With more than 110 stocks in the S&P 500 Index down over 10% this year, tax-loss selling could be 
significant in the weeks ahead. Some of the worst-performing stocks YTD are rate-sensitive and suffered as 
interest rates rose. In rotating out of these stocks, investors may have sold some companies that, in our 
view, are poised to grow strongly once the rate headwind subsides.  
• The timing is also notable, since November and December have historically been among the strongest 
months for U.S. stocks (see also ‘Tis the Season). While past performance is not indicative of future 
performance, we believe select TLCs may be well positioned as tax-related selling fades and investors 
refocus on company fundamentals. In our view, this opportunity may be most pronounced in rate-sensitive 
industries such as housing, automotive and consumer finance, where multiples have compressed despite 
market share gains.
