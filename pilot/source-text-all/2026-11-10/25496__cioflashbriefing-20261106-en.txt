Midterm noise, year-end opportunity 
Despite midterm election noise and AI-
related market turbulence, we remain 
constructive on equities. 
Consumers take their grievances to the polls 
The outcome of the US mid-term elections can be interpreted in 
multiple ways. However, cost-of-living issues were a significant 
driver behind this massive shift in both Congress and state elec-
tions. The war in Iran and the build-out of AI infrastructure have 
both contributed to higher energy costs. AI has become a political 
target and new governors in Texas, Georgia, and Ohio campaigned 
to pause or condition data centre approvals. Democrats now con-
trol states with more than 40% of the planned US data centre ca-
pacity triggering an AI-led correction in equity markets. 
 
But the drawdown for AI related stocks began already before the 
election. Investors started to challenge the viability of the big capex 
spending plans and the respective funding needs. A big driver was 
a rates shock in September: the first Fed hike since 2023 and the 
10-year Treasury yield above 5% stressed highly leveraged debt-
funded companies and shifted the focus toward hyperscalers with 
strong balance sheets. 
 
The sell-off in technology stocks accelerated this week, with the 
biggest correction in companies involved in the AI theme but less 
pronounced in the broader US market. The European equity market 
has been much less affected. The correction so far can be charac-
terized by a rotation out of AI-related stocks into defensive sectors 
such as healthcare. Overall, it looks like a healthy consolidation 
rather than the end of the AI investment cycle. 
 
Specifically, permissions and grid delays are stretching 2028 pro-
jects rather than stopping them, while 2027 capacity is largely 
locked in. The major hyperscalers have signed the White House's 
Ratepayer Protection Pledge, committing to pay the full cost of 
their power usage. In this respect, the situation resembles US rail-
road construction in the late 1800s, when farmer protests led to 
government regulations without impeding the construction pro-
cess. This suggests that current events will eventually result in a 
long-term, robust AI development.

How should investors react? 
We think the midterm election and the market sell-off have created 
more noise and near-term uncertainty for the AI investment cycle, 
which will not dissipate quickly. Investors should be prepared for 
continued volatility in the days ahead, particularly in the tech sec-
tor. However, once the dust settles, seasonality and post-midterm 
patterns should support risk assets. Sentiment has turned bearish 
very quickly and a rebound into year-end is well possible. 
 
We keep our overweight position in equities and stay constructive 
on the global economy into 2027. However, growth is likely to slow 
modestly, so a more defensive positioning may be appropriate. A 
good portfolio diversification remains key also in the coming year.
