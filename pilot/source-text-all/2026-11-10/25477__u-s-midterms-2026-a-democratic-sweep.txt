POTENTIAL FOR COMPROMISE
Democrats Take Both Chambers
Democrats have swept both the House and Senate, reshaping Washington’s 
balance of power. While early polling had pointed toward a more divided 
government, with Democrats gaining control of at least one chamber, the 
broader sweep now gives the party more policy leverage heading into 2027. 
The central dynamic of a Democratic Congress will likely involve more oversight 
hearings and investigations. Democrats may pursue legislation signaling party 
priorities, though such bills are likely to face frequent presidential vetoes. We 
believe investors could see increased political scrutiny of certain sectors and 
companies, slower confirmation votes, and fewer significant legislative changes. 
Budget standoffs and potential government shutdowns are likely, though there 
may be room for limited bipartisan compromises on spending deals. 
Where the Tailwinds May Blow
A Democratic Congress could restore support to solar and wind power, slowing 
the fossil-fuel momentum of the prior two years. Healthcare segments could 
also benefit, particularly hospitals, life sciences tools, the Affordable Care Act 
(ACA), and Medicaid, as Democrats would likely push to restore funding for the 
National Institutes of Health and ACA-related programs.
Where the Headwinds May Lie
A Democratic Congress is likely to agree to modest defense spending 
increases in exchange for funding Democratic priorities, though we believe 
any supplemental funds tied to the Iran conflict could face tougher scrutiny. 
Cryptocurrency and unconstrained artificial intelligence (AI) and data center 
expansion could face additional policy headwinds, as Democrats plan to expand 
oversight of AI development and deployment through a new House Select 
Committee. Healthcare may offer the most potential for compromise between 
a Democratic Congress and the administration, especially on pharmaceutical 
pricing controls, which would be challenging for the sector.

AI Regulation Moves to the States
We think states may take the lead on AI regulation, as 
there have been no federal guidelines or legislation, and 
a lack of votes for federal pre-emption, similar to the role 
California has historically played in driving national fuel 
efficiency standards. Key states to watch for potential 
impact on the AI buildout include Texas, Georgia, Ohio, and 
Pennsylvania. Governors’ races, utility commissions, and 
state legislatures in those states could shape regulatory 
approvals, grid costs, and tax incentives. 
Tariffs and Trade: A Consistent Path
Trump administration tariffs look likely to continue, with 
Section 301 reinforcing tariff levels, alongside continued 
support for U.S. manufacturing reshoring through tax 
incentives and industrial policy. Democratic legislation 
in the new Congress may signal the party’s stance on 
any future tariff adjustments. Democrats appear 
supportive of protecting key manufacturing sectors while 
reducing tariffs on certain consumer goods to address 
affordability concerns. 
The Fiscal Backdrop
Fiscal pressures are likely to remain a central focus in 
2027. Despite market concerns, there is little chance of 
fiscal consolidation after the midterms, while at least one 
government shutdown, potentially paired with another 
debt-ceiling standoff, is likely. The split dynamic of a 
Democratic Congress and a Republican administration 
means budget pressures and shutdown risk persist even 
under new leadership.
Looking Ahead
In our view, the midterm results can offer signals on where 
policy is headed, with more consequential changes and 
an unusually broad range of outcomes in play for the 2028 
presidential election. For investors, a Democratic sweep 
has notable sectoral implications, as renewable energy 
and healthcare gain ground, while defense, crypto, and 
unconstrained AI expansion face new challenges. Tariffs 
and executive actions are difficult to reverse, and the 
reshoring momentum behind U.S. manufacturing looks 
likely to continue regardless of the outcome.
