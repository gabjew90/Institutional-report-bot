The Bullion Report: Precious Metals Rally,
Conviction Still Lags
Jonathan Garber
06-Nov-2026, 10:14 ET (EST)

• Gold: Cautious; physical support improving, conviction limited
• Silver: Stronger physical demand, closely linked to copper
• Platinum: Lending improving, but overall flows remain light
• DeMark Technicals: Gold is bullish, Silver is neutral, Platinum is neutral
• Electronic Trading: Precious-metals activity surged as gold and silver rebounded, with XAG volumes up

The sharp precious-metals rally following the US midterm outcome reflected a combination of improved risk
sentiment, dollar weakness, lower oil prices, and strength across hard assets. The overall view remains
cautious rather than outright bullish. While the political and risk-on catalysts produced a meaningful move,
the underlying fundamentals do not appear to have changed enough to justify chasing the rally. Improving
physical support and meaningful tactical interest are constructive, but conviction in an outright
breakout remains limited.
Gold positioning has become more bearish, although positioning may not be as short as the prevailing
narrative suggests. Net speculative length fell 6.1%, or 1.54 mn ounces, from 25.10 mn ounces to 23.56
mn ounces driven by approximately 997,100 ounces of long liquidation and 541,400 ounces of new
shorts. Gross shorts increased 11.4%, from 4.76 mn ounces to 5.30 mn ounces. There has since been a
modest improvement, but not enough to establish a clear reversal. The reduction in length and increase in
short exposure leave the market vulnerable to systematic short covering, which may have amplified the
rally.
The broader flow picture continues to reflect a transfer from weaker to stronger hands. Official-sector and
physical demand have appeared opportunistically on dips, providing some underlying support even though the
rally does not yet appear to represent a durable regime change. Longer-term real-money buying has
accumulated below roughly 4,000 to 3,900, while fast-money and broader real-money positioning remain
relatively light. Systematic accounts are still described as short, reinforcing the possibility that short covering
has been an important contributor to the move.
Institutional engagement remains subdued. Investors appear to require a decisive breakout before materially
re-engaging in outright gold positions, with roughly 4,100 representing the type of threshold that could
stimulate greater participation. At the same time, available gold axes have been taken selectively, indicating
underlying interest even in the absence of aggressive directional risk deployment. This suggests that demand
exists, but conviction remains limited and investors are still highly price-sensitive.

Chinese activity has become increasingly tactical, characterized by buying on dips and profit-taking as gold
moved through 4,000, alongside better trading volumes and larger ticket sizes. The activity remains relatively
short-horizon rather than conviction-driven.
At the same time, Asian physical demand has been stronger than expected, with frequent requests for bars
indicating that official-sector-related buying interest may be picking up again. Meaningful quantities of metal
have also been moving into China. While the year-end outlook remains uncertain, Q1 appears considerably
more constructive from a flow perspective, particularly if new import quotas become meaningful in January.
Chinese FX policy remains a potential constraint, with a higher FX reserve requirement potentially creating
friction around dollar-related approvals. The overall Asian picture is therefore one of stronger tactical
engagement, improving physical interest, and significant price sensitivity.
India presents a less supportive picture for gold. Gold demand remains dull and intermittent, while silver
is clearly better bid. Expected gold restocking has not materialized, suggesting weak jewelry sales, and the
approaching festive period is unlikely to generate a dramatic last-minute improvement. Indian demand may still
provide some support beneath the market, but it is not currently strong enough to validate the broader
bullish move. Available industry demand data also appears disappointing relative to stronger historical years.
Gold forwards provide some of the clearest evidence that physical flows are having a genuine market impact.
The pickup in physical demand since October appears to have contributed to gold yields rising toward
almost-positive territory, particularly in funding tenors. An inverse relationship with spot has emerged: yields
tend to rise when spot gold declines and become more negative as spot rallies. During the latest move higher
in gold, front-end lending increased and the effect began extending farther along the curve, with one-month
yields moving approximately 11bp lower post US midterms. One-month gold yields are also being
assessed against a six-month historical lookback, suggesting that the relationship between spot, yields,
and physical demand is being evaluated beyond the immediate move.
Liquidity and participation in the forwards market had previously become thin, with declining volumes and
signs of stress, but conditions have since improved materially. Bank of England-related pricing, which had
previously traded at a premium, moved toward flat and subsequently to a slight discount. Physical demand,
rather than a specific client flow, appears to be the principal driver of these forward-market
developments.

Silver is showing a different set of flow dynamics. Profit-taking has occurred through the unwinding of put
spreads following profitable bearish positioning, while short-dated options activity remains highly tactical,
including bearish structures followed by volatility selling after market rebounds. Silver has also remained
closely linked to copper, making developments in the broader industrial-metals complex important to the
outlook. Physical activity in India is notably stronger for silver than for gold, with banks conducting more silver
business while gold remains relatively sidelined.
Platinum has seen a reasonable amount of lending activity, with yields appearing to find a floor before
tightening in London. Zurich activity has also begun to increase, narrowing the basis between the two markets,
although overall franchise activity remains limited. Palladium direct flow is similarly light, but listed options
activity has been unusual enough to warrant monitoring. March and June options around the 1,000,
1,100, and 1,200 strikes have traded in assorted clips, including approximately 40, 50, and 100 lots.
Although these volumes are modest in absolute terms, they are notable given the typically limited activity in
palladium options. The prints do not constitute a confirmed directional signal, but their accumulation merits
attention.
The US political backdrop may influence risk sentiment and the tariff narrative, but strategic stockpiling of
scarce resources is unlikely to be purely partisan, limiting the likelihood that the midterm result alone
fundamentally reverses the strategic-metals trend. The immediate precious-metals reaction is better
understood through improved risk sentiment, dollar weakness, and the market's historical preference
for political gridlock than through a fundamental reversal in the outlook for strategic metals.
Event risk also remains important. Analysis of gold's potential percentage move around an FOMC meeting
indicates that Fed sensitivity remains an important component of the tactical framework, alongside
positioning, yields, physical flows, and options activity. Taken together, these factors leave gold in a continuing
tug-of-war among oil, yields, equities, physical demand, positioning, and the dollar, without a single
dominant driver.
