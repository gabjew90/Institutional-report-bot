Anthropic’s IPO – the peak of the hype cycle? 
9 November 2026 
Anthropic may become a defining AI winner. However, the IPO question is 
whether that success is already fully priced in. 
ANTHROPIC: A VALUATION THAT LEAVES LITTLE ROOM FOR ERROR 
ANTHROPIC'S PROPOSED IPO VALUATION REFLECTS EXCEPTIONALLY OPTIMISTIC ASSUMPTIONS ABOUT FUTURE 
GROWTH, MARGINS AND COMPETITIVE POSITIONING 
 
Source: Anthropic, Bloomberg, The Investment Institute by UniCredit 
Note: Anthropic IPO high and low cases are based on an estimated valuation range of USD 1.5-2.0tn 
THE CONTEXT 
 
The upcoming IPO of Anthropic could become one of the defining market events of the AI era. As the company behind 
Claude, Anthropic has established itself as a leading player in foundation models, supported by strong enterprise 
demand, strategic investors and a narrative that combines technological innovation with AI safety. The excitement 
is understandable. AI has evolved from a software story into a much broader investment cycle, spanning 
infrastructure, models and applications. Governments, cloud providers and corporations are investing heavily in 
computing power, data centres and energy networks. As we argued in our Long View on AI in September, this 
resembles earlier transformative investment cycles that reshaped entire industries.

Yet history offers an important warning. Breakthrough technologies often create immense economic value, but not 
always attractive investment returns. Railways, telecommunications and the internet all transformed the economy, 
yet investors frequently overpaid at the peak of enthusiasm. Anthropic’s prospective arrival on the public markets 
therefore comes at a pivotal moment: Are investors identifying the next generation of dominant technology 
companies, or simply paying too much for future growth? 
THE DATA 
Anthropic’s growth is extraordinary. Revenue rose almost twelvefold to USD 4.6bn in 2025 and 
reached USD 33bn during the first nine months of 2026. Third-quarter revenue alone approached 
USD 17bn, highlighting the rapid pace of enterprise AI adoption. Importantly, the profitability picture 
has improved markedly in 2026. While reported GAAP results still showed substantial net losses, 
these were largely driven by non-cash accounting adjustments related to Amazon’s convertible 
securities. As Anthropic’s valuation increased, the value of Amazon’s conversion rights rose as well, 
resulting in significant accounting charges that were unrelated to the underlying performance of the 
business. On an adjusted basis, net income improved from a loss of USD 2.9bn in the first nine 
months of 2025 to a profit of USD 5.6bn over the same period in 2026. Revenue growth significantly 
outpaced operating cost growth. Adjusted operating profit reached USD 4.1bn in the first nine 
months of 2026, while third-quarter profitability improved further, with USD 2.5bn of adjusted 
operating profit and a 15% operating margin. Operating cash flow reached USD 6.6bn during the 
first nine months of 2026. Yet stronger profitability does not eliminate the industry’s fundamental 
challenge: AI remains extremely capital intensive. Anthropic spent more than USD 7bn on compute 
and infrastructure in 2025, while long-term infrastructure commitments have risen to almost USD 
900bn. These commitments primarily reflect multi-year obligations for compute capacity, data-
centre infrastructure and related services required to support future AI workloads. 
The message from the chart is straightforward: Anthropic would enter public markets at a 
richer EV/sales valuation than Nvidia, Microsoft or Alphabet, despite a much smaller revenue 
base. Such a valuation implies that investors are not buying today’s business, but a highly 
optimistic view of what the business could become over the next decade. 
While the exact timing and utilisation of this capacity will vary, the figure illustrates the sheer 
scale of capital that leading AI companies are committing in order to remain competitive at 
the technological frontier. OpenAI, Google, Meta and Microsoft continue to invest aggressively, 
while model advantages can narrow faster than infrastructure costs decline. Investors are 
therefore underwriting not only rapid growth, but also Anthropic’s ability to sustain 
technological leadership and earn attractive returns on an unprecedented capital base. The 
potential valuation of USD 1.5-2.0tn leaves little scope for results to fall short of expectations. 
OUR VIEW 
Anthropic may become one of the defining companies of the AI age. The real question is 
whether investors are already paying for that success today. Current market expectations 
assume that exceptional revenue growth will eventually translate into exceptional profitability. 
History suggests this is precisely where investors should be most cautious. Periods of 
aggressive capacity expansion often create enormous economic value, but far less shareholder 
value than initially expected.  
The recent correction in AI-related equities highlights this risk. Even some of the market’s most 
celebrated technology companies have seen 20-40% of their market value erased in a matter of 
weeks as investors reassess growth assumptions and the sustainability of the current spending cycle. 
The underlying AI story has not fundamentally changed. Valuations, however, have. Moreover, the 
timing of Anthropic’s IPO is not ideal considering the results of the midterm elections. The pace of 
the AI infrastructure buildout is now being questioned as the Democrats won key gubernatorial races 
in states with high concentrations of data centres. Investors might approach this IPO cautiously and 
if the IPO disappoints, then there might be a more systemic repricing of the AI story. In this sense, this 
IPO is really a test of how convinced investors are about the pace of this technological transformation.

The bullish case is clear: AI becomes a general-purpose technology, adoption accelerates and 
today’s valuation ultimately proves justified. The bearish case is equally compelling: AI 
succeeds exactly as expected, yet the economics prove less attractive than investors assume. 
Competition compresses model margins, while a disproportionate share of industry profits 
accrues to semiconductor suppliers, cloud platforms, infrastructure providers and enterprise 
customers that capture much of the productivity gains. Our conclusion is therefore that 
investors should remain cautious. AI can win. Anthropic can win. Investors can still lose.
