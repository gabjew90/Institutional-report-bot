Stay the course 
Despite increased volatility, we are 
maintaining our positioning with an 
overweight allocation to equities. 
Macro outlook – Strong US cycle points to tighter monetary policy 
The US economy continues to show solid growth, underpinned by 
AI-related capital expenditure, robust consumer spending and a 
strong labour market. Investment in IT infrastructure has become 
an increasingly important source of final demand, whilst stock 
market gains and the associated wealth effects have helped to 
cushion the impact of higher interest rates. The positive effects of 
the investment boom are increasingly spreading from the technol-
ogy sector to the manufacturing industry. However, persistently 
high core inflation and the renewed rise in energy prices suggest 
that monetary policy is still not restrictive enough. We therefore ex-
pect the Fed to further tighten monetary policy in December with 
an interest rate hike to 4.25 per cent, followed by further rate rises 
in 2027. 
 
The euro area continues to prove more resilient than expected, 
supported by an upturn in the manufacturing sector, stronger ex-
ternal demand and Germany’s fiscal policy. Owing to the energy-
driven rise in inflation, we expect the ECB to raise its deposit rate 
to 2.75 per cent in December and to implement a further interest 
rate rise in early 2027. Meanwhile, France is increasingly becom-
ing a cause for concerns. Political instability and the lack of a cred-
ible fiscal consolidation programme are currently driving France’s 
borrowing costs to new highs. However, stabilising France’s debt-
to-GDP ratio would require a significant improvement in the pri-
mary balance, which is likely to be politically difficult to achieve 
ahead of the 2027 presidential elections and is likely to continue 
to weigh on the euro. The Swiss economy remains robust, and sub-
dued core inflation is allowing the SNB to tighten its monetary pol-
icy only gradually. We expect the SNB to raise its key interest rate 
to 0.5 per cent in the first half of next year. Given its high sensitivity 
to energy prices, the UK economy continues to face a difficult com-
bination of modest growth and persistent inflation. The ongoing 
price pressures suggest some more monetary tightening in the 
coming months. 
 
The Japanese economy continues to benefit from robust exports, 
rising real wages and fiscal measures. The global boom in AI in-
vestment has given the semiconductor industry a particular boost, 
whilst the weak yen is providing exporters with additional momen-
tum. Renewed inflationary pressure points to further monetary 
tightening. China’s economic momentum remains mixed. Strong 
exports contrast with subdued domestic demand and persistent 
weakness in the property sector. Whilst consumer spending and 
private investment remain weak, active fiscal measures are likely 
to support a moderate recovery towards the end of the year. Else-
where in Asia, economies that are closely integrated into global 
technology supply chains are relatively well positioned. 
 
Bonds – High risk premiums are pushing up yield levels 
The re-evaluation of the monetary policy outlook and the sustaina-
bility of public finances has increased volatility in government bond 
markets. Short-term maturities primarily reflect expectations of 
further interest rate rises, whilst longer-term maturities have come 
under pressure due to increased inflation uncertainty, rising gov-
ernment debt and higher term premiums. European bond markets

high-yield bonds and AT1 bonds appear to be reasonably valued,

Equities – Moderate gains as AI concerns weigh on the markets 
In October, global equity markets failed to post any further gains. 
This was due to a number of factors, including higher interest rates 
and geopolitical uncertainties, which offset the positive impact of 
robust corporate earnings. US equities held up comparatively well 
despite weakness in the technology sector. Energy shares bene-
fited from higher oil prices, whilst financials continued to under-
perform the market. Looking ahead, we expect ongoing AI invest-
ment and earnings growth to support equities. However, price 
gains are likely to moderate going forward in the face of waning 
macroeconomic support and higher discount rates. We continue to 
favour developed markets over emerging markets, with the US and 
Switzerland being our regional preferences. At sector level, the 
market rotation from cyclical to more defensive stocks, such as 
consumer staples or healthcare, is likely to continue.

Owing to strong earnings growth whilst stock prices have remained 
stable or fallen slightly, equity valuations have fallen significantly 
in recent months. In both the US and Europe, valuations are now 
below their long-term average and have therefore become more 
attractive once again. Market sentiment has deteriorated signifi-
cantly and investors have reduced their exposure to risk. This com-
bination of factors provides a favourable backdrop for a potential 
year-end rally. 
 
Asset Allocation – Slight adjustment to bonds 
High growth rates and above-average corporate earnings continue 
to provide a favourable environment for risk assets. Accordingly, 
we are maintaining our overweight position in equities. We con-
tinue to favour equities from developed markets over those from 
emerging markets. Our allocation to bonds remains slightly under-
weight. However, we have taken advantage of the recent recovery 
in interest rates to slightly reduce our position in emerging-market 
government bonds and increase our position in high-yield bonds 
accordingly. Our neutral allocation to gold and commodities re-
mains unchanged. 
 
We expect the financial markets to end the year on a positive note 
and are sticking to our strategy despite increased volatility. How-
ever, given high oil prices, tighter financing conditions and fiscal 
uncertainty, a more defensive positioning may be appropriate for 
2027.
