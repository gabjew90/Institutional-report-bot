Clean Tech: Brian Lee 
Investor focus on the midterms was fairly muted into the election, though feedback 
suggests the outcome could have a constructive read-through for solar. In particular, a 
Democratic House could reduce the perceived risk of further legislative tightening 
following OBBBA, particularly across higher policy beta names including ENPH, RUN, 
ARRY, SHLS, FSLR and NXT. Historical precedent exists in the 2018 midterms, when the 
TAN solar ETF rose ~40% over the subsequent 252 trading days after Democrats won 
the House, despite Section 201 tariffs remaining in place. That said, we believe the more 
underappreciated read-through today is that stocks are largely pricing less risk of 
further tightening, while investors appear to be assigning less value to potential upside 
from revisions to the existing framework. Democratic willingness to revisit clean energy 
policy is documented, including the 122 House Democrats who backed legislation in 
March 2026 to restore credits curtailed under OBBBA. The stock implications of any 
renewed focus on existing credit differ by provision, with 45X being a particularly 
meaningful earnings driver for FSLR given its US manufacturing footprint, whereas 
ENPH/NXT/ARRY also benefit but retain greater geographical flexibility through contract 
manufacturing. 
We expect FSLR and NXT/ARRY/SHLS to garner the most incremental investor interest 
given their more direct exposure to domestic manufacturing economics or utility scale 
deployment, while ENPH/RUN could face more pushback as a positive election 
read-through counterbalances a still challenging resi backdrop, with elevated rates and 
softer demand. With the policy costs increasingly known, we expect the investor debate 
to shift toward whether the market, already pricing limited risk of further cuts, begins to 
assign value to the potential restoration of curtailed credits, supporting a broader 
re-rating as power demand accelerates.

Metals & Mining: Nick Cash 
For our coverage, a key investor focus following the election is the long-term outlook for 
existing steel tariff policy and U.S. trade relationships with Canada and Mexico. Current 
Section 232 tariffs, enacted under authority provided by the Trade Expansion Act of 
1962, have supported tighter domestic steel supply by limiting the competitiveness of 
certain foreign imports, alongside additional trade protections under Section 301. 
Democratic lawmakers have made active efforts to increase Congressional oversight of 
the administration’s use of trade authorities including calls for greater transparency and 
accountability around Section 232 tariffs. These efforts influence the medium-term to 
long-term policy backdrop, and election results themselves do not alter tariffs currently 
in place absent formal policy action. As a result, we believe the near-term investment 
debate for steel remains centered on market fundamentals, particularly domestic 
supply/demand, imports, and pricing, while investors continue to emphasize capital 
discipline and shareholder returns.

FCX. Investor inbounds focused on FCX’s Bagdad FID this week, with the company 
announcing pre-market on 11/4 that its Board approved the previously outlined $4.5bn 
expansion of its Arizona copper and molybdenum operation. The project is expected by 
the company to roughly double mining and milling rates, increasing long-term annual 
production to ~400mn lbs of copper and >20mn lbs of molybdenum, while lowering unit 
net cash costs by ~20% and positioning Bagdad as the second-largest U.S. copper mine 
and largest U.S. molybdenum producer per FCX. Investor feedback was largely muted 
but focus centered on the high-end of the moly range as well as the potential cost 
reduction within the asset. We view the decision as another positive marker for FCX’s 
execution and capital discipline, while adding meaningful lower-cost production to an 
increasingly strategic domestic copper portfolio. Gold also rallied this week post the mid 
term election which should be another net positive for FCX; however, investor sentiment 
remains cautious against the backdrop of a potential slowing data center build-out in 
the US, lower global growth, higher cost inflation as well as a potential copper tariff in 
2027. Consequently, the copper market broadly experienced limited upward 
momentum.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
