Copper Market November Outlook – Overview
In this latest Copper Outlook deck, we present our latest views and analysis on price outlook (See slide 4 for all price forecast 
revisions), consumption, scrap supply, and inventories. We update our copper S&D model and present our September GCET 
copper consumption tracker update.
We see further modest downside for copper near-term with prices likely to grind lower to the $13,300/t (0-3 mth pt price 
forecast, revised from $14,500/t, and the current 50-day moving average) in the coming weeks on continued broad 
derisking on AI scepticism, limited current cyclical demand optimism and a softer fundamental outlook for copper into 2027. 
We think further selling can be partly offset by consumer dip-buying amid scarce ex-US inventory and ongoing China scrap 
issues. Absent a significant tariff announcement, we now see a copper surplus on paper for 2027 and 2028 at current prices, 
with recovering mine supply and slowing AI and energy transition demand growth next year, even with an assumption of a 
modest cyclical demand pickup. 
However, we highlight that copper remains exposed to significant near-term two-way policy risk from both US copper tariff 
uncertainty and US-Iran conflict. If our base case of further net selling in the coming weeks plays out it could also leave fund 
positioning ultimately much cleaner and prices vulnerable to rapid gains if these policy outcomes subsequently fall bullish (or 
other positive plausible catalysts like fresh China stimulus or major new supply curtailments). We note that net fund positioning 
in copper had already unwound somewhat in recent weeks (from ~8/10 to ~4/10 - where 10 is the record 2024 peak) on long 
liquidation against a comparatively modest pullback in pricing (see slide 7) with limited fresh short additions.

■Further RMB appreciation likely in 2027

domestic demand, especially if exports wane.
BEARISH
■Copper fund positioning remains sensitive to any further 
pullback in equity markets (e.g. on further AI sentiment 
deterioration) 
■Modelling a copper surplus in 2027 and 2028 at current 
prices
■High prevailing interest rates are a headwind for cyclical 
copper demand and carrying inventory heading into 
2027; A conclusive US-Iran deal could ease this somewhat 
but far from certain.
■A pullback in US copper tariff expectations likely to

■Cyclical demand remains sluggish, amid pressure from

GCET update: A weak China weighing on global copper end-use
Source: Citi Research, ICA, Wood Mackenzie, Bloomberg
Implied global copper end-use, as measured by our proprietary Global Copper End-Use Tracker (GCET), grew ~2.4% y/y in Sep'26. 
Growth underpinned by AIDC, power generation and grid spending. Regionally, ex-China implied demand growth is outpacing China. 
A weak domestic consumption environment in China is weighing on global copper end-use. Implied copper end-use in China grew 2% 
y/y despite a sluggish cyclical growth. Improved recent readings are largely underwritten by strong solar installations (+40% y/y from a low 
base), wind (+128% y/y, again from low base) and non-renewables (+50% y/y, seasonal as 2H led heavy loadings). Excluding the impact of 
solar/wind, implied China end-use growth in Sep’26 is broadly flat. The rest of the energy transition sectors such as EVs are supported by 
robust exports. Front loading of EVs/batteries are likely to support copper end-use in the near term however, policy changes from early 
next year (nil/lower VAT rebates, trade agreements) are likely to cloud the outlook for certain energy-transition sectors. Activity ex-China is 
growing as evidenced by improvement in soft indicators (PMIs) but is vulnerable to broader macro-economic uncertainty, geopolitical 
risks, and monetary policies. Although results from the recently held US mid-terms removes the uncertainty overhang to some extent, 
meaningful improvement likely requires de-escalation in the Middle East driving energy prices further lower, a significant China policy pivot 
and/or visible broad productivity gains from AI adoption.
Energy transition and datacentres driving copper end-use growth 
although weakness in cyclical (particularly China) weighing on 
overall end-use
Implied global copper end-use is currently annualising at ~29Mt

Global PMIs (ex-PRC) in expansion but limited support to traditional sectors
Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg, NBS, RatingDog, S&P, ISM
Global Oct PMI prints suggest manufacturing activity continued to expand across major developed economies including US and 
Europe (albeit at a slower pace). However, China’s Oct PMI prints returned to contractionary with both production and new orders 
declining. US ISM manufacturing remains strong with sub-indexes like new orders and production remaining at higher levels. 
Persistently strong AI-driven capex spending has supported the recovery in manufacturing activity. However, the recent deterioration in 
AI sentiment could start to reflect in future readings. A pickup in soft indicators over the last few months has translated into positive 
monthly end-use readings across ex-China markets especially in sectors such as equipment manufacturing, power generation, grid, and 
data centres. We think the improvement in soft prints have largely translated into improved copper use to sectors that are 
directly/indirectly linked to AI/tech capex spending rather than the traditional cyclical sectors such as consumer appliances, buildings 
and construction etc.  China’s weak Oct PMI prints suggests further policy support might be ultimately unavoidable. China’s domestic 
weakness led by prolonged property/construction rout continues to weigh on global end-use.

GCET update: China implied Cu end-use +2% y/y largely on power gen
Source: Citi Research, ICA, Wood Mackenzie, NEA, IWCC
China’s implied copper consumption (as evidenced by our tracker) up 2% y/y in Sep’26 underpinned by power generation installations 
which in turn is largely on low base effect and seasonality rather than structural. Excluding the impact of solar/wind, implied copper 
consumption was broadly flat owing to persistent cyclical weakness. Property, infrastructure and consumer appliances have been 
weighing on China copper end-use. Weak consumer confidence with consumers focused on repairing household balance sheets has led to 
weak domestic sales of discretionary products such as EVs. Record EV exports have helped offset weak domestic demand. However, the 
sustainability of export-led growth is increasingly uncertain, with planned reductions in VAT export rebates and the proposed EU-China 
agreement on PHEV/hybrid exports likely to create headwinds in 2027. While export front-loading may provide near-term support, the 
pace of EV export growth is likely to moderate next year. We believe without a meaningful policy support, implied consumption of copper in 
China is likely to remain subdued.

GCET update: Implied Ex-China Cu end-use grew ~3% y/y in Sep’26
Source: Citi Research, ICA, Wood Mackenzie, Bloomberg, IWCC, Ember, *full data suite not available only estimates
Ex-China’s implied copper consumption (as evidenced by our tracker) grew ~3% y/y in Sep’26. Machinery, Power Grid/Generation and 
AI related data centre capex spending has driven the growth. Rising AI-related capex is helping drive demand for power infrastructure 
equipment, including switch gears and transformers, helping support copper consumption (ex-China end-use consumption in these 
segments is also driving China direct copper demand through the export channel). In addition, continued growth in solar, wind and BESS 
installations outside of China is contributing to incremental demand. However, the near-term deterioration in AI sentiment poses 
downside risk to implied ex-China copper end-use. Manufacturing sentiment has been on an uptrend, with particularly strong PMI prints 
from the US and Europe which we believe is being reflected in ex-China consumption series.  However, its full impact on traditional cyclical 
series is yet to show up which suggests that the sectors exposed to AI have benefited from the uptick in soft prints. Additional defence 
spending and strategic mineral stockpiling offer other potential avenues for future copper demand and price upside.

The global copper market has relied on rising scrap supply in 2026 to meet structurally-driven demand growth given flat mine output. 
However, scrap supply price elasticity has weakened significantly versus the historical relationship.  China scrap import data shows ~9% 
y/y growth for Jan-Aug (partly driven by weakness in domestic generation) while ICSG data suggests just ~5.4% y/y growth in global 
secondary refined output in Jan-Aug’26 (+4.7% in China, +6.6% y/y elsewhere). This compares with ~12% y/y growth that might have been 
anticipated globally based on historical scrap elasticity given copper prices are ~40% higher year-on-year. 
Headwinds for global scrap growth include current elevated energy and fuel costs, softer goods trade-in-support in China relative to 
last year and domestic policy headwinds including a crackdown on provincial subsidies for secondary producers and scrap invoicing. The 
global scrap supply chain is also likely relatively depleted given broader shortages of raw copper inputs for smelters in recent years and 
following  strong scrap growth in 2025.

We don’t expect tariff clarity before 2027, although it remains plausible. Our base case is policymakers react eventually to keep metal 
in the country, but we need to see further arb weakness, some re-export, and US inventory draws before this occurs. To the extent that 
the US administration values retaining or further building domestic copper inventory then the status quo and fear of a tariff remains more 
effective than implementation. Therefore, paradoxically, the greater the pricing for a tariff by the market, the less likely we think one 
becomes in the near-term (and vice-versa). The arb can also come under more pressure near-term as COMEX is more exposed to fund-led 
flat price de-risking on AI equity weakness and again if there is no tariff news by 1-Jan-27 (our base case).
We don’t think the outcome of the mid-term elections makes tariffs any more or less likely but near-term broader risk-off COMEX-
skewed fund copper selling and related arb pressure could force an earlier end to the policy status quo of tariff ambiguity. While S232 
tariffs remain one of the remaining policy levers that President Trump still has, the optics of fresh tariffs about a vote rejection of high cost 
of living are not favourable for cathode tariffs which would also undermine the tariff support for semis producers who lobbied hardest for 
tariffs last year. Near-term tariff announcements could also complicate ongoing Canada and Mexico trade negotiations.

Source: Citi Research, Bloomberg, Wood Mackenzie, ICSG, BGRIMM, Company Reports

uncertain variable, with Chile forecasts risks skewed to the downside after several years of declining output.
