October in review  
Global physically-backed gold ETFs1 attracted US$2.7bn in October, having 
expanded for four consecutive months (Chart 1). Notably, European funds led 
the charge with sizable inflows. Asian investors remained net buyers while North 
America saw marginal outflows. Global gold ETFs’ collective holdings climbed 19t 
to 4,275t, another month-end record. Meanwhile, assets under management 
(AUM) fell 6% to US$542bn as the lower gold price overshadowed inflows. 
During the first ten months of the year, global gold ETFs added US$41bn, the 
third strongest on record, and their holdings rose by 246t. European and Asian 
investors led global flows, each accounting for 44% and 45% of total net inflows, 
respectively. At the country level, the UK recorded the largest inflows, with China 
a close second.

Regional overview 
North American funds saw modest outflows of 
US$512mn, the only region with outflows in October. US-
domiciled gold ETFs were the sole driver of the region’s 
October loss; Canadian funds continued to attract inflows.  
US technology equities came under selling pressure during 
October amid growing concerns around AI-related capital 
spend. The resulting market stress may have contributed to 
the region’s gold ETF outflows as some investors turned to 
their gold holdings as a ready source of liquidity. This 
illustrates one of gold’s key attributes: deep liquidity can help 
investors meet cash needs during market turbulence.  
Equity sell-offs in the month also led to strong safe-haven 
buying of US Treasuries. October saw their strongest 
monthly return since December 2023 and pulled yields down 
from the highest in nearly 25 years.2 Relatively attractive 
price levels and investor repricing of a less aggressive US 
Fed may have increased their appeal. This focus on US 
Treasuries could have diverted investors away from gold.  
Yet it is worth noting that US gold ETFs saw net inflows, albeit 
mild, during the last week of October when local equities 
plunged again. This reflects investors’ strategic allocation, 
possibly driven by a combination of diversification needs 
against political uncertainty – with midterm elections around 
the corner – and persisting fiscal concerns. 
Europe has now seen inflows for four months in a row, 
adding US$1.8bn in October. The UK continued to lead, 
with funds listed in Germany, Switzerland and France also 
registering positive demand.  
It is worth highlighting that the region has experienced net 
inflows for 18 consecutive weeks. This is a trend that has 
persisted despite the gold price weakness, surging yields 
across the region, and changes in market expectations of 
future moves by local central banks.

This suggests that demand for gold ETFs may be increasingly 
strategic, reflecting persistent political and fiscal uncertainty 
across the region. In France, social unrest, political 
fragmentation and a difficult fiscal consolidation have 
weighed on investor sentiment, unsettling domestic equities 
and adding to sovereign risk premia.3 In the UK, elevated 
yields, energy shocks and shrinking fiscal headroom also 
sparked concerns.4 Meanwhile, Germany is experiencing 
weakening economic momentum and growing political 
pressure on its governing coalition. Against this backdrop, 
investors have increasingly turned to gold ETFs for 
diversification, particularly as traditional safe-haven bond 
markets face their own challenges.  
Asian investors bought US$1.3bn in October, also a fourth 
consecutive month of inflows. China continued to lead the 
region’s inflows. Local investor allocation to gold ETFs 
remains strategically linked to the equity market rout and 
falling government bond yields, which reduce the 
opportunity cost of holding gold despite the gold price 
weakness. Japanese gold ETFs continued to attract inflows in 
October, driven by similar factors: the Nikkei pulled back and 
Japanese government bond yields fell amid safe-haven flows 
and less aggressive expectations of the BoJ’s tightening 
ahead for most of the month – the central bank left rates 
unchanged in late October.5 India also saw sustained gold 
ETF buying in the month despite local mutual funds’ 
temporary suspension of fresh subscription to gold fund of 
funds in mid-October.  
Gold ETFs listed in other regions saw mild inflows of 
US$62mn, supported throughout the month by demand 
from both Australia and South Africa.

Volumes eased  
Global gold market volumes6 declined 13% in October, 
averaging US$365bn per day. All sectors saw volumes pull 
back. Over-the-counter (OTC) volumes decreased by a 
marginal 2%, to US$219bn/day – LBMA activities remained 
healthy at US$196bn/day, with only a 2% m/m decline and 
well above the 2025 average of US$161bn/day. Exchange-
traded activity fell the most, shedding 25% in the month to 
US$140bn/day, led by a 22% decline at the COMEX and a 
31% retreat in Shanghai. Liquidity of global gold ETFs 
averaged US$6bn/day in the month, a 20% m/m fall, 
primarily driven by North America which accounted for the 
lion’s share of total.  
Total COMEX net longs fell further, shedding 94t in 
October to 560t, the lowest since end-July. 7 Net longs held 
by managed money reduced 53t in the month. Meanwhile, 
other reportables8 continued to retreat, losing 54t, and 
spreads9 pulled back by 13t. Interestingly, net open 
interest10 of COMEX gold options rose 6% in the month, and 
kept climbing during the last few days of October despite the 
equity sell-off, possibly due to rollovers amid contract expiry 
and potential dip buying.
