Asset Management Market Flash: 
Swinging from euphoria to vertigo, we 
need to take a nuanced approach 
4 November 2026 
• 
The US mid-term elections have given the Democrats a majority in Congress and in several 
key states, which could limit Donald Trump’s agenda whilst preserving his powers in trade and 
foreign policy. 
• 
The markets have reacted with a fall in government bond yields, a drop in oil prices and a rise 
in the price of gold back above $4,050 an ounce, against a backdrop of expectations of de-
escalation in the Middle East and political deadlock in the United States. 
• 
Post-election optimism, however, quickly faded: technology stocks and semiconductors 
triggered a sharp correction in US markets, despite generally solid economic indicators. 
The Democrats swept to victory in the US midterms, winning 237 seats out of 435 in the House of 
Representatives and 54 seats out of 100 in the Senate. They also took control of a number of state 
legislatures like Texas, Georgia and Ohio. The results could put the brakes on some of Donald Trump’s 
initiatives even if his presidential veto will limit changes and necessitate compromise. The president also 
retained control of trade and foreign policies. 
 
For markets, the big issue focused on the Texas governor election because of the number of data 
centres in the state. Republican Greg Abbott, who favoured data centre expansion, lost to Democrat 
Gina Hinojosa who wants to see increased tech giant regulation. Stock market volatility led to falls in 
government bond yields on expectations of a political stalemate and reduced inflationary pressure. The 
dollar stabilised and the gold ounce moved back above $4,050/4,100. Oil plunged to close to $95 on 
hopes for a Middle East diplomatic solution after a draft agreement at the Doha talks fuelled de-
escalation hopes. The midterm results and talk of the secretary of defence resigning also reinforced the 
chances of Washington changing its Middle East approach. 
 
However, post-election optimism quickly evaporated, especially after Bernie Sanders called for a 
moratorium on data centres. On Thursday, US equities had their worst day since April 2025 as tech and 
semiconductor stocks sold off. The Nasdaq entered correction territory after losing more than 10 % from 
its recent highs. Other indices were dragged down in its wake. 
 
Amid all the political noise, economic indicators took a back seat. US manufacturing ISM rose to a 
better-than-expected 55.5 in October while the prices paid index fell to 75.2. JOLTS job openings rose 
to 7.523 million and layoffs fell. In Europe, production prices rose by 11.1 % over a year in September 
due to energy costs. Elsewhere, the Bank of England raised interest rates by 25bp following a rebound 
in inflation and an improvement in the jobs market. The decision was approved by 8 votes against 1 and 
the door was left open for further hikes. 
 
Global economic growth is still robust and company results continue to trend higher so we remain keen 
on equities. Nevertheless, this tech stock correction underlines the need to be more selective and stay 
fully aware of the risks from the AI infrastructure rollout. We are therefore still upbeat on equities but

European equities 
 
Europe’s equity markets swung between optimism when oil prices retreated and a marked return to risk 
aversion driven by an AI stock sell-off. Hopes for de-escalation in the Middle East sent crude oil prices 
tumbling, boosting cyclicals and helping bond yields fall back. But a rebound in oil prices towards the 
end of the week and accelerating production prices in the eurozone served as a reminder that the 
energy shock was still fuelling inflation. The Democrat victory in the US midterms ended up rekindling 
worries that the AI sector might be subject to a stricter regulatory framework. This added to existing 
doubts over the sustainability of AI investments and weighed on AI stocks, and semiconductor plays in 
particular. Investors reacted by moving into government bonds and defensive sectors. European 
equities actually showed some resilience as they are less exposed to technology -and commodities also 
offered support- but they are still sensitive to oil prices and the ECB’s monetary policy decisions. 
 
In company news, consumption still provided a mixed picture. Jewellery continued to underpin the 
luxury sector. Richemont continued to benefit but soft Chinese demand hit more accessible segments.  
Sales at Diageo were practically flat as trading remained constrained by the Middle East conflict. The 
autos sector stayed under pressure: BMW cut its outlook due to persistent difficulties for premium 
manufacturers in China. Industrials were also mixed. Activity in the defence sector stalled. Rheinmetall 
cut guidance due to delays in certain contracts being executed. Infrastructure, in contrast, benefited 
from more favourable catalysts: Legrand is benefiting from data centre demand and Heidelberg 
Materials is continuing with efforts to boost operating efficiency. Tech had a much choppier week as AI 
worries hit semiconductor companies like ASML and STMicroelectronics. In energy and utilities, 
TotalEnergies reinforced its presence in Asia with a long-term contract to provide LNG in China and 
Engie benefited from the contribution of its UK electricity grid. In healthcare, Novo Nordisk raised full-
year guidance thanks to momentum in its GLP-1 treatments. The group also appointed a new finance 
director. Financials continued to trade on governance and consolidation themes. Plans to merge 
UniCredit and Commerzbank advanced and Stéphane Boujnah decided to step down from Euronext in 
May 2027. 
US equities 
 
The S&P 500 tumbled 3 %, the Nasdaq 100 4.4 % and the Russell 2000 2.9 %. Democrats scored 
resounding victories in the midterms and took control of the House of Representatives and the Senate. 
Oil prices tanked on hopes the election results would lead to Middle East talks resuming. WTI plunged 
13.1% to $90 and Brent 13.4% to $98.  
  
Tech plummeted 8.1 %. The SOX semiconductor index tumbled 15.7 %. Companies providing 
infrastructure for data centres sold off with Vertiv down 24 % and Vistra off 18.4%. Nvidia shed 11.3 % 
and Broadcom 10.6 % AMD cratered 19.9 % in spite of a 45 % jump in sales to $13.4bn, or more than 
expected, and guidance which was also higher than consensus expectations. EPS at Qualcomm (-4.9 
%) was in line but guidance fell short of expectations due to rising wafer and memory costs. Sales at 
Palantir (-15.8 %) beat expectations by almost doubling to $2.3bn and commercial revenue surpassed 
US government revenue for the first time.

Healthcare bucked the trend by proving resilient. Medicaid-exposed companies like Centene (+8.4 %) 
and HCA (+7.3 %) rose on indications that planned cuts would be put back and Affordable Care Act 
(ACA) subsidies extended. Adjusted EPS at CVS (-9.9 %) beat expectations by 27 % and the group raised 
annual guidance. Management also announced plans to spin off its insurance and pharma divisions by 
2028. Pfizer edged 0.7 % lower despite beating EPS expectations by 19 % and raising full-year sales 
guidance from $62bn to $64bn.  
  
Consumption (+2.6 %) outperformed. Uber (+5.4 %) saw a 20 % rise in gross bookings thanks to delivery 
services. Sales were better than expected. Adjusted EPS at McDonald’s (+7.7 %) beat expectations 
although like-for like sales dipped 0.2 %in the US; the drop was offset by higher average tickets. Sales 
at Airbnb (+3.5 %) rose 19 % to $4.9bn and the group raised annual guidance. 
Emerging markets 
 
The MSCI EM was down 1.4 % in USD as of Thursday’s close, tracking weakness in US equities as the 
tech-led sell-off accelerated and market volatility approached its March highs. Korea and Taiwan were 
the worst performers, falling 9.1 % and 8.7 %, respectively, while Brazil extended its correction, down 
another 2.6 %. By contrast, China, India and Mexico showed resilience amid the global AI-driven sell-off, 
gaining 7.1 % 6.8 % and 1.73 %, respectively. 
 
China: October’s RatingDog PMI1 edged down to 52.0 from 52.4 in September but remained in 
expansionary territory. Factory output grew at its fastest pace in six months, while export order growth 
moderated, suggesting continued resilience in manufacturing activity despite external uncertainties. 
On the policy front, the PBoC cut the relending rate for share buyback financing from 1.25 % to 1.00 %, 
reinforcing its commitment to stabilising the equity market. The bank also raised the foreign exchange 
reserve requirement ratio to curb the renminbi’s recent rapid appreciation. Memory chipmaker YMTC 
reportedly put its planned IPO on hold. Oracle was reported to have cancelled a multibillion-dollar AI 
chip leasing agreement with Tencent amid US regulatory pressure, while China blocked SoftBank’s 
proposed $5.4bn acquisition of ABB’s robotics business, highlighting growing scrutiny of cross-border 
transactions involving strategic technologies. Macau’s October GGR declined 10% YoY, or broadly in line 
with expectations. 
 
South Korea’s government waived margin loan collateral requirements for 3 months to curb forced 
liquidations. 
 
Taiwan: regulatory developments came into focus as the Financial Supervisory Commission (FSC) 
signalled potential market-stabilisation measures, including tighter short-selling and collateral 
requirements, in response to heightened market volatility. Elsewhere, Micron reportedly paused 
construction at its Taiwan facility. 
 
India: the HSBC services PMI rose to a four-month high of 55.8 in October from 55.2 in September, 
supported by stronger domestic new orders, particularly in financial and insurance services. Reliance 
Industries reported record 2Q FY27 earnings, driven by exceptionally strong profitability in its Oil-to-
Chemicals segment, while its telecom business maintained steady growth. Apollo Hospital, Mahindra & 
Mahindra, Titan and Bharti Airtel all beat earnings expectations, showing EBITDA margin resilience. Sai 
Life beat expectations across the board. On a more cautious note, Britannia’s second-quarter 
performance was a mixed bag with strong volumes but gross margin pressure. In general, earnings for 
the September quarter were a positive surprise. India benefited from foreign inflows this week.

In Brazil, the central bank kept its interest rate unchanged at 13.75%. Itau reported strong results, in line 
with the consensus, but limited growth and cautious guidance. Axia Energia reported better-than-
feared results, driven by higher transmission revenues. Renner and Meli’s results were disappointing,

In Mexico, the central bank’s surprise interest rate cut was the highlight on the macro front. Banorte 
reported upbeat figures, with loan growth improvement and an ROE of 26 %. 
 
Corporate debt  
 
The Democrat blue sweep in the US midterms dominated headlines. The news initially sent oil tumbling 
below $100 while boosting equity and bond markets but eventually triggered a global risk-off 
movement. Thursday was particularly bad for tech stocks exposed to AI as investors anticipated 
tougher regulations for the sector. The S&P 500 tumbled 4.75 %, falling from 7,720 to 7,353 and the 
Nasdaq plunged 7.14 % (24,315, down from 26,185). The EuroStoxx 50 bucked the trend by edging 0.39 
% higher to 6,232 thanks to limited exposure to cloud giants. The rush into safe havens helped 
government bond yields fall. Yields on 10-year US Treasuries fell 10bp to 4.8 4% and by 11bp to 3.24 % 
for the equivalent German Bund. In credit news, the iTraxx Xover was unchanged at 310 but traded 
between 300 and 330bp over the period. 
 
The new issues market remained busy. In high yield, Recordati (B1/B+/BB) raised €2bn in three 
tranches and SAUR/HIME (BB+/BB) €400m due 2032. In hybrids, VW raised close to €2bn in two 
tranches, one at 6.875 % callable in 2032 and the other at 7.25 % callable in 2034. In a sign of strong 
investor appetite, the order book totalled more than €16bn. It was also an active week in financial debt 
with AXA raising €750m with a T2 bond, Unicaja €300m (T2), HSBC selling a dollar-denominated AT1 
and ASN Bank an AT1 in euros.  
 
Euro high yield returned 0.77 % over the week and euro IG 0.45 %. Eurozone government bonds returned 
0.8 %.

GLOSSARY 
 
• Investment Grade: bonds rated as high quality by rating agencies. 
• High Yield: corporate bonds with a higher default risk than investment grade bonds but which pay out higher 
coupons. 
• Senior debt benefits from specific guarantees. Its repayment takes priority over other debts, known as 
subordinated debt. 
• Debt is considered to be subordinated when its redemption depends on the earlier payment of other creditors. To 
offset the higher risk, subordinated Senior debt has priority over other debt instruments. 
• Tier 2 / Tier 3: subordinated debt segment. 
• Duration: the average life of a bond discounted for all interest and capital flows. 
• The spread is the difference between the actuarial rate of return on a bond and the rate of return on a risk-free 
loan with the same maturity. 
• The so-called "Value" stocks are considered to be undervalued.  
• EBITDA: Earnings before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. 
• CTA: quantitative strategy which uses futures to invest in a wide range of financial assets, including equity indices, 
short-term and long-term interest rates, currencies, and commodities.  
• The PMI, for "Purchasing Manager's Index", is an indicator of the economic state of a sector.  
• AT1s belong to a family of bank capital securities known as contingent convertibles or “Cocos”. Convertible 
because they can be converted from bonds to shares (or depreciated entirely) and contingent because this 
conversion only occurs if certain conditions are met, such as the issuing bank's capital strength falling below a 
predetermined trigger level. 
• RT1s: perpetual bond issues with early redemption possible after 10 years. Coupon payments are discretionary 
and non-cumulative.
