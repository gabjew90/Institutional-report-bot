DB CoTD: Market stress, earnings strength.... 
 
Binky Chadha and Parag Thatte have just published their latest review of the Q3 global earnings season 
here, and the headline is that corporate profits remain remarkably strong despite an increasingly demanding 
bar. With a vast majority of the season over, global earnings growth likely held at 33% in Q3, with the strength 
broadening beyond Tech, Energy and Materials, while Europe accelerated sharply and almost caught up with 
the US. Their work also shows forward estimates continuing to be revised higher. Today’s CoTD pulls out the 
key messages from their review. 
1. A high bar coming into Q3: Global Q2 earnings growth of 38% was arguably one of the strongest 
readings ever, while Q3 estimates had been steadily upgraded over the previous year. 
2. Beats have normalised outside Europe: Against that tougher hurdle, earnings beats fell back from 
exceptionally high levels in the US and Japan, while EM missed. Europe was the exception, with 
beats edging higher and remaining above average. 
3. Global earnings growth is still in the 30s: Q3 growth moderated to 33%, still one of the highest rates 
on record. Tech, Energy and Materials did much of the heavy lifting, but growth excluding them was 
still a robust 12%. 
4. The strength is broadening: 7 of 11 global sectors delivered double-digit earnings growth, supported 
by solid sales growth and record margins. 

5. US growth remains exceptionally strong: S&P 500 earnings grew 34% yoy, unchanged from Q2 and 
among the strongest rates outside recession rebounds. Median-company growth was also strong at 
14.4%, while growth excluding Tech, Energy and Materials was 12.6%, near the top of its post-GFC 
range. 
6. Europe is catching up rapidly: Stoxx 600 earnings growth accelerated from 22% to 28%, the strongest 
in four years. The US-Europe growth gap narrowed to just 5.6pp, its smallest in 3½ years, with Europe 
matching or beating the US in 6 sectors, versus only 2 in Q1. 
7. Japan and EM slowed, but from extraordinary levels: Japan decelerated from nearly 60% to 33%, 
while EM eased from 49% to 39% — still the strongest regional growth rate. Korea (220%) and Taiwan 
(61%) remained standouts. 
8. Forward estimates are still being revised up: Consensus forecasts for Q4 2026 and 2027 have 
continued to rise, with particularly large upgrades in EM over the last year, led by Korea and Taiwan. 
US and Japan estimates have also moved notably higher, and Europe modestly higher. 
So 2026 is shaping up as an exceptionally strong year for earnings globally. Growth is expected to moderate 
in 2027, but consensus still has double-digit growth across most major regions. Given the recent sell-off, 
especially in tech, this strength may eventually help markets stabilise. Clearly there could be questions 
about whether some of these earnings are artificial and based on excessive capex. However, for now there is 
little doubt that we’re in a period of uniquely strong earnings. See Binky and Parag’s report for a much more 
forensic look at the season around the world.
