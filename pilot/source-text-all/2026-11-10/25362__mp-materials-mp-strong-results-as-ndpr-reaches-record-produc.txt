beating expectations, largely supported by the sale of SEG+ 
inventory, while underlying NdPr results were broadly in line with our 
expectations. NdPr production increased 41% yoy to a record 1,018

quarter. MP also began operations of its Dy/Tb separation circuit, 
with commercial-scale production expected to begin in 4Q26. 
Importantly, the magnet business reached a key milestone, with GM 
qualification now complete and initial saleable magnet shipments 
expected to begin in 4Q26, followed by a gradual volume ramp 
through 2027. With continued progress across Materials and 
Magnetics, we believe MP is entering an important 
commercialization and growth phase. We remain Buy rated, with our 
full takeaways from the quarter contained herein.

capabilities expand. NdPr production increased 41% yoy to a record 1,018 MT in 
3Q26, with sales volumes rising 94% yoy to 1,020 MT, while MP expects 4Q 
production of ~1,000 MT despite the scheduled maintenance outage early in the 
quarter. Importantly, MP operated at >450 MT/month for portions of 3Q and 
expects to reach a sustainable ~500 MT/month production rate over the next 
several quarters, with 2027 NdPr production expected to exceed 5,000 MT despite 
additional planned downtime associated with chlor-alkali and other expansion 
projects. The company also began operations of its heavy rare earths separation 
circuits during 3Q and expects commercial-scale Dy/Tb production to begin in 
4Q26, supporting the heavy rare earths requirements of magnets produced at 
Independence. MP also monetized a modest portion of its previously stockpiled 
SEG+ inventory at attractive economics while retaining several hundred tonnes of 
feedstock for future production and beginning to reintroduce inventory to support 
the heavy rare earth ramp. Longer term, samarium and gadolinium projects remain 
on track for first production in 1H28, while management expects increasing NdPr 
volumes, chlor-alkali and incremental heavy-REE production to drive further cost 
leverage, particularly in 2H27. 
Magnet qualification complete, with GM shipments beginning in 4Q. MP 
n
completed magnet qualification during 3Q and expects to begin shipments of 
saleable magnets to GM in 4Q26, marking the transition at Independence from 
qualification toward commercial production and a gradual volume ramp. 
Management expects magnet volumes to remain modest over the next several 
quarters as GM deliberately phases MP’s magnets into specific programs and plants, 
before accelerating in 2H27 as volumes increase and production for Apple begins 
contributing to results. Magnetics generated $19mn of revenue and $5mn of adj. 
EBITDA in 3Q26, with approximately $28mn of deferred precursor revenue 
remaining to be recognized over the next several quarters. However, management 
expects the segment’s overall EBITDA contribution to remain modest in 2027 as 
declining precursor revenue offsets the early magnet ramp, with the segment’s full 
earnings power becoming more visible in 2028. Separately, 10X remains on schedule 
for commissioning in late 2028, with management noting that specific customer 
RFQs and demand indications now well exceed the facility’s planned capacity, 
including growing interest from robotics and technology customers. Importantly, MP 
believes advancements in magnet formulations and grain boundary diffusion could 
allow it to address demand exceeding 10X’s planned capacity with formulations 
requiring no heavy rare earths, with management increasingly viewing NdPr, rather 
than Dy/Tb availability, as the binding constraint on future magnet growth. With 10X 
already 100% contracted, management believes it can remain selective in pursuing 
incremental, potentially higher-margin opportunities. 
Maintaining strong balance sheet. MP ended 3Q26 with $1.3bn of cash and 
n
equivalents. YTD, the company has burned $8mn in operating cash flow while 
spending $420mn on capex ($113mn in 3Q26), which includes the acquisition of the 
10X site. Importantly, MP continues to expect $500mn-$600mn of capex in 2026, 
suggesting a similar capex range for 4Q26. Thus, we believe the company maintains 
sufficient liquidity to fund its long-term capital plan.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
