US mid-terms: AI and corporate taxes in focus 
As the results of the November 3 US mid-term election emerge on Thursday 
morning in Asia, we will focus on two key issues:  
1) The extent of voter support for candidates and local ballot initiatives 
that would restrict data center development. The US AI infrastructure boom 
is crucial for the outlook for Asian export and earnings growth over the next 
several years. 
Although much of the recent financial market anxiety about US AI capex 
spending has focused on profitability and financing costs, we think political 
constraints on development are increasingly important. A recent Politico opinion 
poll conducted by PF Global found that over 50% of respondents oppose data 
center development within three miles of their homes. An ABC News/Ipsos Poll in 
October found that 66% of respondents oppose building new data centers in the 
US and 72% oppose having one in their local community. 
Against this background, we highlight below several key races to follow. 
Ohio: Sherrod Brown, the Democratic challenger to Senator Jon Husted, has 
made data center regulation and restriction a core part of his campaign. Ohio 
also has 21 proposals to regulate data centers up for local votes across over a 
dozen jurisdictions. In the race of governor, Democratic candidate Amy Acton 
proposes greater control over data centers and AI than Republican Vivek 
Ramaswamy. 
Michigan: In Michigan’s 7th district, Will Lawrence, the progressive Democrat 
challenger to Republican Congressman Tom Barrett, has called for restrictions on 
data center development, a pause on AI development, mandatory AI safety 
requirements, and a national jobs program for AI-related job losses. At the same 
time, while the outcome of the Senate race between Democrat Abdul El-Sayed 
and Republican Mike Rodgers will be clouded by issues of religion and positions 
relating to Israel and Palestine, El-Sayed has taken a tougher line against data 
centers. 
Texas: For the Senate, Democratic challenger James Talarico argues for data 
centers to cover their utilities costs and has attacked incumbent Republican 
Senator Ken Paxton for his ties to the data center industry. For governor, 
Democrat challenger Gina Hinojosa is pushing for binding legislation that gives 
local governments direct approval/veto power over data center placement before 
state permits are issued. This would be a more restrictive stance than what 
incumbent Greg Abbott has advocated. 
Georgia: For the Senate, incumbent Jon Ossoff has taken an aggressive stance on 
requiring data centers to pay for utilities costs whereas Republican challenger 
Mike Collins has taken a more pro-AI stance. 
Arizona: The 1st Congressional district election is being fought in large part over 
Republican Jay Feely’s Trump-aligned pro-data center stance vs. Democrat Amish

New Mexico: In the race for governor, Democratic Deb Haaland has pledged to

More generally, a broad Democratic-leaning Congress would likely prevent the

2) The success of Progressive candidates and Progressive local initiatives 
that succeed, not just the number of Democratic Party victories. A wave of 
Progressive victories could increase the risk of tax increases on both the very 
wealthy and corporations after the 2028 Presidential elections. The success or 
failure of California’s Proposition 40, otherwise known as the billionaire tax, will 
be important to watch in this regard.  
We view the success of Progressive candidates as important for the US equity 
outlook because of the collapse in US labour income as a share of GDP since 
2000 and the corresponding rise in corporate profit as a share of GDP to new 
highs. Empirical research has attributed this to a mix of China’s entry into the 
World Trade Organisation, increased automation of the US workforce, M&A 
leading to dramatically increased corporate concentration in the US, and the 
decline in labour union membership to historic lows.  
 
 
 
Investors also need to understand that the mid-term results are unlikely to 
change either the tariff or geopolitical landscape. Congressional restrictions or 
reversal on US President Trump’s ability to impose tariffs or on his ability to 
deploy US military forces would require Congress to pass new laws. However, 
realistically, for any new bills that would restrict presidential authority to become 
law, they would need the support of 2/3 of both houses of Congress to override a 
presidential veto. While not impossible, no credible polls and no probability 
markets suggest that the Democrats will win a 2/3 majority in the Senate.

US payrolls: Focus on wage growth more than job growth 
 
Following on from our earlier discussion of the collapse in the US wage share in 
GDP, we would be focusing more on wage growth rather than job growth in 
Friday’s release of US labour market data. We have noted in past issues that the 
collapse in immigration into the US this year greatly restricts the potential for job 
growth by constraining growth in the labour force. This reality shifts the signal 
value for the Fed of the monthly labour market report from job gains to the main 
indicators of labour market tightness i.e. the unemployment rate and, most 
important, wage growth. 
As the chart below shows, the US Q3 Employment Cost Index released last Friday 
indicated that labour compensation growth, including wages and benefits, 
slowed further in the third quarter. Crucially, this came despite the 
unemployment and underemployment rates falling to levels historically 
considered to be characteristic of full employment. Slowing employment cost 
inflation reduces inflation risk and with it, the pressure for the Federal Reserve 
(Fed) to increase the Fed Funds rate. 
 
 
Although we expect the Fed to raise the Fed Funds rate by 25bps in December, 
the further 25bps rate hike the market is currently pricing in will likely depend 
heavily on whether wage growth remains non-inflationary.  Our analysis suggests 
that growth in average hourly earnings needs to rise in the coming months from 
the recent 0.2%mom average rate to at least 0.3%mom and more likely, a 
0.4%mom rate to support the current market pricing for Fed hikes.

Market Review 
___________ 
Global equities were broadly weaker over the week, with the MSCI AC World Index declining 0.3%. US equities were also lower, with the S&P 
500 and Nasdaq falling 0.6% and 2.0% respectively. Emerging Markets lost 0.7%, while Asia Pacific ex Japan increased 0.6%. Japan was down 
0.3% for the week. 
Gold fell 2.5%, while the US dollar index strengthened by 0.1%. Bond markets were positive with Global Corporate Bonds and Global High 
Yield bonds gaining 0.4% each, and Global Government Bonds rising 0.3%.
