USD: Deeper correction lower for AI-related stocks boosts safe haven demand 
The main development overnight has been sharp sell-off for AI-related investments. 
Acute weakness in the US equity market has spilled over into Asian markets overnight 
with the Topix and Kospi equity indices both falling by around 6%. It follows on from a 
similar sized drop in the Nasdaq composite index of around -5.5%. The sell-off in the 
Nasdaq composite index accelerated yesterday after it broke below support from its 
200-day moving average that came in at around 25,090. It is the first time that Nasdaq 
composite index has fallen below the 200-day moving average since earlier this year 
in March and April. After yesterday’s sell-off the Nasdaq composite index now stands 
just over -12% below the peak from last month which compares to the sell-off in the 
first three months of this year totalling around -13.8%. Yesterday’s sharp sell-off 
appears to have been a delayed reaction to the US mid-term elections results which 
have heightened investor uncertainty over AI-related investment returns. The strong 
performance of the Democrats in the has triggered investor concerns that the ongoing 
build out of AI data centres could be disrupted, and policy could shift towards 
implementing tighter regulation on the AI sector in the future. One of the biggest

The spillover impact from the AI-related equity market sell-off into the foreign exchange 
market has remained surprisingly modest so far. The Australian dollar has been hit the 
hardest so far falling by around -1.5% against the US dollar since 4th November 
undermined as well by the recent sharp correction lower in energy prices including 
natural gas. At the other end of the spectrum, the three best performing G10 currencies 
so far have bene the traditional safe haven currencies of the yen, US dollar and Swiss 
franc. It has resulted in the dollar index reversing all of its initial losses since the US 
mid-term election results were announced. The resilience of the US dollar is somewhat 
surprising given the ongoing unwinding of investor confidence in AI-related 
investments in the US, and sharp correction lower in energy prices reflecting renewed 
optimism over a US-Iran deal. US dollar strength this year has been supported by 
higher energy prices and record foreign demand for riskier US assets. If both these 
drivers continue to reverse, it would increase the likelihood of the US dollar weakening.  
At the same time, the sharp sell-off in AI-related investments is contributing to a further 
tightening financial conditions in the US. A development acknowledged by Fed Cahir 
Kevin Warsh at the last FOMC meeting. If financial conditions continue to tighten it 
could make it less likely that the Fed will hike again in December as planned thereby 
adding to downside risks for the US dollar. The US rate market still judges that a 
December hike remains more likely than not although the probability dropped in recent 
days. It is currently pricing in around 16bps of hikes compared to around 21bps of hikes 
on 4th November. The tightening of US financial conditions could prove more important 
in decided Fed policy action in December than today’s NFP report which is expected 
to show stronger employment growth.          
 
GBP: BoE’s hawkish hike provides support but offset by AI-related sell-off  
The market impact of yesterday’s BoE policy update was overshadowed by the 
deepening sell-off for AI-related investments. UK yields and the pound both rose 
initially after the BoE’s hawkish hike although have since reversed those moves after 
the sell-off in US equity market intensified. UK yields have held up better than in other 
major economies supported by the hawkish message from the BoE signalling that at 
least one more hike is likely in the current tightening cycle. We had expected more 
cautious policy guidance from the BoE pushing back against expectations for more 
aggressive rate hikes but that did not materialise.   
Governor Bailey stated clearly that there was a strong case for another rate hike if the 
US-Iran conflict is not resolved. It was a view shared by the majority of other MPC 
members, with Catherine Mann, Huw Pill, Megan Greene and Clare Lombardelli all 
leaning towards at least one more hike. We have been forecasting that the BoE will 
follow up today’s hike with another increase in February next year, but acknowledge 
that there is now a higher risk of a back-to-back hike in December. The UK rate market 
is now attaching around 13bps of hikes by December.  
One important caveat is that Governor Bailey indicated that the BoE will be mainly 
focused on energy price developments heading into year-end. The price of oil has 
dropped sharply over the past week, which was not incorporated in the BoE’s updated

progress towards a US-Iran deal following the US mid-term elections, the BoE may be 
more inclined to hold off on hiking rates again as soon as next month. After one more 
hike, the case for further increases is judged as less clear-cut by MPC members. 
Governor Bailey stated, “I’m not going to sign up to the idea of ‘two-and-done’” while 
emphasising that a series of hikes is “not inevitable”. The BoE will be watching closely 
for evidence of building second-round effects to assess whether further rate hikes are 
required in 2027. At the same time, a deeper sell-off in AI stocks and resulting 
tightening of global financial conditions could dampen the BoE’s willingness to tighten 
policy further. 
The GBP has recently been supported by growing optimism over stronger UK growth, 
higher UK yields and fiscal concerns becoming a bigger market issue in the euro area 
than in the UK. At today’s policy meeting, the BoE acknowledged that UK growth has 
surprised positively, partly reflecting stronger exports and an AI-related investment and 
productivity boost. In the near term, stronger activity suggests less spare capacity in 
the economy and has added to the case for tighter monetary policy. However, the 
inflation implications are ambiguous. The BoE still needs to determine whether the 
recent improvement in productivity represents a persistent increase in the economy’s 
supply capacity. Overall, today’s hawkish BoE policy update should reinforce the 
GBP’s bullish momentum heading into year-end. One downside risk for the GBP would 
be a deeper sell-off for AI stocks and tightening of financial conditions given the GBP’s
