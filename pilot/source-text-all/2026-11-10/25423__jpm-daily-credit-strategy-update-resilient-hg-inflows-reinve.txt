Post-selloff update: Yesterday’s 2.2bp selloff in IG cash brought spreads back to the YTD wides of 105bp. This is the same level 
where spreads closed on Tuesday prior to the election vs. the Nasdaq is down 4.86% since then. In other words, credit 
outperformed quite starkly yesterday. Most notably, IG CDX which tends to have a higher correlation to equities and since the last 
roll incorporates all of the hyperscalers, was only 3.5bp wider yesterday vs an implied move of 6bp using a 5yr lookback versus 
the NDX (see chart below). On the cash side, we note that hyperscalers were just 2.6bp wider versus +2.1bp for JULI ex-
hyperscaler, with TRACE showing a second day in a row of net buying (-$1.3bn yesterday) despite inflows dropping yesterday to 
close to zero.

Fund Flows: HG fund inflows strengthened to $6.6bn (+50% WoW) this week, well above the 4-week average of $3.6bn. Note 
though that this week’s flows include the first day of the month, which almost always tends to see a surge in inflows (Figure 13Daily fund flows) . 
The pickup was led by Mutual Fund flows flipping back to inflows of $1.2bn, the highest in 9 weeks, while ETF inflows 
moderated to $5.4bn (-28% WoW). Our estimate of flows into HG corporate bonds across the fund complex rose to $4.1bn (+26% 
WoW). Within HG sub-categories, Corp-only fund inflows increased to $3.3bn (+13% WoW) driven by the intermediate bucket 
inflows of $2.5bn, the highest in 18 weeks, while short end and long end inflows eased to $675mn (-12% WoW) and $71mn (-
49% WoW) respectively. Aggregate fund inflows improved to $2.6bn (+26% WoW), led by the short end returning to inflows of 
$756mn (+$1.1bn WoW). In contrast, intermediate and long end inflows slowed to $1.8bn (-22% WoW) and $28mn (-68% WoW) 
respectively. Total Return funds reverted to inflows of $766mn (+$1.3bn WoW) amidst positive total returns of +0.64% for the 
fund flow week and +2.5% on a 1m trailing basis. 
Among other asset classes, HY inflows modestly improved to $1.8bn, the highest in 13 weeks, and Leveraged Loans flipped back 
to inflows of $816mn (+$855mn WoW). Muni inflows declined to $1.6bn (-38% WoW). Money Market flows reverted to inflows 
of $130.2bn, the 6th highest weekly inflow on record and the highest since September 2024, and Equities inflows doubled to 
$34bn. 
On the net supply front, $73bn of gross issuance priced this week versus $29bn of reinvestment income, split between $20bn of 
maturities and $9bn of coupon income. Looking ahead, we estimate that reinvestment income will fall to $14bn next week, driven 
by $9bn of maturities and $5bn of coupon income (see Figure 11W
ekly coupon income and maturities below for our weekly coupons and maturities schedule). This is 
51% below both the November weekly average and the 2026 weekly average. Lastly, our estimates suggest that HG dealer 
inventories (>13m) increased from the last Fed reported and are now at their highest levels since July 2024 and HY dealer 
inventories have also increased and are now at 62% of their 3m range (Figure 4HG net dealer inventories estimation).

KEY DATA HIGHLIGHTS – CASH MARKETS:
- The JULI spread widened by 2bp on Thursday to 105bp, while the yield fell by 3bp to 6.01% (Table 4). The JULI Hyperscalers 
spread is at 166bp, +3bp yesterday (Table 2). 
- There was $20.5bn of High Grade issuance on Thursday, across 10 issuers. WTD and MTD $73bn has been issued. YTD supply 
totals $1893bn. Issuance in November has averaged $109bn over the past four years.
- Bonds issued over the past 10 days are 4bp tighter than at issuance on average (based on $115bn across 99 JULI eligible bonds), 
while the index overall is 2bp tighter over the same time period.
- HG public mutual funds and ETFs had $88mn of inflows on Thursday. This was split between $1,142mn of ETF inflows 
(1305%) and $1,055mn of mutual fund outflows (-1205%). This is -91% below the 5d average, -92% below the 10d average and -
89% below the 1m average.
- Our estimate of dealer net positions in corporate bonds >13m increased by $432mn on Thursday (this includes TRACE and ETF 
data). On the week this estimate of positions is up by $8.1bn to $6.9bn, which is at the 100% level in the 3m range. Within the last 
3m range, positions are at the 95% level in the 1-5yr bucket, at the 100% level in the 5-10yr bucket, and at the 81% level in the 
10+yr bucket (Table 1HG net dealer inventories estimation).
- Trading activity in HG bonds was $47.1bn on Thursday (2w average $47.7bn/day, YTD average $44.7bn/day). Dealers were net 
buyers of $1.3bn of bonds (1w avg $2.4bn/day buying, 3m avg $347mn/day buying). Dealers net bought $11.8bn of bonds from 
investors over the past 5 days and net bought $20.8bn of bonds from investors over the past 10 days. 13.8% of trading yesterday 
was executed through Portfolio Trades and over the past 5 days this figure is 13.9%. Over the 5 day period $73bn of new HG 
bonds were issued.
- Our Credit Relative Value Screen highlights broad market relationships which are out of line with their historical trading 
patterns: BBB rated bonds are wide to A rated bonds.
- The 3s5s curve is trading at 16bp (19% of its 1m range), 5s10s is trading at 23bp (32% of its 1m range) and 10s30s is trading at 
16bp (46% of its 1m range). Please see our Curveball: HG Credit Curve Opportunities for details on the bond pairs which 
comprise these curves and the steepest/flattest ones in each segment (Table 2Relative Value Relationships: HG Bond market).
- The spread difference between BBB and A Non Financials ex EM is 42bp, its widest level since May 2025 (Table 2Relative Value Relationships: HG Bond market).

Explanation of Credit Research Valuation Methodology, Ratings and Risk to Ratings:
 J.P. Morgan uses a bond-level rating system that incorporates valuations (relative value) and our fundamental view on the security. Our 
fundamental credit view of an issuer is based on the company's underlying credit trends, overall creditworthiness and our opinion on whether the 
issuer will be able to service its debt obligations when they become due and payable. We analyze, among other things, the company's cash flow 
capacity and trends and standard credit ratios, such as gross and net leverage, interest coverage and liquidity ratios. We also analyze profitability, 
capitalization and asset quality, among other variables, when assessing financials. Analysts also rate the issuer, based on the rating of the 
benchmark or representative security. Unless we specify a different recommendation for the company’s individual securities, an issuer 
recommendation applies to all of the bonds at the same level of the issuer’s capital structure. We may also rate certain loans and preferred 
securities, as applicable. This report also sets out within it the material underlying assumptions used. We use the following ratings for bonds 
(issues), issuers, loans, and preferred securities: Overweight (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to 
outperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected to perform 
in line with the relevant index, sector, or benchmark); and Underweight (over the next three months, the recommended risk position is expected 
to underperform the relevant index, sector, or benchmark). For CDS, we use the following rating system: Long Risk (over the next three months, 
the credit return on the recommended position is expected to exceed the relevant index, sector or benchmark); Neutral (over the next three 
months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to match the relevant index, sector or benchmark); and Short Risk (over the 
next three months, the credit return on the recommended position is expected to underperform the relevant index, sector or benchmark).
