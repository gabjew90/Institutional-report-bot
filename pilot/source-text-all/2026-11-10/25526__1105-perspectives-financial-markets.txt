Interest rates & bonds
AI trade unwind supports government bonds 
USA 
– In October, US Treasury yields declined due in part 
to weak labour market data and worries regarding
AI-overinvestment. USD credit spreads widened, 
with CCC spreads underperforming the most. 
– US inflation remains above the central bank’s target 
amid a resilient labour market. We expect the Fed-
eral Reserve to deliver two additional rate hikes. 
Eurozone 
– German Bund yields declined in October, supported 
by safe-haven demand amid concerns over French 
sovereign risk. EUR IG and HY spreads widened 
more noticeably than their US counterparts. 
– The ECB kept the policy rate unchanged in October, 
as expected. We anticipate one additional rate hike, 
likely in December 2026. 
UK 
– Core inflation increased slightly to 2.8% in Septem-
ber, but markets focused more on the autumn 
budget, which was viewed as solid and led to lower 
10-year Gilt yields. IG spreads widened in line with 
the more cautious tone in credit markets overall. 
– Given above-target inflation, we expect two addi-
tional rate hikes by the Bank of England. 
Switzerland 
– Swiss 10-year government bond yields also declined 
in October, while IG spreads remained unchanged.
– The relatively weak CHF and improved economic 
data provide an opportunity for the SNB to normal-
ise monetary policy to some extent. We expect two 
interest rate hikes of 25 basis points in 2027. 
TMT sector spread widening in October
Triggered by higher interest rates and elevated bond 
supply, credit spreads in the telecom, media and tech-
nology (TMT) sector widened in October. This drove 
higher spread volatility across the AI infrastructure 
ecosystem, including AI cloud providers, firms linked
to leading AI developers, and BBB/BB-rated data cen-
tre project finance bonds. As a result, the debate over 
AI capex versus AI revenues and circular AI financing 
intensified. Combined with elevated AI-related equity 
valuations, market expectations became increasingly
optimistic, leaving little room for weaker-than-ex-
pected earnings. This triggered a broader rotation away 
from highly valued and rate-sensitive AI credit expo-
sure. Expectations of lower AI capital spending would 
imply weaker data centre construction activity, re-
duced corporate borrowing needs, softer commodity 
demand, and a diminished growth and inflation im-
pulse. Hence, an unwinding of the “AI trade” would be
supportive for government bonds. In credit, funda-
mentals remain solid, although risks such as geopolit-
ical tensions, volatile energy prices and higher rates 
persist. We therefore remain neutral on USD IG and
HY spreads. We view the widening in French sovereign 
and EUR IG spreads as overdone and adopt a positive
stance on EUR IG. Given elevated uncertainty, we are
neutral on 2-year and 10-year government bond yields
across the US, eurozone and Switzerland over a 1-
month horizon.

Equities 
The earnings season delivers
USA
– US equities gained slightly in October despite high
bond yields and geopolitical uncertainties. The mar-
ket was supported by solid earnings (see main text). 
– Market leadership remains concentrated in large-
cap technology stocks, while valuations remain
above long-term averages. 
Eurozone
– Eurozone equities declined in October, continuing 
to lag the US year-to-date performance amid rising
bond yields in France and lower economic growth 
expectations compared to the US. 
– Valuations remain reasonable, with high-dividend 
stocks still offering attractive income. 
UK
– UK equities rose in October in line with US equities, 
outperforming continental Europe. 
– Attractive valuations and a defensive market compo-
sition continue to support the market. 
Switzerland
– Swiss equities rebounded strongly in October and 
outperformed most developed markets. 
– Defensive sectors and heavyweight quality stocks 
were the main drivers of performance. 
Emerging markets
– Emerging markets fell significantly in October, with 
weakness in China and India outweighing gains in 
parts of Latin America. 
– Valuations remain attractive, although index con-
centration in Asian technology and AI-related stocks 
remains elevated. 
The earnings season
The earnings season is roughly halfway through and, 
as expected, results have been strong so far. Expecta-
tions remain elevated, with consensus forecasting
earnings growth of 27% in the US (while the median
company is growing earnings by 8-9%), 72% in Emerg-
ing Markets, 22% in Europe, and 11% in Switzerland. 
In addition, upward earnings revisions continue to 
outnumber downward revisions. However, earnings 
growth remains highly concentrated. In the US, the IT
and energy sectors alone are expected to generate 
around 80% of overall earnings growth. Micron and 
Nvidia account for 35% by themselves. In emerging 
markets, 90% of earnings growth is coming from
North Asia and, from a sector perspective, more than
80% from information technology. In Europe, energy
and financials are the main drivers, together account-
ing for roughly 70% of expected earnings growth. 
A broad-based market correction appears unlikely. 
Earnings growth remains exceptionally strong, while 
the ongoing reassessment of AI-related investments, 
circular financing structures and other themes high-
lighted in our AI special report reflects a healthier bal-
ance between opportunities and risks. We do not ex-
pect an imminent unwinding of the “AI trade”. The
true test will come only once the current wave of data 
centre and computing capacity investments becomes 
operational, allowing depreciation charges, potential 
write-downs and capacity leasing costs to be fully re-
flected. Overall, we continue to see modest upside for 
equity markets, supported by our expectation that 
rates will not rise materially further and by historically
favourable fourth-quarter seasonality.

Currencies 
Stabilisation in the EUR after recent sell-off 
USA 
– On a trade-weighted basis, the USD moved sideways 
in October. The meeting of the US Federal Reserve, 
which held policy rates stable in October, did not 
give any fresh impetus for the greenback.  
– The US economic outperformance, which was evi-
dent in the very strong Q3 GDP data, as well as the 
favourable interest rate differential, still speak in fa-
vour of the USD. We thus expect renewed USD ap-
preciation against most major currencies apart from
EUR, which already depreciated substantially in Oc-
tober. 
Eurozone 
– The EUR showed the worst performance of all major 
currencies in October due to French bond market 
turmoils and an uncertain ECB monetary policy 
outlook (see main text).  
– After the strong EUR sell-off, we have now adopted 
a neutral view on EUR/USD and EUR/CHF. 
UK 
– GBP appreciated 1.5% on a trade-weighted basis in 
October, driven by hawkish signals from the Bank of 
England and an autumn budget which was taken 
positively by markets, limiting fiscal risks. 
– We believe that the move has gone a bit too far and 
thus expect GBP to weaken against EUR and USD in 
November. 
Switzerland 
– The CHF appreciated in October as it benefitted 
from its safe haven status. 
– We shift our view on EUR/CHF from negative to 
neutral after the strong move in October.  
Japan
– The JPY was little changed against USD in October.
– The Bank of Japan is widely expected to hike policy 
rates in December after a hold in October. In our 
view the tightening is too slow to remove deprecia-
tion pressure on the JPY vs. USD given the signifi-
cant interest rate differential. 
EUR/USD now trading at around its fair value 
The main feature of the FX market in October was the
pronounced weakness of EUR, which depreciated 
around 2% on a trade-weighted basis. Our negative 
views on EUR versus USD and CHF thus turned out to 
be correct. The main driver for EUR weakness was con-
cerns over the French political and fiscal outlook, 
which led to sell-off in French government bonds. The 
ECB finds itself in a difficult spot. Core inflation re-
mains above target and suggests a need to tighten 
monetary policy further. However, the renewed spike 
in energy prices, which acts as an external shock for the 
eurozone, as well as the tighter financial condition due
to rising long-term yields, cloud the economic outlook 
and will in our view limit the extent of monetary tight-
ening by the ECB. In our base case, we only expect one 
additional policy rate hike in December 2026 and then 
foresee the ECB remaining on hold. While this is still
less monetary tightening than market expectations,
the latter have shifted in our direction in October, 
which likely contributed to the general EUR weakness.
That said, the uncertainty regarding the monetary pol-
icy outlook was amplified at the end of the month by 
the announcement of ECB President Lagarde’s depar-
ture. 
In our view, the EUR/USD has now moved to a level
which is around our estimate of fair value, as suggested 
by 5-year real yield differentials. We therefore adopt a
neutral view on EUR/USD.  
We also expect a stabilisation of EUR/CHF after the 
strong move in October, which was also fuelled by safe-
haven flows into CHF due to general risk-off sentiment
in financial markets.

Asset allocation 
Risk takes a reality check
October in review 
– Global equity markets delivered a mixed perfor-
mance in October as concerns about AI weighed on 
sentiment. Emerging markets, China and technol-
ogy stocks led the decline, while Japan and Switzer-
land bucked the trend and delivered positive returns.
– Sovereign bond yields moved in different directions 
across markets. US Treasury yields moved lower 
across the curve. French yields rose substantially 
amid political turmoil. German yields declined. 
– Credit spreads widened, reflecting a more cautious 
risk environment. The move was particularly pro-
nounced in high-yield and emerging-market credit, 
while investment-grade spreads widened more 
moderately. 
– The USD was flat on a trade-weighted basis. 
Current asset allocation views 
– We maintain an overweight in equities. The recent 
correction has taken some of the excess out of the 
market and, in our view, represents a healthy reality 
check rather than a fundamental change in the out-
look. Underlying support remains intact, with con-
tinued strong earnings momentum. Valuations have 
become somewhat more reasonable and positioning 
less stretched.  
– Within global equities, we favour US and emerging-
market equities for their strong fundamentals, as 
well as European equities given Germany’s improv-
ing economic backdrop.  
– We remain neutral on government bonds, but prefer
shorter duration, as resilient growth and concerns 
about longer-term fiscal sustainability warrant some 
caution at the long end of the curve. 
– We remain underweight credit, as we see a more at-
tractive risk/reward profile in equities and real 
assets.  
– For CHF portfolios, we continue to favour Swiss 
NAV-based real estate and infrastructure and are 
tactically increasing our exposure to Swiss traded 
real estate.  
A multi-asset perspective on risk
Market risk is often viewed through an equity lens. Yet
the VIX captures only one dimension of volatility. The 
MOVE Index, which reflects volatility in US Treasury 
markets, provides a complementary perspective. Re-
cent years have shown that volatility across equities
and bonds can evolve quite differently as inflation,
monetary policy and growth expectations change. 
For multi-asset investors, this matters. Elevated inter-
est rate volatility can affect portfolios through changes
in discount rates, financing conditions and correla-
tions, even when equity volatility remains relatively 
contained. At the same time, equity indices themselves
can mask significant dispersion across sectors, factors
and individual securities.
The relevant question is therefore not simply whether 
volatility is high or low, but where risk is being priced 
and how it may transmit across asset classes. This has 
direct implications for portfolio construction. Higher 
yields have restored the income potential of fixed in-
come, but duration and quality remain important fac-
tors to consider. Within equities, diversification across 
regions, sectors and factors can reduce dependence on 
concentrated return drivers. Real assets, gold and se-
lected alternative strategies can provide additional 
sources of diversification, particularly when traditional
equity–bond correlations become less reliable. For 
multi-asset investors, portfolio construction therefore 
becomes increasingly important when volatility signals 
and correlations differ across markets. The focus
should be on balancing complementary risk premia 
and return drivers, rather than relying disproportion-
ately on a single market regime or source of perfor-
mance.
