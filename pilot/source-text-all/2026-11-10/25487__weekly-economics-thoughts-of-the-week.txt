High Prices Have No Ideology
Democrats paid a political price when inflation accelerated following the pandemic, and Republicans have 
now experienced the same outcome. The lesson is straightforward: Americans dislike higher prices, and 
policies associated with rising prices often carry political consequences. In that sense, inflation and higher 
taxes remain among the greatest threats to incumbents. This is not a new lesson. Rather, inflation remained 
so subdued for so long that many policymakers forgot how costly rising prices can be politically. While 
inflation is generally easier to reduce once it emerges, preventing it altogether is often much more difficult, 
as demonstrated by the worldwide inflation surge during the post-pandemic recovery. 
The Fed Chair has argued on several occasions that "inflation is a choice," a characterization we disagreed 
with then and continue to disagree with today. Had the argument been that bringing inflation down is a 
choice, or that bringing it down more quickly is a choice, we would agree. The Fed possesses a powerful, but 
blunt, tool to combat inflation: raising interest rates enough to push the economy into recession. During the 
post-pandemic period, however, policymakers chose not to pursue that path. Rates were increased but not 
enough to derail economic growth. As a result, inflation declined gradually rather than abruptly. 
Inflation was not the product of a deliberate policy decision to generate higher prices. The Fed certainly 
could have acted earlier and more aggressively, although doing so likely would have triggered a severe 
political backlash. Instead, policymakers waited too long to begin tightening and then proceeded at a pace 
that slowed inflation without forcing the economy into recession. Fiscal policy also played a significant role 
by adding fuel to already-existing inflationary pressures. It was not the only factor, but it helped keep 
inflation elevated for longer than otherwise would have been the case.
Today's inflation environment bears little resemblance to the post-pandemic period, not only because 
inflation is lower, but because its underlying drivers are different. While fiscal policy remains expansionary, 
as evidenced by a large fiscal deficit despite robust economic growth, today's economy is not being 
powered by government transfer payments. Instead, growth is being driven by a powerful investment cycle 
tied to artificial intelligence and data-center construction. 
Fiscal policy is supporting investment directly through immediate expensing provisions and boosting 
consumption indirectly through stronger corporate earnings and higher equity prices. In turn, rising asset 
values have contributed to substantial gains in household wealth.

Consumer Price Index (CPI) A consumer price index is a price index, the price of a weighted average market basket of 
consumer goods and services purchased by households.
Producer Price Index: A producer price index (PPI) is a price index that measures the average changes in prices received by 
domestic producers for their output.
Industrial production: Industrial production is a measure of the output of the industrial sector of the economy. The 
industrial sector includes manufacturing, mining, and utilities. Although these sectors contribute only a small portion of 
gross domestic product, they are highly sensitive to interest rates and consumer demand.
The NAHB/Wells Fargo Housing Opportunity Index (HOI) for a given area is defined as the share of homes sold in that area 
that would have been affordable to a family earning the local median income, based on standard mortgage underwriting 
criteria.
Conference Board Coincident Economic Index: The Composite Index of Coincident Indicators is an index published by the 
Conference Board that provides a broad-based measurement of current economic conditions, helping economists, 
investors, and public policymakers to determine which phase of the business cycle the economy is currently experiencing.
Conference Board Lagging Economic Index: The Composite Index of Lagging Indicators is an index published monthly by the 
Conference Board, used to confirm and assess the direction of the economy's movements over recent months.
New Export Index: The PMI New export orders index allows us to track international demand for a country's goods and 
services on a timely, monthly, basis.
