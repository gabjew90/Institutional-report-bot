GS Pasi - Global Credit Trader Taking Stock of Performance and
Where to Invest From Here 30 Oct 2026
Alisha Pasi  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  FICC & Equities
30 Oct 2026
29 October 2026 | 6:17PM EDT | Research | Credit Strategy
• Our review of aggregate data of the largest U.S. corporate credit mutual funds and ETFs confirms that
performance has been challenged over the past few months. This is most visible in HY, which has not
benefited as much (vs. IG) from the recent rally in rates. And when isolating to the largest actively
managed funds, we find that performance over the past month has been uniformly challenging across IG
and HY (vs. benchmark returns).
• The split between AI and non-AI risk makes correlation an increasingly important consideration for asset
allocators, as industry diversification can help insulate portfolios from further AI-related supply
indigestion. In IG, Utilities, Financials, and Healthcare/Pharma have shown lower correlation to AI-related
spreads, while Energy also stands out in HY. Adding these new ‘defensive’ sectors to portfolios is a
prudent risk management tool, in our view.
• The HY market has traded in three parts: AI-related, performing HY, and distressed. We remain neutral
on HY AI-related credit, particularly project finance structures. In the distressed segment, we remain
underweight CCCs, as macro and refinancing stresses are unlikely to abate in the near term. Within
performing credit, we expect BBs to absorb the brunt of future AI-related financing (and potential duration
headwinds if rates drift higher once more) and prefer to own carry in the B-rated cohort instead. That said,
we expect dispersion within single-Bs to rise, as there is little room for error when it comes to
fundamentals as we discussed recently.
• We expect compression in non-AI IG and continue to favor BBBs over their higher rated peers. For
crossover investors, we prefer moving down in capital structure seniority (to capture a subordination
spread premium) over moving down in credit quality. Corporate hybrids and the subordinated debt of U.S.
Banks both screen as attractive, in our view.
• EUR credit technicals have (so far) benefited from less AI-related issuance. However, elevated energy
prices and, more recently, sovereign and fiscal risks have offset this tailwind, pushing spreads wider. This
combination of headwinds underpins our preference for USD over EUR credit. We remain underweight
French Banks but are constructive on the broader Banks sector in the region.
Source: Goldman Sachs Research, Credit Strategy (Amanda Lynam, Sara Grut, Shamshad Ali), circulated by Alisha Pasi, FICC &
Equities, Marquee, as of 30 Oct 2026.
