Magna reported 3Q adjusted EBIT and EPS above the Street. The 
company lowered its CY26 revenue and adjusted EBIT margin 
guidance at the midpoint citing softer China production and mix, 
but it maintained the midpoint of its EPS guidance (aided in part by 
lower net interest expense and a lower tax rate). 
Specifically, 3Q revenue/adjusted EBIT/adjusted EPS was $10.1

guidance at the midpoint is $10.2 bn/$745 mn/7.3%/$1.93 which

Positively, the company showed continued strong operational 
execution (as evidenced by the ~90 bp increase in operating margin

Results 
Magna reported 3Q26 revenue of $10.1 bn (down 8% qoq, down 4% yoy, and down 2% 
organically), compared to GS at $10.2 and the Street (FactSet) at $10.1 bn. Magna 
stated it grew 0.5% over its weighted markets in 3Q (with growth over market of 1% 
when excluding Complete Vehicles). 
Non-GAAP EBIT was $683 mn, compared to the Street at $623 mn and GS at $611 mn, 
with a total company adjusted EBIT margin of 6.8% (compared to GS at 6.0% and the 
Street at 6.2%). 
Non-GAAP EPS was $1.83, compared to GS at $1.51 and the Street at $1.58. 
Adjusted FCF was about $584 mn and Magna used $129 mn for dividends, and 
repurchased $529 mn of shares in the quarter. Management also noted that it 
completed its existing buyback authorization in October (purchasing the remaining 1.3 
mn shares) and its board approved a new NCIB for 25.8 mn shares. 
Key commentary from the call 
On supporting AVs, the company highlighted its upfitting work for Waymo in Arizona, 
and it is now doing upfitting on two new vehicle models.  
On the cost environment, Magna discussed that the environment got incrementally 
worse both in 3Q and 4Q vs its prior outlook, and that it would expect a similar size 
headwind in 4Q as 3Q (input costs were a 50 bp headwind in 3Q). The company noted 
steel and aluminum were pretty well covered with resell programs or have a pass through 
mechanism. For resins, management commented that ~40% is protected through 
indexes and that the company would have to negotiate with customers for recoveries for 
the balance. Lastly, on DRAM and electronic components, the company mentioned that 
this is where it is seeing the largest cost increases, and recoveries would largely need to 
be negotiated with customers. 
On operational excellence, management commented that for the year it is tracking 
toward 50 bp of improvement which is better than its initial expectation of 35-40 bp. 
The company also commented that it is looking to capture another 35-40 bp next year. 
Outlook 
Magna noted that its updated guidance primarily reflects lower expected vehicle 
production in China and the unfavorable impacts of program mix. 
For CY26, Magna adjusted its light vehicle production expectation to 15.0 mn units in 
North America (unchanged), 30.4 mn in China (vs. 31.2 mn prior), and 16.8 mn in Europe 
(unchanged). 
Magna now expects CY26 revenue of $41.2 bn-$42.0 bn ($41.6 bn and down 1% yoy at 
the midpoint) from $41.3 bn-$42.5 bn previously ($41.9 bn and about flat yoy at the 
midpoint) prior, which compares to GS prior at $42.1 bn and the Street at $41.9 bn. 
For the full year management noted that it now expects to grow about in line with its 
weighted vehicle production (with GoM of ~1% excluding complete vehicles) vs. 0-2%

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
