Complicated picture 
◼ 
TTF breaching EUR100/MWh just one day before the ECB policy decision 
complicates duration outlook.  
◼ 
Questions or reference to EGB spreads will be under the most scrutiny 
during today’s ECB meeting and Q&A. 
◼ 
There were no surprises from the October FOMC meeting, where officials 
held rates steady. Treasury curve steepened as investors interpreted Fed 
Chair Kevin Warsh's comments dovishly. 
European natural gas has surged past EUR100/MWh for the first time since the 
2022 energy crisis, driven by escalating geopolitical tensions in Iran that threaten 
regional supply chains. This spike compounds existing Eurozone inflation concerns 
and puts pressure on the ECB's policy path. EUR rates face modest near-term 
headwinds as markets await today's ECB decision, where no rate hike is expected. 
The macro backdrop remains decidedly hawkish on inflation. Surging energy costs 
keep upside risks intact, even as growth headwinds and rising sovereign spreads 
temper near-term rate hike expectations and weigh on longer-term market 
sentiment. 
Question or references to EGB spreads will command particular attention in today's 
ECB meeting, as OAT valuations remain under scrutiny while maintaining elevated 
beta status. Market headlines continue to focus on France's unfolding budget 
process, which carries significant implications for fiscal credibility. Yesterday's 
statement from French Finance Minister Roland Lescure signalled the 
government's commitment to deficit reduction, with plans to deploy all available 
tools, including article 49.3 or “Budget d’ordonnance”. This headline helped tighten 
OAT spreads relative to Bunds intraday. The confluence of fiscal uncertainty, 
elevated spreads and ECB policy decisions creates a complex backdrop for fixed-
income markets, requiring careful navigation of both near-term volatility and longer-
term structural risks. 
There were no surprises from the October FOMC meeting, where officials kept the 
policy rate in a target range of 3.75-4.00%. Treasury curve steepened as investors 
interpreted Fed Chair Warsh's comments dovishly. The market is now pricing a 
21bp Fed hike premium for the December meeting and 47bp through next March, 
roughly in line with our house view of two 25bp hikes by March.
