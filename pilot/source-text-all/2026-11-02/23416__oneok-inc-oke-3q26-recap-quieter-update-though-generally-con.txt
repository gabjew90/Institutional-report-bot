ONEOK Inc. (OKE): 3Q26 Recap: Quieter Update, Though Generally 
Constructive Tone on Volume Growth
29 October 2026 | 6:26PM EDT
Following 3Q26 earnings for ONEOK (OKE, Neutral), we revise our estimates. The 
quarter was quiet with segment level performance broadly in line: stronger refined 
products margin (blending upside) offset lower NGL expectations (lower Permian 
rates) vs. our model. FY26 guidance increased to $8.5-8.6b (from $8.2-8.5b) on 
stronger YTD results and initial Brazos contributions ($75m). Implied 4Q results 
screen low, down -2% on headline EBITDA and flat excluding one-time benefits, 
though commodity and optimization assumptions appear conservative. Color on 
2027 EBITDA growth was generally helpful - notably commentary on stronger Brazos 
volumes and a larger, higher-priced hedging position, providing upside to initial 
expectations alongside full-year organic and inorganic growth contributions.  Other 
updates were generally quiet, though OKE announced a 245 kb/d NGL pipeline 
extension into the Northern Delaware (we look for more color on contracting but 
assume some incremental contributions now). Overall, following the release, we raise 
our EBITDA estimates through 2030 by ~1% and are now ~1-3% ahead of consensus. 
Focus remains on capital allocation following enhanced balance sheet flexibility 
post-Apollo equity sale and OKE’s updated sub-3.25x leverage mandate - we look 
for continued used of capital, including larger growth project announcements, 
potential shareholder return acceleration, and M&A. Overall, we seek more 
confidence in underlying performance and follow through of ongoing asset 
connectivity before underwriting significant incremental growth in our model above 
our current ~5% EBITDA CAGR.  
Key Takeaways 
 
 
Earnings Summary 
3Q26 operationally in line. OKE reported 3Q26 EBITDA of $2,238m vs. GSe of 
$2,191m and consensus of $2,165m. Refined Products and Crude outperformed on 
blending upside, offsetting lower NGL rates (higher ethane recovery in Permian). As 
expected, gas pipeline EBITDA declined QoQ on limited Waha to Katy spread upside. 
The G&P segment was also in line with expectations with stronger fee-based margin 
offset by lower POP (hedging). The quarter also benefited from $53m of transaction 
and minority equity sale adjustments.

Key Takeaways 
2026 guide increased, though implies declines for 4Q26. OKE raised FY26 EBITDA 
guidance to $8.5-8.6b from $8.2-8.5b on YTD performance and initial Brazos Midstream 
contributions. FY guidance implies 4Q26 EBITDA of ~$2,194m, down -2% QoQ or flat 
when excluding $53m of transaction and debt benefit recognized in 3Q. The incremental 
contribution from Brazos (+$75m gross or +$50m net of transaction costs), and the 
commissioning of projects in the quarter (Medford Phase I, Cutter I, and Cassidy I) are 
therefore assumed to be offset by declines elsewhere. We see potential for 4Q26 
outperformance against what appears to be conservative baseline commodity 
assumptions, though watch for the implied run-rate performance of OKE’s underlying 
business. Management pointed to ~$100m of stronger base business performance vs. 
2Q26 expectations, though we believe the majority of this has already been recognized 
in 3Q26.  
Increasingly Positive on 2027+ Growth Runway. Management delivered a constructive 
2027 outlook:  full-year organic and inorganic contributions should drive meaningful 
earnings expansion alongside a supportive upstream production backdrop. ONEOK 
noted broad-based volume growth led by ongoing Permian outperformance from 
producer efficiency gains, with resilient activity across the Barnett Shale and 
Mid-Continent. OKE also flagged an upsized 1H27 hedge book secured at higher 
realized prices than 2026, which we believe could help offset the year-over-year 
normalization of Waha basis spreads. The call pointed to higher growth expectations 
than heading into the quarter, reinforced by legacy Brazos processing throughput 
tracking ahead of initial underwriting. Over the longer term, management reaffirmed its 
mid- to high-single-digit EBITDA CAGR over the next five to seven years, expressing 
increased confidence in achieving the upper end of that range following the Brazos 
integration. While we are increasingly positive on the 2027+ outlook, we continue to 
monitor upcoming project announcements and base-business volume durability form 
here to help support EBITDA growth expectations.  
Brazos. Initial Brazos 2026 EBITDA contributions came in below expectations due 
processing capacity in-service schedules, but point to a more meaningful step-up 
entering 2027. As noted above, legacy Brazos G&P throughput is outperforming 
management’s initial acquisition underwriting, supported by robust customer drilling 
activity.  As OKE advances system connectivity, its expects to unlock operational 
redundancies built into the Brazos system and pursue commercial optimization to 
maximize operating leverage across the Midland basin. Cassidy I and II are both 
expected to ramp quickly following commercial in-service (by YE26 and 3Q27, 
respectively), and constructive customer feedback on future activity signals volume 
upside for both facilities -  driving higher throughput and associated NGL volumes across 
OKE’s integrated value chain. The transaction implied ~$590 million in 2027 EBITDA 
(~7.5x acquisition multiple, inclusive of synergies); we look for potential upside to this 
baseline on the steepness of the volume ramp following the Cassidy I startup in 4Q26.  
Upside on NGL growth. Management was most constructive on NGL supply growth, 
emphasizing that system connectivity and processing plant additions through 2028 
should deliver substantial feedstock growth. Beyond legacy Brazos and OKE volume 
contributions, OKE also highlighted rising third-party NGL flows: management 
announced a 245 MBbl/d NGL takeaway expansion into the Northern Delaware by 3Q27, 
upsized from initial plans in response to strong producer demand. Critically,

allocation flexibility. Management affirmed a revised leverage philosophy of 3.25x net

high-return organic expansions and strategic M&A. On spending, 2027 capex is 
expected to be near $3b —trending below FY26 guidance of ~$3.5 billion, though still 
elevated as projects reach completion into 2028. Management framed $2.5 billion to 
$3.0 billion as its long-term normalized annual capex run-rate, dedicated to high-return 
organic projects that underpin its mid- to high-single-digit Adjusted EBITDA CAGR. On 
capital returns, OKE remains focused on dividend growth with opportunistic buybacks.  
M&A. Management maintained an appetite for accretive M&A, while noting the broader 
universe of attractive transactions has narrowed following recent sector consolidation. 
Priorities center on expanding NGL market share, with a preference for assets backed by 
strong producer counterparties and long-term acreage dedications - derisking future 
deployment. We continue to watch for incremental M&A from here as OKE maintains 
balance sheet flexibility.  
Updating Estimates 
 
 
We increase our estimates by 1% vs. prior. We forecast 2026 EBITDA of $8,678m vs. 
consensus of $8,524m and $8,613m prior. Our estimates reflect 3Q26 actuals and 
stronger refined products contributions. We increase our refined products volume 
expectations and product margin basis capture following commentary on the call. Our 
FY26 estimate remains just above 2026 guidance of $8.5-8.6b on a continued 
constructive commodity backdrop, though lower upside QoQ. This offsets lower EBITDA 
generation from the Brazos assets vs. our prior estimates, where we now assume a larger 
step up into 1Q27+. Our G&P, Pipelines, and NGL segment EBITDA decline vs. prior. For 
2027+, we see modest upside to our G&P segment estimates for better 3Q26 volumes 
and better products and crude EBITDA on better blending logistics costs (now reflecting 
commentary of ~10c as per the 3Q26 call). For the NGL segment, we now include the

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
