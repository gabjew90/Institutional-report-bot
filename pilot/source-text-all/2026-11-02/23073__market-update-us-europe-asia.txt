U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.28% | Nasdaq +0.27% | Dow +0.19%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher on Wednesday: the S&P 500 rose 0.28%, the Nasdaq 0.27%, the Dow 0.19% and the Russell 2000 0.41%. Breadth
was slightly negative. The AI and momentum trade outperformed for a second day, with semiconductors and memory largely higher on
strong chip earnings and an Oracle analyst day update guiding to much stronger free cash flow in FY30. Treasuries were mostly weaker with
the curve steepening and long-end yields up 5-6 bp. WTI crude settled up 2.9%, back above $100 a barrel, on more reports of likely military
action after the midterms.

The Fed left policy unchanged, as widely expected, with two dissents in favour of a hike, and Chair Warsh offered no forward guidance.
Long-term rates still backed up despite the lack of surprises. Market pricing implies about an 85% chance of a 25 bp December hike. The
September advance goods trade deficit widened to $134.9B versus $129B expected. PCE inflation and the first Q3 GDP estimate are due
Thursday.


Energy, money-centre banks, credit cards, investment banks and restaurants outperformed, while software, regional banks, insurers, online
brokers, transports, homebuilders, staples, media and telecom lagged. Vertiv rose 16.5% on a large beat and raised guidance, Wendy's
gained 15.2% after confirming a $1.4B take-private offer from Trian, and Oracle climbed 10.6% on its investor day outlook. F5 fell 20.4%
as billings and deferred revenue came in below consensus, Enphase fell 16.0% on weak US and France residential solar demand, and CoStar
fell 14.0% after net new bookings dropped 56% y/y.
Macro

Fed holds rates, with two dissents in favour of a hike
The Fed held rates steady at 3.75%-4.00% at its October meeting, as widely expected, following September's 25 bp hike. Cook and Hammack
dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike. The statement upgraded its assessment of economic activity to expanding at a strong pace from a solid
pace, while inflation was again described as elevated. The Fed removed September's language that its hike would support a timelier return
of inflation to 2%, saying instead that the accumulation of more information will help determine the appropriate policy stance. Early analyst
reactions called the statement largely as expected, though with a slightly hawkish lean. At the press conference, Warsh said the Fed will
change its FOMC schedule beginning next fall while retaining eight meetings per year. One 2027 meeting will focus on strategy and will not
include a policy-rate vote or statement, and Warsh expects six policy-setting meetings in 2028.

US reportedly warns Gulf allies of possible post-midterm strikes on Iran
Media reports said the US has told several Gulf countries to be prepared for a short, intense military campaign after next week's midterm
elections. The US is deploying an additional Patriot missile defense system from Texas to Israel, restocking munitions and preparing bomber
aircraft for potential strikes on Iran infrastructure and nuclear sites. The reports cautioned that Trump has not decided to go ahead but is
leaning toward an attack. Qatar and Pakistan are leading mediation efforts for a new peace deal between Iran, the US and Israel, with a
proposed draft establishing a 7-day timeline for confidence-building measures to resolve the conflict that began in February (Reuters).

Oracle targets over $30B FY29 free cash flow and self-funded growth
At its Investor Day, Oracle projected $30B in free cash flow for the fiscal year ending May 2029, compared with consensus of -$7.3B for
that year (CNBC, Reuters). It also projects $225B in revenue by FY30, a 35% compounded annual growth rate. Executives said they would
begin self-funding growth in FY29 and committed to limiting additional funding to $20B between FY27 and FY30, split equally between
equity and debt. Following the update, neoclouds including Coreweave also rallied on potential positive signaling around ROI dynamics,
cash conversion and the capex peak.

AI trade bounces as financing overhangs clear
The AI trade bounce has been the big story this week. The Nasdaq 100 posted back-to-back gains of more than 1% on Monday and Tuesday
for the first time in about three months, with semiconductors up about 6.5% and memory up about 7.5%. The bounce is largely attributed
to recent financing announcements, including Meta's nearly $100B capital raise, SpaceX's $40B GPU deal and Broadcom's $63B custom-
chip SPV. Anthropic's widely anticipated S-1 filing also helped, as it leaves the November IPO timeline intact while highlighting $650B+ in
compute commitments. Goldman Sachs noted hedge fund net exposure is among the lowest of the year, with the largest two-week institu-
tional tech liquidations on record.

Hyperscaler earnings put cloud growth and AI capex in focus
Alphabet and Microsoft report Wednesday after the bell, followed by Amazon on Thursday. After last week's Meta results, which showed
strong revenue but sold off on aggressive spending plans, the companies are under pressure to justify a combined $600B in projected annual
AI capex (Reuters, Bloomberg). Street previews highlight cloud backlog growth and conversion as a proxy for AI capacity and demand. Some
previews see possible Google Cloud outperformance versus consensus of about 89% y/y growth, Microsoft Azure growth re-accelerating
(consensus 44% y/y) as Copilot workloads grow, and AWS outpacing consensus growth of 39% y/y.

European Markets Recap
Germany +0.4% | France unch | UK +0.1%
Synopsis

European equities ended mostly higher in narrow trade, with the DAX up 0.4%, the FTSE 100 up 0.1% and the CAC unchanged. Trading
stayed in tight ranges ahead of the FOMC and the next round of hyperscaler earnings. The AI trade performed well again and tech earnings
read-throughs were upbeat, though some focus remained on a high bar. Bund yields rose 2 bp to 3.37%, while gilt yields fell about 2 bp after
the UK Budget. European natural gas prices stayed at multi-year highs near EUR100/MWh.

The UK Budget was relatively low-key, as expected. The OBR cut forecast fiscal headroom against the rules to £15B from £24B, cut its 2027
growth projection and raised net borrowing forecasts by £65B. Planned DMO gilt issuance for 2026-27 is £250.6B versus expectations of
£249.6B. The macro calendar was otherwise quiet, with German GfK consumer confidence a little weaker than forecast.

Autos and Energy led, while Chemicals, Media and Health Care lagged. Stellantis rose 15.4% on a quarterly adjusted EBIT beat and Genmab
gained 29.1% after petosemtamab met both primary endpoints in a Phase 3 head and neck cancer trial. Guidance quality mattered more
than headline beats: raised targets at Standard Chartered, Deutsche Bank and ASM International were rewarded, while guidance cuts at
Adyen and Sandoz were punished hardest. Symrise fell 10.2% after guiding to the lower half of its margin range and Logitech fell 10.8% as
supply constraints capped sales. UK banks and gambling firms rose as the Budget brought smaller tax hikes than feared, while UK home-
builders fell on a lack of clarity on the first-time buyer plan.
Macro

UK Budget draws limited reaction as fiscal headroom is cut to £15B
There were no surprises in Chancellor Healey's Budget. The fiscal watchdog OBR confirmed fiscal headroom of £15B versus £23.6B in
March, as expected, and cut its 2027 GDP growth forecast to 1.0% from 1.6%, anticipating recovery from 2028. Borrowing is forecast to fall
to 4.2% of GDP this year and to 1.8% in 2030-31, while debt will hit 83.8% next year and fall to 81.7% in 2031-32. Several cost-of-living
measures were confirmed, absorbed by a reduction in fiscal space and tax rises on banks, gambling firms and property investment and
savings. The bank surcharge will be 3% over 15 months, less onerous than expected, and the VAT increase for online gambling firms is 25%
versus a prior 20%. Market reaction was limited, with the UK 10-year yield off 3 bp just ahead of 5.30%, and the DMO raised its planned
medium-term gilt sales via syndication to £33.8B versus April's £20B.

BoE seen starting a short, precautionary tightening cycle in November
The FT reported that it expects the BoE to begin a short and precautionary tightening cycle in November, in line with a recent Reuters poll
in which a majority of economists expect a 25 bp hike to 4%, though there is uncertainty over the need for further increases given difficult
trade-offs. This contrasts with market pricing for up to four rate increases next year. The longer the energy shock persists, the harder it is
for policymakers to look through the acceleration in headline inflation, but Chief Economist Pill highlighted that the inflation shock from
2022 and 2023 is different to the one now being faced. The labor market is much weaker than in 2022-23, household consumption is weak
and business confidence surveys have deteriorated, and economists argue that soft demand, tighter financial conditions and one or two rate
hikes may be enough to limit inflation.

German consumer climate edges lower in November on inflation fears
Germany's GfK Consumer Climate Indicator for November slipped to -30.8 from -30.6 in October versus a -30.9 consensus, the weakest
reading since May and well below the -22.5 level of a year earlier (Reuters). Economic expectations rose for a sixth straight month to 2.1
from -3.4, turning positive for the first time since the Iran War, but income expectations edged down to -15.7 from -15.0 and willingness to
buy fell to -11.9 from -10.8. Price expectations moved further into positive territory at 1.0, consistent with a September inflation rate of
3.3%, the highest since December 2023, driven by higher fuel prices. Willingness to save declined to 17.2 from 21.5, providing some support
to the overall climate.

European gas prices hold near multi-year highs as winter risks stay elevated
Dutch TTF gas eased marginally but remains near its highest level since December 2022 amid persistent Middle East supply uncertainty
and a gradual shift to colder weather. Persian Gulf LNG exports averaged only about 30% of normal in October and Italy's Edison said
QatarEnergy cargoes will not resume until end-December (Reuters). European storage was about 74.9% as of the latest GIE data, well below
the five-year average, and sites are expected to move into net withdrawal mode imminently. Goldman Sachs said Dec-26 TTF at about
€95/MWh embeds a roughly 73% market-implied probability of a low Middle East LNG supply winter, up from 51% a week earlier, and
sees risks to the winter TTF outlook skewed to the upside.

European equities rotate from AI leaders to defensives
The Stoxx 600 rebounded in October, up about 1.5%, but analysts flag pronounced internal rotation. Morgan Stanley noted that a basket of
European AI enablers, including semiconductors, certain hardware and construction stocks, has underperformed MSCI Europe by 25%
since the June peak, while banks are down 12% from their August high and medtech, life sciences, pharma and tobacco stocks are up 14%.
Bank of America remains negative on the Stoxx 600, projecting about 10% further downside by Q2 2027, and Citi flagged rising bearish
bets. More constructive views remain: Rothschild expects Stoxx 600 EPS growth of about 19.1% this year and 8.9% in 2027, with the index
at about 13.9x earnings, and UBS forecasts about 15% earnings growth in both 2026 and 2027.

Synopsis

Asian equities ended mostly higher on Wednesday. The Kospi closed up 6.63%, the Nikkei 225 rose 1.08%, the Shanghai Composite rose

Macro

SK Hynix Q3 beats forecasts and sees memory shortfall lasting through 2030
SK Hynix Q3 operating profit of KRW83.3T beat consensus of KRW77.5T, with revenue of KRW104.6T also ahead of the KRW99T forecast.
DRAM (DDR5 and HBM4 ramp) and NAND were above consensus, underpinned by a 20% q/q increase in ASP, while the PC and smartphone
segment was weighed down by lower shipments amid high memory costs. The company repeated that it expects the memory supply gap to
last through 2030, and said it has signed LTAs with 14 key customers locking in volumes corresponding to more than 50% of production
capacity in 2027 and 2028. It outlined a KRW20T buyback and a total cash dividend of KRW14T, bringing total return for 2027 to around
KRW100T ($75B), and anticipates a significant increase in FY27 capex.

China set for a flurry of government bond issuance to finish the year
Bloomberg cited estimates from China Chengxin International Credit Rating and Industrial Securities pointing to net central and govern-
ment bond issuance of at least CNY2.2T ($330B) in the final two months of the year, the second-largest amount on record for the period
after 2024. The comments implied a catch-up in issuance that had fallen behind schedule, and the influx of funding is expected to stem
declines in infrastructure investment. The MOF said last week that CNY550B in remaining quotas for local government bonds earmarked
for project construction will be fully issued and used by year-end. November may be the actual peak in issuance on seasonal factors, and
the liquidity impact is expected to be smoothed by PBOC open-market operations.

Hotter Australian CPI lifts November RBA hike odds to almost 80%
Australian headline inflation rose to 4.3% y/y in September from 4.0% in August, above the 4.2% consensus, while trimmed mean inflation
was unchanged at an in-line 3.6%. Over Q3, trimmed mean inflation rose to 3.7% from 3.6% in Q2, also in line with consensus. September's
rise was driven by a sharp rise in fuel prices. In prepared remarks to a Senate committee, RBA Governor Bullock said upside inflation risks
are materializing and stressed the importance of preventing higher inflation from becoming embedded in expectations. Deputy Governor
Hauser said inflation is still too high. The market reaction was hawkish, with the Australian yield curve bear flattening and November rate
hike odds rising to almost 80% from 70% before the data.

RBNZ unexpectedly leaves the OCR unchanged at 2.75%
The RBNZ left the OCR unchanged at 2.75% against consensus for a 25 bp increase to 3.00%, in a close 4-3 vote. While the MPC agreed
further OCR increases are likely, they would be conditional on the outlook, and the board division stemmed from a debate over raising at
this meeting or waiting until December. More concern was expressed about the global economy, with members seeing significant risks
including geopolitical events, high energy prices, rising debt and AI investment uncertainty. The data drove a dovish market reaction, with
the 2Y yield falling more than 10 bp and the kiwi dollar down more than 1% to a two-week low.

Reliance's Jio Platforms lists in India and becomes the second-largest stock
Jio Platforms listed in Mumbai in India's biggest ever IPO, with shares up around 21% by early afternoon, creating the country's second-
largest company by market capitalization. The stock opened at 1,358/sh versus an offer price of 1,119/sh, valuing the company at around
INR12.5T ($131.5B) compared with parent Reliance Industries at $170.1B, and Bharti Airtel is now third just behind Jio. Bloomberg anal-
ysis noted Jio's PE ratio of 31x is comparable to Bharti's 27x despite the latter having pretax income around 12% higher, indicating a
national champion premium on the first day. The IPO finished more than 22 times oversubscribed, with institutional demand more than
70 times oversubscribed.
