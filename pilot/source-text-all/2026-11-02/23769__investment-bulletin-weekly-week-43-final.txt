Central banks on hold will allow a focus on 
Tech results 
___________ 
 
A slew of earnings announcements this week from IT hardware producers across 
Asia and the US and from one of the US hyperscalers will test our thesis from last 
week that the AI capex spending outlook remains largely intact. To recap, we 
argued that although recently higher financing costs may trim 2027 – 28 capex 
estimates at the margin, growth is likely to remain rapid. We also argued that 
recent falls in markets – Nasdaq and Kospi are down 6% and 21% from their 
October highs – have priced much of this risk and reduced long positioning. 
Against this background we find it encouraging that GPU rental rates for H100 
and B200 GPU racks have climbed to new highs while rates for the now six-year-
old A100 GPU have bounced back to late September levels. We also note that 
Google has increased rollout of its AI agent framework it announced on October 
8 in what appears to be a response to Meta’s highly downloaded Muse release on 
September 8. We view this as important for the AI infrastructure outlook for two 
key reasons. 
1) Token use and so compute requirements should rise as consumers 
increase adoption and use of these AI agent products for management of 
daily life.  
2) These releases suggest to us that US hyperscaler desire to compete 
across the AI product space remains intense and that implies continued 
aggressive infrastructure spending. 
As IT hardware producers report we expect markets to focus on growth in order 
backlogs. Within the hyperscalers, market focus is likely to be on revenue and 
cash flow generation. We agree with the general idea that hyperscaler share 
prices are likely to trade inversely with credit spreads until they begin to show 
revenues from AI beginning to catch up to capex expenditure.

New risk to duration from central bank pauses 
The market expects the US Federal Reserve (Fed), the European Central Bank, 
and the Bank of Japan to keep policy rates unchanged at this week’s respective 
meetings. 
Focusing on the US Fed, market pricing for Thursday’s meeting rests mainly on 
speeches by New York Fed President Williams, Vice Chairman Jefferson, and San 
Francisco Fed President Daly arguing against a rate hike this week and the fact 
that Warsh did nothing to push back against these views in a subsequent speech. 
This has contributed to the rally in the US 10-year Treasury yield from 5.3% in 
early October to 4.97% last Friday. 
If the Fed follows through with this guidance and keeps the Fed Funds rate 
unchanged this week we think it will shift Treasury market risk back to sell-offs 
and higher yields until the December 10 FOMC meeting.  
A key reason is that renewed tension in the Middle East is driving energy prices 
higher. Iranian attacks on ships in the Persian Gulf rose to a new high of 28 this 
month, up from only 16 in August and 15 in September. Crucially, Houthi attacks 
on Saudi infrastructure have added to risk premia in market prices for oil. The 
combination of greatly increased oil shipping logistics costs and market risk 
premia have added about $30bbl to the oil price from pre-war levels even for the 
now roughly unchanged output levels.

Yet, we think the clear risk is that energy prices could rise further. Multiple press 
reports suggest that the US Administration will resume kinetic attacks on Iran 
after the US Congressional mid-term elections. Similarly, it seems highly likely 
that fighting between the Houthi’s and Saudis will escalate before any truce 
agreement emerges.  
If this scenario does emerge, the Fed’s failure to have hiked in October could lead 
markets to perceive it slipping behind inflation. The chart below shows that the 
S&P Global US PMI prices paid index rose to a new cycle high in October - driven 
in large part by higher energy and transportation costs - and that this implies 
inflation risk.  
 
 
The Fed will also incur the risk that US economic data prove robust, supporting 
inflation. The Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow estimates US GDP grew 3.5% and the 
Bloomberg consensus is for 3.2% versus estimates of potential GDP growth of 
close to 2%. Unsurprisingly, labour market indicators have been robust. Initial 
and continuing unemployment insurance claims have fallen to close to cyclical

lows and although the S&P US PMI Employment Index dipped slightly in October 
it remained at its highest level since July 2022, a time of rapid payroll growth. 
 
Against this background the risk we see is that employment growth in October 
and November proves strong enough to pull the unemployment rate lower. The 
U-6 unemployment rate, essentially the US underemployment rate, has begun to 
fall. This suggests labour market tightening on the margin that may begin to pull 
up wage growth. If this were to happen it would create a new, negative dynamic 
to US inflation being persistently too high.

Market Review 
___________ 
Global equities were broadly weaker over the week, with the MSCI AC World Index declining 0.9%. US equities were also lower, with the S&P 
500 and Nasdaq falling 0.5% and 0.7% respectively. Emerging Markets lost 2.6%, while Asia Pacific ex Japan declined 2.2%. Japan was down 
1.7% for the week. 
Gold fell 4.4%, while the US dollar index was flat at 0.0%. Bond markets were mixed. Global Corporate Bonds rose 0.1%, Global High Yield 
Bonds declined 0.4%, and Global Government Bonds gained 0.3%.
