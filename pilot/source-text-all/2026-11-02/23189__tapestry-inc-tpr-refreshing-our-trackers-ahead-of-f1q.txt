Tapestry Inc. (TPR): Refreshing our trackers ahead of F1Q
28 October 2026 | 6:40PM MDT
We update our estimates ahead of F1Q27 earnings. We remain constructive on TPR 
but acknowledge the near-term pace and cadence of stabilization of Coach NA 
growth momentum is critical for the stock.  
Bottom line: Our recent tracker updates point to some moderation in Coach brand 
momentum. Coach continues to screen favorably across several brand health metrics 
but moderation has been observed through September. Price and markdown data 
remain generally supportive, with average product prices above prior-year levels and 
promotional activity still below historical peak levels despite a recent uptick in 
promotional depth. We continue to view Coach as one of the strongest brands in the 
category, while Kate Spade remains in the early stages of a turnaround and is due to 
remain a drag on consolidated growth. We believe the cadence and pace of 
stabilization of Coach North America growth momentum remains imperative for 
near-term stock performance given recent data moderation.  
Key questions heading into the quarter: (1) The sustainability of Coach’s growth 
algorithm as AUR and unit growth normalize from FY26 levels; (2) Management’s 
latest views on China demand and the contribution from digital and marketplace 
channels; (3) The magnitude of tariff, freight, and sourcing headwinds alongside 
planned mitigation; (4) Evidence of progress in the Kate Spade turnaround and 
management’s latest views on the path back to sustainable profitable growth; and (5) 
Sustainability of sales momentum in Greater China as comps toughen. 
Our brand momentum trackers remain supportive, though trends have 
moderated: Google search interest remains ahead of competitors, with continued 
interest across Tabby, Brooklyn, and Empire. However, Y/Y search interest suggests 
that the strength is moderating from prior levels, with several franchises showing 
moderated interest vs. fall calendar 2025. While Coach’s net purchase intent and net 
promoter scores moderated through September, both metrics remain above the 
broader peer set. Store traffic was positive Y/Y in four of the five months since May, 
including a return to growth in September following an August decline, while web 
traffic remained volatile across Coach’s full-price and outlet banners. Bloomberg 
Second Measure data pointed to softer summer sales trends, though adjusted 
observed sales returned to +15% Y/Y in September. Recent sales growth has been 
supported by both transaction growth and positive average transaction values, 
which is directionally consistent with management’s expectation that both units and 
AUR remain contributors to growth. Our promotional trackers suggest higher Y/Y 
prices in aggregate, with lower discount breadth but higher discount depth worth

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
