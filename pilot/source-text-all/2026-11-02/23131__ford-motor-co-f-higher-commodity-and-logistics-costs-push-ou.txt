Key takeaways and implications 
We believe that this was a mixed report with Ford reporting 3Q26 
revenue and EBIT ahead of the Street (FactSet), and raising the 
midpoint of its full-year EBIT guidance, but the midpoint of the 
implied 4Q26 EBIT guidance is below the Street, driven by higher 
commodity costs. The company is seeing increased costs for 
commodities, logistics, and memory that management expects, in 
total, to be a ~$2 bn yoy headwind in 2027, and management 
currently expects roughly flat yoy EBIT for 2027.  This implies 2027 
EBIT of ~$10.75 bn, compared to the Street at ~$11.2 bn.  
Specifically, 3Q revenue/EBIT/EPS were $49.5 bn/$2.7 bn/$0.49, 
compared to GS at $51.1 bn/$2.3 bn/$0.38 and the Street at $49.1 
bn/$2.4 bn/$0.41. Ford raised its 2026 EBIT guidance to 
$10.5-$11.0 bn from $10.0-$11.0 bn prior, which compares to GS 
and the Street at $10.6 bn. The implied 4Q adjusted EBIT guidance is 
~$2.1 bn at the midpoint, which compares to GS prior at $2.4 bn 
and the Street at $2.2 bn. 
We believe that Ford is executing well on reducing 
structural/warranty costs and driving better profits via mix, but the 
underlying cost environment is incrementally challenging. This was 
evidenced by the company noting that it expects 2027 EBIT to be 
about in line with 2026, with cost headwinds offsetting positive 
drivers such as the Oakville F-Series ramp. While we now assume 
flattish EBIT in 2027, we do expect improvement in 2028, in part as 
Ford ramps its Energy business and its UEV platform. We also 
believe that Ford is doing well to grow its high-margin software & 
physical services business, which the company said is now about 
10% of total revenue. Our Neutral rating reflects what we think is a 
muted volume environment (i.e., the US market being at a relatively 
normalized levels, and a challenging competitive environment in 
international markets), and a premium multiple relative to peers.

Results 
Ford reported 3Q26 total company revenue of $49.5 bn, compared to GS at $51.1 bn 
and the Street (FactSet) at $49.1 bn. 
Total wholesale automotive units were 1,049k units in the quarter, below GS at 1,103k 
units. We estimate Ford Blue ASP at approximately $46.2k (down 1% quarter over 
quarter and up 6% year over year), Model e ASP at approximately $36.0k (down 2% 
quarter over quarter and up 1% year over year), and Ford Pro ASP at approximately 
$51.8k (up 3% quarter over quarter and up 4% year over year). 
Total company adjusted EBIT was $2.7 bn, or 5.4% of sales, compared to GS at $2.3 bn 
(4.4% of sales) and the Street at $2.4 bn (5.0% of sales). Ford attributed the yoy increase 
in EBIT to better volume/mix (~$0.2 bn), pricing (~$0.1 bn), and lower regulatory 
expenses and higher software and services contributions (~$0.4 bn), partly offset by 
~$0.7 bn in higher costs. 
Non-GAAP operating EPS was $0.49 compared to GS at $0.38 and the Street at $0.41. 
Adjusted FCF was $4.7 bn in the quarter. 
Key commentary from the call 
Auto-adjacent businesses (including software, services, and parts) were ~10% of total 
revenue in the quarter and the company expects the growth in these businesses to 
offset certain macro headwinds expected at Ford Credit in 2027. 
On Ford Energy, the company remains on track to bring the full 20 GWh of capacity 
online by the end of next year. Ford expects 2028 capacity to be fully committed by 
1Q27, with negotiations for 2029 and 2030 capacity already underway. 
The Ford Fathom electric pickup truck still remains on track to launch in 4Q27, with 
pre-orders expected to begin in 1Q27. The starting price will be $28,350, and the lineup 
will extend into the high-$30K range. 
On F-Series, management noted that added Super Duty capacity at Oakville is expected 
to roughly offset planned production downtime for the next-gen F-150 in 2027. 
Ford expects higher Model e investment in 2027 relative to 2026 related to ramping its 
UEV platform (including the Ford Fathom) and Ford Energy, as well as its European 
partnerships (including with Geely and Renault). The company’s 2029 target for Model e 
breakeven remains unchanged, but Ford noted that progress toward that target will not 
be linear. 
Ford expects commodities, memory, and logistics, to be a $2 bn yoy headwind in 2027. 
The company is looking to offset this through revenue and mix optimization, 
auto-adjacent revenue growth (including software and parts), and cost-saving 
initiatives.  
Outlook  
Ford updated its CY26 EBIT guidance to $10.5-$11.0 bn (from $10.0-$11.0 bn), which 
compares to GS prior and the Street at $10.6 bn.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring
