FX Daily: Dollar holds gains into
midterms
At the start of a very busy week for FX markets, the dollar is largely
holding onto recent gains. In focus are the US midterms, where betting
markets attach a 70% probability to a clean sweep for the Democrats.
Away from politics, we'll receive updates on the US jobs market this
week and are expecting rate hikes in the UK, Australia and Sweden
The US midterm elections are set to dominate a busy week in FX. A clean sweep for the Democrats is
expected, but the macro story can keep the dollar supported
USD: Contemplating the midterms
The dollar is fractionally lower at the start of the week. It may be taking its cue from oil prices,
which are off 2-3% on reports that US President Donald Trump wants to let diplomacy run its
course with Iran. That reverses Friday's sentiment, where some had positioned for a fresh
attack this weekend. Please see our latest thoughts on the oil market here.  
In focus this week are the US midterm elections. Betting websites give the Democrats a
70-72% chance of taking both the House and the Senate. One could make the case that the
dollar should come lower on such an outcome, since it would likely lead to gridlock on the debt
ceiling, a prolonged government shutdown next year and could encourage President Trump to
pursue an even more activist foreign policy agenda. Many of those views are speculative,

however. What is not speculative is the US economy posting a strong 3.4% quarter-on-quarter
annualised growth rate in the third quarter, inflation remaining above target for a sixth year in
a row, and the Federal Reserve readying to tighten again. 
Feeding into the US macro debate this week will be ISM business surveys and jobs data.
Business surveys are expected to show continued optimism, along with high price pressures.
And Friday's October jobs report is expected to show a modest rebound after a soft September
figure. Barring a wholly disastrous report on Friday, we doubt the jobs data really moves the
needle on the Fed tightening story. Price data is more important here.
There will be continued focus on the tech sell-off, however. Apple reports after the market
close today. The 9% Nasdaq sell-off last month has driven a softening in short-dated US rates,
and any further large sell-off in this sector is probably the biggest risk to long dollar
positioning.
Expect DXY to continue trading around 102.00, and we do not see a strong case for a
correction lower.

EUR: Fitch puts the boot in
Unwelcome news for French politicians late Friday came in the form of an unscheduled ratings
review from Fitch. It shifted its outlook on French sovereign debt from stable to negative and
promised another ratings review by the end of February. It currently rates France as A+.
Reasons for the outlook shift are familiar by now, but they centre on the government's failure
to improve public finances at a time of weak growth. Let's see whether this unscheduled move
puts much pressure on the OAT-Bund spread today. We would think that the news limits the
potential for EUR/USD to rebound on softer energy prices. 
We suspect there will be more EUR/USD sellers in the 1.1200/1250 area should it make it that
far. The news should also keep EUR/CHF pressured towards Friday's lows at 0.9180/85. 
Chris Turner
GBP: 25bp BoE rate hike fully priced for Thursday
Sterling is trading reasonably well. Helping it has been the view that high energy prices leave
the Bank of England no alternative but to hike rates on Thursday. A 25bp rate hike to 4.00%
looks like a done deal. Upside economic surprises, UK fiscal policy seemingly heading in the
right direction and warmer ties with the EU all seem to be helping sterling too. Notably,
speculators are still running reasonably large short positions in sterling, which suggests this
period of sterling outperformance can continue.

CEE: Central bank divergence meets renewed FX pressure
With the start of a new month, the CEE narrative is becoming more attractive once again.
Today, PMI data for the region will be released. Since the onset of the US-Iran conflict, these
figures have either remained stagnant or trended upward, indicating that the economies are
currently showing strong resilience to the energy shock.
Tomorrow, we will see Turkey’s inflation figures for October. Our estimates suggest a monthly
acceleration from 1.8% to 2.4%, while the year-on-year figure is expected to tick lower to
29.6%.
On Wednesday, Hungary’s industrial production data and the Czech Republic’s October
inflation figures will be published. We anticipate an acceleration in headline inflation from 2.5%
to 2.6%, and a further rise in core inflation from 3.1% to 3.3%. The National Bank of Poland is
also expected to keep rates unchanged at 3.75% and present a new forecast. We do not
anticipate the first rate hike until the first quarter of 2027.
On Thursday, the Czech National Bank will follow, likely raising rates by another 25bp to 4.00%
while also presenting a new forecast.
Trading on Friday saw a return of risk-off sentiment, following a brief period of relief after the
Fed and European Central Bank meetings. The combination of rising energy prices and a
stronger dollar globally, alongside generally dovish central banks within the CEE region, will
continue to exert pressure on local currencies.
We maintain our dovish bias for this week as well. EUR/PLN has returned to the 4.400 level
following a brief dip below 4.390. Should the NBP confirm a dovish stance this week, we could
see the pair test new highs above 4.410. In line with our expectations, EUR/CZK has returned to
the 24.300–350 range, which we consider fair for the time being. Although we anticipate a
hawkish stance from the CNB, the bar is high given that 100bp of tightening is already priced
in and the koruna has a tendency to weaken slightly following CNB meetings. We could see
the pair move slightly above 24.350 again.
Frantisek Taborsky
