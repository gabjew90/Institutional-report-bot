Overview of US Consumer Credit Trends 
 
 
Non-mortgage delinquencies improved y/y in September. Delinquency rates, which 
are among the most reliable indicators of consumer credit health, improved y/y across 
non-mortgage categories. The 60+ day bankcard delinquency rate declined to 2.74% 
from 2.83% in the prior year, but remained above the 2.50% long-term average since 
2009. The 60+ day personal loan delinquency rate decreased to 3.26% from 3.28% and 
remained below the 3.76% long-term average. The 60+ day auto loan delinquency rate 
declined to 1.50% from 1.57%, but remained well above the 1.02% long-term average. 
Continued y/y improvement in delinquencies could support a gradual easing of 
underwriting standards and benefit credit origination volumes. 
Mortgage delinquencies remain low. In contrast to card and auto delinquency rates, 
which remain above their respective long-term averages, mortgage delinquencies 
remain benign and well below their historical average, reflecting tighter mortgage 
underwriting standards since the Global Financial Crisis. The 90+ day mortgage 
delinquency rate increased to 1.01% in September from 0.73% in the prior year, but 
remained well below the 2.47% long-term average since 2009. Low mortgage 
delinquencies indicate limited risk of a broad wave of defaults, though elevated 
mortgage rates, limited housing inventory and high home prices continue to constrain 
housing affordability and purchase activity. 
Consumer credit drivers remain mixed. Broader indicators of consumer credit health 
present a mixed backdrop. The US unemployment rate increased to 4.2% in September 
from 4.1% in August, but remained below the 5.6% long-term average since 1990. 
Household debt represented 57.8% of nominal GDP in 2Q, below the 69.5% average 
since 2003, and debt service payments represented 11.1% of disposable income, below 
the 12.4% average since 2005. That said, real wages declined 0.7% y/y in September, 
indicating modest pressure on consumer purchasing power. Personal savings 
represented 4.1% of disposable income in August, below the 5.8% long-term average 
since 1998.

US Personal Loan Trends 
 
 
Personal loan originations increased double digits y/y. Personal loan account 
originations increased 10.9% y/y in July, the latest data available, accelerating from 9.9% 
growth in June, with prime originations up 7.4% and subprime originations up 17.0%.

year, and the near-prime delinquency rate rose to 1.21% from 1.09%.

Investment Conclusions 
 
 
Against a backdrop of uneven consumer credit conditions and elevated regulatory and 
competitive uncertainty across the mortgage credit ecosystem, we favor business 
models with pricing power, leading market positions, recurring revenue streams, durable 
earnings growth, idiosyncratic catalysts and strong incremental margins. We believe 
Buy-rated FICO best satisfies these criteria, supported by its entrenched position in 
credit decisioning, robust earnings growth and strategic response to evolving mortgage 
score distribution, though regulatory developments have increased risks to pricing, 
volumes and market share. 
Fair Isaac (FICO, Buy, $961 12M PT): FICO continues to generate robust Scores revenue 
growth, but regulatory and competitive developments have increased uncertainty 
around the durability of its mortgage economics. Mortgage origination revenue 
increased 97% y/y in F3Q despite low-single-digit industry volume growth, following 
127% growth in F2Q, and now represents 62% of Scores revenue. Broader VantageScore 
availability and FHFA’s planned unified mortgage pricing grid create potential market 
share risk for Classic FICO, and a possible transition to bi-merge could reduce mortgage 
score volumes. That said, the FHFA Director’s approval of FICO’s Mortgage Direct 
License Program establishes an alternative distribution channel, with signed agreements 
covering ~73% of reseller volume and potential coverage of ~90% if another large 
reseller signs. DLP also expands FICO’s pricing flexibility and could mitigate competitive 
and volume pressures from VantageScore, waterfalling and bi-merge. Within Software, 
Platform ARR growth accelerated to 62% y/y from 49% in the prior quarter, with 
Platform ARR exceeding non-Platform ARR for the first time, supported by new 
customer wins, expanded use cases and higher customer volumes. We believe FICO’s 
pricing power, DLP rollout and Software transformation can sustain robust revenue 
growth, EBITDA margin expansion and double-digit EPS growth, though greater 
regulatory and competitive uncertainty warrants a lower valuation multiple. 
Equifax (EFX, Neutral, $169 12M PT): EWS Verifier momentum remains strong, with 
diversified revenue growth of 13% y/y in 3Q, supported by record additions, pricing, new 
products, customer penetration and government wins. Government represents an 
increasingly visible 2027 catalyst, as EFX expects substantially all of the $150mn of new 
state ACV signed year-to-date to contribute in 2027, with new Medicaid and SNAP 
requirements increasing state engagement beyond signed contracts. VantageScore 
adoption is accelerating, with ~2,000 lenders using the score and volumes up >230% 
since April, though paid adoption remains limited. EFX’s outlook for 100 bps of ex-FICO 
EBITDA margin expansion reflects operating leverage and progress toward its $150mn 
multi-year AI productivity goal despite an $80mn mortgage revenue shortfall versus its 
prior outlook. That said, current run rates imply a high-single-digit decline in 2027 
mortgage market transactions, with the greatest pressure in 1H, and bi-merge presents 
~$80mn of annual revenue exposure before mitigation. We believe EFX’s valuation 
appropriately balances diversified growth and government and AI upside against 
sustained mortgage pressure and modest slowing in FI, auto and hiring activity. 
TransUnion (TRU, Neutral, $78 12M PT): TRU’s non-mortgage trends remain resilient, 
with company-wide revenue excluding mortgage growing ~5.5% y/y, auto increasing 
13% despite lower industry volumes and International delivering broad-based growth. 
Insurance is providing an increasingly durable source of non-credit growth, supported

by core credit and driving contracts that are ramping but have yet to reach their full 
revenue contribution. Mortgage remains the primary source of uncertainty, as higher 
rates drove a 16% y/y decline in 3Q inquiries. TRU expects mortgage inquiries to decline 
more than 20% y/y in 4Q, contributing to a deceleration in OCC revenue growth

appears manageable, with TRU estimating that a complete shift of FHFA-regulated loans 
to bi-merge would reduce annual revenue by ~$33mn and EPS by $0.15 before

pulls. TRU has achieved $130mn of annual OneTru opex savings and plans to extend the

margin expansion outlook to flat to 20 bps, and higher interest expense creates a 
~$60mn headwind in 2027. We believe resilient non-mortgage growth, Insurance 
momentum and the OneTru rollout are balanced by declining mortgage volumes, lower 
underlying margin expansion and higher interest expense. 
Valuation and Risks 
 
 
Fair Isaac (FICO): We are Buy-rated on FICO. Our 12-month price target of $961 is 
based on 18.0x our NTM + 1 YR EPS estimate of $53.39. Our target multiple comes at a 
premium to the Info Services peer group median of 17.2x to reflect FICO’s superior 
earnings growth, margins and pricing power, partially offset by concerns regarding 
potential pricing power erosion and market share loss. Key risks include an inability to 
sustain elevated pricing increases in the Scores business, competitive threats, a macro 
and credit market downturn, and a lack of traction with FICO’s decision management 
software transformation. 
Equifax (EFX): We are Neutral-rated on EFX. Our 12-month price target of $169 is 
based on 14.25x our NTM + 1 YR EPS estimate of $11.83. Our target multiple is lower 
than the Info Services peer group median of 17.2x in the context of EFX’s higher 
transaction revenue mix and mortgage market uncertainty, mitigated by its wide moat vs 
AI risks. Key risks to the downside for EFX shares include macro and interest rate risk, 
subprime consumer credit origination headwinds, and mortgage origination volume 
volatility. Key risks to the upside include a rebound in mortgage origination volumes, 
expansion in non-mortgage credit inquiries, rapid product introductions, further 
adoption of EWS offerings and healthy international expansion. 
TransUnion (TRU): We are Neutral-rated on TRU. Our 12-month price target of $78 is 
based on 12.5x our NTM + 1 YR EPS estimate of $6.26. Our target multiple comes at a 
discount to the Info Services peer group median of 17.2x to reflect TRU’s higher mix of 
macro-sensitive transaction revenues and exposure to the fintech and subprime 
categories, mitigated by its wide moat vs AI risks. Key risks to the downside include a 
macro and credit market downturn, mortgage origination volume declines, growth and 
margin dilution from M&A, FX headwinds and financial leverage. Key risks to the upside 
include a strengthening economic backdrop, healthy growth in international and 
emerging markets and traction in US emerging verticals.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
