ExxonMobil Holdings Corp
Refining leverage, scale and diversification push in
LNG
Our view: XOM's 3Q earnings showcased its refining leverage, while
upstream continued to be weighed down by Middle East outages. With
the deals in Papua New Guinea and Mozambique, XOM appears to be
seeking to diversify and scale its LNG portfolio, perhaps also in recognition
of its over-concentration to Qatari LNG. We make changes to our estimates,
incorporating our new price deck. We maintain our Sector Perform rating
and $180 price target.
Key points:
Refining leverage comes through. 3Q26 saw record refining performance,
with management noting that underlying unit earnings in energy products
have tripled since 2019 on a constant price basis. XOM also highlighted that
it continues to upgrade its refineries in the US, with aims to structurally
increase distillate and lubricant yields. As one of the largest refiners in the
western world and with its own long-term charters, XOM should be well-
placed to manage ongoing volatility in the oil market and deliver strong
capture rates, as it has done for the past two quarters in an exceptional
environment. Our margin indicator points to QTD refining margins of $45/
bbl for XOM in 4Q26, implying earnings of ~$5.5bn.
Middle East an ongoing drag. Offsetting headwinds persist from the Middle
East, with a ~350kboed impact guided for 4Q26, concentrated in Qatari
LNG. UAE production is restored, but sales are deferred due to logistics
constraints around the SOH, and management expects disruptions to
persist "well through next year."
Mozambique deal, seeking to scale and diversify in LNG. Alongside results,
XOM announced an asset swap with Eni on the Mozambique LNG project,
with XOM now holding a 50% interest in the onshore complex (previously
25%) and Eni gaining more share in the FLNG project offshore. This makes
strategic sense in our mind, given XOM's proven track record in large-
scale, complex developments. Nevertheless, the onshore project entails
significant risk, with progress already significantly delayed and FID yet to be
taken (expected by YE26). This, plus increasing its stake in the Papua LNG
project, suggest XOM is looking to scale and diversify its LNG portfolio, and
is perhaps recognition of its over-concentration to Qatari LNG.
Estimate changes. Our 2026 EPS moves up by 6%. We incorporate our
new (higher) price deck, which drives most of our earnings revisions in
outer years. On forward pricing, XOM trades on a 9.4/10.3x EV/DACF in
2026E/27E, compared to CVX on 7.7/7.8x and offers 7%/6% FCF yields in
2026E/27E compared to CVX at 9% in both years.

Valuation
Our one-year price target of $180 for ExxonMobil is based on
an 11x 2027E EV/DACF multiple, slightly above its historical
average for its core business and higher than peers, reflecting
XOM's defensiveness and historical returns track record.
We also incorporate estimates for its nascent low-carbon
businesses, assuming a conservative 6% ROCE. Our price
target supports our Sector Perform rating.
Upside scenario
We assume $100/bbl Brent in 2026, as well as mid-cycle
refining & chemicals earnings, which assumes a strong
recovery vs. 2020. With this assumption, our upside scenario
is $210.
Downside scenario
Our downside scenario assumes $40/bbl Brent in 2026, as well
as weaker refining, chemicals, and marketing earnings nearer
to recession levels in the downstream, using earnings in ’08–
09 as reference points. With this assumption, our downside
scenario is $120.
Investment summary
Our Sector Perform rating on ExxonMobil is based on our
belief that earnings momentum and the risk-reward are more
balanced following continued outperformance.
Key points driving our thesis:
• Downstream headwinds. While we continue to see value
in XOM’s growth profile, we expect earnings momentum to
moderate, as chemicals margins remain challenged amid a
tough economic backdrop.
• Growth story looks recognised in the valuation. XOM
shows strong headline earnings growth over the coming
years, however given the premium valuation, the buyback
looks more modest than peers, and we expect distributions
as a percentage of market cap to lag the peer group. The
valuation also drives more modest EPS growth.
Risks to rating and price target
We see several risks to our price target and rating for
ExxonMobil Corporation. Upside risks include better-than-
expected profitability and productivity in the Permian, as well
as upside to economics at future projects such as Liza in
Guyana. The largest downside risk is weakness in global oil or
gas pricing. XOM also has a number of major capital projects
under construction, so delays or budget overruns could be
a drag on the stock. If XOM continues to be acquisitive, the
company could face integration risk. Additionally, XOM has
significant production in unstable parts of the world, exposing
the company to political risk.
