 
Copper's Growth Signal Muddled By AI, Rates 
Stress 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
THURSDAY, OCT 29, 2026 - 12:15 AM 
Authored by Frank Monkam, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
Copper’s perceived role as a macro barometer of economic activity is morphing as the impact of 
conventional drivers such as rising interest rates and demand from China collide with the 
metal’s increasing sensitivity to risks surrounding the artificial intelligence trade.  
Historically dubbed “Dr. Copper” for its ability to diagnose the global economy’s health, the metal 
has long maintained a close relationship with manufacturing activity. 
 
Yet despite the connection between copper and economic activity, historical regression and 
correlation measures signal their relationship is largely non-deterministic. The correlation 
between copper futures and ISM Manufacturing PMI has been a mere 0.25 with an R-squared 
of 0.06. 
 

 
  
In the current regime, elevated rates volatility adds a critical layer of uncertainty, contributing to 
challenges around copper’s ability to reconcile supportive economic fundamentals with an 
increasingly restrictive cost of capital. 
The more consequential development lies in copper’s evolving relationship with the AI equity 
complex. As demand expectations become further intertwined with data-center construction, 
electrification and semiconductor investment, copper’s exposure to the technology cycle has 
become its more notable macro signal. 
That dynamic introduces a potentially destabilizing risk factor. Rising real yields and rates 
volatility have lead to tighter credit conditions, threatening the capital-intensive AI investment 
cycle. As stress builds in lower-quality segments of the credit market and leads to deteriorating 
investment expectations, pressure on AI-related equities risks leaking into copper — even 
before physical demand materially adjusts. 
The metal’s correlation with the AI trade is near cycle highs over a three-year period, while its 
sensitivity to rates volatility has become more negative. This shift puts copper in the crux of the 
repricing in macro volatility that’s underway. 
 

 
 
China remains a fundamental constraint, given the economy commands about 56% of global 
demand for refined copper. Persistent headwinds in the property sector, choppy industrial 
activity and a declining credit impulse are real challenges to the outlook for consumption of the 
red metal going forward despite copper premium trading near record highs driven by restocking 
following the Golden Week holiday period. 
Although structural demand from electrification and AI infrastructure offer a counterbalance to 
some extent, it may prove insufficient to absorb the combination of deteriorating Chinese 
demand and a rising cost of capital. 
 

 
 
Meanwhile, cyclical market technicals and volatility dynamics point to fading momentum, 
underscoring the tension between copper’s cross-currents. 
With copper’s implied volatility hovering near one-year lows, the market appears reluctant to 
price a decisive directional move despite mounting macro uncertainty. 
While volatility compression doesn’t imply an imminent selloff, it leaves copper vulnerable to a 
repricing should the competing forces supporting its equilibrium become more difficult to 
reconcile. 
 

 
 
Copper sits at the intersection of two competing macro regimes:  
1.​ Resilient growth that supports industrial demand on one end, and  
2.​ tightening financial conditions threaten the AI investment cycle on the other.  
The balance of risks increasingly favors the latter. 
With Chinese demand offering limited insulation and the AI equity complex vulnerable to a 
repricing, copper’s growing financial-market beta may prove a liability rather than an asset at 
this juncture of the macro cycle.
