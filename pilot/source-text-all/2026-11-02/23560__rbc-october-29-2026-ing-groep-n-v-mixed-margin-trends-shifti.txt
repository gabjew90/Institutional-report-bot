Mixed margin trends, shifting focus to CMD
Our view: Liability margins continued to increase in Q3 close to
expectations, but we now model a lower expansion in lending margins.
Our estimates give some credit for higher returns in wholesale banking but
the CMD mid next year might provide further upside. Our PT increased to
EUR34.
Key points:
Liability margins moving higher, lending margins affected by mix: The
liability margin expanded in Q3 by 6bp (post 3bp move in Q2) to 113bp.
For the year ING guides to 110bp which implies a liability margin of close
to 116bp for Q4. While ING describes the 2027 and 2028 guidance will
temporarily exceed 100-110bp we model a further expansion to 120bp
in 2028. Our Vault data (in the note) shows that deposit competition in
the Netherlands remains somewhat contained while Germany remains
competitive. In Germany, ING launched a campaign for new customers
with a rate of 3.5% (for a limited time), but remaining below top rates
in the market. Deposit growth in Q3 was relatively strong especially in
wholesale banking (cash management & payments). On the other hand,
lending margins continued to come down (by 1bp) to 123bp and ING guided
to an unchanged level in Q4. The decline in the margin is, according to ING,
a reflection of ING’s focus on higher return lending (mortgages, IG grade).
We had factored in that lending margins continue to increase but now only
model to 126bp in 2028 (previously 129bp).
Reshaping wholesale banking – more detail at CMD: ING announced that
it is changing the set-up of its wholesale banking division (renamed to
Corporate & Institutional Banking) leading to cost saves of EUR75m pa from
2028 (EUR175m restructuring charge in Q4). ING aims to lift its ROE in
wholesale banking further (previous 2027 target of >12%, 11% Q3 2026),
where capital optimization is likely to play a key role over time. We have
included a 20bp benefit from SRTs by 2028 taking our 2028E wholesale
banking ROE to 13%. The CMD (14th June 2027) is likely to lay out further
ROE expansion by 2030. Every 1pt improvement in our 2028E wholesale
ROE would add 2% to profits and 0.4pt to group ROTE. In addition, capital
optimization allows it to increase capital distributions. We continue to
model an increase in special distributions to EUR4bn in 2028.
PT upgrade to EUR34: Our estimates had given credit that liability margins
would increase. Considerable uncertainty remains: a 1% change in loan/
deposit volume growth corresponds to close to 1% of 2028E PBT; a 1bp
margin change impacts 2028E PBT by 1%. We lower our lending margin
forecasts which reduces our estimates. We roll our valuation forward to
derive our 12- month forward PT of EUR34 based on 2028 estimates.

Valuation
We value ING using a price to book model factoring in 3%
growth based on our 2028 estimates. We assume a COE of
10.5%. This approach, and the specific COE used, is consistent
with the method we use for ING’s peers. Our resultant price
target of EUR34ps underpins our Sector Perform rating.
Upside scenario
The upside scenario of EUR45 looks out to 2028E, assumes 1%
higher volume growth across loans and deposits (to 5% pa)
and a COE of 10% as general macro and geopolitical concerns
have ebbed down.
Downside scenario
Our downside scenario of EUR23 values ING on our 2026
estimates not factoring in a further return improvement and
assumes a higher risk premium at 12%.
Investment summary
ING's business mix is diversified with a strong foothold in core
European retail banking providing better earnings resilience
and potential for capital generation. The bank is highly geared
to Net Interest Income (64% of Group Revenues in 2025),
but has a geographically diversified business mix across the
Benelux countries and Germany and has initiatives in place
to grow fee income. Longer term, its focus on digital banking
should position it well in a banking industry that continues
to transition to more digital offerings. With Q2 2026 results
(confirmed with Q3 results), ING raised its target ROTE for
2026 to above 15% and to above 16% in 2027. ING upgraded
the Commercial NII guidance for 2026 twice this year, and with
Q2 results upgraded total income guidance while maintaining
cost guidance unchanged for 2026 and 2027 (confirmed with
Q3 results). With Q3 results, ING announced they will host a
CMD on 14 June 2027, when they will set 2030 targets.
Risks to rating and price target
• Our PT, estimates and rating might be impacted by wider
implications of current geopolitical events, the spike in
energy prices and the consequences for inflation/growth in
Europe. A European recession would impact earnings across
divisions.
• ING's exposure to Russia is relatively high, and it has
exposure related to Ukraine.
• Net interest income accounts for a larger part of revenues
than at other banks. A change in interest rates and/or
volume growth impacts our estimates for ING more than for
other banks.
• ING’s exposure to the energy sector is higher than for other
banks.
• Litigation is a sector-wide risk to which ING is exposed,
and the recent threat of a Lawsuit from 'Friends of the
Earth' in the Netherlands demonstrates the risk to ING more
specifically given its exposure to energy-intensive industries.
• Any change in regulation that impacts ING's capital level,
funding and liquidity ratios can have an impact on our price
target (target ratio, risk weightings to name a few).
• We have assumed buybacks in our estimates - regulatory
headwinds and/or a higher capital requirement could
reduce the buyback potential. A higher share price would
reduce the benefit to EPS from our assumed buybacks. ING
could decide to acquire businesses instead of using excess
capital for buybacks.

Key sustainability questions
This section is intended to highlight key sustainability discussion points relevant to this company, as well as our views on the outlook. Both the
questions we highlight and our responses will evolve over time as the dialogue between management, analysts and investors continues to
advance. We welcome any feedback on the topics.
Our view
What are the most material
sustainability issues facing ING?
ING's Double Materiality Assessment (DMA) identifies the following as material:
Climate change (E1) both as a negative impact through financed emissions and as
a financial risk/opportunity, Biodiversity & Ecosystems (E4), Own Workforce (S1)
(gender equality, pay gaps, harassment risks), Consumers & End-users (S4) (access
to quality information, data privacy), and Business Conduct (G1) (anti-corruption,
whistle-blower protection). Climate change mitigation is confirmed material across
the entire value chain, with stakeholder engagement further reinforcing this finding.
Does ING integrate sustainability
considerations into its strategy?
Sustainability is described as a "core pillar" of ING's 'Growing the Difference'
strategy. It is embedded at all governance levels: the Supervisory Board has a
dedicated ESG Committee, sustainability is a regular topic for the Executive Board
and Management Board Banking, and sustainability-related KPIs (e.g., sustainable
volumes mobilised, transition risk management) are tied to Executive Board
remuneration. The ESG Risk Framework is also integrated into ING's broader
Enterprise Risk Management and credit granting processes.
What is diversity like at board /
management level?
The Supervisory Board (11 members) comprises 7 men and 4 women, meeting
the Dutch statutory requirement of at least one-third female. The Executive Board
consists of 2 men and 1 woman (against a 30% gender diversity target). At the
broader senior management level (GJA 22+), women represent 35% in 2025 (up from
32% in 2024), and 31% of the leadership pipeline. The unadjusted gender pay gap
stands at 27%, while the adjusted pay gap (accounting for job level and country) is
3%.
How does ING manage its exposures
to climate-sensitive sectors?
ING uses its Terra approach (established 2018) to manage decarbonisation across
nine high-emitting sectors (power, oil & gas, automotive, aviation, shipping, cement,
steel, commercial and residential real estate), which together represent 75% of
global CO₂ emissions. Management tools include sector-specific lending policies,
an ESG Risk Assessment tool, a Transition Risk Scorecard, Climate Stress Tests, and
Client Transition Plans (CTPs) for Wholesale Banking clients. Physical risk is assessed
through an in-house Physical Risk Tool covering 13 acute/chronic hazard types.
What targets does ING have to
support the transition to a more
sustainable economy?
Based on ING's 2025 Annual Report, the bank has a set of SBTi-validated 1.5°C-
aligned decarbonisation targets across carbon-intensive lending sectors. In power
generation, ING targets a 78% reduction in emissions intensity by 2030 (vs. 2018),
of which 85% has already been achieved, while upstream oil & gas financing is set
to be fully phased out by 2040, with an interim 35% reduction in loan limits by
2030. In hard-to-abate industrial sectors, ING targets a 29% reduction in cement
emissions intensity (vs. 2020), zero portfolio misalignment for steel by 2030, and a
49% reduction in automotive emissions intensity (vs. 2020). Complementing these
sector targets, ING mobilised EUR166bn in sustainable finance volumes in 2025 and
committed EUR9.6bn in renewable power generation financing, and aims for a 23%
reduction in its own operational CO₂ emissions by 2030 vs. a 2023 baseline.

Valuation
We value ING using a price to book model factoring in 3% growth based on our 2028 estimates. We assume a COE of 10.5%. This
approach, and the specific COE used, is consistent with the method we use for ING’s peers. Our resultant price target of EUR34ps
underpins our Sector Perform rating.
Risks to rating and price target
• Our PT, estimates and rating might be impacted by wider implications of current geopolitical events, the spike in energy prices
and the consequences for inflation/growth in Europe. A European recession would impact earnings across divisions.
• ING's exposure to Russia is relatively high, and it has exposure related to Ukraine.
• Net interest income accounts for a larger part of revenues than at other banks. A change in interest rates and/or volume growth
impacts our estimates for ING more than for other banks.
• ING’s exposure to the energy sector is higher than for other banks.
• Litigation is a sector-wide risk to which ING is exposed, and the recent threat of a Lawsuit from 'Friends of the Earth' in the
Netherlands demonstrates the risk to ING more specifically given its exposure to energy-intensive industries.
• Any change in regulation that impacts ING's capital level, funding and liquidity ratios can have an impact on our price target
(target ratio, risk weightings to name a few).
• We have assumed buybacks in our estimates - regulatory headwinds and/or a higher capital requirement could reduce the
buyback potential. A higher share price would reduce the benefit to EPS from our assumed buybacks. ING could decide to acquire
businesses instead of using excess capital for buybacks.
