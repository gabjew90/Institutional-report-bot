Unilever (UBS Research Rec Sell) Q3 Review: UBSe +1%/+3%/+3%; Momentum meets headwinds
Report by Guillaume Delmas & Andrei Condrea
Robust Q3 but increasingly challenging from Q4
• Led by another strong performance in emerging markets (60% of sales, 6.0% volume growth), Unilever's
Underlying Volume Growth (UVG) came in ahead of street's expectations (was +2.5%) for the third
consecutive time this year.
• Yet, shares declined by -2% yesterday, underperforming the EU Food & HPC sector by 2%. Why? In
our view, 3 main factors weighed on the name:
• Persistent weakness in Foods which reported flat Underlying Sales Growth (no improvement vs Q2)
including some decline in UVG (deterioration vs. Q2); lack of market share momentum combined with
guidance for sequentially higher commodity costs next year signals a challenging 2027 and thus acted as a
reminder to investors of the risks associated with being a Unilever shareholder ahead of the completion of the
McCormick (MKC) deal (ULVR shareholders to own 55% of new MKC).
• Gross margin 2026 outlook revised down reflecting higher input cost headwinds and adverse mix; concern
here being about the sustainability of the current input cost under-recovery - which is boosting volume shares
in the short term but, in the absence of corrective measures, weakening group's earnings growth model.
• A gradually less favourable basis of volume comparison from Q4 onwards, especially in emerging
markets (average UVG of +5.2% in 4Q25-3Q26 vs. +0.5% in 4Q24-3Q25), pointing to some marked UVG
normalisation.

2
• One significant overhang and risks to 2-3% UVG ambition over the next 12 months make a material
re-rating of the shares unlikely, in our view.
What to expect from next week's CMD in NYC
• On November 4th, Unilever will host a CMD in NYC.
• UBS expect the company to provide more colour on the substantial progress made in the past few years,
its new operating model and its medium term prospects.
• As the company completed its €800m productivity programme at the end of June (plan originally announced
in March 2024), UBS would anticipate Unilever to announce a new savings programme aiming at
compensating the €400-500m in stranded costs associated with the separation of Foods and at
generating additional fuel for growth.
• UBS believe the company will reiterate its ambition for 4-6% USG (incl. 2-3% UVG) and modest annual
margin improvement - and, potentially, indicating a medium to long term ambition of reaching 21-23%
underlying operating margin (vs. 19.1% in 2025).
Change to estimates: FX driving 1% & 3% upgrades to FY26 & FY27 respectively
• For 2026, UBS now model 5.0% USG with UVG contributing +3.3%; implies Q4 USG of 5.0%, o/w
UPG+3.0% and UVG +2.0%.
• UBS look for U/L operating margin of 20.1%, up +10bps yoy.
• Our FY26 EPS is increased by 1% led by more favourable FX.
• For 2027, UBS continue to have +3.5% USG with 1.0% UVG.
• UBS forecast FY27 U/L operating margin of 20.2% (+10bps).
• FY27 EPS increased by c. 3% led by FX.
Valuation: Trading on ~17x 2027E P/E, in line with large cap EU Food & HPC

Report by Guillaume Delmas & Andrei Condrea
During the conf-call, management highlights were:
• 2026 outlook: Upgraded volume outlook, to at least 3% UVG, with H2 USG of around 5%. Continue to
expect a modest improvement in underlying operating margin vs 2025. Expect to deliver hard currency
earnings growth. Gross margins to be lower than initially planned. Expect c.3% net interest costs (unch.). In
Q4, expect the US to return to growth in Q4. Pricing to accelerate in Q4.
• 2027 outlook: Expect pricing to continue accelerating, while focused on growing UVG >2%. 2027 pricing
should be higher than 2026, to cover both 2027 inflation and recover 2026 inflation. From H2 27, Wellbeing
should return to outperforming the market. On finance costs, will get proceeds from Foods transaction, with
some going to paying down debt to manage the balance sheet, so will not need to refinance, only manage
short term commercial papers. Net interest costs should be c.3%.
• Margin outlook: Current view for FY26 is €900mn (top end of the previous €800-900mn range). Global
crude output back to pre-war levels, supply now normalised, but pricing includes a very high risk premium. Half
the inflation was coming in Home Care, of which 70% from Emerging markets. For 2027, we expect
€700-1,000mn (or +2.5-3.5% inflation). In HPC, inflation should be lower than 2026, with Foods likely to be
higher. Seeing inflation in vegetable oils and palm oils (trying to cover via hedging when prices dip). While H1
inflation is manageable, H2 inflation will be further impacted by El Nino. By segment: B&WB should be broadly
in line with the 2.5-3.5%, Home Care slightly below that.
• Foods separation: Committed to giving the business over to McCormick in good shape. EM momentum is
good, but Developed performance is disappointing. Germany and Eastern Europe challenging. Corrected
pricing in some segments of Mayo, setting up new price pack architecture. Will not be a short term fix. 2027
should improve vs 2026. Antitrusts discussions going well, will have to dispose of some assets in some
markets. Making good progress on TSA agreements.
• Balancing volume growth and gross margin protection: There is always some lag between price and
costs. Priority is to protect 2% volume growth floor. India and Indonesia have high exposures to Home Care,
where inflation is highest. To protect % margins, need to price at 1.4-1.5x inflation. Gross margins of c.35% in
Home Care sit below the group's 46-47%, but also see lower BMI investment.
• Pricing approach: Can only increase prices relative to the market while strengthening brand equities and/or
innovation-linked pricing.
• Q3 highlights: 60% of turnover gaining or holding share.
• Power brands: 79% of turnover, grew +5.8% (UVG +3.3%). 10 of the Power Brands growing +DD in Q3.
Some work to do in the rest of the portfolio.

4
• Beauty & Wellbeing: Beauty grew +7.5% (6% in Europe, +DD in EMs). Wellbeing: declined HSD in Q3,
partly driven by Amazon Phasing, broadbased across brands. Olly grew +MSD YTD (lapping distribution
gains, but declined in Q3). Nutrafol declined YTD and HSD in Q3, having good retention, but much higher
consumer acquisition costs, looking at value equation. Gruns saw strong growth (but not in organic metrics).
LiquidIV grew YTD (declined in Q3) - hydration powder market growing +HSD, but club introducing private
label, so currently working on expanding channel mix. The view that Wellbeing is highly attractive has not
changed, do not see any structural issues.
• Skin care: Vaseline grew +DD across both Emerging and Developed markets. Hourglass, Dermalogica and
Paula's Choice grew well.
• Personal Care: +DD growth in Sunsilk, Clear, Dove and K18. Deo grew +MSD led by volumes. LatAm grew
+DD. Dr Squatch grew +DD. Skin Cleansing +HSD, balanced between volume and pricing. Oral Care
declined, led by Europe.
• Skin cleansing: Performance improving in some core markets (Dove 40% of PC sales, grew +11.5%). India
accelerating, Indonesia improving. Coming with premium innovations in the category, with previous launches
landing well.
• Home Care: Volume grew +DD in India and Brazil. Price growth accelerated in a response to inflation.
Gaining share in value and volume in the vast majority of their markets. Fabric Cleaning grew +DD, Enhancers
+MSD. Started to build Cif in China via social commerce. Cif Infinite Clean now available in 8 markets. Local
competitors lowering marketing investments.
• Foods: flat performance, held back declines in developed markets. US Condiments were below
expectations. UFS grew +LSD led by North America (accelerated to +MSD, while China, its largest market,
grew +LSD). Hellmans leading the category, but seeing increased competition in premium and from discount
brands.
• APA: Grew +10.1%. India: Grew 15% with volume +7%, lapping the GST comparator, broad-based with
strong +DD growth in Home (+22%) and Beauty & Wellbeing, while Foods and PC growing +HSD (Horlicks
+DD). Indonesia: +7% led by volume, with +MSD growth in Foods (local brands). Home and Hair were very
strong. China grew +LSD (vol +MSD), with Beauty & Wellbeing and Personal Care benefitting from premium
innovations, while Home Care was +MSD. Close to +4% volume growth, driven by Douyin and Pindodo.
• North America: declined, but gained volume share. Personal Care +MSD. Expect the US to return to growth
in Q4. Prestige beauty +DD, Dove +7.5%. Hair Care +HSD. Nutrafol having good retention, but much higher
consumer acquisition costs, looking at value equation.
• Europe: remains subdued. B&WB and PC grew, offset by declines in Foods and Home Care. Beauty +6%,
Home Care . Negative pricing on account of pressure from buying alliances and promo pressures. Germany
and Eastern Europe challenging. Corrected pricing in some segments of Mayo, setting up new price pack
architecture. Will not be a short term fix.
• Emerging Markets: seeing very strong performance, with performance improving in Foods. Saw market
growth accelerate. Seeing the underlying markets getting slightly better e.g. LatAm and SEA, as well as India.
Outperforming Brazil, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, or Vietnam. Local competitors cutting marketing
investments, particularly in Home Care, coupled with some caution around pricing. Do not see supply security
issues, as the supply of oil has improved.
• Brazil grew +8% YTD, reignited growth in deodorants. Do not expect a particularly negative impact following
the elections. Tax changes: Have not had new news on the subject, the local business is prepared for these
changes, and the guidance includes any impact from these
Unilever (UBS Research Rec Sell) – Q3 USG of +5.2% came ahead of cons +4.8%, entirely driven by
Emerging Markets, as Developed missed. UVG guidance upgraded already in consensus numbers

Report by Guillaume Delmas & Andrei Condrea
How did the trading update compare vs expectations?
• Unilever Group Q3 Underlying Sales Growth (USG) of +5.2% came in +40bps ahead of the
company-compiled consensus of +4.8%, decelerating vs the +5.8% posted in Q2.
• The beat was driven by Emerging Markets, and volume/mix (UVG) of +2.9%, +40bps ahead of consensus
at +2.5%.
• Meanwhile, price (UPG) of +2.3% was in line with consensus.
• With a +0.6% tailwind from FX and +0.8% net M&A impact, ULVR Group's Q3 turnover was €13,271mn,
+1% vs cons.
What were the most noteworthy areas in the results?
• By product divisions, all three HPC categories beat. Home Care USG was +9.1% (VA +6.7%, Q2
+9.1%), with UVG +5.4% (VA +2.9%).Personal Care delivered +6.3% USG (vs VA cons: +5.4%, +5.9% in
Q2), driven by UVG at +3.8% (VA cons +2.8%), with HSD growth in Emerging and +MSD in the US.Beauty &
Wellbeing posted +5.7% USG in Q3, ahead of VA cons +5.2% (Q2 +8.1%), with +3.6% UVG and +2.1%
UPG. Wellbeing declined in Q3, partly down to Amazon Prime Day phasing, down -1% YTD.Foods USG
was +0.1% (cons +1.3%), with UVG -1.1% below expectations, but UPG +1.3%, with weakness in developed
markets.
• By region, Asia Pacific Africa posted +10.1% USG, +340bps ahead of expectations (Q2 +8.8%), driven
by UVG at +5.8% (VA cons at 3.8%).
• LatAm printed +8.5% USG in Q3, in line with VA cons (Q2 +8.9%), driven by UVG at +6.5% (VA cons at
+5.1%).
• Conversely, North America delivered -0.7% USG, -210bps vs cons of +1.4% (Q2 +3.6%), with UVG -2.5%
(VA cons +0.7%).
• Europe -1.7% (vs VA cons +0.5%, Q2 -1.3%), with UPG -1.3%.
• Power Brands delivered USG +5.8%, with volume growth of +3.3%.
• Lastly, Hindustan Unilever posted +14.8% USG (UVG +7%) in the quarter, while ULVR Indonesia grew
+7% USG led by volumes (but gross margin down -390bps YoY / -190bps QoQ).
What is the guidance and how does it compare to consensus?
• Unilever reiterated its FY26 guidance, with USG 4-6% (unch), but upgraded its UVG expectations to "at
least 3%" (from "around 3%"), with H2 USG of "around 5%", more balanced between UVG and UPG (from
"pricing led").
• Margin-wise, it reiterated its expectation of a "a modest improvement in underlying operating margin for
the full year versus 20.0% in 2025" (cons for 20.1%).
• Currency is now expected to be a -2% headwind (from -3% previously).
• However, UBS see limited changes in consensus, as expectations were already there (H2 USG 4.8%, UVG
2.2%, UPG 2.7%).
• Consensus for FY26 is currently for +4.8% USG, with +1.6% price and +3.2% vol/mix; Adj. EBIT margin
at 20.1%, +20bps y/y; and Adj. EPS of €3.23, +5% y/y.
