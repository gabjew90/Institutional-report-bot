Transitioning to mid-cycle 
• 
We now favour a more balanced stance between stocks and bonds. Near-
term event risks and high earnings expectations prompt us to trim equities to a 
Core holding. The US and Asia ex-Japan (AxJ) remain our preferred regions. 
Within AxJ, we upgrade India to Overweight. 
• 
Higher bond yields are improving the outlook for future returns and offering 
increasingly attractive income opportunities. We are Overweight Emerging 
Market (EM) USD bonds and favour corporate over government bonds, but limit 
duration to 3-7 years. 
• 
We remain Overweight gold. Prices are likely to grind higher as near-term 
headwinds from higher bond yields are offset by EM central bank demand. 
Great expectations 
Individual risk events, high earnings expectations and a higher cost of capital 
point to a more moderate return environment ahead. In the very near term, 
financial markets must navigate key event risks, such as (i) the US mid-term elections, 
which have historically been associated with elevated market volatility and (ii) renewed 
geopolitical tensions, with several reports suggesting the US is considering renewed 
strikes on Iran. These concerns have pushed the Brent oil price well above USD 
110/bbl despite significant ongoing crude oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz.  
Higher bond yields combined with high earnings expectations raise the risk of 
an expectations reset. The US Q3 earnings season is attracting significant attention 
given high earnings expectations, particularly for the AI sector. However, higher US 
bond yields may start to bite as debt-financed capex takes on a greater share of total 
capex over the coming year. This, combined with already-high expectations, raises the 
risk of disappointment and a near-term repricing of expected earnings growth pace.  
Despite the current challenges, healthy growth continues to underpin the 
economic backdrop. The US economic outlook remains well supported, and the 
absolute level of year-ahead earnings expectations is high. We do not expect Fed rate 
hikes to result in overly restrictive policy. Our caution, thus, stems from the risk of an 
expectations repricing rather than an outright economic slowdown.

A more balanced stock-bond mix 
We trim global equities to a Core holding, leaving us 
Neutral on both equities and bonds and Overweight gold. 
The concerns laid out earlier point to near-term volatility risks 
and a somewhat lower long-term growth trajectory for 
equities. Our quant model also reflects this cautious outlook, 
having downgraded equities to a level usually associated with 
flat-to-modestly-negative returns. 
From a relative perspective, though, the higher yields on 
offer from bonds also raise the bar by presenting an 
increasingly compelling return outlook after several 
years of very low yields. We believe this argues for portfolios 
that are increasingly balanced between equities and bonds. 
Ways to diversify equity exposure 
We 
continue 
to 
favour 
global 
equity 
market 
diversification, with a preference for US and AxJ equities. 
We remain Overweight US and AxJ equities, expecting them 
to outperform other major regions. Earnings growth remains 
the main driver of this view – while growth rates could 
decelerate somewhat, we still expect them to remain higher 
than those of other major regions.  
Japan and Euro area equities remain attractive as 
regional diversifiers. Our positive view on both markets’ 
financial sectors supports our Core holding view on them.   
Within Asia, we are Overweight Taiwan and India. Indian 
equities have lagged the region this year, in part due to limited 
listed AI-related exposure. However, we believe this is 
increasingly likely to become an advantage as investors seek 
greater diversification away from excessive concentration in 
the AI theme. This comes at a time when the economic and 
earnings growth outlook for Indian equities is starting to 
improve. We retain a more Neutral view on Chinese equities 
amid a still-challenging growth outlook. 
We also favour greater sector diversification. In the US, 
we favour diversifying across our Overweight sectors of 
technology, financials, materials and healthcare. We also 
favour financials in Japan and the Euro area.  
Changes to our equity opportunistic ideas also reflect a 
more defensive stance. In recent weeks, we have closed our 
global semiconductors, MSCI Taiwan, US communication 
services and global power & electrification ideas and have 
opened new ideas on US healthcare and MSCI Switzerland. 
Riding the yield train 
We believe bond markets offer an attractive opportunity 
to lock in yields. The 10-year US government bond yield is 
now in a range last witnessed in 2007. While the process of 
rising yields results in lower bond prices – as painfully 
experienced in recent months – the yield level itself starts to 
significantly improve the outlook for future returns. 
Favour corporate over government bonds, but check 
your duration. Still-high credit quality means that we continue 
to favour taking on credit risk to earn a higher yield (EM USD 
bonds are preferred, but we also see investment-grade (IG) 
and high-yield (HY) bonds as Core holdings). However, 
limiting duration to 3-7 years remains key to avoiding 
excessive exposure to any inflation surprises. 
Changes to our bond opportunistic ideas also reflect a 
more balanced stance. We close our EU bank contingent 
convertible bonds (CoCos2 – please refer page 27) and US 
utilities corporate hybrids ideas. We also close our 
opportunistic idea on US 10-year government bonds following 
the recent pullback in yields, locking in gains. 
Alternatives and gold to remain relevant 
Alternatives are an effective tool for portfolio risk 
management. Hedge funds and private markets provide a 
differentiated return stream, while rotating into alternatives 
helps reduce equity beta exposure. We examine downside 
capture and how beta is reduced via hedge fund sub-
strategies and private markets in our asset allocation section.  
Gold remains a key Overweight. We trim our three- and 12-
month forecasts modestly, but see gold forming a strong base 
above USD 4,000/oz. Rising bond yields and the USD were 
recent headwinds, but we expect a softer USD and continued 
EM central bank demand to result in 12-month gains.

Key themes 
Core scenario (soft landing, 45% probability): An economic soft landing remains our core scenario, with the global economy 
overcoming this year’s oil price shock thanks to a surge in AI investment in the US and Asia. Record wealth generation driven 
by the stock market rally is supporting US consumption. Fiscal easing in the US, Germany and Japan is supporting growth. 
Meanwhile, inflation has reaccelerated due to a rebound in fuel prices as the Middle East conflict drags on. Against this backdrop,
the Fed, ECB and BoJ all hiked rates in the same month (in September) for the first time. Although all three subsequently paused 
in October, we expect the Fed to hike twice more, the ECB once more and the BoJ thrice more by June 2027. Nevertheless, we 
expect the energy and tariff effects on inflation to fade by H2 2027, enabling the Fed and the ECB to cut rates by Q3 2027. 
Meanwhile, China is likely to target credit easing and accelerate fiscal spending in H1 2027 to revive domestic growth.  
Upside risk (no landing, 30% probability): We maintain the probability at 30% as AI-driven investment growth continues.
There is a reasonable chance that lower oil prices next year, the AI-fuelled wealth boom and fiscal easing in Germany and Japan 
will boost ‘animal spirits’. A potential global defence spending boom, led by the US and Europe, could also lift global growth. 
Downside risk (25% probability): We raised the risk of a recession to 15%, especially if central banks are forced to accelerate 
the pace of rate hikes to douse rising inflation. A stock market downturn negating the wealth effect or a bond sell-off due to fiscal 
concerns are other tail risks. We cut stagflation probability to 10% as we see low risk of oil prices sustaining above USD 120/bbl.

Policy rates watch  
We expect the Fed to hike twice more by Q2 2027, then 
ease in H2 2027, as AI-led expansion continues. The US 
economy remains firmly in expansion. In Q3, annualised GDP 
growth accelerated to 3.4%, led by AI investments and 
consumption. 
In 
October, 
the 
composite 
Purchasing 
Managers’ Index (PMI) remained strong at 57.6. Since our last 
monthly report, the consensus estimate for 2026 GDP growth 
has been nudged up to 2.3% from 2.1%, and the 2027 
estimate has risen to 2.2%. That matches the Fed’s upgraded 
growth projection of 2.3% for 2026, but remains below the 
Fed’s 2.4% estimate for 2027. AI investment, fiscal support 
and household wealth are the main growth drivers, although 
consumer activity lags business spending. Higher bond yields 
and elevated oil prices are headwinds, but recession risks 
look contained. Our core scenario remains a ‘soft landing’, at 
45%, with a meaningful 30% chance of a ‘no-landing’ upside. 
Rising gasoline and diesel prices pushed US core personal 
consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation to 0.3% m/m and 
3.1% y/y, matching estimates. Consensus sees core PCE 
inflation slowing from 3.2% in Q4 2026 to 2.4% by Q4 2027 
as energy and tariff effects fade. Underlying price momentum 
is softening, and recent PCE methodology revisions have 
lowered core inflation. However, fuel costs passed on to wider 
consumers and AI-driven chip shortages remain upside risks. 
In October, the Fed held rates at 4.0%, after hiking in 
September for the first time since 2023. We expect the Fed to 
hike rates by 25bps in December and 25bps more in H1 2027 
due to elevated near-term energy inflation. It is likely to follow 
with a 25bps cut to 4.25% in Q3 2027 as disinflation returns. 
This aligns with the Fed’s projected policy path, although it 
implies a more dovish trajectory than money markets, which 
are pricing another 56bps of hikes over the next 12 months. 
Political uncertainty after the mid-term election is a key risk.

Global Market Outlook 
8 
ECB to hike once more in December, then pause. The 
Euro area recovery is firming. GDP grew 1.2% y/y in Q2 2026, 
up from 0.6% in Q1, and the manufacturing PMI stayed robust 
at 52.4 in October. Consensus 2026 growth has been raised 
to 1.0% from 0.9%, and 2027 is unchanged at 1.3%. The main 
supports are resilient exports and German infrastructure and 
defence spending. However, cautious consumers and soft 
private investment limit the recovery, and energy costs are 
squeezing household incomes and business margins. 
Recession is not the central expectation. French fiscal stress 
and sustained high energy costs remain the key risks. 
Higher energy prices pushed Euro area headline inflation up 
to 3.8% y/y in September from 3.2% y/y in August, while core 
inflation rose to 2.5% y/y. Consensus expects headline and 
core inflation to ease to 2% y/y and 2.4% y/y by Q4 2027, 
respectively. Encouragingly, energy has had limited impact on 
core prices, and longer-term inflation expectations remain 
stable at around 2.1%. Together, these should allow inflation 
to moderate in 2027. The main risk is that energy, and 
especially gas, prices stay high for longer than expected. 
The ECB held its deposit rate at 2.5% in October after raising 
it by 50bps this year. Inflation data continue to favour an 
additional hike in December. We expect an ECB rate hold in 
H1 2027, followed by a cut by Q3 2027 as disinflation returns. 
This policy path is more dovish than money markets, which 
are pricing more than 60bps of hikes over the next 12 months. 
China to provide targeted support to defend growth floor. 
China's growth momentum recovered in September. Both the 
manufacturing PMI (50.1) and the non-manufacturing PMI 
(50.2) moved back into expansion. The construction PMI rose 
above 50 for the first time this year as infrastructure spending 
started to take effect. Exports grew 36.3% y/y, pushing the 
trade surplus to a record USD 133.5bn. We expect growth in 
2026 to meet the lower end of the 4.5-5.0% target. Growth is 
increasingly bifurcated, though, with technology firms and 
exporters doing well, while household demand stays weak. 
Prices are rising again, but only narrowly. Consumer inflation 
edged up to 0.9% y/y in September, and producer inflation 
surged to 5% y/y, its highest since 2022, driven by energy and 
metals prices and concentrated in upstream sectors. With 
domestic demand weak and a high comparison base next 
year, the boost from energy prices is likely to fade in 2027. 
Policy is in ‘targeted easing’ mode. We expect a modest fiscal 
package to defend the growth floor rather than a large 
stimulus. The PBoC cut a key rate by 25bps to 1.5% to fund 
‘Six Networks’ infrastructure projects and raised its relending 
quotas. A new interest subsidy on first-home mortgages 
provides limited support to demand. We expect no policy rate 
cut this year. There is still room to speed up fiscal spending, 
including by using CNY 500bn of unused local-government 
debt quota. If the PBoC switches to an overnight reverse repo 
rate as its policy rate, that would imply a de facto rate cut as 
the overnight rate is lower than the 7-day official policy rate. 
BoJ to hike at a quarterly pace until Q2 2027. Japan's 
economy remains solid. Wages are growing above 4%, and 
the labour market is tight. The main headwind is high energy 
prices, which is slowing growth. Fiscal spending gives some 
support: the FY27 draft budget includes ambitious spending 
plans, alongside a proposed two-year cut to the consumption 
tax on food and a new five-year defence programme. 
However, this extra spending raises questions about fiscal 
sustainability. The BoJ unveiled plans to triple the pace of ETF 
sales from January 2027, potentially funding the tax cut. 
Price pressures now look firmly established. Headline and 
core inflation rose above 2% for the first time this year, and 
core-core inflation (ex-fresh food and energy) jumped to 2.4% 
and is expected to rise towards 3% in Q1 2027. Breakeven 
inflation rates have stayed above 2% most of this year. Real 
interest rates remain negative, hurting savers and pensioners. 
The BoJ held its policy rate at 1.25% in October, after raising 
it in September, only three months after its previous hike. This 
points to an accelerated quarterly hiking pace. We expect the 
quarterly pace of hikes to continue, taking the rate to 2% by 
June 2027. Money markets have caught up with our hawkish 
view in recent months. The BoJ noted for the first time that 
underlying inflation has reached its 2% target, marking a shift 
from creating inflation to containing it. Politics could slow the 
hiking cycle in H2 2027. Two government-appointed board 
members voted against the September hike, and the two most 
hawkish members are due to leave in July 2027.

Our view 
Foundation: Bond yields remain near multi-year highs, offering an attractive entry point. We view fixed income as a Core holding
(ie, a Neutral allocation) in our multi-asset portfolios. Within the asset class, we maintain an Overweight stance on EM USD 
government bonds, given their attractive risk-reward. We hold an Underweight view on Developed Market (DM) government 
bonds due to the tail risks associated with term premia. 
The Fed’s decision to hold rates at 3.75-4.00% in October amid still-elevated energy prices suggests that every Fed meeting 
from here is ‘live’, with a rate change possible on incoming data. With US inflation still running hot and the labour market tight, 
we expect two more 25bps hikes (one by year-end and one in H1 2027), bringing the Fed’s target range for the federal funds 
rate to 4.25-4.5%. We continue to favour the 3-7-year part of the US government bond yield curve as the best hedge against 
any risk-off move driven by AI-related growth fears. As inflation pressures ease in H2 2027, we see room for the Fed to consider 
a rate cut, which should pull nominal yields lower. Solid corporate fundamentals and supportive technicals should keep spreads 
tight, but there is limited room for them to tighten further. We still favour EM USD over local-currency (LCY) government bonds.
Opportunistic ideas: We are bullish on US TIPS, AAA-rated CLOs, broad US HYs and Australian dollar (AUD) corporate bonds.

DM rates – Underweight 
We remain Underweight DM government bonds. In the US, 
government bond yields have retraced meaningfully after a 
sharp bear steepening through September and early October. 
We expect real yields to stay elevated over the next six 
months as AI capex and productivity growth continue to drive 
above-trend economic growth. We expect two more 25bps 
rate hikes by June 2027.  
Over the next few months, we expect US government bond 
yields to remain rangebound. The near-term path hinges on 
five factors: 1) the 3 November US mid-term election, where 
a ‘Democratic sweep’ would likely be bond-supportive by 
constraining US President Trump’s fiscal spending agenda, 
while a ‘Republican sweep’ would likely weigh on bonds as 
markets price in further fiscal expansion and a higher term

DM corporates – Core holding 
We maintain a Neutral allocation to both DM IG and DM HY 
corporate bonds, with a tactical bias to add exposure on 
episodic weakness, as carry continues to dominate total 
returns across DM credit. Credit spreads have widened from 
their lows but remain close to historical tights, while yields are 
still attractive, reinforcing a carry-led stance. This tight-spread 
regime is anchored by robust issuer fundamentals, strong 
profitability and resilient balance sheets, despite a visible 
moderation in rating upgrades over the past quarter.  
Technical supply dynamics are reshaping sector dispersion, 
especially in the IG space, where heavy AI-related issuance 
is weighing on the long end of the curve. AI-driven supply is 
surpassing USD 250bn in 2026 and making up a growing 
share of US IG gross issuance, but there are growing signs 
that hyperscalers are turning to private markets for funding, 
which should help ease public bond market supply pressures.  
We remain selective at the long end of the yield curve and 
favour a duration of 3-7 years, where risk-adjusted carry 
remains most durable. Issuance-driven concessions in 
sectors with strong capex cycles (notably technology, capital 
goods and basic industries) present opportunities for investors 
who can stomach near-term volatility.

Shifting to a mid-cycle bull market  
The last few years have seen strong annual returns of 15-20% 
for global equities, consistent with the early cycle of a bull 
market, driven in large part by a rapid AI buildout. As the AI 
infrastructure buildout is increasingly debt funded – we 
estimate 40% of AI capex in 2027 would be debt-financed – 
the market is increasingly concerned about the quality of this 
growth and execution risks. We remain positive on the 
structural AI theme, but we expect investor focus to shift from 
rapid buildout to monetisation. During this transition, we 
expect market leadership to broaden beyond technology. 
We believe this shift will also be accompanied by higher near-
term volatility and a moderation in global equity returns, 
consistent with a transition to a mid-cycle bull market.

Summary 
Our stock-bond model (3-6 months) is at -2 (minimum: -5) in October, signalling a modest Underweight (UW) in global 
equities. Historically, a decline in the model score to -2 has been associated with flat to slightly negative returns. The shift was 
driven by stretched valuations in both global and Asian equities, weaker fundamentals and deteriorating market technicals. On 
fundamentals, market risk aversion has risen sharply, while PMI new orders have declined over the past three months, pointing 
to a slowdown in the pace of industrial expansion. On technicals, the share of equity markets trading above their 200-day moving
averages, a key gauge of long-term support, has fallen below 60% for the first time since January 2025. The model rebalances 
in the middle of each month and has generated 11bps of alpha from the equity Underweight initiated on 15 October 2026, lifting 
YTD alpha vs. the 60/40 equity-bond benchmark to 4.0%.  
Our 1-3-month equity market regime model indicates contained bear market risks across US and global equities.
Estimated bear market probabilities are 4.2% for the S&P500 and 7.7% for the MSCI AC World, compared with 0.9% and 6.4%
respectively in the September outlook. Across both models, volatility measures are the main factors keeping probabilities low, 
supported by momentum signals. Together, these outweigh modest upward contributions from macro and cross-market factors, 
leaving little evidence of a bear market regime over the next 1-3 months.  
Our market diversity indicator flags reversal risk in MSCI Europe Real Estate and MSCI China Industrials, which are down 
over 12% and 19% respectively from their August peaks. US equity and USD positioning remain broadly neutral.

Principles 
that stand the 
test of time 
Adhering to time-tested principles, to ensure your 
investment 
decisions 
remain 
robust 
and 
consistently applied, is paramount to your 
success Today, Tomorrow and Forever. We use 
five Wealth Principles to guide and guardrail your 
wealth decisions. 
 
Discipline – ensure consistency and 
prudence over your emotions 
• 
Reacting to emotions, such as optimism and 
fear, can lead to poor investment decisions at 
the worst times 
• 
Have a plan and stick to it – this helps you to 
stay focused on the bigger picture 
 
Diversification – simply put, don’t put 
all your eggs in one basket 
• 
Reduce risk by holding a variety of financial 
assets. Multi-asset diversification in your 
Foundation portfolio is important 
• 
As a guide, make sure your portfolio contains 
a variety of asset classes and investments 
that have low correlation with one another 
 
Time in the Market – a more robust 
strategy than timing the market 
• 
Predicting market selloffs is challenging, and 
timing your exit and re-entry is difficult 
• 
Missing out on the best performing days of a 
market can have a significantly detrimental 
impact on your portfolio 
• 
‘Time in the market’ and buying the market 
with a longer-term view provide more 
consistent returns that can ride out bumps 
along the way 
 
Risk and Return – make sure the risk 
is worth the return 
• 
To achieve higher investment returns, you will 
likely have to accept a greater level of risk in 
your portfolio 
• 
Therefore, it’s important to understand the 
risks and manage these on an ongoing basis 
 
Protection – don’t let the unexpected 
catch you unprepared 
• 
Even though you may feel healthy, or 
financially stable now, protection offers the 
ability to overcome times of financial 
uncertainty and mitigate the long-term impact 
of unforeseen events on your wealth 
• 
A good protection plan not only safeguards 
your wealth today, but also considers the 
value of your future earnings over your 
lifetime, in today’s terms
