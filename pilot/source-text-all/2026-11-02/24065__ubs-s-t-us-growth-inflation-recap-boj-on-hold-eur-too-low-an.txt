US Growth & Inflation Recap, BoJ on Hold,
EUR Too Low, and Canada Has to Deliver
Dimitrios Laloudakis
30-Oct-2026, 07:22 ET (EDT)
*Sales desk commentary. Not a product of UBS Research. For institutional investors only.*
• United States: Limited economic data releases, but it may be useful to refresh a few charts across growth
and inflation on this quite US macro morning. All things considered, the status of growth is robust across most
dimensions outside of real estate / housing (figure #1). Cyclical survey indicators are highlighting an economy
that should produce hot nominal growth, labor market signals point to cooling but not cracking with U3 low
against historical ranges as well as against NAIRU, and personal consumption growth being robust despite
income growth lagging spending. Regarding inflation dynamics, UBSe for headline and core inflation to move
lower in 1Q27 / 2Q27 (figure #2) as core goods inflation should no longer contribute to higher readings, energy
base effects will and inflation expectations are well contained e.g. 5y5y inflation swaps remain in comfortable
range. As an aside, the recent equity stress has led to the equal weight index outperforming the market cap on
a YTD basis (figure #3).
• Eurozone: During the press conference, a decent reversal occurred in EURUSD price action as EUR spot
flirted with extending to new one-year lows. However, as outlined in yesterday morning’s note, I find it
increasingly unlikely for EUR to fall below 1.11 figure, let alone stay there in near-term. The underlying macro
fundamentals have not deteriorated but improved e.g. FY27 GDP estimates revised higher and almost to
levels seen pre-US-Iran conflict, ERZ7 pricing is stable and reflects ~ 3 implied hikes vs. SFIZ7 with > 4
implied hikes, OATs stress off local peaks, and option implied positioning improving. While by no means am I
the EUR bull, the overarching view is that spot ought to converge. Figure #2 plots tactical macro implied fair
value indicating that the recent leg lower has fallen outside of what is implied from macro pricing. In fact, long
EURUSD was added to the systematic portfolio (on 10/27, see figure #4) and will be further discussed in the
upcoming weekly FX Divergence.
• Japan: The BoJ left rates unchanged at 1.25% in an 8-to-1 vote split. The December stub is all but confirmed
with market implied pricing of ~21bps. However, JPY 1y1y has come off recent highs seen earlier this month
with almost a full 25bps of implied hikes taken out. With USDJPY trading in the 157 range, with 1m realized
volatility falling below 6vols.
• Canada: Later this morning, August monthly GDP estimates are due with the average analyst estimate of
+20bps rendering a y/y of +1.64%. Assuming in-line with analyst estimates, this will be the highest y/y reading,
outside of June’s pop to 2.1%. Given how rosy the Bank of Canada’s growth outlooks were in both personal
consumption as well as fixed asset investments, it is integral to see a solid path of m/m growth rates. With that
said, USDCAD spot at 1.3950 reflects implied spot fair value and offers less asymmetry in initiating longs. See
figure #5 for tactical macro model.
