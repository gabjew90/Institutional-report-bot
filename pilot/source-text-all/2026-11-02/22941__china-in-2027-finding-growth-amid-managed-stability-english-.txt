China in 2027: Finding growth 
amid managed stability
Yifan Hu, Regional CIO & Head Macroeconomics APAC
Eva Lee, CFA, Head CIO Equities Greater China
Summer Xia, CFA, Equity Strategist
Kathy Li, CFA, Macro Strategist
China's economy is likely to settle into a slower but 
relatively stable growth trajectory in 2027, with GDP 
growth expected in the mid-4% range following 4.6% 
in 2026. Beneath the stable headline, however, the 
composition of growth should continue to evolve: export 
momentum is likely to moderate, property may remain 
a drag, but infrastructure and manufacturing investment 
should become more important sources of support. Against 
this backdrop, we expect policy to remain modestly 
expansionary, with resources increasingly directed toward 
strategic investment and selected areas of consumption 
rather than broad-based stimulus.
For equity investors, this argues for selectivity rather 
than simply seeking broad market exposure. We remain 
Neutral on Chinese equities overall and Attractive on 
A-shares, where we see greater exposure to structural 
opportunities in AI infrastructure, technology localization, 
and advanced manufacturing. Together with innovative 
health care, globally competitive Chinese companies, and 
quality dividend income, these areas form the core of our 
preferred positioning for 2027.
Changing drivers, stable growth
Exports have been remarkably resilient in 2026, rising 
around 20% y/y as demand related to AI investment 
and green industries helped offset a challenging global 
trade environment. With AI-related products accounting for 
around half of export growth, however, the moderation of 
AI capex growth and a higher comparison base should see 
export growth slow to the high single digits in 2027.
More trade barriers may emerge given China’s widening 
trade imbalance with the rest of the world. Recent US-
China leadership engagement has reduced the risk of near-
term escalation, while discussions with Europe on hybrid 
vehicles quota arrangement have also lowered some tail 
risks. However, unresolved structural tensions mean tariffs

Targeted support rather than a major stimulus
The changing composition of growth should also shape 
policy. We expect fiscal support to remain focused on 
technology localization, strategic infrastructure projects, 
targeted consumer subsidies, and social welfare, with 
government bonds and policy-bank financing providing 
much of the funding.
Property is unlikely to benefit from the same degree 
of support. Mortgage subsidies and purchase relaxations 
should help stabilize activity at the margin, but without a

return to large-scale property stimulus, the sector is likely to 
remain a drag on growth.
Monetary policy should remain accommodative through 
RRR cuts, expanded credit facilities, and liquidity injections. 
However, elevated global bond yields are likely to constrain 
the room for large policy-rate cuts. Taken together, the 
policy mix should be sufficient to help stabilize growth, but 
not designed to engineer a strong cyclical rebound.
For equities, look beneath the index
This relatively moderate macro outlook need not translate 
into equally moderate corporate earnings. We forecast 2027 
earnings growth of 10.8% for MSCI China and 12% for 
the CSI 300, with the divergence in sector exposure helping 
explain our preference for the onshore market.
A-shares offer more direct exposure to information 
technology and industrial companies that should benefit 
from AI investment, technology localization, and industrial 
upgrading. IT accounted for around 30% of the CSI 300 
versus 11% of MSCI China, while industrial exposure 
was also considerably larger in the onshore index. This 
composition should matter increasingly if China's growth 
model continues to tilt toward strategic investment rather 
than property and consumption-oriented stimulus.
Within technology, we prefer semiconductor equipment 
and selected hardware companies where differentiated 
capabilities, order visibility, and earnings delivery provide 
greater confidence in the investment case. Advanced 
manufacturers and globally competitive exporters offer 
another source of growth as Chinese companies continue 
to upgrade their products and expand internationally.
We are more selective toward offshore internet platforms. 
While their AI ambitions create longer-term opportunities, 
greater investment in computing infrastructure and cloud 
capacity could weigh on near-term free cash flow and 
shareholder returns before monetization becomes more 
meaningful. For these companies, valuations alone are 
unlikely to be enough; we would look for stronger core-
business profitability and clearer returns from AI investment.
Diversifying structural growth
Technology is important, but we do not think the 2027 
opportunity should be viewed solely through an AI lens. 
Health care provides a complementary source of innovation-
led earnings growth, particularly among pharmaceutical 
companies benefiting from commercialization, out-licensing 
and international expansion, as well as leading contract 
development and manufacturing organizations with 
resilient outsourcing demand.
We also continue to favor quality high-dividend sectors, 
particularly large Chinese banks, where improving net 
interest margins and earnings growth, together with higher 
payout ratios, should support shareholder returns. This 
income exposure can help balance the greater earnings 
and valuation sensitivity associated with structural growth 
sectors.
We therefore favor a barbell approach for 2027, combining 
selected technology, health care and globally competitive 
Chinese companies with quality bank dividends. With 
overall economic growth likely to remain relatively stable, 
the more important investment story may lie beneath the 
headline GDP number: where investment is increasing, 
where earnings can compound, and where China's 
changing growth model is creating durable opportunities for 
shareholders.

Balancing resilience and carry
Eve Li, CFA, Credit Strategist
Min-Huey Chong, Credit Strategist
Performance
• Month to date, Chinese investment grade (IG) bonds 
gained 0.7% (+0.3% YTD, 5.6% yield-to-maturity), while 
high yield (HY) declined 1.0% (+3.3% YTD, 11.9% 
YTM) due to spread widening, mainly dragged down by 
select property and industrial names. Meanwhile, the US 
Treasury curve bull-flattened on a flight-to-quality move, 
with the 10-year Treasury yield declining 32bps from its 
peak to 5.0%.
Key trends
• Within the Macau gaming sector, the Macau government 
recently launched the MOP 20bn Government Guidance 
Fund (GGF) program, with the six gaming operators 
required to contribute an aggregate MOP 9bn. While 
these contributions will count toward their committed 
non-gaming investments, they could add liquidity 
pressure for select operators, depending on the timing 
of capital calls and whether additional contributions 
are required for future phases of similar programs. 
Meanwhile, the October MTD gross gaming revenue 
(GGR) run rate remained soft, following a 1.2% y/
y decline in September, which marked the fourth 
consecutive month of y/y contraction.
• Within the China property sector, Chinese regulators 
announced a mortgage subsidy policy in late September. 
Given its relatively stringent eligibility criteria, including 
caps on purchase price, floor area, and mortgage 
principal, we expect the policy to provide only marginal 
support for housing demand, with narrow eligibility 
appearing to primarily target lower-tier cities. Meanwhile, 
primary market data deteriorated further in September, 
with both national sales and home prices continuing to 
trend downward. With limited visible stimulus to drive a 
meaningful recovery in the primary market, we continue 
to favor developers with larger investment property 
portfolios and stronger balance sheets.
Asia credit remains well positioned to 
deliver steady income with defensive 
characteristics.
Investment ideas
• We maintain a balanced stance on Asia credit. We see 
opportunities to selectively add high-quality bonds at 
current compelling yield levels, and we would avoid ultra-
long duration given higher volatility. From a strategy 
perspective, we favor a barbell approach, combining 
defensive mainland China and Hong Kong IG credits 
with yield-enhancement opportunities in subordinated 
bank and insurance capital instruments, as well as select 
shorter-dated high-quality Macau gaming bonds.
• Non-USD credits, including CNH, AUD, and SGD bonds, 
remain viable portfolio diversifiers for investors, in our 
view.

CNY: Strong external flows 
continue to support appreciation
Teck Leng Tan, CFA, FX Strategist
The yuan has extended its appreciation trend since the start 
of the year, with USDCNY recently reaching around 6.67. 
CNY strength despite intermittent USD rebounds is not a 
new development, but rather reinforces a pattern evident 
since January. We continue to see China's strong external 
position and associated FX conversion flows as important 
drivers of yuan appreciation.
The People's Bank of China's fixing behavior also remains 
broadly consistent with the pattern that has prevailed this 
year. The official USDCNY fixing has continued to move 
lower, while remaining weaker than market expectations. 
Importantly, the gap between the daily fixing and market 
expectations has remained broadly stable year-to-date. We 
therefore see little evidence from the fixing mechanism 
that the PBoC has become less tolerant of gradual CNY 
appreciation.
China's external position continues to provide fundamental 
support. Export performance has remained resilient 
alongside CNY appreciation, sustaining large trade surpluses 
and associated foreign-currency receipts. As these receipts 
are converted by exporters, they create underlying demand 
for the CNY. Corporate FX conversions have therefore 
remained an important source of support for the currency.
At the same time, the domestic economy remains 
considerably softer. This leaves policymakers facing a 
familiar tension: strong external accounts support further 
currency appreciation, while weak domestic demand limits 
the case for tighter domestic financial conditions. So far, 
however, there is little evidence from the fixing mechanism 
that this tension has led the PBoC to materially change its 
approach to the currency.
Broader USD dynamics remain relevant, but are not the 
primary explanation for the yuan's strength this year. 
CNY appreciation has persisted through intermittent USD 
rebounds since January, underscoring the importance of 
China's balance-of-payments dynamics. Over our forecast

Stay invested, stay selective
Delwin Kurnia Limas, CFA, Equity Strategist
Xueqiong Huang, Equity Strategist
Technology stocks have tumbled over the past month, falling 
by a single-digit percentage after a strong September, when 
CPU names rallied sharply on growing expectations that 
Meta’s Muse would emerge as the next blockbuster AI 
product. The optimism, however, quickly faded following 
earnings releases from leading technology companies.
Concerns over the scale and financing of AI infrastructure 
spending triggered a renewed selloff in big tech, with Meta 
emerging as a key pressure point. Despite an earnings beat, 
its shares tumbled after the company announced plans to 
issue a new class of shares to raise capital, with the weakness 
spilling over to Asian technology stocks as worries about AI 
spending broadened.
While rising capex, whether funded through debt or equity, 
could put pressure on companies, we believe that the 
structural AI thesis remains intact and that the continued 
build-out could support equity performance. We forecast 
AI capital spending will remain robust, underpinned by 
healthy AI demand and improving monetization. Rising GPU 
rental prices and strong semiconductor results point to 
robust demand for AI compute, while average cloud revenue 
growth is expected to accelerate from 48% year over year 
in the June quarter to 58% y/y in the September quarter. 
Nevertheless, as investment continues to scale, concerns 
over the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending are likely 
to persist, making selectivity increasingly important.
Against this backdrop, we continue to favor a barbell 
approach within technology: high-quality semiconductor 
and hardware companies that benefit directly from AI 
infrastructure spending, alongside mega-cap platforms and 
defensive technology names that could prove more resilient 
if the AI capex cycle moderates.

requirements as mutual funds; (2) may have performance that is volatile, and investors may lose all or a

practices that may increase the risk of investment loss; (4) are long-term, illiquid investments, there is 
generally no secondary market for the interests of a fund, and none is expected to develop; (5) interests of 
alternative investment funds typically will be illiquid and subject to restrictions on transfer; (6) may not be 
required to provide periodic pricing or valuation information to investors; (7) generally involve complex tax 
strategies and there may be delays in distributing tax information to investors; (8) are subject to high fees,

Interests in alternative investment funds are not deposits or obligations of, or guaranteed or endorsed by, 
any bank or other insured depository institution, and are not federally insured by the Federal Deposit 
Insurance Corporation, the Federal Reserve Board, or any other governmental agency. Prospective investors 
should understand these risks and have the ﬁnancial ability and willingness to accept them for an extended

alternative investment fund as a supplement to an overall investment program. 
In addition to the risks that apply to alternative investments generally, the following are additional risks 
related to an investment in these strategies: 
• 
Hedge Fund Risk: There are risks speciﬁcally associated with investing in hedge funds, which may

Managed Futures: There are risks speciﬁcally associated with investing in managed futures

Real Estate: There are risks speciﬁcally associated with investing in real estate products and real

Private Credit: There are risks speciﬁcally associated with investing in private credit. This could

Foreign Exchange/Currency Risk: Investors in securities of issuers located outside of the United

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
