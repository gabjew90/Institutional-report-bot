Europe Open - Measured approach
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Post FOMC moves are modest, with USTs continuing their rally but momentum stalling, Antipodeans catch up to their
moves with JGBs trading on the backfoot. RBNZ Breman indicated that a Dec hike was likely but elicited muted
response given that it's priced in. Major FX pairs trade in ranges, oil maintains downside after somewhat constructive
comments from Iran over Wed. Mixed tech earnings with Alphabet and Samsung missing estimates though Microsoft
beats. Kospi leads losses with Nasdaq futures -1% at print. It's a heavy earnings slate tonight with Amazon, Intel among
reporting, which should keep price action choppy.
ECB is widely expected to hold rates, but more focus on Lagarde on how she addresses the French fiscal situation, and
whether she would open up on intervention. Ultimately, policy guidance will be limited, and we should remain on track for
a Dec hike. Data dump in the US with core PCE, Q3 GDP, personal consumption/spending/income and weekly jobless
claims, plus a US 7y auction later in the day. In EM, two rate decisions in Ukraine (+50bp) and Egypt (hold). It's a local
holiday in Turkey.

In Focus‌
Rates: FOMC held rates in line with expectations in a 10-2 vote, Cook and Hammack dissented in favor of a hike. Primary
dovish highlight was Warsh saying that he does not think that the economy is running hot, which drove USTs higher.
Reduction of "live" meetings next year to 7, which will drop further to 6 in 2028, also limits the scope of rate hikes, according
to Citi Economics. We continue to think that the Fed hikes in Dec, but it wouldn’t be too surprising if further risk-off
developments lead to no additional hikes this cycle. USTs still modestly higher, with flows from the desk seeing better selling
in the long end and better buying in the intermediates. Antipodeans catching up to bullish moves. RBNZ Governor Breman
said that there is a high likelihood of a Dec hike, which is in line with market pricing hence the bullish reaction in rates. JGBs
slightly weaker, but our macro strategist still likes long 30y JGBs. 
Equities: Weaker tech earnings (led by Alphabet which missed on Q3 revenues and was down as much as -10% in pre-
market trading, Samsung also missed overnight, versus broad-based Microsoft beat) weighing on the Asian complex with
Kospi -2% weaker though Nikkei holding gains at print. FOMC was largely a non-event for for US equities with futures

dipping lower on earnings (Nasdaq -1%). CETS continues to like the combination of large cap growth and strong balance
sheet stocks through QQQ, hyper-scalers (CGUSHYPR). More earnings to come with Amazon, Eli Lilly, Mastercard, Intel,
Merck, Caterpillar, Shell, Linde, TotalEnergies, Gilead, Sandisk, Anhueser-Busch, Banco Bilbao, Bristol-Meyers Squibb, Altria,
Stryker, Starbucks, Trane Technologies today. 
FX: FOMC failed to move major pairs away from recent ranges, with EURUSD holding at 1.1138, 158 capping gains in
USDJPY. Overall, interbank volumes were 6% higher than 30d averages with our desk seeing more interest for Asia pairs
such as USDCNH (+60%) and USDSGD. Flow wise, we saw JPY, EUR buying overnight versus USD selling. ECB and US
data dump makes a packed calendar between 13:15 - 13:45 GMT, the busiest time of the day. We continue to be biased short
EUR, via crosses, and USDJPY. 
Commodities: Mixed headlines this week but on net positive geopolitically, which keeps oil slightly lower. Iran Supreme
Leader had reiterated conditions with the aim of ending the war, in comments released in the afternoon Wed session. Iran
Foreign Minister Araghchi also added that Tehran "continues to give diplomacy a chance to succeed" and is working with
mediators. This followed with WSJ reports that US VP Vance sent a letter to Iran on the peace framework, at direction of
President Trump. ICYMI Global Commodities - Israeli political gridlock draws a more binary outlook for oil, but a deal by
yearend remains our central case. Political uncertainty in Israel and approaching US midterm elections leave room for either
escalations or diplomacy, with wingy implications for oil prices. We see a higher probability of a more volatile and disruptive oil
path.
Elsewhere‌
SEK: Sep/Q3 GDP releasing at print 2.1% YoY/0.4% QoQ vs 3.5%/1.4% respectively prior. Soft trends reiterate that the
Riksbank is unlikely to hike in Nov. We continue to see Dec hike as plausible depending on inflation trends.
ILS: Final general election results are expected tonight after absentee ballots are counted but unlikely sway the current result.
A deadlock scenario, though PM Netanyahu's coalition is projected to win 53 seats versus the opposition Eisenkot's bloc,
below expectations at 48. Two Israely Arab parties doubled their seats to 19, making them the potential kingmakers during
negotiations to secure a majority. Recall 61 seats are required for a majority, Netanyahu will remain in power as coalition
negotiations begin. USDILS flat overnight at 3.09. 
EUR: ECB Rate Decision at 13:15 GMT followed by President Lagarde press conference at 13:45. The ECB is widely
expected to hold rates, with attention instead on whether Lagarde addresses French spread weakness and the potential use
of ECB tools. Any support is likely to remain rhetorical rather than actionable. A Dec hike remains in price, but explicit
guidance will likely be avoided. Spread volatility is reaching a tipping point which could see markets start to price a greater
probability of no hikes, despite escalating energy prices. Our strategists like receiving Dec26 ECB €STR in this scenario. We
remain bearish EUR across various crosses - EURAUD, EURGBP, EURCHF. EURNOK is FX Strategy's preferred vehicle
which should benefit from US/Iran re-escalation. 
Keep an eye on ECB sources post meeting. Kocher speaks at 16:00 GMT. Before the meeting, Eurozone Economic
Confidence prints at 10:00 GMT for October. Belgium HICP for October also releases at 10:30 GMT, expected at 4.9% YoY
(Citi e., vs 4.5% prior).
USD: Data dump at 13:30 GMT including core PCE MoM, Advance Q3 GDP, Personal Consumption/Spending/Income and
weekly jobless claims. Our economists expect core PCE at 0.31% MoM post CPI/PPI data, with supercore rising to 0.35%.
Uptick in GDP would be considered hawkish, but details will matter. Continued AI-related capital expenditures should support
activity higher at 3.1%, but residential investment will likely remain a drag. Markets will likely be more sensitive to downside
surprises in growth than details of PCE.
CHF: SNB's Tschudin speaks at Money Market Event in Geneva at 17:00 GMT so will unlikely be policy relevant.
JPY: Tokyo CPI YoY for October at 23:30 GMT. Bloomberg consensus expects headline unchanged at 2.7% YoY with core
(Ex fresh food, energy) easing to 2.7% YoY vs 3% prior. Inflation running above 2% supports expected hawkish messaging
from the Oct BoJ meeting. Also on the radar - jobless rate (23:30 GMT), retail sales and IP for September at 23:50 GMT.
CAD: BoC Deputy Gov. Vincent opens John Kuszczak Memorial Lecture. Remarks will not be published on the BoC website
though live audio/video webcast will be available.

BRL: FGV Inflation IGPM MoM for October at 11:00 GMT. Our economists expect IGP-M inflation at 5.26% YoY, on the back
of higher wholesale prices with the MoM reading at 1.50%.
UAH: Key Rate Decision at 12:00 GMT. Our economists expect NBU to raise rates by 50bps to 16.50%, thus delivering third
hike in a row. The decision would be warranted by inflation developments, where headline CPI increased already to 10% in
September.
EGP: Deposit Rate Decision. Market consensus is for a rate hold at 19%.
