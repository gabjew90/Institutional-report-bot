Europe Open - Hang in there
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Weaker tech sentiment drags equities lower again, with treasuries holding gains amid safe haven flows. FX held ranges
to end the week, with USDJPY standstill following a BoJ hold in rates, and so far neutral Ueda (presser ongoing, he
reiterates upside inflation risks). As we go to print, he states the bank will keep judging a rate hike at each meeting,
including Dec. Oil prices marginally higher - on Thu, Iran said it received a letter from US VP JD Vance though signals no
shift in the peace deal terms. Ahead, regional Eurozone HICP releases with German headline printing at 13:00 GMT. Q3
GDP also prints with EZ out at 10:00. Also on the radar: UK DMP survey, Canada GDP, US ECI, GDP prints in Hungary,
Czechia, Taiwan and Mexico. Poland watches inflation data, Banrep expected to hike rates by 25bp (18:00 GMT). It's
month-end, with final FX hedge rebalancing needs pointing to buying of all currencies and selling USD.

In Focus‌
BoJ held rates at 1.25% in a 8-1 vote (Takata dissenting) in line with expectations, and indicated that underlying inflation had
reached its 2% target. Governor Ueda presser speaking at print, where so far he reiterates the importance to stabilize the
price trend around 2%, and that there is risk that the price trend going above 2%.The bank also confirmed that the change of
ETF sales pace by 330bn will start Jan 2027, which according to our desk, avoids stacking two tightening actions
simultaneously in Dec (which would amplify market vol). We continue to think Ueda would support our base case of a 25bp
hike in Dec, currently +20bp priced. Changes to BoJ forecasts include an upside revision to 2027 core CPI to 2.6% vs 2.4%
prior, core ex-energy also 0.1pp higher for 2027/2028. GDP is increased for 2026, but lowered for 2027. Earlier, Tokyo
headline CPI printed slightly stronger at 2.8% YoY vs 2.7% prior, but core missed 0.1 coming at 2.6% YoY for ex-food, energy.
Rates: Bullish drift led by NZ bonds overnight, while JGB long end also outperformed post BoJ (15s up +7.6bp). UST rally
supported by domestic receiving flows in Tokyo before duration took a U-turn and saw better selling midday. Overall, the desk
saw decent buying in the long end and intermediates, alongside some month-end rolling activity in the short-end. EU FI

traded well on Thu, after the ECB held rates on Thu. While the dovish Oct statement saw yields drop to fresh lows, a
marginally hawkish Lagarde saw bottoming out. We maintain expectations of a 25bp hike in Dec, supported by ECB sources
post meeting and potentially Mar, but have limited conviction beyond, especially given the expected change of leadership.
Our economists think that when the new President is unveiled (either de Cos or Knot, hopefully by Nov 13), they hope to have
a clearer perspective on the direction of policy. Full take.
Equities: Further misses in tech earnings (Amazon, Intel) weighing on regional indices again, with Kospi unsurprisingly the
weakest down -3.4%, Nikkei -2%. HK shares benefited from its negative correlation with AI sentiment, with HSI the lone
outperformer, up ~2%. Nasdaq futures down -1.9%, SPX e-minis -1%. 
FX: USDJPY flat post BoJ and ongoing presser, our trader expects the pair to trade with downside pressure today, where
rallies towards/above 158.00-50 continue to attract supply. The combination of equities lower, USTs higher is seeing risk beta
USD put vols repricing higher. EURUSD beta is outperforming on the USD put side whilst vols sold off aggressively overnight
on vol unwinds, with the commods bloc also underperforming. It's month-end today, with final FX hedge rebalancing needs
continuing to point to buying of all major currencies and selling of USD today. The magnitude has strengthened somewhat
due to better performance of US equities this week.
Commodities: Oil prices marginally higher - on Thu, Iran said it received a letter from US VP JD Vance though signals no
shift in the peace deal terms. As we go to print, Reuters cites Qatari mediators who have arrived back in Tehran for US-Iran
talks. 
★NEW★ Cheatsheet - US Midterms: A final markets guide Our final Midterms guide ahead of Nov 3. The Senate remains too
close to call, while narrowing House battlegrounds increasingly favor a Democratic retake. Dem sweep is increasingly priced
in, but with macro dominating the near-term narrative, the election is more likely to impact the medium-term policy outlook.
We also provide an election-night timeline, focusing on key swing-state races and historical call times.
Elsewhere‌
EUR: HICP releases for October for France (07:45 GMT) with state-level German CPI printing ahead of the national print at
13:00 GMT. Spain CPI due at 08:00 GMT. Inflation is expected to rise in most countries where the gas-to-HICP passthrough is
faster (e.g. Italy) but will rise further in the others in early 2027 (e.g. Germany). German HICP is expected at 2.9% YoY vs
3.3% prior. For now, our economists expect Eurozone HICP at 3.9% YoY with core unchanged at 2.5%.
Q3 GDP releases in France (06:30 GMT), Spain (08:00) and Eurozone (10:00). Eurozone growth will have likely slowed, from
0.6% QoQ in Q2 to a likely 0.2% QoQ for Q3 (Citi e.), but excluding Ireland, the underlying pace will lie remained roughly
unchanged (and marginally softer than the ECB’s forecast of 0.3%).
GBP: Monitor trends in DMP survey results for 1-Year CPI Expectations, 3M/1-Year CPI Expectations, and Output Price
Expectations for October at 09:30 GMT.
CAD: GDP for August and the advanced GDP estimate for September at 12:30 GMT. Bloomberg consensus expects a
modest recovery in the MoM print towards 0.2%, no forecasts for YoY though the previous print came at 1.4%.
USD: Employment Cost Index for Q3 at 12:30 GMT. Citi is in line with cons. expecting ECI to ease towards 0.8% QoQ vs
0.9% prior. The ECI is an important read on labor cost pressures. Later, Fed's Daly Gives Introductory Remarks at 16:00
GMT, and Fed's Musalem Delivers a Keynote Address at 16:30 GMT.
THB: BoP current account balance releasing at print, with trade balance out at 07:30 GMT for September. Our economists
expect export growth to slow towards 14.4% YoY vs 24.3% prior.
HUF: GDP data for Q3 P at 07:30 GMT. Our economists forecast the GDP growth to improve in Q3 to 1.9% YoY from 1.7%
recorded in Q2. Monthly activity data is pointing towards further recovery, though impact of EU funds flow may be not
reflected until Q4.
CZK: GDP QoQ for Q3 A at 08:00 GMT. Bloomberg consensus forecasts GDP to rise towards 0.5% QoQ/1.6% YoY vs
0.3%/1.8% respectively prior.

TWD: GDP YoY for Q3 A at 08:00 GMT. Our economists expect another strong quarter, with GDP growth accelerating by
13.4% YoY vs 12.93% prior, supported by net exports and investment amid broadening AI demand. Watch consumption
strength to gauge effects of positive wealth effects from stock market gains.
PLN: Preliminary CPI data for October at 08:30 GMT. Our economists estimate that headline inflation declined in October to
3.8% YoY from 4.1% prior. The government measures to lower fuel prices is set to be the main driver of decline in CPI, as our
proxy points to 10.7% MoM decrease in fuel prices at the pump.
MXN: GDP NSA YoY for Q3 P at 12:00 GMT. Our economists expect growth to remain near 2.1% YoY, with upside risks given
IP evolution and AI exports.
COP: Overnight Lending Rate Decision at 18:00 GMT. Banrep surprised markets with a 25bp hike in September and our
economists now expect one final 25bp increase in October to bring the policy rate to 12.50%.
