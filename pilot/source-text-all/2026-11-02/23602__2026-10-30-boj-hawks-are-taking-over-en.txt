BOJ – HAWKS ARE TAKING OVER
Rory Green
The doves that didn’t coo. The significance of today’s BoJ meeting is less about what was said and more about
who did not dissent.
In September, Takaichi appointees Asada and Sato voted against hiking, citing “below target inflation" and
consumption concerns. Today, BoJ Governor Ueda stressed that the committee unanimously agreed that, for the
first time, underlying inflation was generally consistent with the bank’s 2% target. Significantly, two doves that had
projected 2% CPI in 2028 with downside risk moved to neutral. 
The hawkish shift in Asada and Sato’s stance signals a continued move under Takaichi away from her preference
for lower rates and a weaker yen. And that is the right decision: BoJ forecasts and its dot plot have inflation at or
above target through to 2029, as it has been for most of the past three years.
Declaring inflation has reached 2% is part of what Ueda calls a new phase of monetary policy, where the focus is on
anchoring inflation and the degree of financial accommodation.  The statement and presser today, along with the
bank’s CPI measure “excluding institutional factors” and its recently released semi-annual Financial System Report,
make it evident that the BoJ considers rates overly accommodative and the risk of overshooting its inflation
target to be material.
Ueda did not commit to a December hike but he did say that every meeting is live. He also noted the need for
vigilance about the risk of inflation overshooting and said “we will not just sit and wait” for inflation to become
anchored.

We think the macroeconomic backdrop will remain sufficiently constructive for the BoJ to hike in December, March
and July bringing rates to 2% before taking a pause to assess the impact of tightening. 
There was one big surprise at this meeting – ETF sales. The BoJ announced plans to accelerate equity disposal: it
will now take 43 years, compared with the previous estimate of 112 years. The move will help the deficit somewhat:
 funds transferred to the MoF are projected to equal roughly  0.7% of next year’s budget.
All in all, markets are generally underpricing BoJ hawkishness – a December hike is at 81% and terminal priced at
1.9%, while consensus has the latter at 1.75%. We remain positive on the Yen and reckon returning Japanese
capital could help long-end JGBs outperform DM rates.
