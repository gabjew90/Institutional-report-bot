Thought of the day
The European Central Bank (ECB) kept interest rates unchanged, as widely 
expected, but warned that risks to inflation remain firmly tilted to higher 
readings as oil and gas prices continued to rise. The ECB said underlying 
inflation remained broadly stable and wages had not materially responded to 
higher prices, while acknowledging that the scale and duration of the energy 
shock could lift inflation and weaken growth. Financing conditions have 
also tightened, particularly amid rising French borrowing costs, creating a 
difficult balance between preventing inflation from becoming embedded and 
avoiding market turbulence or a sharper economic slowdown. The decision 
came only hours after President Christine Lagarde announced that she would 
leave office early on 7 February 2027.
The ECB decision follows the Federal Reserve, who also kept rates unchanged 
at 3.75-4% at its policy meeting on 28 October. Its statement struck a mildly 
hawkish note, upgrading the assessment of economic growth to “strong” 
from “solid,” while two policymakers preferred a 25-basis-point increase. 
But Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference was more balanced. He pointed to 
improving inflation expectations and tighter financial and credit conditions, 
while stopping short of describing overall conditions as tight.
Fundamentals, not an early ECB president exit, remain the main driver 
for Eurozone rates. The ECB has already raised rates twice this year and 
signaled that further tightening may be needed if the energy shock generates 
more persistent inflation or second-round effects. The latest decision suggests 
policymakers are not in a hurry to move without clearer evidence, although 
the rise in energy prices and inflation running at roughly twice the 2% 
target keep a December increase in play. A smooth leadership transition 
and policy continuity should follow President Lagarde’s exit, while the near-
term policy path will likely remain data dependent and shaped primarily

Eurozone sovereign yields should follow fundamentals and decline 
into next year. While French fiscal and political risks could keep sovereign 
spreads volatile (and further widening remains possible without credible 
fiscal consolidation), we still expect European borrowing costs to decline into 
next year. The ECB acknowledged that tighter financing conditions are likely 
to weigh on both inflation and growth, while stopping short of signaling 
immediate intervention in French bond markets. If market pressure is judged 
to be unjustified and disruptive to monetary policy transmission, its first line 
of defence would probably be the flexible reinvestment of maturing bond 
holdings, with the never-used Transmission Protection Instrument available 
beyond that. As such, we don't see the central bank ratcheting up its 
hawkishness at a time when intra-Eurozone sovereign spreads are under 
pressure. All told, we continue to think investors are well compensated for 
taking duration risk, as we believe energy market and inflation developments 
are unlikely to justify market expectations of an additional 75bps of ECB 
tightening. A recent divergence of performance within European sovereign 
markets does, however, reinforce the benefit of extending duration selectively 
in the highest-quality issuers.
The path for the euro remains upward. Lagarde’s resignation does not 
change our base case for the euro. Our analysis suggests the decline in 
EURUSD has exceeded what fundamentals alone would imply, and we expect 
the pair to recover toward 1.16 by year-end. The path may remain volatile, 
however, as relative monetary policy, energy prices, sovereign spreads, and 
geopolitical developments pull currencies in different directions.
So, we continue to see selective opportunities to add duration in high-quality 
European bonds. We favor short- to medium-term maturities, including 
select agency, covered, and corporate bonds, as well as stronger investment 
grade issuers at medium maturities. We remain cautious on the longest 
maturities, where fiscal concerns and heavy issuance may keep volatility 
elevated. In currencies, we believe investors should review their strategic 
currency exposure and reduce excess US dollar holdings progressively where 
appropriate. For investors able to bear the risks, we also like selective yield-
generation opportunities, including selling the risk of EURUSD falling below 
1.1140 and EURCHF falling below 0.9150 over a one-month horizon. Options 
and structured strategies involve material risks, including potential losses, and 
may not be suitable for all investors.

Caught our attention
Cloud growth supports AI outlook. The Nasdaq fell 1.4% on Thursday 
as mixed technology earnings highlighted the elevated expectations facing 
the sector. Alphabet shares dropped 12.3% after most of its core businesses 
reported September-quarter sales below market expectations, while Amazon 
fell 7.3% in after-hours trading as its e-commerce business recorded its 
slowest growth in three years and capital spending exceeded forecasts. But 
cloud growth accelerated across major hyperscalers. Google Cloud revenue 
rose 103% from a year earlier, Microsoft Azure grew 48%, Oracle’s cloud 
growth quickened to 62%, and Amazon’s cloud unit accelerated for a sixth 
consecutive quarter. Microsoft also reported an eight-fold increase in large 
Copilot accounts, while Alphabet said Gemini reached 1 billion users.

Our view: AI remains a key growth engine for markets, although elevated 
expectations and company-specific outcomes are likely to drive continued 
volatility. Accelerating cloud revenue and rising AI-product adoption provide 
encouraging evidence that infrastructure investment is translating into 
monetization. Meanwhile, continued capital spending should support 
demand across the compute complex: we forecast aggregate AI capex of USD 
1tr in 2026, USD 1.4tr in 2027, and USD 1.5tr in 2028. We continue to see 
opportunities in well-funded hyperscalers and high-quality semiconductor 
and hardware beneficiaries, while remaining cautious on specialized AI 
cloud providers facing greater funding and execution risks. Investors with 
concentrated technology positions should diversify across sectors and regions 
and consider more defensive areas of tech.
Inflation firms as growth accelerates. The personal consumption 
expenditures price index rose 0.5% in September, the largest monthly 
increase since March, lifting annual inflation to 3.6%. Excluding food and 
energy, core PCE increased 0.3% on the month and 3.1% from a year earlier, 
in line with consensus expectations. Meanwhile, 3Q gross domestic product 
expanded at a 3.4% annualized rate, ahead of the 3.1% estimate and up 
from 2.2% in the second quarter. The releases followed the Federal Reserve’s 
decision to keep its policy rate unchanged at 3.75-4%.
Our view: The combination of above-target inflation and resilient economic 
activity supports our expectation that the Fed will tighten further, with one 
25-basis-point increase each in December and March. But we do not believe 
a moderate tightening cycle will derail the US economy or equity rally. 
Healthy consumption and firm growth should continue to support corporate 
earnings, helping equities absorb moderately higher interest rates. Investors 
should maintain diversified equity exposure while using elevated yields to add 
selectively to medium- and longer-duration high-quality bonds, which offer 
appealing income and diversification benefits.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
