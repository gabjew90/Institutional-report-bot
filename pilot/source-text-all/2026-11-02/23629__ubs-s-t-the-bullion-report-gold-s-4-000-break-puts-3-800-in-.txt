The Bullion Report: Gold’s $4,000 Break
Puts $3,800 In Focus
Jonathan Garber
30-Oct-2026, 09:47 ET (EDT)
• Gold: cautious below $4,000, with $3,800 emerging as the next potential support region
• DeMark Technicals: Gold is bullish, Silver is bullish, Platinum is neutral
• Electronic Trading: Gold activity concentrated around key events, with liquidity remaining resilient
Gold has shifted to a materially more cautious near-term setup, with the optimism of several weeks ago
fading as carry pressures and weaker price action become more apparent. Recent rebounds have looked
increasingly like short-covering or relief rallies rather than sustained fresh buying, while the diminishing
size of bounces around $4,000 points to a fragile technical backdrop. Gold has moved back below $4,000,
reinforcing the view that recent strength has lacked follow-through and remains vulnerable to renewed
selling. With $4,000 now broken to the downside, $3,800 as the next potential support region, while the
former $4,000–$4,200 support zone could now act as resistance. A more constructive technical outlook would
require a sequence of stronger closes, and there currently appears to be limited justification for a material
near-term move higher outside of positioning-driven covering.
The principal risk to the bearish setup is positioning. Systematic strategies appear heavily short, while
discretionary participation is more subdued, creating the potential for a meaningful squeeze if price action
turns higher. Weekly positioning has also become more bearish, with net speculative length falling 6.1%, or
1.54 million oz, from 25.10 mn oz to 23.56 mn oz. This reflected the closure of 997,100 oz of longs and the
addition of 541,400 oz of new shorts, while gross shorts increased 11.4%, from 4.76 mn oz to 5.30 mn oz.
The latest confirmed weekly data are for Oct. 20; positioning improved modestly on Oct. 27-28, but not enough
to establish a clear reversal.
The options market has also turned more defensive, with skew moving back toward puts and downside
protection after previously sitting closer to parity. Options have recently provided a useful signal for
underlying price moves, while renewed intraday volatility has increased demand for protection. This has been
particularly evident in silver, where a roughly 3% rebound from the lows on Tuesday illustrated the
magnitude of recent swings. Silver and the broader white-metals complex have otherwise taken a secondary
role to gold, with recent price action also running against the more bullish precious-metals narrative that
had emerged from a recent industry conference survey.
Broader client flow remains quiet and lacking strategic conviction. In New York, activity has been subdued
following challenging recent outcomes for some fast-money flows, although larger intraday moves in silver
have renewed interest in volatility after several weeks of reluctance to own it. Asian interest remains
subdued, with underlying appetite not yet translating into meaningful transactions. The physical market is
somewhat more supportive. In Switzerland, pension-related investors have shown interest in index exposure,
industrial users have become more comfortable buying gold around prevailing levels, and physical purchasing
has emerged around $4,000, including interest assessed against Swiss francs. Indian activity has eased
after an earlier burst, while Diwali-related demand remains an area to monitor.
China continues to provide evidence of meaningful physical demand, including strong buying of kilo bars
and continued indications for both large and kilo bars versus CNH. Gold yields remain an important gauge of

The Bank of England market has moved to a discount to London, reversing the previous premium and
indicating that the demand behind that premium has faded for now. Exchange-related pricing has similarly
traded at a healthy discount to OTC, reaching approximately $2 below OTC fair value at one point in the prior
session.
The key signals from here are whether $4,000 can be reclaimed and held, any unwind of systematic
shorts, ETF and options flows, Asian activity, Chinese physical demand and gold yields, and front-end
forward liquidity as the market moves into the new month.
