Investing Through Disruption
Why Durability Matters More in Today’s Environment
Today’s market is rewarding growth, but not always the kind that endures. The rapid build-out of AI infrastructure has concentrated 
market leadership in a narrow group of perceived near-term beneficiaries, widening the gap between the growth currently favored 
by markets and growth underpinned by earnings that can steadily compound over the long term.
That distinction is central to SGA’s investment philosophy. We believe durable, growing cash flows are the foundation of enduring 
value creation, and that long-term shareholder returns are ultimately determined by business fundamentals. These beliefs have 
shaped our investment culture and the repeatable process we use to identify companies capable of delivering sustainable earnings 
growth at attractive valuations – an approach reflected in our firm’s name.
This philosophy is particularly relevant for investors today. When market sentiment overwhelms fundamentals, we believe a consistent 
focus on the key drivers of long-term growth becomes essential to separating signal from noise, helping investors distinguish 
temporary share price momentum from the enduring earnings power that historically drives long-term value.
Growth Bond Investing 
Durability is the foundation of what we refer to as “growth bond investing.” Applied to equity markets, the objective is not simply to 
participate in growth, but to compound capital through a more predictable pattern anchored in company fundamentals. We believe 
this creates a differentiated return profile for equity investors – one with meaningful participation in market advances, reduced 
exposure to market drawdowns, and attractive risk-adjusted returns over a full market cycle.
Across our U.S., Global, International, and Emerging Markets strategies, we seek to build high-conviction portfolios of attractively 
valued, quality businesses with durable growth characteristics; portfolios that we often refer to as “growth bonds” given the bond-
like predictability offered by the underlying earnings and the long-term capital appreciation potential of equities. Our portfolios 
are not designed to maximize upside capture in every market environment; however, over the long term, they have historically 
delivered compelling excess returns supported by earnings compounding, valuation normalization, and resilience through periods 
of disruption and uncertainty.

The Durability Test
In the current phase of AI-driven disruption, the challenge is no longer identifying which companies may benefit from change, but 
determining which can translate that change into persistent earnings growth. That distinction has become increasingly difficult to 
underwrite. In some cases, markets have rewarded businesses with plausible AI-adjacent narratives before there is clear evidence of 
incremental earnings power, while discounting high-quality compounders perceived to be at risk despite competitive advantages remaining 
intact. Against this backdrop, durability has become harder to assess at precisely the moment it has become more valuable to own.
Our process strives to identify and underwrite durable growth. We typically require greater visibility into forward earnings than most 
equity investors demand. To support that longer term horizon, we focus on evidence of sustainable competitive advantage, enduring 
customer relevance, pricing power, and a long, often seven to 10 year runway for growth – characteristics we believe help differentiate 
companies capable of compounding through cycles from those whose growth may prove more dependent on temporary forces.
Following is our view on the current AI-driven rally and how we think – perhaps differently – about durability in this context.

The same durability test applies beyond AI-related businesses. Across sectors, investors have increasingly rewarded near-term 
growth expectations while discounting companies whose longer-term earnings power may be more predictable but less directly 
aligned with current investor enthusiasm. As the range of forward scenarios widens – including the potential for slower hyperscaler 
capital spending, a reset in AI monetization expectations, broader market leadership, or weaker macro growth – durability becomes 
even more valuable because fewer businesses can compound across such varied environments. This is where we believe SGA’s edge 
lies: identifying and underwriting businesses with the fundamental resilience to compound through uncertainty, rather than relying 
on a single market theme to continue.
When Markets Favor Momentum Over Durability
Recent market dynamics have materially favored high-beta, cyclical, and high-momentum stocks, supported by hyperscaler AI 
capital spending, and amplified by speculative margin leverage, elevated concentration in mega-cap equities, and increased retail 
momentum trading. At the same time, rate volatility has compressed multiples on long-duration cash flows, often with little regard 
for business quality or earnings consistency. The result has been a meaningful divergence between fundamentals, valuations, and 
returns – which has pressured SGA’s relative returns (and that of our peers) over the last 24 months. However, it has also created 
what we believe is an unusually attractive opportunity for investors today.
Periods of narrow, narrative-driven market leadership have historically created temporary discounts for high-quality durable 
compounders and today appears to be no exception. The result is one of the widest valuation discounts in SGA’s history, with 
portfolios now trading near their steepest relative discount to indexes since their respective inception.

The Valuation Disconnect: Durable Compounding at a Discount 
For today’s discount to be justified, investors must believe SGA’s portfolio companies will either materially underdeliver on growth 
or face structural disruption – two assumptions we believe are becoming increasingly difficult to defend given recent fundamental 
delivery. With earnings continuing to compound and valuations meaningfully compressed, the return setup has become increasingly 
attractive, in our view: continued earnings growth can drive value creation, while even partial valuation normalization could provide 
an additional source of return.
We have observed similar de-rating patterns before: in 1999, 2007, and early 2021. In each case, the same dynamic played out: 
Capital concentrated in a handful of dominant businesses, and durable compounders de-rated, even as underlying fundamentals 
remained resilient. When leadership broadened and the durability of earnings growth became more important, those discounts 
narrowed. We believe today’s valuation disconnect creates a similar opportunity, with strong fundamental delivery not yet reflected 
in share prices, as highlighted in the exhibit below.

What Would Change the Market’s Mind 
In this type of environment, investors may question whether SGA’s investment approach remains well suited for the opportunity 
ahead. We strongly believe it does.
Our process is not static; it evolves as opportunity sets change while remaining anchored in the same long-term focus on durable 
earnings power, fundamental strength, and valuation discipline. Temporary dislocations do not invalidate enduring investment 
principles; over time, we believe markets will return to rewarding fundamental strength.
A key question, however, is whether today’s elevated growth expectations, buoyed by AI enthusiasm and leverage-fueled capital 
flows, will continue to move higher and sustain momentum for longer. Alternatively, if growth expectations are approaching an 
inflection point – or if crowded positioning in more cyclical AI beneficiaries begins to unwind – the backdrop could become more 
supportive of our investment approach. As the following charts show, forward earnings growth expectations remain above long-
term averages and near levels more commonly associated with post-recession snapback periods. At the same time, consensus 
estimates point to near-term moderation in growth rates.

How this resolves is not inconsequential. Recent momentum-led leadership has relied on the continuity of hyperscaler AI capital 
expenditures, sustained confidence in future AI monetization, and investor willingness to pay elevated multiples for businesses tied 
to those themes. In our view, those assumptions need not break outright to matter – they need only be questioned. As investors 
scrutinize the durability of AI hardware-related capital spending, circular financing, and/or the timing and magnitude of returns 
on invested capital, they may reprice that confidence well before that spending slows. In other words, the market may begin to 
distinguish more sharply between companies with consistent, repeatable earnings growth and those whose pricing assumes today’s 
conditions persist.
This transition is unlikely to unfold in a straight line. However, we have seen early signs of a potential momentum reversal, suggesting 
that the process may be underway. Historically, this type of shift has been supportive of SGA’s relative performance, reflecting the 
counter-cyclical role durable earnings can play when uncertainty rises and market leadership changes, and our growth bonds have 
begun to benefit.

Final Thoughts
Today’s market is continuing to reward growth, but we believe it is not always the kind that endures. AI disruption, narrow market 
leadership, and elevated growth expectations have narrowed the set of businesses capable of compounding across the range of 
outcomes ahead. The case for durability is unchanged, but what has changed is the price of owning it. Our portfolio companies have 
kept compounding earnings even as their multiples contracted, leaving portfolios at their steepest relative discount to indexes since 
inception – a discount that, in prior occurrences, narrowed once leadership broadened.
Therein lies opportunity for investors who are willing to remain anchored to fundamentals through periods when markets become 
captivated by prevailing themes – not as an act of blind faith, but because the underlying evidence supports it. Conviction must 
be underwritten, in our view, and distinguishing businesses that can compound through disruption from those that merely appear 
positioned to is where our process does its work.
We believe the case for growth bond investing remains intact: exceptional businesses whose resilience and compounding earnings 
are not reflected in today’s valuations. We cannot predict when the market will recognize this – only that durable earnings power 
tends to be most needed when it is least wanted.

on, among other things, the applicable fees schedule and portfolio size. SGA’s fees are available upon request and also may be found in Part 2A of its 
Form ADV. The annual dispersion presented is an asset-weighted standard deviation calculated using gross returns for the accounts in the composite
