A combination of disappointing US tech earnings and higher oil prices on signs that the 
US is girding for a major escalation of war against Iran is weighing upon risk appetite. 
US stock futures are down by 1% (S&P) to over 2% (Nasdaq). The main driver is a pair of 
after-market earnings reports from Intel and Amazon that broadly missed a blend of 
measures from EPS to revenue and revenue guidance. They followed the earlier miss by 
Alphabet. TSX futures are also off by -¾%. European cash markets are faring a little less 
badly with a slight rise in London, a flat CAC40 and declines elsewhere.  
Interestingly, the dollar is not picking up safe haven flows as every major currency is 
rallying against it. 
Oil prices are up by about 2%+ with WTI at $104 and Brent over US$117. Yesterday’s 
headlines from the WSJ that war plans are afoot and Trump has been briefed on attack 
options against Iran are the culprit. A “short, intense campaign” has been guided. Where 
have I heard that before…oh yeah, about nine months ago!  
As for rates, pricing for the December FOMC decision has backed off from nearly fully 
priced a couple of days ago to about 18bps of a quarter point hike priced this morning. I’m 
not sure that’s the correct direction with oil flaring and adding to how Warsh may be likely 
to view inflation and despite the tech wreck. US Ts are rallying in slight bull steepener 
fashion. Most other global benchmarks are slightly dearer as well. 
US Employment Costs Continue to Rise Faster than Inflation 
The US Employment Cost Index is an all-in measure of cash and benefits and is expected 
to post another sizeable gain of 0.8% q/q this morning (8:30amET). They’ve been rising at 
a steady annualized pace well above the Fed’s 2% target for an extended period but well 
off the pandemic peak (chart 1).

FOUR WAYS THE BOJ REINFORCED DECEMBER HIKE PRICING 
Nothing that the Bank of Japan did overnight swayed Japanese markets or affect pricing for the next move. December’s meeting remains 
priced for 20bps of a quarter-point hike with 1–2 more thereafter into next summer. The yen is a middle of the pack performer to the dollar 
this morning. JGBs slightly rallied in bull flattener fashion which probably has more to do with global risk aversion. 
You could argue there were three ways in which a December hike was bolstered: 
Numbers: The BoJ raised the core CPI forecast. While remaining at 2.5% y/y for this year, the 2027 forecast was increased by two-tenths to 
2.6% with 2028 remaining at 2%. GDP was increased a tick to 0.7% y/y this year, slightly lowered in 2027 to 0.7% and left at 0.8% in 2028. 
Lacklustre growth takes a back seat to a sustained overshoot of the central bank’s inflation target. Tokyo CPI was released just before the 
communications and posted a cooler but still firm gain in October (chart 2). 
Each meeting is ‘live’: Governor Ueda said “At every policy meeting, including the next one, we 
will assess the likelihood of our outlook materializing, as well as the risks, and make our 
decisions accordingly.” Some drew parallels to similar language in July that preceded the 
September hike.  
Further, it’s what wasn’t said here: given an opportunity to lean against and choosing not to 
do so, he basically validated December hike pricing. 
Ueda also noted that the BoJ has achieved its 2% target with upside risk which is a slight 
upgrade from previously saying they were getting close. 
CANADIAN Q3 GDP TRACKING TO BE REFRESHED 
Canada refreshes GDP for August with details along with a first estimate for September sans 
details. Most expect growth at 0.2% m/m in August based upon Statcan’s earlier flash and 
because my simple regression against known readings has no quarrel with 0.2. September 
GDP might be on the backfoot with what we know by way of developments like the drop in 
hours worked. Growth of about 2% q/q SAAR is being tracked for Q3 overall and will be 
refreshed post-release (chart 3).  
And who cares. The BoC is forward-looking; it doesn’t have Kevin Warsh as its leader. The BoC did the right thing hiking the other day but 
perennial doves who failed to predict tightening out of the pandemic and again now are replaying their song book; it’s not them, it’s the 
BoC that messed up. 
EUROZONE—FIRM GROWTH, MIXED INFLATION 
Eurozone GDP growth during Q3 beat expectations at +0.4% q/q SA nonannualized (0.2% 
consensus). Charts 4–5. That follows 0.6% q/q nonannualized growth in Q2 for decent back-
to-back gains relative to Europe’s norms. Within the Eurozone, Spain led the way at 0.6% q/q 
SA nonannualized. France’s economy shrank by -0.1% q/q. Germany’s expanded by 0.2%. 
Italy was flat.  
Eurozone inflation will be released next Wednesday but the incomplete consensus estimate of 
0.3% m/m SA is looking a little light compared to the breakdown of the countries that 
released this morning. 
France’s CPI was up 0.4% m/m (0.3% consensus). Spain matched consensus at 0.5%. Italian 
CPI was much firmer than expected at 1.2% m/m (0.4% consensus). Germany will release 
national CPI at 9amET, but the individual states release ahead of time and at readings 
between 0% and -0.3% m/m, they are tracking beneath consensus expectations for the 
overall tally (0.1% m/m).
