SpaceX: Update and raise target to $268 - Terrestrial AI build and
enterprise broadband add to Orbital AI thesis; Outperform
We raise our SpaceX outlook primarily on terrestrial AI build plans. We believe the case for
a multi-trillion dollar valuation rests on success of orbital data centers. But, the company’s
outlook for terrestrial AI is a material add to the overall revenue path. The Enterprise &
Government Connectivity subsegment also appears to be outperforming. SpaceX now
projects $500bn to $1tn in revenues exiting 2027. Our estimate is well below, with full 2028
at $424bn. But, that is an increase. And, as we have said, you do not need to go all the way
to the company’s plan to make SpaceX attractive. Elon talked of a possible Tesla merger and
SpaceX is reportedly discussing a telco tower deal. We make no assumptions on either here.
We now assume a total of 13GW of terrestrial compute by the end of 2027 - 5GW above
our prior estimate. Most importantly, the company stressed prices it is getting for compute
capacity, suggesting that it is above the $30-50/watt level of recent deals. We have taken
our pricing up, although not to that level. We we add capacity in 2028 up to 20GW. These
additions come with capex, which we estimate at $35bn/GW. If the company achieves its
pricing expectations, there could be more upside, both for terrestrial and orbital.
SpaceX plans first orbital data center launches in late 2027, with operations beginning
mid-2028. Orbital data centers were not discussed on the call, Starship launches were.
Flight 15 should be within a few weeks, with a plan to capture both booster and Starship. The
company said the heat shield performed well on Flight 14, which could set up the first reuse
of Starship on Flight 16. Reusability is key to making orbital data centers successful.
We update our model: 1) Assume terrestrial AI compute capacity is 2.8GW at the end of
2026, 12.8GW at the end 2027, and 20.1GW at the end of 2028 and increase our AI capex
assumptions, 2) Raise our average price per watt for compute to $26 in 2027, before
gradually declining thereafter. 3) Lower our AI multiple to 17x from 21x as AI-oriented stocks
have softened. 4) Raise our capex assumptions to support growth in terrestrial AI capacity.
Investment Implications
We rate SPCX Outperform and raise our target to $268 (from $248) primarily on higher
AI revenues (pricing and terrestrial capacity), partially offset by a lower AI multiple, as AI
companies in general have seen multiple contraction.

DETAILS
We raise our 2026 and 2027 adj EPS to -$0.01 and $1.44 from -$0.19 and $1.37 primarily driven by higher terrestrial compute
capacity and better $/watt economics. For deeper background on SpaceX, see our initiation report: “Space Exploration
Technologies Corporation: Doing Time in the Universal Mind - Initiate at Outperform; Target Price $239”
RAISING THE OUTLOOK
At Q3 earnings, the company described a significantly stronger revenue growth outlook. An annual revenue run rate of at least
$100bn is expected by year-end, but with now a run rate of $500bn-$1,000 expected by end of 2027 - mostly driven by
terrestrial compute. The company now expects to be at 2.5-3GW by the end of 2026. By the end of 2027, capacity is expected
to be at least 13GW (and possibly a total 23GW). The company said pricing is now tracking above the $30-$50/watt range
given by Musk at Q2. Short term compute contracts will remain the focus to take advantage of rising demand.
Prior to this earnings report, we modeled 2031 revenues of $655bn, well below the company’s prior 2031 revenue estimate
- and if SpaceX can even achieve our prior numbers, there is already huge upside. We have raised our revenue and earnings
outlook, with the addition of more terrestrial capacity and higher compute pricing. This takes our 2031 revenues up to $784bn.
Our target price increase to $268 (from $248) to reflects this terrestrial compute capacity increase, but partially offset by
increased capex and lower valuations for AI comparables. Valuations of AI-oriented stocks have fallen over the last two months,
which also lowers the upside in our valuation methodology for the AI segment. We also increased modestly increased our
outlook for Enterprise & Government Connectivity. Lastly, we raised our Space segment discount rate from 10% to 15% to
reflect increased uncertainty represented by this essential part of the business.
STRONG Q3 WITH AMBITIOUS PLAN FOR TERRESTRIAL
Q3 results were positive, although it is the longer-term outlook that matters. SpaceX reported Q3 diluted EPS of $0.35,
significantly ahead of consensus $0.15. Revenue reached $14.58bn, exceeding consensus of $12.88bn. Segment operating
income was $2.79bn, well above consensus $1.21bn, driven by smaller-than-expected losses in the Space segment, positive
AI operating income and stronger Connectivity margins of 45%, compared to consensus 38% (Exhibit 1). AI Capex of $16.44bn
slightly below consensus $16.90bn.

Space - Starship is the linchpin and it appears to be going well
We continue to see full reusability of Starship as the most important factor for SpaceX long-term growth. SpaceX has
demonstrated reuse of the Starship V2 booster and has expressed confidence in the ability to reuse the V3 booster. The more
challenging step in full Starship reuse has been the second stage. This year, SpaceX hopes to have the first fully reusable orbital
flight next (Launch 15). SpaceX will try to catch both first and second stage on this flight.
The company said that its goal is to be performing one launch per week by the end of 1H27 and twice a week by end of 2027.
At that point, SpaceX plans to have five operating launch pads (2 in Texas and 3 in Florida). If production of Starship second
stages can be performed at least at one every two weeks with reasonable reuse frequency, the planned launch cadence would
be feasible. We have not assumed, however, that the company progresses that rapidly. Besides, SpaceX aims to re-fly a Starship

Connectivity - Strong E&G momentum with mobile roadmap taking shape
Connectivity is the only segment generating cash at this stage and its operating margin of 45% beat margin estimates of
38%. Because this business becomes heavily about leveraging an inherently global constellation, there should be strong
opportunities to expand margins and/or profitably add subscribers in lower income geographies at lower rates with negligible
added cost. Revenue of $5.25bn was well above consensus of $4.86bn. On Starlink overall, management reiterated its core
message that a significant capacity and performance step-up is expected with the deployment of V3 satellites. Besides, the
production of V5 kits has started, easing inventory constraints. Our outlook for Connectivity is shown in Exhibit 3.
Starlink consumer broadband sequentially gained 1.6m consumer subscribers vs 2Q26 to reach 13.6mn subscribers in Q3.
Sequential subscriber growth slowed to 13% from 17% in the prior quarter, which makes reaching the company’s prior goal of
17m subscribers look unlikely. The ARPU was sequentially improved, reaching $69, benefiting from favorable subscriber mix.
The key takeaway, in our view, is that Starlink’s economics are improving faster than its consumer subscriber base. We slightly
lower our Consumer Broadband revenue driven by a slower ramp up in subscribers than expected, albeit mostly compensated
by a maintained ARPU

The company highlighted the growing importance of Enterprise & Government, with revenues up 91% in the quarter,

The company emphasized its commitment to the Mobile business, where we have been most skeptical. Comments

AI - Increased terrestrial capacity with a better $/watt
High pricing and more capacity. SpaceX AI segment generated $7.56bn of revenue, c.20% above consensus. This was
driven primarily by a faster capacity ramp of terrestrial compute and an high $/watt. The operating income of $431m was above
consensus of -$14m, essentially driven by the higher $/watt pricing that SpaceX achieved. AI Capex came in at $16.44bn. For
its AI segment, SPCX reported 1.7GW of nameplate compute capacity. That compute capacity is expected to be in the range of
2.5-3 GW by year-end. Some investors remain concerned that SpaceX’s capex over the next two years will build capacity ahead
of demand. However, these strong results and the company’s upgraded compute guidance should ease those concerns and
reinforce its case for continued investment. The justification for this investment is linked to the pricing the company is seeing
on short-term contracts for compute capacity. This started with deals with Google and others at $30-$50 per watt. The view at
SpaceX is that short-term deals are best because they see pricing going higher, with evidence from negotiations the companies
are having with new customers today. In these negotiations, management indicated pricing above that $30-50 level.

Accelerated AI buildout driving meaningful revenue growth, but also materially higher capex. The company guided to
Q4 capex of $35-$40B, well above consensus (~$22B), reflecting an accelerated AI infrastructure buildout. Looking further
out, management now expects "almost certainly" an incremental 10GW of compute capacity by the end of 2027, with a "non-
zero probability" of reaching 20GW. Elon Musk indicated that the company's objective is to have sufficient power, cooling, and
supporting infrastructure in place to enable 20GW of capacity by year-end 2027, highlighting infrastructure availability as
the primary bottleneck to scaling. While management aims to maintain excess infrastructure capacity relative to immediate
deployment needs, it continues to view a 20GW outcome as “not impossible”.
Substantial capex ahead. Despite the substantial increase in investment, management emphasized that demand remains
exceptionally strong, with current payback periods below one year and potentially falling to six months next year. This
investment ramp underpins Q1 revenue guidance of $33B-$37B (midpoint $35B), well ahead of consensus estimates of
roughly $23B. Elon further suggested a potential 2027 revenue exit rate (December annualized) of $500B-$1T, driven by
higher installed capacity and sustained revenue per GW. For reference, current consensus estimates FY29 SpaceX revenue at
approximately $290B. On the orbital AI front, the company indicated there has been no material change to its pre-IPO roadmap
(not planned to be operational before mid-2028).
Terafab coming, aggressive targets. While acknowledging that the company is just beginning to figure out chip fabrication,
Elon outlined targets for the ramp, with first test chips targeted in 2027 and volume production by Q328. He at least admitted
freely that these are very aggressive targets (we agree), but his Terafab aspirations appear genuinely real if not in concrete just
yet.
Power build and turbine blade plans. As Elon has previously said, the bottleneck in producing industrial gas turbine power
for a 20GW AI buildout is turbine blades, which come primarily from three major suppliers (Howmet, PCC, CPP). Starting from
the capabilities SpaceX internally has to make castings used in rocket motors, the company believes it can produce blades
for industrial gas turbines (IGT). But, the blades used in rocket motors are not for turbines, but for pumps that drive propellant
into the motor. It is not clear to us how easily that capability will transfer to IGT blades. In any case, our understand is that the
company will not be producing any castings that could be used in IGTs before 2028. This means that these casting would make
no contribution to the power ramp in 2027. Nevertheless, our discussions have indicated that SpaceX has been aggressively
working to capture IGT capacity from suppliers worldwide.
We have revised upward our terrestrial AI buildout assumptions from 2026 onward. We now forecast installed capacity of 2.8
GW by year-end 2026, rising to 12.8 GW by year-end 2027 and 20.1 GW by year-end 2028. Thereafter, we expect capacity
expansion to be driven exclusively by orbital deployments (Exhibit 4). As a result, we increase our capex build out in line with
the deployed terrestrial capacity (Exhibit 5). We assume $35bn/per additional GW deployed. We expect capex to peak in 2031,
driven by rising maintenance requirements for the expanded terrestrial compute capacity and investment in orbital data center
development.
Our AI revenue forecasts are driven by two primary factors: compute capacity (GW) and pricing ($/W). Alongside our higher
capacity assumptions, we have increased our pricing estimates, reflecting our view that the company is well positioned to
capture premium contract rates in the near to medium term. Assuming the persistent scarcity of AI compute supply relative
to demand as discussed above, we expect management to maintain strong pricing discipline and achieve higher $/W than
previously assumed. As a result, we significantly increase our $/W assumptions for Compute & Intelligence. But, our forecast for
pricing is well below what the company is describing. So, there may be upside to our numbers if the company can continue to
realize the pricing it has achieved or if Grok becomes much more successful. Nevertheless, we continue to assume pricing will
decline gradually over time as supply catches up with demand (Exhibit 7). But, that supply/demand balance for compute over
the medium to long-term is a source of ongoing debate.

VALUATION: WE INCREASE OUR PT TO $268
We use a sum-of-the-parts approach, with AI most important. Our approach is as follows:
• Valuation based on 2031 EBITDA. It is necessary to apply a multiple on outer years because that is when the most
important business (orbital data center compute) is material. If one uses an earlier year (e.g., 2028) it would be applying a
multiple to a business that has little to do with the ultimate source of growth.
• AI is the dominant source of value. We project AI to deliver $571bn in revenues in 2031, with EBITDA of $477bn (Exhibit
8).
• Multiple for AI business in 2031. We use a basket of AI-proximate stocks (e.g., NVDA, AVGO, ASML, CRWV) with a median
forward EV/EBITDA multiple of 17X (Exhibit 9). The multiple has declined in recent months, given the sector’s high volatility.
For Connectivity, there are no good comparables - i.e., you cannot use satellite communications companies like AST Space
or network operators like Verizon or T-Mobile. We apply an above S&P forward EV/EBITDA multiple of 15X. Space is small,
given limited growth in third-party revenues. We apply a current Aerospace & Defense forward EV/EBITDA multiple of 13X.
• Late stage venture discount rates. We use a high end late stage venture discount rate of 35% for AI. We go to the low end
for Connectivity of 25% and increase our discount rate for Space to 15% (from 10%) to reflect the rise in market-wide costs
of equity.
