ExxonMobil Corp. 
3Q26 Review: Beat driven by refining, 
long-term project backlog is building 
Reiterate Rating: NEUTRAL | PO: 181.00 USD | Price: 183.29 USD   
   
 
 
Refining Strength Drives Beat 
Exxon announced a beat to headline earnings with EPS of $4.23/sh vs BofA/Street 
$4.13sh. The result was primarily driven by refining throughput (91% utilization) and 
refining capture ahead of expectations, supported by XOM’s highest global diesel yield 
and North American diesel volumes as well as optimized maintenance despite downtime 
at the Joliet refinery and impacted refining operations in the Middle East and Asia. 
Refining strength was partially offset by weaker than expected upstream and specialty 
products ($7.3bn/$1.3bn vs Street $7.4/$1.4bn respectively). Free cash flow was in line 
with expectations ($16.5bn) on in-line capex of $7bn, which, alongside a flat buyback 
cadence ($5bn), allowed XOM to use excess cash to pay down debt and further 
strengthen its industry-leading balance sheet. YTD, the company has reduced net debt 
by $11bn, lowering net debt to capital to a peer-leading 7.5%. XOM raised its dividend 
4% in the quarter and looking ahead, announced plans for another $20bn of share 
repurchases in 2027, in-line with 2026 and underscoring their commitment to 
shareholder returns through the cycle. Exxon continues to execute against its core 
strategic priorities, with advantaged asset production on track to reach 65% of total 
volumes by 2030 (58% YTD) and structural cost reductions up $0.6bn QoQ to $16.9bn. 
Downstream outlook supports Investments 
On the call CEO Darren Woods mentioned that refining cracks might not return to 
normalized levels until after 2027, underscoring the attractive risk/reward of incremental 
brownfield expansions across XOM’s refining portfolio. Management remains focused on 
high grading its downstream assets through investments in refineries with scale and 
ability to produce higher value products, a strategy that has helped increase diesel yields 
by 3.5% since 2019. XOM highlighted the recent $2bn Beaumont expansion (+250kbd in 
2023), which achieved a three-year payback. Looking ahead, incremental capacity growth 
includes the USGC asset reconfiguration (2028-2030), expected to add another 100kbd 
of distillate capacity.  
Upstream and LNG outlook are bright 
Exxon highlighted continued growth opportunities across its advantaged upstream 
portfolio, implying Guyana has significant potential in 2030+, while ongoing Permian 
advancements and efficiencies could drive further upside to the company’s 2030 
guidance even though FY26 guidance was held flat at 1.8mbd. In LNG, XOM announced 
asset swaps to secure operatorship and greater control over PNG LNG and Rovuma LNG, 
allowing the company to better leverage its capabilities and expertise in building large 
scale projects. Key LNG projects remain on track with Rovuma LNG (Mozambique) still 
expected to FID in 2026 and Golden Pass train 2 expected to be mechanically complete 
before YE26 and Train 3 mechanically complete by mid-2027.
