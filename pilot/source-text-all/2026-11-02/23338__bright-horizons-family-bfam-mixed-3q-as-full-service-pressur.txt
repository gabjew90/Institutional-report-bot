Bright Horizons reported mixed 3Q results, with revenue modestly 
below consensus, EBITDA margins in-line and EPS above 
expectations, though the company lowered its full-year revenue and 
EPS outlook. Full Service enrollment declined modestly y/y as new 
family starts fell short, leaving occupancy roughly flat y/y in the 
mid-60% range, and BFAM expects additional enrollment pressure 
in 4Q from the UK regulatory review. Center closures also reduced 
Full Service revenue growth by ~200 bps, and Australia remained a 
meaningful drag on enrollment, margins and the tax rate, prompting 
BFAM to evaluate strategic alternatives with urgency. Back-Up Care 
remains BFAM’s primary growth engine, with strong fulfillment, 
healthy new-user growth and penetration below 5% of eligible 
employees supporting the company’s 11%-13% long-term growth 
target. Underlying Full Service operating margins also increased 40 
bps y/y excluding Australia, supported by tuition increases ahead of 
wage growth, operating efficiencies and center rationalization. We 
believe a potential Australia exit, continued Back-Up Care growth, 
and improving underlying Full Service profitability support solid 
long-term EPS growth, though weaker enrollment, continued center 
closures, and reduced 2026 guidance point to softer near-term 
earnings growth. Maintain Buy. 
3Q 2026 Financial Highlights

increased 4.8% y/y, in line with our forecast but slightly below 
FactSet consensus growth of 5.0%. Full Service revenue grew 1.3% 
y/y, slightly above our 1.0% estimate, as tuition increases were 
partially offset by lower enrollment. Back-Up Care revenue rose 
13.0% y/y, in line with our forecast, driven by growth in unique users 
and utilization. Educational Advisory revenue declined 2.4% y/y, 
modestly below our estimate for a 2.0% decline, reflecting lower 
participant utilization in EdAssist.

3Q 2026 Positives / Negatives 
Positives:  
Lower-occupancy centers showed progress. The share of centers below 40% 
n
occupancy improved to 9% from 12% a year ago, reflecting enrollment gains and 
center rationalization, and enrollment continued to grow across the middle and 
bottom cohorts. That said, the share of centers above 70% occupancy declined to 
42% from 44%, as preschool graduations outpaced new family starts in the top 
cohort. 
Australia strategic action could remove a persistent drag. BFAM is reviewing 
n
strategic alternatives for Australia and said it is moving with urgency after 
reassessing the market’s longer-term prospects. A transaction or exit could simplify 
the Full Service portfolio, improve its underlying growth and margin profile, and 
reduce the tax pressure associated with Australia’s nondeductible losses. 
Back-Up Care execution and growth runway remain strong. BFAM delivered high 
n
fulfillment during peak summer demand after adding new and often exclusive care 
partners, expanding capacity in high-demand markets, and improving 
personalization and booking. New-user growth remained healthy across care types, 
and penetration remains below 5% of eligible employees. Care Advising, 
AI-supported navigation and One Bright Horizons should help drive further 
participation and utilization, supporting the company’s 11-13% long-term Back-Up 
Care growth target. 
Underlying Full Service margins improved outside Australia. Full Service 
n
operating margin declined 25 bps y/y to 3.7%, but increased 40 bps excluding 
Australia despite a ~50 bps enrollment decline. Tuition increases ahead of wage 
growth, operating efficiencies and center rationalization offset lower enrollment, 
and BFAM expects Full Service operating margins to increase ~70 bps in 2026 
excluding Australia. 
Negatives:  
Enrollment softened as new family starts fell short. Full Service enrollment at 
n
centers open more than one year declined ~50 bps y/y excluding Australia, with 
Australia creating an additional ~100 bps headwind to overall enrollment. Family 
retention remained steady, but new starts did not fully replace children graduating 
into kindergarten, with most of the enrollment decline excluding Australia coming 
from the US. Occupancy averaged in the mid-60% range in 3Q, flat y/y and down 
seasonally q/q, and enrollment pressure is likely to increase in 4Q as the larger 
impact from the UK regulatory notice emerges. 
Center closures remain a meaningful revenue headwind. BFAM closed 11 centers 
n
in 3Q, including four in Australia, bringing closures through September to 42, with 
~20 additional closures planned for 4Q. Net closures reduced 3Q Full Service

Valuation and Risks 
 
 
We lower our 12-month price target from $93 to $80, based on 11.25x (14.0x prior) our 
rolled-forward NTM + 1YR EPS estimate of $7.14. We reduce our target multiple to 
reflect lower near-term revenue visibility given weakness in Australia and the UK. Our 
target multiple is at a discount to the Business Services peer median of 18.1x and 
represents 0.6x the S&P500’s NTM P/E, a discount to BFAM’s 10-year average 
SPX-relative NTM P/E multiple of 1.7x, to account for the ongoing recovery in occupancy 
rates. 
Risks to the downside for BFAM shares include a slower-than-anticipated recovery in 
childcare center attendance, labor supply shortages, structurally reduced demand for 
on-site childcare services with an increase in work-from-home, wage inflation, financial 
leverage and FX risk.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
