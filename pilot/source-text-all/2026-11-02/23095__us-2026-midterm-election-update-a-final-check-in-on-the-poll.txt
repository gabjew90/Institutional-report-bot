US 2026 midterm election update: a final check-in on 
the polls 
◼ 
We offer another relatively short update this week, providing a final 
check-in on the polls barring a major unexpected shift over the coming 
week, as we plan to focus next week’s election morning update on 
logistics for the day ahead such as when the polls close and when 
results may be available. 
◼ 
Polls for the generic Congressional ballot have not shifted much over 
the past couple of weeks, yet once again the Cook Political Report has 
shifted additional races towards the Democrats. In total, the Cook 
Political Report shifted another 19 races towards Democrats, including 
six from ‘Toss-up’ to ‘Lean D’, and now sees the most likely outcome as 
Democrats gaining between 12-22 seats. Even the lower bound of this 
range would be well more than the minimum of three seats they would 
need to regain control of this chamber, keeping a Democratic House as 
a near certainty, with odds of around 90%, in our view. 
◼ 
In the Senate, the Cook Political Report has shifted a couple of notable 
races, one in favour of Republicans (Minnesota from ‘Likely D’ to ‘Lean 
D’) and one in favour of Democrats (South Carolina from ‘Likely R’ to 
‘Lean R’). However, nothing has changed in terms of the ‘Toss-up’ 
ratings, and we continue to lean towards Democrats being able to gain 
the net four seats they would need to take control as the base case, 
though this is much more up in the air than the House as we would 
place the odds around 65%. 
◼ 
In terms of the policy and economic impact, we will again refer to a 
previous article for more details, as we maintain our view outlined a few 
weeks ago: Senate control is not necessarily a game changer, as a 
Trump Presidency combined with a Democratic House would likely 
mean legislative gridlock either way. However, there are some ways in 
which the result would make a difference, including in terms of judges 
and other appointments as well as oversight & investigations. 
◼ 
Aside from the direct impact of what is possible on the policy front 
based on shifts in power in Congress, we would keep an eye on 
Trump’s reaction. If Republicans experience a comprehensive defeat, 
there is always a risk that Trump could lash out and increase his 
assertiveness in areas where the executive tends to have more ability 
to act without Congress, such as foreign policy and/or trade policy. 
Cook Political Report shifts additional House races towards Democrats 
As in our last update, the major polling for the House has been fairly steady 
in the interim period. Looking again at some of the metrics we have highlighted 
in the past, Donald Trump’s overall approval rating remains stuck in the high 30% 
range, putting him severely underwater compared to his disapproval rating and 
suggesting a possibly substantial loss of seats in the House when looking at the 
historical relationship between the President’s approval rating and his party’s 
performance in the House in past midterms. President Trump’s approval ratings 
for specific issues such as the economy (32.5%) and inflation (26.8%) are even 
worse.

Interestingly, however, despite the fairly steady polling, the Cook Political 
Report’s race ratings showed another clear shift toward Democrats in the 
latest update. Here, the Cook Political report shifted an additional 19 race 
ratings towards Democrats, after 18 were shifted in this direction just prior to our 
last update, including moving six races from ‘Toss-up’ to ‘Lean D’, four of these 
seats currently  held by Republican incumbents. 
Taking this into account, the Cook Political Report now sees the most likely 
outcome as Democrats gaining between 12 and 22 seats, up from a range 
of 9-19 seats previously, which would equate to between 227-237 seats out 
of the 435 total. Given that Democrats would only need to gain a net of three 
seats to reach the 218 needed for a majority (if we assign the two vacancies to 
the party that last held the seat), then even the lower end of the Cook Political 
Report's projections would result in a clear Democratic majority. This remains 
consistent with our view, shared by betting markets, that Democrats are a very 
strong favourite to emerge with a House majority, with odds of around 90%.

As we have noted in the past, the silver lining for Republicans is that 
redistricting fights (eg, gerrymandering) have limited the number of seats 
that are truly in play, making a wave election with a 30-40+ seat gain an 
unlikely occurrence this go around. Thus, Democrats may only end up gaining 
a relatively slender majority, but it would still be a major surprise if they did 
not re-take control.

We once again reiterate the view we have outlined in the past that the 
Senate can be a different animal that comes down to a handful of individual 
races depending on the cycle’s map in that year, but we still see polling as 
suggesting a Democratic advantage in 2026. That said, this is a much closer 
call, with Republicans probably having roughly a one-in-three chance of narrowly 
hanging onto their majority, in our view, similar to the latest Polymarket odds 
which moved sharply in favour of Democrats earlier in the autumn but have been 
more stable over the past few weeks (see Figure 2).

Figure 3 shows the key races to watch according to the Cook Political 
Report, and in this case, only a couple have really shifted, and none of the 
‘Toss-up’ races. Specifically: 
⚫ 
Minnesota was moved to ‘Lean D’ from ‘Likely D’, suggesting a glimmer of 
hope that Republicans could flip this seat, though we would still consider 
Democrats a favourite to hang on this this seat. 
⚫ 
Offsetting this, South Carolina was moved to ‘Lean R’ from ‘Likely R’, still 
putting Republicans as a favourite to defend this seat, but adding to the list of 
solidly red states where the result is at least somewhat at risk. 
Fig 4. Summary of Senate races to watch 
 
Source: Cook Political Report, RealClearPolitics, Crédit Agricole CIB 
As usual, we provide more information in Figure 4, including a comparison 
of the latest polls to those from two weeks ago. Overall, there has not been a 
major shift, with a couple moving towards Democrats (eg, the North Carolina lead 
jumping even more, the margin in Ohio widening slightly), but a couple towards 
Republicans (eg, taking a slight lead in Iowa, the race in Texas tightening). 
That said, polls in most of these states would still give a slight edge to 
Democrats. While we acknowledge that the polls can be off (eg, we have 
previously highlighted that Susan Collins in Maine has a history of 
outperforming), we still think the latest would paint Democrats as a modest 
favourite to gain the net four seats needed to re-take control, though this is 
a close enough call that a shift in couple of states over the ensuing weeks 
(or a couple of polling errors) could make a difference in the outlook.
