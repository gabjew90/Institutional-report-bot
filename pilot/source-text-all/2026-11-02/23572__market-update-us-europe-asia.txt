U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.30% | Nasdaq -1.38% | Dow -0.25%
Synopsis

Major US indices finished mostly lower on Thursday, with the S&P 500 down 0.30%, the Dow down 0.25% and the Nasdaq down 1.38%,
while the Russell 2000 gained 1.01%. Breadth was very positive: S&P 500 gainers outnumbered decliners by four to one and the equal-
weight index outperformed the cap-weighted index by about 150 bp. The AI trade was weaker as semiconductors and memory gave back
some of their large weekly gains and neoclouds came under pressure. Alphabet was the main Magnificent 7 drag, and Microsoft could not
hold early gains as its $25B bonding plan came under scrutiny. Treasury yields fell 3-8 bp after Wednesday's bearish steepening, the dollar
index fell 0.2% and WTI crude settled 0.8% lower.

Data were supportive: first-read Q3 GDP of 3.4% beat the 3.2% consensus, September core PCE rose 0.3% m/m in line with consensus, and
initial jobless claims of 194K were below the 199K forecast. Analysts still largely expect a December Fed hike after Wednesday's hold. Oil
was choppy as Iran diplomacy headlines contrasted with expectations of renewed military action after the midterms.


Software and cybersecurity lagged: ServiceNow fell 27.0% on a softer cRPO guide and Fortinet fell 20.1% on an underwhelming beat and
raise, while Alphabet fell 12.6% on Search deceleration despite Google Cloud growth of 103%. Pharma and biotech, restaurants, banks,
payments and homebuilders outperformed. Glaukos rose 33.7% on a revenue beat and higher guidance, Crocs rose 25.5% after raising
guidance for the third time, and Eli Lilly rose 6.6% on Mounjaro and Zepbound sales ahead of consensus. Marvell fell 13.2% on a report it
is in talks to acquire Lumentum.
Macro

Alphabet Search growth slows, but Google Cloud accelerates
Google came under pressure following mostly softer Q3 results. Search, the key area of scrutiny, grew +12% versus +17% in Q2 and the
+15% consensus. The company cited FX, tough comps, softer retail and media spending, and lower monetization of AI Mode queries, which
received outsized attention given concerns about AI headwinds on Search economics. The company said it feels good about its monetization
trajectory, noting most AI Mode queries are incremental. Google Cloud was the standout, with growth accelerating to +103% from +82%.
Operating margin expanded about 1,250 bp to 36.2% and backlog reached $561B, with about 55% expected to convert within 24 months.

Microsoft Azure growth accelerates in fiscal Q1 results
Microsoft posted better fiscal Q1 results. Azure grew +48% in constant currency against about +45% consensus, driven by Foundry and
better CPU monetization, and the company guided to further acceleration to +49-50% in the Dec Q despite some third-party capacity slip-
ping to 2H. M365 Commercial Cloud grew 19%, Copilot daily users rose nearly 50% q/q, Foundry revenue more than tripled, and commer-
cial RPO rose 73% to $677B. More cautious commentary revolved around slower M365 seat growth, an 11% decline in commercial bookings,
no updated Copilot seat count, and capex set to accelerate in 2H.

Core PCE in line, Q3 GDP beats, jobless claims stay subdued
September core PCE increased 0.3% m/m, in line with consensus, while August was revised up to +0.3% (was +0.2%). Annualized core came
in at +3.1%, and headline PCE rose 0.5% m/m, the highest since Apr-26. September personal spending rose 0.5% m/m versus +0.6% con-
sensus and personal income increased 0.2% versus +0.3% consensus. The first read of Q3 GDP came in at +3.4% versus +3.2% consensus
and Q2's +2.2%, helped by solid consumer spending and investment. Initial jobless claims came in at 194K versus 199K consensus, and
continuing claims at 1701K versus 1720K consensus.

US and regional mediators push diplomatic track with Iran
VP Vance sent a letter to Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei to establish a framework for potential peace negotiations, with the outreach
authorized by Trump; reports said Vance was testing whether both sides could find enough common ground to persuade Trump not to order
an attack on Iran in coming weeks. Semafor reported the White House is in ongoing negotiations around a temporary peace deal, and
mediators led by Qatar are pushing for a deal to allow UN nuclear inspectors into Iran to avoid a massive post-midterm bombing campaign.
Proposed terms include US sanctions relief and release of frozen funds in exchange for nuclear site access. Sticking points remain, including
Iran's demand that the US lift its naval blockade and allow oil sales, and the US demand that Iran restart nuclear talks before sanctions
relief.

Analysts still largely expect a December Fed hike after the FOMC hold
Wednesday's FOMC meeting kept rates on hold, as broadly expected. Analyst recaps noted statement changes advocating 'the accumulation
of more information', and Warsh's press-conference commentary suggested an every-other-meeting cadence for potential further hikes;
BofA suggested the lack of a dissent from hawkish Logan may mean she is comfortable with this pacing. Warsh highlighted strong economic
growth and noted some impacts from September's hike are being felt, but said this has not yet become 'uncomfortable' and he could not call
conditions tight. There was a lot of comment on Warsh's plan to hold only seven policy votes next year and turn one meeting into a strategy
session. The meeting resulted in little change to market consensus for another Fed hike in December.

European Markets Recap
Germany +1.3% | France +1.1% | UK +0.3%
Synopsis

European equities finished higher on Thursday, with the DAX up 1.5%, the CAC up 1.1% and the FTSE 100 up 0.3%. Financial services,
construction/materials, autos and industrials outperformed, while food/beverage, basic resources and banks lagged. Sentiment was buoyant
on strong earnings: the market rewarded guidance raises and capital returns and sold misses and cautious outlooks even where the headline
number was a modest beat. Bond yields fell 2-6 bps after Wednesday's bearish steepening, with the Bund 2 bps lower at 3.36% after the
ECB decision and gilts 5 bps lower at 5.29%.

The ECB left key rates on hold, as expected, and said financing conditions have tightened since the last meeting, which is likely to weigh on
inflation and growth; it also confirmed that Lagarde will leave her role on 7-Feb. Eurozone economic sentiment eased in October, UK BoE
mortgage approvals were the weakest since December 2023, and the UK Budget largely met expectations with a muted impact on bond and
FX markets.

Oil & gas, chemicals and aerospace led. Salzgitter rose 10.1% after raising FY26 EBITDA guidance for the third time this year, Aixtron rose
8.2% as Q3 order intake rose 122%, and Renault rose 7.2% on guidance above consensus. Shell rose 1.6% to a record high after lifting its
quarterly buyback to $5B. Haleon fell 15% after missing Q3 expectations and cutting FY26 sales growth guidance, Knorr-Bremse fell 6.9%
on weak order intake, and Lloyds fell 5.2% on a Q3 revenue miss. Technology was under some pressure, with SAP down 1.2% on weak Q4
software guidance from US peers.
Macro

ECB holds rates and flags tighter financing conditions
The ECB left key rates unchanged, as expected. The statement pointed to the ongoing impact of rising energy prices (upside inflation risks,
downside growth risk) but emphasized that underlying inflation has remained broadly stable and wages have not shown a material response
at this stage. It highlighted that since the September meeting financing conditions have tightened, which is likely to weigh on inflation and
growth. The market was almost fully priced for unchanged policy after President Lagarde commented on the impact of higher bond yields
in recent weeks. The ECB reiterated that it remains well positioned to navigate elevated uncertainty, and consensus is for the Governing
Council to deliver another rate increase in December, with inflation forecast to accelerate into early 2027.

ECB confirms Lagarde will leave the presidency on 7-Feb
The ECB published a statement confirming that President Lagarde informed the Governing Council and European Council President Costa
that she will step down on 7-Feb, earlier than the end of her term in October 2027. A successor will be appointed following the nomination
process outlined in the EU treaties. The two most likely candidates include Spain's de Cos and the Netherlands' Knot, and economists
surveyed by Bloomberg earlier this month said BIS head de Cos is the favorite. Germany has not put forward a candidate, though Bundes-
bank governor Nagel is keen on the role, and France is said to be pushing for the chief economist role.

Eurozone economic sentiment weakens more than expected in October
The EU Commission said the ESI dropped to 96.9 from 97.5 in September, versus consensus 97.7. The decline was the second in a row and
the weakest reading since June, driven by lower confidence in all sectors except industry, with elevated energy prices linked to the Iran
conflict weighing on consumers and businesses. Consumer confidence weakened for a second straight month to its lowest level since May.
ING noted the Eurozone economy has so far shown relative resilience, with PMIs indicating continued expansion this month, albeit at a
slower pace, and Capital Economics noted sentiment is consistent with at-trend quarterly GDP growth of about 0.3%.

UK Chancellor Healey defers big fiscal decisions in lowkey Budget
UK Chancellor Healey deferred big fiscal decisions in Wednesday's lowkey Budget, which included many small measures to support house-
holds and businesses, but mostly very modest in size. He was rewarded with a relatively muted bond market reaction, with a modest fall in
yields, although takeaways highlighted a lack of clarity over fiscal consolidation. Sell-side economists pointed out that consolidation mostly
takes place towards the end of the parliament, which might be unrealistic into a general election, and next year's Spending Review becomes
increasingly important amid sell-side estimates of a fiscal gap of at least £25B. Healey vowed in post-Budget interviews that the welfare bill
would be cut.

Eurozone inflation set to rise further in October on energy and food
Preliminary October regional inflation prints from European countries start tomorrow, ahead of the 4-Nov Eurozone flash HICP release.
Consensus looks for headline inflation to edge up from September's 3.8% toward about 3.9% y/y, on track to approach 4% by year-end, with
core expected to hold near 2.5% y/y. Wholesale gas and electricity prices continue to pass through, keeping energy HICP at about 20% y/y,
and food inflation is also set to firm. The Street remains hawkish: Goldman Sachs sees core peaking at 2.7% in Q1 2027, ABN Amro and
MUFG see headline moving above 4% in coming months, and Commerzbank sees headline not declining until spring 2027.

Asian Markets Recap
Japan +1.10% | Hong Kong -0.59% | China -0.82%
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mixed on Thursday: the Nikkei 225 gained 1.10%, the Hang Seng fell 0.59%, the Shanghai Composite fell 0.82% and
the Kospi fell 1.24%. Taiwan's Taiex also ended lower, mainland China benchmarks were led down by tech stocks and Shenzhen's main
board, and the Hang Seng dipped sharply into the close, while Australia, India and Singapore posted small gains. JGB yields were higher.
Technology drove the divergence: Advantest's well-received earnings supported Kioxia and Tokyo Electron in a chip-led Japan rally, while
the Kospi came under pressure after Samsung Electronics' Q3 net profit missed and no fresh shareholder return program was announced.
China's STAR market lost 4.7%, with analysts saying growing competition is adding to valuation concerns.

Ahead of Friday's BOJ decision, consensus expects no change, with the Outlook Report expected to declare that underlying inflation has
effectively reached the 2% target, which would bolster the case for a December hike. The RBNZ governor said the chances of a rate hike in
December are 'high', and South Korea's October manufacturing sentiment weakened marginally.

Topsports rose 11.7% on H1 revenue ahead of estimates and Advantest rose 6% after raising FY guidance. Daewoo Engineering & Construc-
tion fell 18.6% as talks on the Czech Dukovany contract reportedly collapsed, New Oriental fell 14% on a non-GAAP operating income miss,
and Hitachi and UMC each fell 9.8% on results below estimates. Samsung Electronics fell 4.1% despite record Q3 operating profit, as its
chip division missed forecasts. Siam Cement and PTT Global are to merge their petrochemical units in a $5.5B deal.
Macro

Samsung Q3 net profit misses; no shareholder return plan disappoints
Samsung Electronics posted record Q3 operating profit of KRW107T ($80B), in line with the company's preliminary announcement and an
almost ninefold increase y/y amid strong AI-related memory demand. Revenue of KRW195T was also in line, but net profit of KRW75T
missed the consensus of KRW86.1T, and the lack of a fresh shareholder return plan disappointed investors. Guidance was positive: the
company said the chip shortage will likely deepen next year, expects foundry revenue to grow by 'double-digit' percent in Q4, and said capex
will rise almost 60% to KRW84.4T ($63B) next year versus the consensus of KRW74.4T. Semiconductor profits of KRW106.5T were just
below the KRW108.5T consensus, while Mobile lost KRW600B.

BOJ widely expected to hold rates; inflation language in focus
The BOJ is widely expected to leave the policy rate unchanged at the 29-30 October meeting. No additional reports from Nikkei followed
last week's front-page article affirming no policy change while echoing earlier articles indicating the Outlook Report will declare underlying
inflation has effectively reached the 2% target. The latest Bloomberg poll showed unanimous calls for no rate change, with the focus on the
BOJ's inflation narrative, which is seen bolstering the case for a December hike, although this has long been the main scenario. The meeting
also brings the neutral rate back to the fore: the BOJ has been reluctant to be more specific than its estimated range of 1.1-2.5%, and
consensus terminal rate forecasts are tracking at around 1.75%.

Chinese tech stocks sell off again as ChiNext and STAR 50 slump
Chinese tech stocks tumbled again on Thursday with ChiNext down 4.5% and STAR 50 falling 4.3%, as both indexes head for six straight
weeks of losses. AI hardware, semiconductors, consumer electronics and software stocks all saw heavy selloffs despite some companies
reporting impressive quarterly results (Bloomberg). On the STAR 50 side, Biwin Storage Technology plunged 15.8% after a more than 500%
rise in profit, while Moore Threads Technology slid 12.5% despite sales surging nearly 1,800% y/y in Q3. The index slumped nearly 20%
MTD to its lowest since Sep-25 but is still considered expensive at about 38x forward earnings, compared with less than 5x for the Kospi.
CNBC added that the sell-off started as a valuation correction and some profit-taking but has now led investors to question whether earnings
can support valuations.

SK Hynix Q3 beats forecasts and sees memory shortfall lasting through 2030
SK Hynix Q3 operating profit of KRW83.3T beat the KRW77.5T consensus, with revenue of KRW104.6T also ahead of the KRW99T forecast,
underpinned by a +20% q/q increase in ASP. The company repeated that it expects the memory supply gap to last through 2030, and said
LTAs signed with 14 key customers lock in volumes corresponding to more than 50% of production capacity in 2027 and 2028. It anticipates
a significant increase in FY27 capex. Capital return was a focus: the company outlined plans for a KRW20T buyback (50% returned to
shareholders and 50% to employees) and a total cash dividend of KRW14T including a special dividend from the Kioxia investment sale,
bringing total return for 2027 to around KRW100T ($75B).

Japan faces another wave of food price hikes as Middle East turmoil lifts costs
Nikkei discussed the prospects of a second wave of surging food prices in Japan as Middle East turmoil drives up prices for naphtha-derived
container feedstocks and freight rates for crude oil tankers. Teikoku Databank figures showed prices were raised on 4,965 food and beverage
items in September, more than triple the number a year earlier; the number falls to 3,153 in October, in line with a year earlier, and is
projected to decline further to 1,469 in November. Rates for Middle East shipments to Asia are now nearly six times higher than before the
closure of the Strait of Hormuz and have hit a record high. The article noted it generally takes about six months for higher crude oil prices
to be passed on to end consumers.
