Downgrade of global 
equities to a Core allocation 
Summary 
Event: We are downgrading global equities from Overweight 
to a Neutral/Core allocation. We continue to maintain a 
Neutral allocation to fixed income, Overweight on Gold and 
Underweight on Cash. There are more details in our Global 
Market Outlook publication on 30 October 2026. 
What does the equity downgrade mean?  
This downgrade implies a slightly lower allocation to global 
equities within a diversified portfolio. For our CIO Foundation 
Balanced portfolio, this would imply an equity allocation of 
52.5%, down from 53.2%. We continue to expect positive 
returns for global equities in the coming 6-12 months, 
though equity investors may need to recalibrate expectations 
lower, compared with the 15-20% annualised gains 
experienced in the last few years. Our capital market 
assumptions still suggest a mid-to-high single digit annual 
returns over the next few years, consistent with a transition 
to a mid-cycle bull market. Consensus estimates point to 
16% earnings growth for global equities in 2027, which 
should overcome various headwinds to deliver positive 
returns for equities over the next 12 months. Investors should 
thus, continue to maintain exposure to equities within a 
diversified portfolio. 
In addition, we see a changing playbook to prepare for 
higher volatility, more active management and greater 
diversification, as discussed below. 
What does it mean for regional equities 
positioning? 
Within a global equities portfolio, we remain Overweight US 
and Asia ex-Japan (AxJ) equities, which are expected to 
deliver the highest earnings growth in 2027 amongst the 
major regions. This growth is led by the technology sector, 
which carries the largest weight in both regions. Within the 
diversified equities portfolio, we maintain Core allocations to

What are the implications for equity 
opportunistic ideas?

Global High-Dividend offers higher-income equity allocation 
with historically lower volatility than global equities. US 
healthcare is supported by wider adoption of obesity 
treatments, advances in oncology and innovative medical 
devices, that support earnings growth. Euro area banks 
continue to see steady earnings growth adding to their 
excess capital, which is being returned to shareholders via 
attractive dividend and share buyback yields. Japan banks 
continue to enjoy net margin expansion from BoJ policy 
normalisation,

weight, providing exposure to relatively resilient demand that 
is more insulated from economic cycles and AI trends. It also 
has a sizeable financial sector supported by steady wealth 
management growth and well-capitalised insurers with 
disciplined underwriting and resilient premium income. 
We see the case for more active management in a mid-
cycle transition, as higher market dispersion should create 
greater opportunities for active sector and stock selection, 
particularly as leadership within equities evolves. 
What are the implications of higher equity 
market volatility?  
A more uncertain political backdrop and potentially maturing 
growth rates in certain market segments may contribute to 
more frequent bouts of market volatility. Higher volatility 
presents an opportunity to use structured strategies to gain 
exposure to equities. Such strategies may be used to 
generate income or to gain exposure at a discounted entry 
price. Investors can also consider strategies that are 
structured to return principal or partial principal at maturity.

Equity long/short hedge fund strategies would retain growth 
participation with less market exposure. Fund managers buy 
companies they expect to outperform and short those they

What are the implications on fixed income 
and FX markets? 
Bonds: This year’s surge in bond yields means bonds now 
offer a hedge against any equity market weakness. We 
believe the 3-7-year part of the US bond yield curve offers 
the best hedge against any equity sell-off caused by AI-
related growth worries. This part of the bond curve has priced 
in most Fed rate hike expectations, which could reverse if 
growth concerns return. Meanwhile, we favour US Treasury 
Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) as a hedge against any 
fuel price-led inflation scare.  
Credit: For corporate bonds, adopting a defensive stance in 
a volatile environment typically involves: i) increasing credit 
quality, favouring higher-rated issuers; and ii) moving up the 
capital structure, by reducing exposure to subordinated debt 
and favouring secured debt. From a sector perspective, 
large, diversified banks, utilities, and quasi-sovereigns tend 
to be more defensive, particularly relative to cyclical sectors, 
such as metals and mining, exploration, and technology.        
FX: We favour bearish AUD/JPY as a tactical hedge against 
any equity market correction. Review of 21 past S&P500 
correction sessions (2010-2025) shows AUD weakened 
against USD in 86% of cases, while JPY strengthened 
against USD in 67%, supporting our bearish AUD/JPY 
outlook during any equity market stress.
