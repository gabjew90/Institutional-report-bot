Governor’s Press Conference: Appropriate Monetary Policy Operations 
to Anchor Inflation Expectations Are Necessary 
The BOJ held its MPM on October 29-30, and as was widely expected, decided to 
maintain its policy rate guidance on the overnight call rate (TONA) at 1.25% (by a 
majority vote). Board member Hajime Takata dissented, proposing a rate hike to 1.50%. 
In the Outlook Report, the BOJ revised up its underlying inflation outlook from the 
previous expectation of rising to 2% “between the second half of fiscal 2026 and fiscal 
2027” to state that the underlying inflation rate “is at a level that is generally consistent 
with the price stability target of 2 percent.” 
Questions at the post-meeting press conference centered on the anchoring of 
underlying inflation at 2% and the BOJ’s response to upside risks to underlying inflation. 
Governor Kazuo Ueda explained the rationale for revising the current assessment of 
underlying inflation, stating that indicators of underlying inflation, namely, price 
indicators excluding special factors, inflation expectations, and trend inflation estimated 
from economic models have risen to near 2%. He noted all nine board members 
unanimously agreed that underlying inflation has reached a level broadly consistent with 
the 2% price stability target, given these indicator movements and underlying 
circumstances. 
He then emphasized the continued importance of anchoring underlying inflation at 
2%, as he did at the September MPM. Noting underlying inflation has already reached 
2%, he reiterated his intention to respond appropriately to upside risks, as a visible rise 
from the level is undesirable. 
Regarding upside risk factors for underlying inflation, the Governor cited the same 
three risks as at the September meeting: the Middle East situation, AI-related demand, 
and exchange rates. Furthermore, as noted in the Outlook Report, he expressed stepped 
up vigilance over the effect on Japan’s economy and prices from changing global 
financial conditions—driven by foreign central banks shifting to rate hikes amid 
global inflation stemming from these risk factors. At the press conference, he specifically 
mentioned that the US and European rate hikes could transmit via multiple mechanisms, 
such as medium- to long-term bond markets and exchange rates. 
Asked what constitutes underlying inflation anchored at 2%, Governor Ueda 
described a state where (1) headline CPI inflation returns to 2% even after deviating 
upward due to a temporary shock, and (2) inflation expectations remain anchored at 2% 
without deviating upward when shocks occur. He added that appropriate monetary 
policy operations will anchor inflation expectations at 2%, leading underlying 
inflation to also anchor at 2%. 
While stating the timing of the next rate hike is basically data-dependent, by noting 
the BOJ will make decisions by examining the likelihood of realizing its outlook and risks 
at every MPM, including the next meeting, Governor Ueda did not deny the possibility of 
a December rate hike. When asked about the pace of approaching the neutral rate, he 
said the frequency such as once every few months, cannot be decided in advance. 
On the effect of cumulative rate hikes on the economy so far, the Governor said the 
BOJ would check this gradually while closely monitoring financial conditions, carefully 
examining the tightening of corporate financing through various data and corporate 
hearings, especially for small and micro enterprises. This suggested the future pace of

Now Expecting the Next BOJ Rate Hike This December 
We are bringing forward our forecast for the next BOJ rate hike to December 2026, from 
January 2027. We make this change for the following reasons. 
First, the Governor’s communication highlighted vigilance toward upside price risks, 
keeping the December rate hike an open possibility. He repeated that monetary policy 
conduct will place more importance on anchoring underlying inflation at 2%, and in 
doing so, appropriate policy responses are important to anchor inflation expectations at 
2%. He clearly stated that since inflation has already reached 2%, a visible rise from the 
level is undesirable. In addition, there were no messages at the press conference 
attempting to derail market expectation for a December rate hike as mentioned above. 
Second, as mentioned above, the BOJ indicated its caution regarding the impact of rate 
hikes by foreign central banks on Japanese prices and other factors. In the Outlook 
Report, the BOJ added wording that many central banks, including those in the US and 
Europe, have shifted toward a policy of raising interest rates since the summer, and that 
it is necessary to pay attention to how these changes in global financial conditions could 
affect Japan’s economic activity and prices via global financial markets. Governor Ueda 
also commented on the risk of foreign rate hikes on the Japanese economy through the 
channel of exchange rate, and we interpret this as indicating BOJ’s caution over yen 
depreciation risks stemming from rate hikes by foreign central banks. 
Our US economics research team forecasts a rate hike at the December FOMC, and if the 
BOJ were to leave the policy rate unchanged at its December MPM eight business days 
later, the yen could depreciate on widening interest rate differentials. Given that the BOJ 
cites yen depreciation as an upside risk factor for underlying inflation and views the 
policy shift toward raising interest rates by foreign central banks as an economic activity 
and price risk factor via global financial markets (including forex), it would be difficult to 
make a policy decision that could bring about substantial yen depreciation. This raises 
the likelihood of a December rate hike, in our view. 
Emphasizing the risk that overseas rate hikes pose to the Japanese economy through yen 
depreciation, could ultimately lead to higher correlation between monetary policy 
conduct overseas (especially the US) and in Japan. Therefore, should the global 
developments in activity and prices lead to reduced probability December rate hikes by 
foreign central banks, especially the FRB, we note the timing of the BOJ’s rate hike could 
also be pushed back accordingly.  
Thus, depending on the probability of the December US rate hike, we note the likelihood 
of the BOJ’s next rate hike being pushed to January could rise again. 
Third, concerns over the deterioration of the Japanese government’s fiscal health in the 
bond market appear to have receded to some extent. We had expected that a 
December rate hike, which would coincide with the Cabinet approval and announcement 
of the initial FY2027 budget and JGB issuance plan, would be avoided as it could 
increase bond market volatility excessively. While the term premium on 10-year yields 
had risen rapidly primarily due to fiscal deterioration concerns, it has declined by over
