Macro Research 
 
   
As expected, major central banks from the US to Europe and Japan, kept rates 
on hold this week. Oil prices fluctuated in the range of USD 113-118 as 
markets speculate with the possibility of a renewed US attack on Iran after the 
midterms, and European gas futures broke the EUR 100/MWh level for the 
first time since December 2022. The French 10-year bond yield stabilised 
below 5% after Moody’s kept the country’s rating unchanged last Friday. 
The Q3 reporting season is about halfway through, and the overall 
performance is still looking solid. Results from the large tech have been a 
mixed bag, but investor reception has been weaker than the revenue growth 
alone would suggest. On the macro side of things, the investment boom 
seems intact. Taiwanese GDP grew 14.4% y/y in Q3, and exports from Taiwan 
and South Korea have continued their strong growth. 
This week, the Federal Reserve left policy rates unchanged at 3.75-4.00%, in 
line with consensus and our expectations. Even though market reaction was 
to the dovish side, we read Warsh’s comments on the economic outlook as 
hawkish, and maintain our call for two 25bp rate hikes at the December and 
March meetings. Risks are tilted towards more hikes. Read more in Research 
US – Fed Review: Fed pauses, but tightening cycle is not over, 28 October. 
The ECB also kept the deposit rate at 2.50% as expected. Despite the dovish 
tilt in the press release, we expect a 25bp hike to 2.75% in December, which 
President Lagarde clearly signalled during the press conference. Lagarde also 
announced she would step down early, on 7 February 2027. The two favorites 
to replace her are Klaas Knot of the Netherlands and Pablo Hernández de Cos 
of Spain. Read more on ECB Review – A no-hike decision, 29 October, and 
Research Euro Area – ECB’s game of musical chairs, 25 September. 
Next week’s main event will be the US election on Tuesday. Tentative results 
will be available by Wednesday morning, but final results could take weeks or 
even longer. Polymarket is giving a 69% chance of Democrats winning the 
Senate, while winning the House looks like a done deal for them. Join us for 
the Webinar: US Midterms Morning Update – First glance at the results. 
A key question for energy markets is whether the US will launch a new attack 
on Iran after the midterms. This is by no means certain, especially as US 
munitions stockpiles have depleted. The CFR wrote in August, how the US has 
used 64% of their Patriot air-defence interceptors and 40% of their THAAD 
ballistic missiles interceptors, and how replenishing the stocks will take years. 
The outcome of the Israeli parliamentary election this week was undecisive. 
Forming a government may turn out difficult, and in the meanwhile, 
Netanyahu’s caretaker government will remain in charge. 
Next week’s key data points will be the private PMIs from China, and the US 
ISM indices due on Monday and Tuesday, as well as the October jobs report 
and Michigan survey on Friday. Euro area flash inflation print for October will 
be released on Wednesday. We expect Riksbank to hike rates to 2% on 
Wednesday, while Norges Bank will remain on hold on Thursday.

Market Movers Scandinavia 
 
 
• In Denmark, the unemployment indicator from the Danish Agency for 
Labour Market and Recruitment is released on Wednesday. This is the first 
indication om unemployment for the month of October. While 
unemployment has edged slightly higher by 3900 people year to date, 
there are no clear signs of a broader slowdown in job growth, with 
businesses still indicating stable hiring intentions. The increase in 
unemployment mainly reflects more people being registered as available 
for work rather than weaker labour demand. Overall, the labour market still 
appears to be moving steadily forward. 
Danmarks Nationalbank will publish figures for the FX reserve on Tuesday. 
If they show that Danmarks Nationalbank did not intervene in October, it 
will be the second consecutive month without intervention. This should 
further dampen any remaining speculation about an imminent 
independent rate hike. If France succeeds in passing a budget with a deficit 
of less than 5% of GDP before year-end, it could create further calm in the 
market, bring buyers of EUR/DKK back into the market and send EUR/DKK 
up towards 7.4760, which would trigger more intervention from Danmarks 
Nationalbank. For more details, see our Reading the Markets Denmark: 
Strong public finances support DKK and DGBs, 29 October.  
On Friday, industrial production for September will be released. The August 
figures showed a 0.9% m/m increase in industrial production (SA). 
Excluding the pharmaceuticals industry, production rose by 2.2% from 
June to August, compared with 1.0% when including it. This suggests that 
other sectors of the economy are gaining momentum. While industrial 
production remains volatile, other indicators continue to point to solid 
business confidence. 
 
 
• In Sweden, the Riksbank will make its policy rate decision on Wednesday 
the 4th November. In line with market expectations, we expect a hike to 
2.00%. This meeting is an interim meeting without any new forecasts, and 
focus will instead be on guidance ahead. Given that both the ECB and Fed 
held rates, an easing pressure on SEK, and recent figures pointing to strong 
continued growth, we expect guidance to keep the door open for 
tightening, without signalling for a hike in December, reiterating the 
previous policy path. Read more in Reading the Markets Sweden, 30 
September. 
On Monday and Wednesday, respectively, we’ll get the manufacturing and 
services PMI releases, with previous readings at 58.1 and 57.4, 
respectively. On Thursday, we’ll get the flash CPI figure.

• In Norway, we expect Norges Bank to keep the policy rate unchanged at 
4.5% at next week's interim MPC meeting, which will produce only a press 
release and press conference. At the September meeting, the committee 
signaled that 'It will likely be necessary to keep the policy rate elevated for 
a time, and the Committee is prepared to raise the policy rate further if 
needed'. Since then, the economy has evolved broadly as expected, 
though marginally higher inflation and lower unemployment point to a 
tighter rhetoric. A new round of oil and especially gas price increases has 
also strengthened the exchange rate, which, combined with elevated 
inflation and high inflation expectations, raises upside inflation risk and 
supports a more hawkish tone. Offsetting this, global policy rate 
expectations have fallen since September, giving Norges Bank room to 
await actual inflation developments without risking a weaker exchange 
rate. On balance, we expect Norges Bank to repeat its readiness to raise 
rates if necessary, while noting the overall picture has not changed 
materially since September, keeping the December meeting alive.

Scandi Update 
 
 
In Denmark no important key figures were released this week  
 
 
 
Sweden - Solid growth but rising inflationary pressures 
Figures from Sweden suggest that growth remains solid, while inflationary 
pressures are building. GDP remained on a strong path at 2.8% y/y, while the 
NIER survey indicated increasing upside risks to the economy with the index 
climbing to 108.1. However, consumer confidence fell, erasing the gains of 
the previous three months and returning close to its June level at 94.3. 
On inflation, firms’ selling price expectations for the next three months rose 
from September and now point to price increases to a much greater extent 
than normal. Similarly, PPI increased to 11.2% y/y in September, up from 
6.8%. The rise was broad-based, with higher prices across import, export and 
domestic markets, where for the latter, higher prices for electricity trading 
services made the largest upward contribution. Together with September’s 
CPI figures showing signs of higher core goods prices, we expect prices to 
continue to rise as higher underlying pressures feed through to consumer 
prices. 
 
 
Norway – mixed bag of news 
The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was unchanged at 2.0% in 
October. Gross unemployment rose by 600 people from September, while the 
number of vacancies rose slightly. Overall, the figures point to a continued 
tight labor market. Retail sales fell 0.4% in September, leaving Q3  unchanged 
from the previous quarter. The downward trend that began at the turn of the 
year thus appears to have stopped, but retail trade is still down more than 1% 
compared to September last year. Lower real wage growth, higher interest 
rates and, not least, higher savings appear to be the most important reasons 
for the weak consumption trend.
