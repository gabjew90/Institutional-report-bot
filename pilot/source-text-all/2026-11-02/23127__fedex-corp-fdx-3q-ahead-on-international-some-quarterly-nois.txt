Bottom Line: FDX reported 3Q 2026 adjusted EPS of $3.74 per 
share which was well ahead of GS/Visible Alpha consensus of 
$3.18 and $3.31.  The beat was largely attributable to international 
margin (3.9%) and EBIT coming in well ahead of forecast – an 
encouraging sign given the longer term margin target pegged at 8% 
in 2029. That said, while the company raised its full year guide to 
$18.00-$18.60 (from $16.90-$18.10), we think there may be some 
concern given that Domestic margin was down 20 bp YOY with the 
expectation that it will be down again somewhat in the 4Q.  While 
the company cited fuel headwind which should be transitory, it also 
mentioned elevated purchased transport costs as well.  And while 
they feel they have levers to pull to reduce the PT impact in 2027 – 
giving rise to an expectation for yoy Domestic margin 
improvement on a full year 2027 basis – there could be some 
concern as to the timing of inflection and a need to see that cost 
mitigation come to fruition.   
That said, we knew there could be some quarterly noise and 
volatility as the company transitioned from a fiscal to calendar year 
basis post the LTL spin (and we adjust to new seasonal trends), and 
we do feel confident that with volume growth and core price 
discipline that both domestic and international margin potential 
should materialize as we move through the next three years.   
We continue to rate the shares a Buy and note that the stock 
trades at just 14.8x  our calendar 2027 forecast.  We look for 
strong international in 2027, and with that backdrop still look for 
healthy double digit EPS growth - although on the lower domestic 
margin trajectory exiting 2026, we do lower our 2027 forecast to 
reflect a slower margin start to the year; however our three year 
earnings path remains intact with no change to our 2029 forecast 
(details below). 
We remain Buy rated on FDX as a standalone parcel carrier.

Underpinning our favorable rating is 1) their multi-pronged commercial and 
idiosyncratic cost and efficiency initiatives (higher yield B2B, selective B2C, cost 
transformation, AI integration), 2) capital discipline with FCF conversion (note capex 
4.0% in FY 2026 v. 7.2% in FY 2022), 3) long-term EPS potential approaching $25 by CY 
2029, 4) potential for a freight demand recovery driving down fixed cost per shipment 
with operating leverage torque, and 5) reasonable valuation in the context of potential 
freight recovery and upside potential from idiosyncratic efficiency and cost saves. 
Guide: FDX updated its full-year CY26 EPS guide to $18.00-$18.60 ($18.30 
midpoint) from $16.90-$18.10 ($17.50); GSe $17.85 and VA $17.80 going into the 
quarter. Underlying the full year range is revenue guidance of +12% YoY and EBIT of 
$5.9b (from 11% for revenue and $5.8 for EBIT).  
 
Bridging from CY 2025 EBIT of $5.0B to CY 2026 of ~$5.9B (at midpoint; from ~$5.8B) 
(Exhibit 2), management expects volume (net of cost) to be a $800M tailwind (from 
$600M) while yields are expected to be a $3.7B tailwind (unchanged), with $100M in 
Freight stranded cost reduction; these tailwinds are offset by expectations for 
headwinds of $2.7B from base expense increases (from $2.6B), $800M in variable 
incentive compensation, and $200M from the new pilot contract (starting in July).

3Q26 Trends: 
U.S. Domestic: US Domestic volume growth of +2% YoY came in about inline with 
n
our +2.1% forecast and yield growth of +7% YoY was also about inline with our 
+7.3% forecast. US Air Express volume growth of +1.3% YoY was essentially inline 
with our +1.9% forecast and yield growth of +8.4% YoY was essentially inline with 
our +8.1% forecast. Volume growth through the quarter for Express U.S. Domestic 
(Priority and Deferred) came in at +2%/-1%/+4% YoY for Jul/Aug/Sep. Management 
highlighted growth driven by leaning into their high value B2B strategy. US ground 
volume growth of +2% YoY was inline with our +2.2% estimate while yield growth of 
+7% YoY was inline with our +7% estimate. Through the quarter, ground package 
volume growth came in at +3%/+2%/+2% YoY for Jul/Aug/Sep. The positive growth 
in Ground Commercial (3Q volumes +3.5% YoY) was a reflection of leaning into 
their high value B2B verticals.  Home delivery/economy volume growth moderated 
in 3Q (+1% YoY) and the economy piece is expected to remain negative as FDX 
focuses on improving revenue quality mix. FDX Domestic US margin was 6.6% v. 
GSe 7.1% due to higher than expected purchased transportation cost and fuel 
related headwinds (about 30bp), which is expected to continue into 4Q. 3rd 
party PT around trucking is 10-12% of transportation miles (company drivers 
and service providers are 70%, rail 15%), and the company highlighted two 
levers it can pull to mitigate cost into 4Q and 2027 through insourcing or raising 
rates to cover the elevated cost. 
International Express: International Express volume growth of -4% YoY was weaker 
n
than our -2.5% forecast, but International Express yield growth of +17% was about 
inline with our +16.9% forecast. Through the quarter, International Export Package 
volume growth came in at +8%/+7%/+9% YoY for Jul/Aug/Sep. Management noted 
strength in their end to end service product, volumes as well as product mix shift 
away from international domestic to higher yielding export. Importantly, FDX 
highlighted strength in their international freight business, up 15% in pounds and 
29% in gross revenue from increased demand.

Looking ahead: FDX continues to flex its pricing power, expecting 12% revenue growth 
YoY, driven primarily by strong yields in Domestic and International, GSe 7% and 17%, 
respectively, to end 2026 (partly aided by fuel surcharge revenue). As we have seen with 
other transports this quarter, driver tightness in 3rd party PT is causing higher than 
expected costs for FDX in US Domestic, that is likely to weigh on margin in 4Q26, and 
into 2027, along with other headwinds such as incentive comp and the new pilot 
contract. That said, FDX is working to mitigate those higher PT costs through insourcing 
and/or raising rate to cover the higher PT costs, however, we contemplate this more 
completely lapping as we move into 2H27. Nonetheless, it is important to highlight the 
earnings strength in International from leaning into premium parcel such as higher 
yielding export volumes as well as implementing operational changes in Europe that we 
have seen similar in the US with station consolidation, which should provide meaningful 
margin improvement over the next several years.  
Estimates: Our 2027 EPS moves from $17.85 to $18.38 on the higher 3Q26 and better 
outlook for 4Q26. Our 2028 EPS moves from $21.00 to $20.60 on the lower trajectory to 
Domestic margins in 2027 due to higher PT to start the year. Our 2028 EPS moves from 
$23.55 to $23.50 on the lower base and our 2029 EPS is unchanged at $25.80. 
Price Target and Risks: Our 12-month price target is $390 (unchanged) based on a 
17.5x P/E (unchanged) applied to our updated Q5-Q8 EPS estimates ($22.50). 
Key risks include 1) an inability to attract labor which would cause ongoing network 
operating inefficiencies and in turn make achieving our margin forecast more 
challenging; 2) an inability to get core pricing upgrades would likely limit margin recovery 
potential given various inflationary pressures; 3) a slowdown in the industrial or 
E-Commerce economy, impacts to global trade from issues with overseas partners, 
rising inflation, tariffs and/or an increase in interest rates; 4) an inability to execute cost 
savings through DRIVE and Network 2.0 initiatives; 5) any attempt by labor to challenge 
the generally non-union nature of FedEx operations; and 6) an inability to accurately 
model calendar year estimates until restated financial statements are released.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
