STABLECOINS enter the mainstream: A shift in global payments
Source: Deutsche Bank Research, FT, Bloomberg Finance LP, Reuters.
2014 - 
2016
Stablecoins are on the cusp of mainstream adoption 
in 2025 as the US pushes forward with landmark 
legislation (GENIUS and STABLE Acts) by August. 
Despite last week's Senate resistance, we still expect 
progress this year.
The numbers are striking: market cap surged from 
$20bn in 2020 to $246bn, with $28 trn in transfer 
volume last year, outpacing Visa and Mastercard. 
They now power over two-thirds of crypto trading, 
offering unmatched speed, 24/7 access, low-cost, 
programmable payments.
More than just financial tools, stablecoins are fast 
becoming strategic assets. With 83% pegged to the 
US dollar and Tether ranking amongst the largest 
holders of US Treasuries, they’re reinforcing dollar 
dominance in a fragmenting world.
In this chartbook, we explore (i) market cap trends; 
(ii) real-world use cases; (iii) transaction volumes; (iv) 
corporate adoption; (v) regulatory momentum; and 
(vi) the strategic role stablecoins play in reinforcing 
dollar supremacy.

With stablecoin usage increasingly diversifying into payments and remittances, 
corporations are tapping into this sector to take advantage of the potentially

❑Mentions of stablecoins in SEC filings more than doubled in early 2025. From 
Jan 2024 to Jan 2025, stablecoins mentions averaged 48 times per month. In 
February - April 2025, the figure surged to 103 average monthly mentions – 
showing how stablecoin adoption is increasing along with heightened 
geopolitical and trade risk.

STABLECOINS 2025: The mainstream shift into global payments?
Source: Deutsche Bank Research, US Treasury, Atlantic Council.
Impact on bank deposits
Consequences for US debt
Implications on US monetary supply 
Under a more competitive landscape, 
banks may be required to increase 
interest rates to maintain funding or find 
alternative funding sources (i.e., 
wholesale funding).
Stablecoin adoption will lead to 1) overall 
increased demand for US treasuries and 
2) an overall shift to the front-end of the 
curve (GENIUS Act requires stablecoin 
issuers to hold <93d T-bills).
Demand for USD-pegged stablecoins 
could drive currently non-USD liquidity 
holdings into USD.
❑
Strategic relevance: They are now integral to digital dollar infrastructure, transforming FX, liquidity, capital flows, and 
payment innovation.
❑
Policy certainty approaching: US regulation will bring clarity, enabling mainstream use cases and deeper financial 
integration.
❑
Risks require due diligence: Market participants should differentiate between regulated and unregulated stablecoins – 
balancing liquidity vs. transparency.
❑
Action points:
❑
Track incoming US legislation and stablecoin issuer reserve disclosures.
❑
Evaluate use cases in payments, FX, and liquidity management.
Key adoption risks
Lack of audit 
Both the GENIUS and STABLE ACTs fail to properly regulate offshore stablecoin issuers (i.e., Tether). Tether has 
never completed a full independent audit.
Systemic risk
A depeg or run on a coin like USDT would severely impact crypto markets and possibly U.S. Treasuries due to mass 
liquidation of assets.
Risk of illicit finance Although the GENIUS Act implements measures such as freezing and seizing stablecoins suspected of illicit 
activity,  more comprehensive action is needed to monitor illicit transactions, sanctions evasion, etc.

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments

higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to 
receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency

sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates – these are common in 
emerging markets.  The index fixings may – by construction – lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly 
important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in a currency that differs from
