Cemex SAB (CX): 3Q26 Model Update: An Attractive Entry Point; 
Reiterate Buy
29 October 2026 | 6:25PM BRT
Our take: We update our Cemex estimates following another quarterly beat, the 
third in a row. Higher energy costs, upcoming cement capacity in Mexico and a weak 
US residential outlook represent headwinds going into 2027. We believe Cemex can 
mitigate these pressures respectively through pricing, robust Mexican residential and 
infrastructure demand, and continued US infrastructure and industrial activity. Our 
estimates do go 2% lower for FY26 due to management guidance on volumes and 
margins; PT is also impacted slightly going from US$15.5 to 15. CX trades at 5x 
2027E EV/EBITDA vs ~7x (in-line with historical average) implied by our US$15.0 PT 
(+50% return). Improving margins, a strong balance sheet and disciplined capex 
drive a 10% 2027E FCFE yield versus an approximately 1% dividend yield, providing 
capacity for higher shareholder distributions. We reiterate our Buy rating. 
Investor interest in Cemex has increased and skews bullish; while valuation 
continues to offer an attractive entry point. Investors, ourselves included, have 
been impressed by Mexico’s demand resilience and margin performance. This was 
particularly evident in 3Q26, when the expected margin moderation largely failed to 
materialize following Mexico’s strongest first-half EBITDA margin since 2021, at 
approximately 37%. Mexico has served as a key driver for the Cemex story, 
responsible for 80% of the +22% EBITDA growth this year; we expect Mexico to 
continue to be a key growth driver with overall EBITDA margin (we expect ~100 bps 
to 22%) helping drive the 8% EBITDA growth for the year.    
We found management’s conference-call tone constructive. Management noted 
that: 1) it views a 21% consolidated EBITDA margin as a floor, approximately 300bps 
above the 2010–25 average; 2) energy costs are expected to increase at a mid/high 
single digits, yet half of total energy exposure is hedged including electricity, direct 
diesel, natural gas and alternative fuels while pricing and efficiencies should provide 
additional offsets; 3) Mexican demand should remain resilient, supported by 
government-backed housing and improving infrastructure execution helping 
mitigate the impact of new supply; and 4) US residential demand is unlikely to 
improve in 2027 but is also not expected to deteriorate materially, while remaining 
IIJA funds and industrial investment should continue supporting 
infrastructure-related demand, which supports are flat growth outlook for cement 
for the yeat. 
Our model changes are muted. We adjust our 2026 estimate to account for lower 
volumes in ready mix for Mexico and US and aggregates in Mexico, MEA and SCAC.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
