The Fed delivered the expected skip, but the market was less convinced about the guidance. 2Y UST yields 
had pushed towards 4.78% ahead of the meeting, with 2s10s briefly through 24bp, before Warsh’s press 
conference triggered a sharp reversal. His acknowledgement that tighter financial conditions suggest policy is 
working fell short of the hawkish message priced beforehand, pulling the front end lower and sending 2s10s back 
towards 34bp. Still, with around 21bp priced for December, we would be careful extrapolating yesterday’s rally too 
far. Ultimately, the Fed did little to challenge a December hike and the bigger question is now the pace of tightening 
in 2027. That puts today’s data firmly back in focus. September PCE and the first Q3 GDP estimate are the 
highlights, with our US senior econ seeing room for an upside GDP surprise. A strong combination of activity and 
inflation would make it difficult for USTs to extend the post-FOMC rally, particularly with $44bn 7Y UST supply 
later. Tech remains another rates input, with Amazon tonight after Alphabet’s miss revived some of the recent AI 
concerns. Oil is offering some relief overnight. Reports that JD Vance reached out to Iran to explore renewed 
talks have taken some geopolitical premium out of crude, although military action remains on the table after the 
midterms. Brent is around $114.75pb, still broadly rangebound. Against that backdrop, we would fade a month-
end-led UST rally unless equities weaken more materially. The DXY fell after the FOMC but has recovered 
slightly overnight to around 102.25. 
The ECB is expected to keep rates unchanged today, so the discussion is really about December rather 
than October. With a Dec hike still heavily priced, Lagarde needs to balance an inflation picture complicated by 
higher energy prices against a clear tightening in financial conditions. Tuesday’s Bank Lending Survey showed 
another tightening in credit standards, while higher outright yields and wider EGB spreads are already doing some 
of the ECB’s work. That leaves Lagarde’s characterisation of those two forces as the main rates catalyst today.
We expect her to keep the meeting-by-meeting language while retaining a tightening bias, but any emphasis on 
tighter financial conditions could see December pricing tested. Equally, with other meeting until Dec, she cannot 
be too vague if December remains the central case. 10Y Bund yields are trading in a relatively contained 3.32-
3.39% range this week, while 10Y OAT-Bund spreads remain below 160bp. EUR/USD briefly recovered towards 
1.1150 but is back below there this morning. Supply-wise, Italy brings €7.75bn of supply today.  
The Budget cleared the immediate hurdle for Gilts, but we would stop short of calling it structurally bullish. 
£15bn of headroom was enough to avoid a fiscal-stress scenario, while the remit was broadly as expected and the 
reduction in long-end issuance provided some welcome technical support. That helped the curve flatten yesterday, 
while long-end Gilts richened slightly on ASW. The problem is simply pushed further out. Borrowing has 
deteriorated versus the March OBR path and growth was revised materially lower for 2027, while some of the 
tougher spending choices have effectively been deferred. For now, that combination can keep flattening pressure 
in place, particularly given the lighter long-end remit. But 2027 increasingly looks like the real test. Higher interest 
costs or weaker tax receipts would quickly eat into the fiscal cushion. Today it will be the ECB and US data that
will dominate outright direction. GBP/USD was well supported by the Budget and softer post-FOMC dollar 
yesterday and is consolidating around 1.3325 overnight. 
Souring sentiment in Asia, following some weak earnings in tech. In Japan, JGBs continued to bear-steepen, 
with the front-end more anchored thanks to a strong 2Y JGB auction while selling in the long end continued through 
most of the session. USD/JPY fell below 158 overnight after testing 158.25 before yesterday’s FOMC.
