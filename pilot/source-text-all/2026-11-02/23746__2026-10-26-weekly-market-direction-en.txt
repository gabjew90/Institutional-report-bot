Confidence in the artificial intelligence theme has weakened 
in recent weeks, with the MSCI World Technology Index 
giving back all of the previous month’s gains. This was likely 
driven by a combination of mixed corporate earnings and 
renewed concerns about AI-related capital expenditure. We 
think markets have now moved beyond the phase in which 
the mere mention of an AI roll-out could excite investors. 
Markets are now increasingly demanding clarity on cash 
generation from these sizeable AI investments. By contrast, 
the MSCI World High Dividend Yield Index gained 8% in the 
second half of the year as markets sought steadier ground, 
typically found in sectors such as consumer staples, 
healthcare and utilities — the traditional home of dividend-
paying companies — which held up well as the broader 
market came under pressure. This suggests diversification* 
opportunities through more attractively valued sectors with 
strong, stable businesses.

stocks that spread more broadly. Bond yields in some countries, such as the 
US, declined amid safe-haven demand, whereas fiscal concerns in other 
countries pushed yields higher. In commodities, the oil price was affected by 
a flare-up in Middle East tensions. Gold prices declined amid concerns that 
persistent inflation could prompt central banks to raise interest rates.

Eurozone growth momentum slows in October
The EZ’s preliminary business activity reading for 
October came in below expectations, signalling a modest 
loss of momentum. The composite PMI index fell to 51.9 
from 
53.1 
in 
September, 
as 
both 
services 
and 
manufacturing slowed. All three readings remain above 
the 50 threshold separating expansion from contraction, 
but the pace of growth is clearly decelerating. Weaker 
investor and consumer confidence also point to growing 
caution about the near-term economic outlook.
US labour market holds firm, housing stays under 
pressure
Initial jobless claims fell to 194,000 in the week ending 17 
October, below expectations and consistent with a firm 
labour market. The housing sector, however, continues 
to struggle: September housing starts fell to an 
annualised 1.22 million units, sharply below forecasts 
and down from the prior month. The releases point to a 
divided 
economy, 
with 
elevated 
borrowing 
costs 
continuing to weigh on builders and buyers, leaving 
residential construction under pressure despite labour 
market resilience.
Japan inflation rises
Japan's inflation excluding fresh food and energy rose 
to 2.4% in September, above expectations and up from 
August, signalling renewed underlying price pressures. 
Meanwhile, the auction of 20-year government bonds 
drew 
record 
demand, 
while 
domestic 
investors 
increased purchases in the first half of the fiscal year, 
reflecting stronger appetite at elevated yields.
