BP p.l.c.
How many (good) quarters makes a pattern?
Our view: Most of the debate over recent months on the BP investment
case has been what the right level of debt is. While we continue to
debate this with investors, BP's debt is rapidly declining, supported by
an exceptional macro environment and strong operational performance.
3Q was another example of this. We see net debt falling by another
$12bn into year end, which implies a ~$30bn reduction, or halving of total
liabilities since the onset of the US-Iran war. We maintain our Outperform
recommendation, raising our Price Target to 720p.
Key points:
Debt reduction ongoing, BP needs more than a good balance sheet to
entice investors. Total liabilities look set to reduce materially this year,
and while BP's year-end debt guidance may prove conservative, we see
reported net debt on a trajectory to go below zero in 1H27. Management
has said it expects to utilise excess cash flow to potentially repay the
Macondo settlement in full, and while it is unclear how this could play
out, it is potentially a positive move over the long term. Still, a healthy
balance sheet does not necessarily equate to a strong investment case, as
ultimately the balance sheet is there to facilitate long cycle investments,
opportunistic M&A and consistent shareholder returns. This is what BP will
need to communicate at the CMD on December 15th, while also outlining
what is the 'appropriate' debt levels to restart buybacks.
Market not recognising shifting EV. YTD BP's EV has increased by $17bn,
which equates roughly to the change in consensus FCF this year. Effectively,
the market is implying limited duration in commodity price strength,
perhaps reflecting the source of earnings (trading / refining), or the lack of
trust in management. This looks at odds versus peers, where the underlying
re-rating reflects significantly more than one year's worth of revisions. A
credible plan, a return to growth and a "fixed" balance sheet should all help.

Valuation
We value BP based on a 50/50 weighting between SOTP
and normalized EV/DACF using a multiple of 5.5x – a wider
discount to its historical average than we use for some peers,
reflecting some uncertainty on the outcome of its longer-term
strategy. This leads to a price target of 720p, which supports
our Outperform rating.
Upside scenario
Our upside case is based on a $100/bbl scenario in 2027, as
well as base case refining & chemicals, which assumes a small
recovery versus 2019. We use average multiples vs history. On
this basis our valuation would be 860p.
Downside scenario
Our downside scenario assumes $40/bbl Brent in 2027, as well
as weaker refining, chemicals and marketing earnings nearer
to recession levels in the downstream, using earnings in ’08-09
as reference points. On this basis our downside case would be
460p.
Investment summary
We rate BP with an Outperform rating and 720p price target.
Given BP’s balance sheet and higher debt levels than peers,
our positive stance is predicated on our bullish commodity
view, as this should drive material debt reduction through
2026-27E. We believe BP’s leverage will be closer to peers
within 12-18 months, with the company having already
reached its year-end 2027 net debt target ($14-18bn reported
net debt) at 3Q26, and we see reported net debt on a
trajectory to go below zero in 1H27.
We still see BP in the early stages of its turnaround journey,
and its potential FCF generation leaves its valuation multiples
looking undemanding relative to peers. Given BP's balance
sheet, outside of macro conditions, the key thing to watch is
progress on asset sales, with the LightsourceBP farm-down
one of the potential divestments not currently announced as
far.
Beyond de-leveraging, with a new CEO in place, we believe
BP is set to accelerate its cost reduction and efficiency drive,
and expect positive news flow on this front through 2026, with
greater emphasis on its core oil and gas business, and less on
the transition businesses, where BP has not developed a track
record for shareholder value creation.
Risks to rating and price target
Risks to our price target and rating that are specific
to BP include: risk on oil and gas prices. In general,
all international integrated oil companies are exposed to
resource price fluctuations, political/security risk, execution
risk, and environmental/permitting risks. Given the operating
and financial leverage, lower commodity prices would be
more negative for BP than peers. Issues around governance
and the Board could remain an overhang if investors are
unconvinced of the quality of Board personnel.

Company description
BP plc is one of the four largest publicly traded Western oil companies with its headquarters in London and a primary listing on
the London Stock Exchange. BP has a presence in more than 80 countries worldwide.
Following the Macondo tragedy in April 2010, BP embarked on an aggressive divestment programme, revised the company’s
strategic direction, and overhauled the senior management team. BP’s new strategy places an increased focus on safety and
operational risk management, rebuilding trust with its global stakeholders, investing for growth in its upstream business, and
reshaping its downstream portfolio.
