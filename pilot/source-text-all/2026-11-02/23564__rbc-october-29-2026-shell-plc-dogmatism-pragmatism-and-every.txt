Shell PLC
Dogmatism, pragmatism, and everything else
Our view: Another strong quarter for Shell adds to a robust balance sheet
and helps support an increase in the buyback to $5bn, a run-rate we expect
to continue into 2027. Confusing communication around buybacks earlier
this year appears to have now been cleared up, and we expect greater focus
on the upstream portfolio and longevity post 2030 at the strategy update
alongside 4Q results.
Key points:
Dogmatism, pragmatism, and everything else. Following the tactical
buyback cut with 1Q results, and some conflicting commentary around
committing to its payout ratio with 2Q results, Shell has now cleaned up
the commentary on distributions, highlighting that it expects shareholder
returns to "keep pace" with underlying cash flows, and that its 40-50%
payout ratio guide on CFFO is through cycle and not for a given
calendar year. More importantly, the business continues to perform
well operationally and appears extremely well set up for the current
environment. On top of this, the balance sheet continues to improve from
already conservative levels. We incorporate $5bn buybacks per quarter for
4Q26 and 1H27.
Should Shell rebalance distributions towards dividends? The combination
of the ARX acquisition adding structural FCF, improvements in the upstream
hopper for 2030+ (Bay du Nord, LNG Canada, potentially in Brazil), as well
as potential portfolio change in FCF dilutive areas like Chemicals, all support
an absolute dividend that is materially higher than it currently sits. In fact,
we calculate Shell's FCF breakeven after dividends in the region of ~$35/
bbl, well below peers. We see room for a 20% increase in DPS, which could
come alongside the strategy update in early 2027 and could support the
investment case. We incorporate this into our estimates.
Changes to estimates. We incorporate RBC's latest commodity price deck,
which drives increases to our estimates. Our PT moves to 4100p and we
maintain our Sector Perform rating.

Valuation
We value Shell based on a 50/50 weighting between SOTP
and normalized EV/DACF, in line with peers. Our EV/DACF uses
a multiple of 5.5x, below historical levels given the current
commodity price environment. This leads to a price target of
4,100p, which supports our Sector Perform rating.
Upside scenario
Our upside scenario assumes $100/bbl Brent in 2027, as well
as base case refining & chemicals, which assumes a small
recovery versus 2019. We use average multiples vs history. On
this basis our valuation would be 5,000p.
Downside scenario
Our downside scenario assumes $40/bbl Brent in 2027, as well
as weaker refining, chemicals and marketing earnings nearer
to recession levels in the downstream, using earnings in ’08-09
as reference points. On this basis, our valuation would be
2,400p.
Investment summary
Key reasons for our neutral stance:
Attractiveness well appreciated, but investor focus shifting.
Shell has outperformed Super-Major counterparts on a total
returns basis since new management took over in 2023.
However, we have noted a structural shift in investor focus
away from buyback-driven returns and towards portfolio
longevity and underlying production growth. With Shell's EPS
accretion over 2024-30E more heavily weighted towards share
count reduction than peers, we think Shell will continue to
screen weaker on the metrics investors are now prioritising.
ARX deal helps, but upstream hopper still needs
supplementing. The ARX transaction adds ~16% to Shell's
production base and provides feed gas optionality into a
potential LNG Canada Phase 2 FID. However, Shell's reserve
life remains weak at ~8 years versus ~12 for TotalEnergies and
~11 for ExxonMobil. Given the 'liquids gap' in the 2030s, we
expect continued inorganic activity, but limited options and an
increasingly competitive market for direct access deals mean
execution risk remains.
M&A challenges persist. Despite the >25% reduction in
share count since the pandemic (~$45bn worth of buybacks
since 2023), Shell's valuation multiple has remained relatively
stagnant. We think this creates a challenge for Shell's M&A
ambitions, as the company may ultimately need to utilise
a combination of cash and equity to make the numbers
work on future targets. Lacklustre exploration success and
a de-emphasis on frontier exploration in recent years has
effectively left M&A as the main route to replenish resources,
placing greater weight on management's ability to execute at
the right time and at the right price.
Earnings headwinds beyond the upstream. Shell appears
to be running uphill on its chemicals restructuring given
challenging market dynamics, while we see headwinds across
international gas and trading as elevated margins normalise.
Our numbers assume the low carbon business flips from a -
$500m loss in 2024 to +$1bn positive by 2030, and similarly
forecast a +$1bn improvement in Chemicals — but much of
Shell's earnings growth therefore comes from restructuring
underperforming businesses rather than organic upstream
expansion. Shell needs more of a growth story beyond
restructuring and buybacks in order to drive a re-rating relative
to peers.
