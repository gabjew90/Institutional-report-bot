Of course, past performance is not indicative of future results, but seasonality is worth acknowledging. 
Over the past 30 years, November and December have been among the strongest months for U.S. 
equities, as the chart above illustrates. The S&P 500 has gained 3.7% on average across the two 
months combined, while mid cap growth stocks, as measured by the Russell Midcap Growth Index, 
have averaged more than 4.6% over the same stretch. We believe this partly reflects investors turning 
their focus to the following   a ’s  a  i  s, w ic  has tended to favor companies with stronger 
fundamentals.

On that measure, mid cap growth stocks stand out. Consensus estimates call for Russell Midcap 
Growth earnings to rise 23% in 2027, compared with 16% for the S&P 500, driven in part by companies 
providing artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, developing software whose demand is rising with AI 
adoption, and advancing life sciences.1 That outlook has not been reflected in performance, however, 
as mid cap growth stocks have trailed their larger peers for much of 2026. As investor attention turns to 
next year, the earnings growth differential may begin to receive the recognition it has lacked, in our 
view.
