Komatsu (6301.T): 2Q Follow-up: Maintain bullish stance, focus on 
improvement from FY3/28
3 November 2026 | 2:01AM JST
We conducted follow-up research on Komatsu’s 2Q results. Although top-line trends 
were positive, Komatsu’s share price has underperformed the machinery sector 
following its results release, as investors have reacted negatively to profit margins 
missing market expectations, even if largely because of one-off costs. However, we 
think investors’ disappointment mainly regarding 2Q owes primarily to temporary 
factors, and that the outlook for profit growth heading into FY3/28 is actually 
opening up. We present our views on the details of the company’s 2Q results and 
FY3/27 guidance. 
Re-examining 2Q results and full-year guidance; no key concerns over temporary 
margin deterioration, expect mix improvement in FY3/28 
Both 2Q3/27 operating profits and new full-year guidance missed our estimates and 
market expectations, but our follow-up research reinforces our view that concern is 
unwarranted. This is because top line momentum for the main growth drivers we are 
focused on is strong, and the company has explained that the deterioration in 
profitability seen in 2Q is temporary. The main factors behind 2Q profits missing the 
market consensus/GSe by c.¥15-20 bn included one-off costs associated with the 
response to the Thailand floods and quality issues at overseas suppliers (divergence 
of c.¥5 bn vs. market consensus), the timing of tariff refunds being recognized on the 
P&L statement (c.¥5-10 bn), and a timing mismatch in the sales and shipments of 
mining machinery bound for Australia (low single-digit billions of yen). As these were 
all temporary factors or timing mismatches, we see underlying business as essentially 
in line with expectations. 
We could also say that, despite the strong top line, positive operating leverage and 
mix effects were not significant enough to offset the aforementioned temporary 
factors. Komatsu expects a significant negative impact from these factors, 
estimating the yoy profit drag from them at over ¥11 bn in 1H and just under ¥20 bn 
for the full year. While North American general construction machinery sales are 
strong, growth in high-margin mining machinery and components is relatively weak, 
and the profit mix improvement we had anticipated has not yet materialized. 
However, we see no major concerns regarding demand in itself. In addition to 
continued growth in North American general construction machinery and a recovery 
in mining machinery for copper mines, we expect the mix deterioration to run its 
course and margins to improve heading into FY3/28, driven by a bottoming out in 
demand for mining machinery for Asian coal mines and a recovery in the sales mix of 
components/services.

Mining machinery: Copper and gold to lead for now, with potential improvement in 
Indonesian coal 
In the mining machinery business, inquiries remain strong from copper mines mainly in 
South America and gold mines mainly in Africa. Mining machinery demand itself is 
tracking above Komatsu’s initial expectations, led by these regions, and as the company 
has mentioned previously, it is seeing sentiment/leading indicators for Indonesian coal 
mines (a key earnings driver) start to bottom out. While uncertainty remains in Indonesia 
over government coal production permits, indicators suggesting improvement are 
beginning to emerge, such as a drop in the idle rate from c.20% at the end of 1Q to 
12-13% in September. Komatsu remains cautious on coal production volumes and 
permitting trends for calendar 2027, but considering that the hurdle for yoy comparison 
is lowering, we believe demand is set to start bottoming out. Komatsu’s 2H outlook for 
mining machinery sales in Asia (AeCJ) also assumes a yoy turnaround and continued 
growth. As this is a highly profitable segment, we see ample scope for improvement 
heading into FY3/28. 
General construction machinery: North American AI/data center demand continues to 
drive growth; rental demand notably strong 
In general construction machinery, the strength of the North American market stands 
out. The company estimates that North American construction machinery demand rose 
15% yoy in 1H, with rental demand particularly strong at 55% growth. Komatsu and 
several other construction-related companies have noted that AI and data 
center-related investments are driving an expansion in demand. According to the 
company, customers prioritizing the early start of construction work are increasingly 
favoring rentals over new machinery purchases, and Komatsu is capturing this demand 
through inventory restocking for dealer rent-to-sell. Although there are some signs of a 
slowdown in recent non-residential demand, the demand environment for data centers 
remains favorable, and there are currently few signs of a significant deterioration in 
demand. While inquiries from customers remain strong, the company believes it should 
maintain a cautious stance as it is currently seeing a substantial volume increase in the 
form of rentals, where short-term demand is easy to capture. We see Komatsu’s strong 
caution as somewhat of a surprise, and given current conditions we view its assumptions 
as conservative. 
Watching buoyancy and sustainability in North American rental market, where 
competitors are also focusing 
The strong demand for rentals in North America is not unique to Komatsu. At its 2Q and 
3Q 2026 results briefings, competitor CAT also said dealer rental fleet loading is 
contributing significantly to growth in sales to users (STU) in its Construction Industries 
business. CAT has launched a rental business for large projects jointly with dealers, 
positioning fleet expansion as a pillar of its medium- to long-term market share 
expansion strategy. North American STU in 3Q maintained high growth at 27% yoy, and 
CAT said newly introduced rental machinery is being swiftly deployed to construction 
sites. This comment points to the same industry trend as Komatsu’s commentary. That 
said, the timing of bookings in CAT’s dealer owned rental fleet (DORF) business differs 
from retail demand/share data in Komatsu’s rent-to-sell business, making it difficult to 
simply compare the sales trends of the two companies. However, both companies are 
quite positive about the outlook for the North American market, driven by this rental

Our view: Fine-tuning our estimates, assume accelerating profit growth heading into

Based on Komatsu’s commentary at its 2Q results release, we took a fresh look at the 
timing of changes in mix and the emergence of one-off costs. As the mix impact in 
FY3/27 looks poised to be greater than our previous assumption, we revise down our 
operating profit estimate by 1%, but our assumption that this can be recovered from 
FY3/28 onward remains unchanged, and we leave our FY3/28 operating profit estimate 
intact at ¥700 bn. We see a high likelihood of profit growth at Komatsu from FY3/28 
onward, and we reiterate our Buy rating. Our 12-month target price is also unchanged at 
¥9,000.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2

departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
