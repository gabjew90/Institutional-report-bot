Ripping the band-aid
The lack of funding needs is a significant positive
development. There will be questions about vendor
financing, but we read this as more positive than some bears
have suggested. Spreads likely pause in the near term given
expectations for a bond deal, but that could be a clearing
event. 
Trade idea: Buy ORCL 2056s
The bar for the investor day was raised with spreads rallying 25-30bp earlier in the week, but
ORCL delivered a much stronger and more articulate message than we expected. The guidance
of only $10bn of new gross debt through 2030 is much better than the market was braced for,
the potential for this funding to come soon is better than letting the overhang linger, and the
added detail on margins and cash flow profiles adds further credibility to the story, in our view.
 Oracle still has to execute, but it was hard not to come away from the event more positive than
going in.
We are cognizant that no good Oracle deed seems to go unpunished by the market. In this vein
we suspect the bears will lean heavily on vendor financing as a negative and play up the
"circular" narrative. We are taking a far less negative view. The compute market is increasingly
undersupplied, which gives Oracle leverage to lean on customer prepays and bring-your-own-
hardware arrangements (see Jalapeño discussion below).  Vendor financing is also going to
contribute, but comments from ORCL CFO suggest that the company is not bringing a chip-
financing deal to the market. We reiterate our Overweight and recommend buying the 2056s.

Sticking with our Overweight
We admit to some serious gut checks over the past couple months, but we continue to see value
at these levels. It won't be a straight line from here, and we expect the market to take a "fool me
twice… " approach after the February rally. However, spreads are still wider from earnings and
the story has materially improved from a funding perspective.  
In the very near term, we expect the structure to trade sideways or even give up some of the
recent rally as the market braces for a bond deal. Our expectation is for a $10bn all USD offering;
while a EUR deal is possible, we think is less likely in this next transaction and likely to be used
as a refinancing tool in the future. Given that the curve is still relatively steep, we prefer
positioning in the long end and recommend buying the 2056s. Based on the curve and where
all-in 30y yields are, we would not be surprised if the pending deal skews slightly shorter in
duration, which could be a catalyst for existing 30y spreads.  
The takeaways for credit
• $20bn total incremental capital left through 2030. The funding will be $10bn debt and
•
$10bn equity. This excludes refinancing of maturities, so gross supply could still be low-to-
mid single digits each year, but the total financial debt figure will peak once the $10bn of
incremental funding is put in place. Our impression has been that investors were expecting
$50-60bn of remaining funding ($20bn in 2027, $30-40bn in 2028, similar to our model), but
the level of equity financing was uncertain. Clearly this is materially better than expectations.
We've long debated which hyperscaler would be the first to say "this is the last piece of
capital we need" — ORCL appears to be raising its hand. 
• Issuing soon is better for the market. CFO Hilary Maxson said that given how much lower
•
their needs are versus expectations, they could come as soon as this year. Based on how deal
overhang tends to punish hyperscaler spreads, we think it would be better for the market if
ORCL comes soon rather than sticking to the prior guidance (no issuance until 2027). While
we believe the EUR markets remains of interest to ORCL, it still may be hard to issue there at
the moment despite the positive announcements, though it could be a tool for refinancing
activity in the future. We expect ORCL to issue the "final" $10bn of net new unsecureds in the
near-term and think the tenor could skew somewhat shorter than its recent deals, given the
steepness of the curve and all-in yields in the 30y bucket.
• More gigs… and more funding levers. ORCL said that it will add 7GW of capacity between
•
fiscal 2027-28 and end 2028 with 10GW of capacity. We modeled +5.25GW in 2027/28 but not
reaching 10GW until 2029. Normally this would mean that capex and funding needs are
moving higher, yet ORCL's funding guidance implies the opposite. The delta appears to be
heavier reliance on prepays, BYOHW and vendor financing. The company didn't size the
magnitude of these levers or their relative share. Prepays and BYOHW have been part of the
narrative for some time and, based on the tightness of the market, it is plausible that the use
of these tools has grown. Leaning into them could be ORCL's way of capitalizing on market
conditions (since it does not have spot capacity to sell).
• Vendor financing is going to raise some eyebrows. We suspect that the vendor financing

quarters. The category is vague and can take many forms such as extended payables, or
private/direct loans (recall NVDA disclosed that it is extending payables for certain IG

recently (see ORCL Is (Also) Exploring Chip Financing), so it is hard to rule out some kind of
transaction. However, comments from ORCL's CFO suggested that there will not be other
