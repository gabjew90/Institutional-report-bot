Looking Ahead: SAIA’s is guiding sequential margin performance (3Q to 4Q) to 
outperform the company’s historical seasonality of ~250-300 bps of margin 
deterioration QoQ (per management comments on historical 3Q to 4Q performance) 
with the guide at ~200 bps deterioration. The guide implies a 4Q OR at ~87.6% which 
is slightly ahead of overall expectations for 4Q margins coming into the print 
(Consensus was 89.7% and GS 90.1% for 4Q coming into the print). Note that our 
new 4Q OR estimate is 89.6%. The company cited October’s performance (details 
below) as a reason for being able to beat historical seasonality. 
Current Trends/Results: For SAIA in 3Q26, tonnage per day growth accelerated to 
+8.9% YoY versus +8.4%/-2.1% in 2Q/1Q26 due in-part to an easier compare (last 
year’s 3Q tonnage growth was -1.5% YoY compared to +1% in the prior quarter); the 
slightly above-forecast full-quarter tonnage per day growth (GSe was at ~+8.5% YoY) 
came with slightly higher revenue per hundredweight growth (excluding fuel) at -2.2% 
YoY (vs -3% GSe) as weight per shipment was more robust at +8.1% (mix efforts), while 
rev/cwt including fuel also grew slightly above expectations at +7.4% YoY. 
SAIA’s preliminary October tonnage growth trend was also given by management on 
its conference call: tons per day in October at up ~10% YoY, or above the ~8.6% YoY 
full-4Q consensus estimate coming into 3Q print (8.0% GSe) which is about in line 
with the September growth rate (+10.3%); given we were anticipating tonnage growth 
of +8.0% YoY in 4Q26 coming into the print (consensus +8.6%), the +10% growth 
run-rate is tracking above our previous estimate (we now forecast tonnage at +9.0% YoY 
for the full-4Q vs +10% in Oct given slightly tougher tonnage comps into Nov/Dec vs 
Oct). Based on the yield and weight per shipment trendline, our yield growth 
expectations ex-fuel remain negative in the near-term as the mix of freight becomes 
heavier (a good thing over the long run). The company highlighted that yield ex-fuel 
increased 1.5% QoQ despite the increase in weight per shipment, and noted that its new 
terminals’ rev/shipment ex-fuel improved YoY at a higher pace than SAIA’s legacy 
terminals; prospects for ongoing price look favorable given recent contract rate renewals 
up 10.8%. Once mix normalizes, we would expect this to translate to better core yield 
performance. 
Estimates: Our prior FY26/27/28 EPS estimates of $11.15/$14.25/$17.00 now stand 
at $11.43/$14.35/$17.10. Updated estimates primarily reflect the 3Q beat and higher 
revenue and margin trajectory. Looking ahead to 2027/2028, our EPS growth 
trajectory and expected incremental margins are approximately unchanged and 
very robust at over 20% EPS growth and ~30% incremental margins. 
Rating, Price Target, Risks: We are Buy rated on SAIA. Our price target is $472 
(unchanged) based on a target P/E multiple of ~28.5x (lowered from ~30x to be

applied ODFL multiple) applied to our updated and rolled-forward Q5-Q8 EPS forecast 
($16.54).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank

across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
