 
This week in Business & Information Services, several companies reported 3Q results 
including SPGI, CSGP, TRU, PBI and BFAM, with investor discussions focused on where 
improving fundamentals can translate into more durable revenue growth and 
potential catalysts. SPGI delivered a beat-and-raise quarter, with favorable investor 
reaction to the CapIQ strategic review balanced by questions around near-term 
Ratings growth and Energy visibility. Weaker bookings at CSGP increased investor 
focus on the trajectory of 2027 revenue growth, TRU’s non-mortgage and Insurance 
strength was tempered by mortgage and margin headwinds, and BFAM’s back-up 
care strength and improving underlying full service margins were offset by weaker 
enrollment trends, particularly in Australia. Meanwhile, PBI showed improving 
underlying trends across Presort and SendTech ahead of a transitional 2027. 
Strategic actions were also a theme outside earnings: ADT agreed to be acquired for 
$10/shr, FICO introduced mortgage pricing intended to defend share and MH 
announced plans to extend its proprietary content into Microsoft 365 Copilot 
workflows. Finally, our consumer credit work showed improving non-mortgage 
delinquencies and continued origination growth, and we reduced BCO estimates for 
incremental Latin America FX headwinds and KFY estimates for more moderate TOS 
growth and fewer share repurchases following the AMS acquisition.

GS Business & Information Services Research This Week 
Americas Business & Information Services: September credit delinquencies improve 
as originations continue to grow. September consumer credit trends remained 
constructive y/y, with delinquencies improving across non-mortgage categories, though 
lower-tier performance remained mixed. The 60+ day bankcard delinquency rate 
declined to 2.74% from 2.83% in the prior year, personal loan delinquencies decreased 
to 3.26% from 3.28% and auto loan delinquencies fell to 1.50% from 1.57%, with card 
and auto delinquencies remaining above their long-term averages. Subprime and 
near-prime delinquencies increased in cards and personal loans, whereas subprime auto 
delinquencies improved and near-prime auto delinquencies were stable. Credit supply 
remained constructive, with originations increasing 2.7% y/y in cards, 10.9% in personal 
loans and 1.3% in auto. We continue to favor Buy-rated FICO given its pricing power, 
entrenched position in credit decisioning and robust EPS growth, supported by the 
Mortgage Direct License Program and Software transformation, though the unified 
mortgage pricing grid and potential bi-merge adoption increase uncertainty around 
mortgage pricing, volumes and market share. We remain Neutral on EFX and TRU, as 
resilient non-mortgage growth, government and Insurance momentum and potential 
VantageScore upside are balanced by mortgage weakness, evolving score economics, 
potential bi-merge volume pressure and EBITDA margin headwinds. Link to our 10/30 
note here. 
McGraw Hill (MH, Buy): Plans to launch MCP solution to expand the reach and utility 
of proprietary educational content. McGraw Hill announced plans to launch a 
Microsoft 365 Copilot plugin that will allow higher education and school district 
customers with enterprise Copilot access to use licensed MH course materials through 
Copilot. The plugin will enable educators and students to summarize, translate and 
adapt MH’s proprietary K-12, higher education and professional learning content and 
create personalized study aids, lesson plans and other learning tools by securely 
connecting Copilot with MH’s licensed course materials through Model Context Protocol, 
or MCP. MH also plans to enable customers to build specialized AI agents grounded in its 
content and pedagogy. We view the announcement as strategically positive, though 
commercialization remains early, with a limited beta planned for early 2027 and no 
details available on pricing, revenue sharing, customer commitments or financial targets. 
We believe the strategic value lies in establishing MH’s proprietary content and 
pedagogy as a governed input into third-party AI workflows, which could expand 
distribution and engagement without requiring MH to own every interface through 
which its content is accessed. The collaboration reinforces our thesis that MH’s trusted, 
expert-developed content, broad customer relationships and expanding digital 
capabilities position the company to benefit as education AI shifts toward more 
specialized, curriculum-aligned applications. Link to our 10/30 note here.

ADT (ADT, NR): To be acquired by Thrive Holdings for $10/shr. On October 29, 2026, 
ADT announced that it has entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Thrive 
Holdings in an all-cash transaction. Under the terms of the agreement, ADT stockholders 
will receive $10.00/shr in cash, implying an equity value of ~$7.6bn and total enterprise 
value of ~$15.3bn. The purchase price represents a premium of 45.6% to ADT’s 
unaffected closing share price of $6.87 on October 27, 2026 and 52.1% to its 30-day 
VWAP ending October 27, 2026. ADT’s Board has unanimously approved the 
transaction, which the company expects to close in 1Q27, subject to customary closing 
conditions, including approval by ADT stockholders and receipt of US antitrust approval. 
The transaction is not subject to a financing condition. Under the merger agreement, 
ADT may solicit alternative acquisition proposals through November 28, 2026, with a 
potential 10-day extension for certain parties that submit proposals during the initial 
go-shop period. ADT plans to report 3Q 2026 results on November 3, 2026 and hold a 
conference call at 10am ET, though the company will not take questions given the 
pending transaction. Link to our 10/29 note here. 
Pitney Bowes (PBI, Neutral): Solid 3Q as underlying trends improve ahead of a 
transitional 2027. Pitney Bowes reported solid 3Q results, with EBIT, EPS and FCF 
improving y/y despite a 1% revenue decline, accompanied by increases to the midpoints 
of full-year revenue and FCF guidance. PBI enters 2027 with improving underlying trends 
in both operating segments, though several transition-related headwinds will likely 
obscure that progress in reported results. Presort should benefit from the carryover of 
~2bn annualized pieces won following PCI’s exit, network optimization, more rational 
industry pricing and new sortation equipment, providing a path toward sustainable 
margins in the mid-to-upper-20% range. We note PCI accounted for the majority of 3Q 
volume growth, leaving the ex-PCI trajectory unclear, and the larger acquired customers 
carry lower revenue per piece and structurally lower margins. SendTech is positioned to 
return to underlying growth in 2027 as retention improves and PBI invests in shipping 
software and product innovation. That said, the SendTech contract runoff will create a 
$60-80mn revenue headwind and pressure margins through stranded costs. Meanwhile, 
PBI’s strategic review has entered its external phase, adding potential optionality 
without a defined timeline or outcome. We believe Presort’s volume and margin 
opportunity and SendTech’s improving core revenue trajectory are offset by lower PCI 
unit economics, the SendTech contract runoff and lower FCF in 2027. Link to our 10/29 
note here.

CoStar (CSGP, Buy): Mixed 3Q as bookings weaken and underlying outlook declines, 
but margin story remains intact. Following 3Q results, we believe CSGP’s investment 
case increasingly rests on rebuilding sales capacity and converting improving bookings 
into revenue growth, with cost reductions providing near-term EPS support. Annualized 
net new bookings declined 56% y/y to $37mn in 3Q, reflecting weakness concentrated in 
Apartments.com and Homes.com as customer budget pressure, elevated cancellations 
and insufficient productive sales capacity weighed on performance. Underlying revenue 
guidance was also reduced by $35mn on a comparable basis, split evenly between lower 
Domain transactional revenue and Residential subscription revenue, despite a reported 
guidance increase that included ~$60mn from Zonda. That said, CSGP expects bookings 
to have bottomed in 3Q and improve sequentially in 4Q as sales capacity rebuilds, with 
healthy renewal rates and LoopNet’s strongest bookings quarter of 2026 indicating that 
weakness is not broad based. The company is shifting Homes.com recruiting toward 
experienced field sellers and plans to expand total sales headcount in 2027, which 
should improve customer acquisition and account coverage. We believe at least 
~$200mn of additional efficiencies over the next 12-18 months will drive progress 
toward the company’s $1.25bn 2028 EBITDA target with margins of ~26% vs 22% in 
2026. Link to our 10/28 note here. 
TransUnion (TRU, Neutral): Solid 3Q with resilient non-mortgage growth mitigated 
by mortgage and margin headwinds. TransUnion reported solid 3Q results, with 
revenue, EBITDA margins and EPS outperforming our estimates and consensus and the 
full-year revenue and EPS guide raised. Non-mortgage trends remain resilient, with 
company-wide revenue excluding mortgage growing ~5.5% y/y, auto increasing 13% 
despite lower industry volumes and International delivering broad-based growth. We 
find that Insurance is providing an increasingly durable source of non-credit growth, 
supported by ramping core credit and driving record contracts that have yet to fully 
ramp. Mortgage remains the primary source of uncertainty, in our view, as higher rates 
drove a 16% y/y decline in 3Q inquiries. TRU expects mortgage inquiries to fall more 
than 20% y/y in 4Q, contributing to a deceleration in OCC revenue growth excluding 
FICO royalties to 3-5% from ~5.5% in 3Q. That said, bi-merge exposure appears 
manageable, with TRU estimating a complete shift of FHFA-regulated loans to bi-merge 
would reduce annual revenue by ~$33mn and EPS by $0.15 before mitigation. 
Meanwhile, VantageScore adoption reached ~35% of mortgage credit pulls. TRU has 
achieved $130mn of annual OneTru opex savings and plans to extend the platform 
internationally, though reinvestment and legal costs reduced TRU’s 2026 underlying 
margin expansion outlook to flat to 20 bps and higher interest expense creates a 
~$60mn headwind in 2027. We believe resilient non-mortgage growth, insurance 
momentum and the OneTru rollout are balanced by declining mortgage volumes and 
lower underlying margin expansion. Link to our 10/27 note here.

Brink’s (BCO, Buy): Updating estimates for incremental Latin America FX 
headwinds. We lower our BCO revenue and EPS estimates to incorporate incremental FX 
headwinds, led by recent weakness in the Mexican peso and other Latin American 
currencies. We reduce our revenue estimates by ~1% across 2026-2028, including 
reductions of 0.6% in 2026 and 1.5% in both 2027 and 2028. The revisions are primarily 
concentrated in Latin America, where we lower revenue by $28mn in 2026, $71mn in 
2027 and $76mn in 2028, reflecting a stronger US dollar. We largely maintain our 2026 
and 2028 adjusted EBITDA margin assumptions at 19.0% and 20.1%, and lower our 
2027 forecast by 10 bps to 19.5% to reflect the mix impact from lower revenue in BCO’s 
higher-margin Latin American markets. Our adjusted EPS estimates decline from $9.23 
to $9.08 in 2026, from $10.60 to $10.25 in 2027 and from $12.26 to $11.88 in 2028. As 
a result of our estimate changes, we lower our 12-month price target from $138 to 
$134. We continue to believe BCO’s execution of its DRS/AMS strategy will support 
sustained revenue mix improvement, mid-single-digit organic growth and 50-70 bps of 
annual EBITDA margin expansion over time. Link to our 10/26 note here. 
Korn Ferry (KFY, Buy): Updating estimates for TOS growth and post-AMS capital 
allocation. We lower our KFY revenue and EPS estimates to reflect more moderate 
Talent & Organizational Solutions growth and fewer share repurchases following the 
AMS acquisition. We reduce our TOS revenue assumptions by ~1% in F2027-F2029 to 
reflect the time required for recently hired Digital sellers to reach full productivity, 
lowering consolidated revenue by ~0.3% in each year. We maintain our margin 
assumptions, resulting in corresponding reductions to adjusted EBITDA and net income. 
We also increase our diluted share count estimates as we assume KFY directs more free 
cash flow toward delevering following the AMS transaction and completes fewer 
buybacks than previously modeled. Together, the revenue and share count changes 
reduce our adjusted EPS estimates from $5.77 to $5.69 in F2027, from $6.51 to $6.32 in 
F2028 and from $7.44 to $7.16 in F2029. We continue to believe KFY’s integrated selling 
model, diversified business mix and greater exposure to longer-duration contracted 
revenue can drive market share gains and more durable growth, with additional upside 
over time from AMS revenue synergies and a recovery in hiring demand. Link to our

Chart of the Week 
Real wages have declined for six consecutive months. Real wages fell 0.7% y/y in 
September 2026, the sixth consecutive decline and the weakest reading since early 
2023. The reversal reflects pressure from both sides, as nominal wage growth slowed to 
3.0% y/y and CPI inflation remained elevated at 3.7% y/y. Real wages increased through 
most of 2023-2025 as inflation moderated faster than wage growth, with growth 
peaking at 1.8% y/y in March 2025. That support has since faded as labor-market 
cooling has slowed wage gains and renewed price pressures have lifted inflation. 
Continued declines in real wages would constrain household purchasing power and 
represent an incremental headwind to consumer spending and consumer credit, 
particularly for lower tier consumers.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
