Canadian Pacific Kansas City Ltd. (CP.TO): 3Q26 EPS Light on Margins; 
Double-Digit EPS Growth Guide Reiterated for FY26
28 October 2026 | 9:47PM EDT
Bottom Line: CP (Neutral) reported 3Q26 adjusted core combined operating EPS 
of C$1.28 versus C$1.31 forecasts for GSe/Visible Alpha Consensus Data. Adjusted 
core combined OR of 61.9% was 80bps behind the 61.1% consensus forecast 
(60.9% GSe). Net/net, EBIT was -3% below our forecast on about in line revenue 
(reflecting about inline revenue per carload growth at +10% YoY and inline carload 
growth at up ~5% YoY). 
Net/net, despite the slight miss we think the company did a good job running an 
efficient railroad and navigating a tough fuel headwind environment. Despite some 
near-term cost pressure, we leave estimates unchanged looking forward on the back 
of solid volume and yield potential beyond 2026.

Results: Revenue was inline with our forecast, driven by the combination of inline 
yield growth (revenue/carload +10% YoY vs +10% forecast) and about inline carload 
growth (+5.2% YoY vs +4.8% forecast); non-freight revenue came in slightly above 
forecast (C$80M vs C$73M GSe). Adjusted core combined OR of 61.9% was behind 
our forecast (vs 60.9% GSe) due primarily to higher-than-forecast salaries and fuel 
expense, partly offset by below-forecast purchased transportation expense. CP 
noted that higher stock based comp and wage inflation drove higher compensation 
expense, partly offset by productivity. The company expects full year headcount to 
be down slightly in 2026. 
Current Trends: CP’s carload growth trajectory accelerated in 3Q26 to +5.2% YoY 
versus flattish 2Q26 carload growth with improvement in both ex-intermodal 
carload growth (+3% vs +1% in 2Q) and a meaningful improvement in intermodal 
growth (+9% vs -1% in 2Q). While still early in the quarter, the first few weeks of 
rail traffic data for 4Q26 are indicating CP total carload growth remaining 
positive at up ~+4% YoY with growth tilted towards intermodal vs ex-intermodal 
business. In terms of operating metrics, CP saw average train weight up flat (vs 
1%/+9% in 2Q/1Q), train length -1% YoY (vs flat/+7% in 2Q/1Q), and train speed 
+8% YoY.

Estimates: Our prior FY26/27/28 EPS estimates of C$5.10/C$5.90/C$6.65 now stand 
at C$5.07/C$5.90/C$6.65. Essentially unchanged estimates reflect the 3Q miss against 
an unchanged 4Q estimate (C$1.50), reflecting a higher revenue trendline offset by a 
slightly lower margin trajectory. Our 2026 EPS is up ~10% YoY compared to 
management’s guide of low double-digits (and vs our prior estimate of up ~11% YoY). 
Looking Ahead: We think the key focus for CP investors continues to center on the 
medium-to-longer-term synergy and broader growth capture opportunity with focus 
likely to remain on progress on revenue/margin improvement post the company’s 
merger with KSU. In that context, CP exited 2024 with revenue synergies at more than 
double the ~$350M exit-rate for 2023 (i.e., >$700M), exited 2025 at an annual rate 
of ~$1.2B of synergies, and has noted expectations for a $1.4-$1.5B synergy 
run-rate by the end of 2026; furthermore, CP has highlighted continued support arising 
from domestic intermodal opportunities via its MMX180/181 cross-border Mexico-US 
business, which continues to provide a truck-like service offering. CP expects continued 
strong labor productivity in 2026 with head count down modestly against mid 
single-digit volume growth. 
Separately, CP management commented on potential Class 1 rail consolidation — 
reiterating that it believes further consolidation is not necessary at this time 
(stressing the fact that rail mergers must enhance competition versus just preventing 
harm, and highlighting the scale of a UNP/NSC merger). 
Rating/Price Target/Risks: Our 12-month CP.TO price target of C$134 (from C$132 
prior) is based on a ~21.0x P/E multiple (unchanged) applied to our updated and 
rolled-forward Q5-Q8 EPS forecast (C$6.46); our CP 12-month $97 price target (from 
$96) is derived from our CAD price target using a USD/CAD exchange rate of 1.38 
(unchanged). 
Key risks to the downside include macro risk to volume recovery (tied to in part tariff 
implications), execution risk on combining and generating synergies from the KSU/CP 
networks, falling short of synergy expectations, extended/renewed labor stoppages due 
to strikes, and lower near-term spot trucking rates potentially making it more difficult to 
take immediate share (particularly if service does not continue to show improvement). 
Key risks to the upside include volume growth above expectations due to outsized 
share gains or economic uplift, pricing/yield growth stronger-than-expected due to 
favorable mix and/or service gains leading to higher intermodal pricing relative to trucks, 
and faster cost takeout leading to above-normal margin improvement. For CP, we 
continue to use a premium multiple to the overall rail sector given the growth 
opportunities via synergies that CP stands to gain, which are separate from the economy 
and what other rails might earn. It is notable, too, that CP has traded at a higher 
premium in the past when it had the ability to improve margin at an accelerated pace 
due to idiosyncratic levers (i.e., initial PSR implementation led to a valuation uplift 
relative to the market).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
