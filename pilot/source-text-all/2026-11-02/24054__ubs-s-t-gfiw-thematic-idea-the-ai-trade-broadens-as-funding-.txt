GFIW Thematic Idea: The AI trade
broadens as funding becomes the
differentiator
Thomas Hänni
02-Nov-2026, 10:08 ET (EST)
Central Thesis:
• The AI trade is moving into phase two. The focus is shifting from growth at any price to how companies
fund the next phase of AI investment. Balance-sheet flexibility, cash generation and access to low-cost capital
should increasingly drive performance dispersion. In an increasingly capital-constrained backdrop,
hyperscalers remain the higher quality relative exposure within the AI complex.
• Europe’s “second phase of industrial acceleration” creates a supply-side capacity opportunity. As
AI-driven demand strains existing capacity, investment is broadening to the equipment and materials needed
by suppliers to expand production.
• Cleaner positioning, elevated shorts and resilient AI fundamentals create scope for a tactical
short-covering rally. De-grossing in hyperscaler names has resumed, short exposure remains above the
80th percentile over five years and SOX is down 14% MTD. Near term, earnings and the midterms are key
catalysts, with favourable November seasonality providing additional support.
A Visual Perspective: Capex phasing: Tier 1 sell to datacentre developers. Tier 2 sell to Tier 1 to build
capacity. AI capex is broadening beyond direct hyperscaler suppliers. As capacity-constrained Tier 1
companies invest to expand, their capex becomes orders for Tier 2 equipment and materials suppliers, driving
further demand for Tier 3 components.

Thought Leaders’ Deep Dive:
• AI demand remains resilient, but financing conditions are becoming a more important differentiator.
The debate is shifting towards the cost, structure and availability of capital required to fund the next phase of
AI infrastructure. Companies able to self-fund expansion should be better positioned than those reliant on
external financing.
• Balance-sheet strength should increasingly differentiate performance across the AI complex.
Cash-rich hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet can sustain AI investment through a tighter
funding environment, while leveraged AI infrastructure and NeoCloud models are more exposed to higher
credit spreads, refinancing requirements and utilisation.
• Current valuations already imply a meaningful moderation in technology-sector returns. HOLT
forecasts Global Technology CFROI to reach a record 25% in 2027E, while valuations imply a significant

3
decline thereafter, particularly for Semiconductors. Investors are therefore already discounting some
normalisation in returns despite strong near-term fundamentals.
• Higher financing costs and project delays remain important downside risks. Rising funding costs,
delayed power connections and lower utilisation could weaken project economics and defer revenues. For
leveraged operators, refinancing ahead of the expected cash-flow ramp could add pressure to both equity
valuations and credit spreads.
• Near-term positioning argues for a measured approach to adding AI exposure. Weak post-earnings
reactions across the hyperscalers and the prospect of further systematic selling suggest adding exposure
gradually rather than all at once.
• European AI-related equities have remained relatively resilient despite renewed US technology
weakness. The UBS EU AI Winners basket was close to +10% for the week by Thursday before falling 5% on
Friday as US Technology weakness spread to Europe. AI Industrials (+5%) and Healthcare (+4.8%)
nevertheless led the region, while SX5E finished 1% higher.
• Seasonality, positioning and the US midterms provide potential near-term support for equities.
November has historically been the S&P 500’s strongest month, averaging +1.9% since 1950, with only one
negative November over the past decade. Low net exposure and multi-year high gross exposure increase the
potential for short covering, while the S&P 500 has been higher six months after every US midterm election
since 1950.
CIO View:AI investment is expected to remain a positive driver of global equities in 2027, but the next phase
of the theme calls for greater selectivity. As attention shifts from spending commitments toward monetization
and returns, investors should limit single-stock concentration and diversify across the AI value chain,
according to CIO. Preference should go to financially strong technology and infrastructure leaders with sound
valuations, pricing power and lower excess-capacity risk, complemented by broader sector and asset-class
diversification and disciplined use of
volatility.******************************************************************************
