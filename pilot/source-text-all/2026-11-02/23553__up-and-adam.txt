Google’s slowing search revenue pushed the S&P 500 Communication Services sector down 6.6% yesterday, its worst day since 2022. 
In recent weeks, rising hyperscaler capex has fueled AI financing concerns, while SpaceX’s reported pursuit of access to 80,000 cell sites 
has raised worries about telecom competition. The sector is now over 20% below its recent high, the steepest decline of any sector. 
While Communication Services includes some AI beneficiaries, we continue to prefer Technology for exposure to the AI megatrend.
Friday, October 30, 2026
Microsoft’s $25b bond offering, its first since 2017 and reportedly drawing $115b in investor demand, lifts YTD issuance by the top five 
hyperscalers to ~$254b, including non-USD deals. YTD investment grade issuance has now surpassed 2020’s record. Treasuries rallied 
despite the heavy supply, easing concerns that corporate and government borrowing may crowd each other out. As we recently 
explained, massive AI capex remains a key driver, though spending is likely to moderate as the investment cycle matures.

The US economy expanded at a QoQ pace of 3.4% (annualized) in 3Q26, propelled by consumer spending and business capital 
investment. Notably, equipment capex surged 20.9%, reflecting the data center buildout and associated modernization of the electric 
grid. The trade deficit—which lowers reported GDP growth—was a sizable drag, as we flagged earlier this month. Encouragingly, 
headline PCE inflation cooled from 5.0% in 2Q to 2.1% in 3Q, supporting the Fed’s decision this week to hold rates steady.

This Week’s ‘Other’ Rate Decisions: ECB and Bank Of Japan Hold Steady, While Bank Of Canada Hikes 
Source: Bloomberg
Chart of the Day: A Record Year for IG Bond Issuance
In line with consensus expectations, the European Central Bank maintained its policy rate at 2.5%, following hikes in June and 
September. Likewise, the Bank of Japan maintained its policy rate at 1.25%, having hiked twice YTD. One day earlier, the Bank of 
Canada raised its policy rate 25 bps to 2.5%, its first hike since July 2023, as Canadian inflation has remained above target for seven 
months. The Bank of England is now the only G7 central bank yet to hike in 2026, though markets expect it to follow suit on November 5.
Gold remains slightly above its July lows, but silver and platinum fell this week to their lowest levels of 2026. As we have noted 
throughout the Iran conflict, precious metals are less attractive with bond yields near multi-decade highs. More surprising, some 
industrial metals, notably cobalt and lithium, are also at YTD lows. While unwelcome news for the mining industry, lower metals prices 
reduce input costs for a wide range of manufacturers, with some savings potentially passed on to consumers.
