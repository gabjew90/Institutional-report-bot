argument. Additionally, as GATX continues to work through synergy benefits related to 
its book-value acquisition of Wells Fargo’s railcar fleet (plus ongoing strength in 
secondary market gains on sale), we think the bias around estimate revisions remains to 
the upside for our Buy-rated name. We note new railcars are more expensive as new 
railcar prices have already been rising, reflecting broad input cost inflation over the last 
year in addition to section 232 tariffs on tank cars that has likely pushed new railcar 
costs/prices even higher. 
Recall that GATX management has stated that it expects the recent Wells Fargo deal to 
be modestly accretive in Year 1 before seeing more significant accretion thereafter; our 
take remains that there is runway over the next 12-24 months to see GATX capitalize on 
its newly acquired fleet (particularly as its share of the fleet increases over time given the 
call options GATX has on acquiring the JV), lending room to outperform around EPS 
relative to consensus expectations. Given this, we feel comfortable reiterating our 
Buy-rating. Our Buy-rating on GATX is also relatively above ratings on more 
manufacturing/railcar-building exposed peers TRN (Neutral) and GBX (Sell) as we see 
near-term manufacturing headwinds outweighing still-solid leasing fundamentals. 
Drivers vs GSe: North America Rail overall segment profits came in higher than 
expected ($121M vs $100M GSe) due to higher margins and as gains on sale came in 
higher-than-forecast ($50M vs $48M GSe). As we stated initially during 1Q earnings, 
we thought GATX’s better gross margin trendline alongside expectations for continuing 
strength around gains on sale and synergies—in addition to continued support from 
engine leasing segment profits—potentially put the company’s guidance in the 
conservative camp; we think the raised guidance is more reflective of the trendlines 
more apparent in the 3Q ($2.92 EPS vs $2.46 consensus). We also note the company 
reiterated that Wells Fargo synergies are running on pace to double the original 
20-30 cents target for 2026 deal-related benefits, an encouraging sign as synergies 
build further into 2027 (as management as stated more accretion to come after Year 1). 
North America Rail total segment profit margins were significantly above our 
forecast at 27.1% (23.1% GSe) with higher revenue and below forecast expenses 
(maintenance and depreciation lower), in addition to +4% higher gains on sale than 
forecast partly offset by slightly higher interest expense; International Rail margins were 
about inline with forecast at 33.1% (33.7% GSe). GATX’s engine leasing core gross profit 
of $17M was slightly below our $20M forecast (excluded affiliate earnings), while total 
engine leasing segment profit (including affiliates) came in lower at $51M ($63M 
forecast) primarily due to a lower other income, though engine leasing’s share of 
affiliates earnings was above forecast at $46M vs $42M forecast. 
Trends Update: GATX’s railcar fleet utilization increased in 3Q to 98.4% in North 
America (vs ~98% in 2Q and 1Q) while its Lease-Price-Index (LPI) stood at +12.4% (vs 
17%/+22% in 2Q/1Q), meaning new railcar leases are still being signed at higher levels 
versus expiring leases (though with some moderation reflecting mix and Wells Fargo’s 
cars in the book) – supporting continued lease rate growth YoY into 2026 as the 
company continues to resign its lease book. Note that at the time of the Wells Fargo 
acquisition, GATX’s fleet utilization was 99.0% while Wells Fargo’s was 96.5%; therefore, 
the slightly lower recent utilization of 98.4% is a byproduct of the fleet combination and 
consistent with expectations outlined by management at the beginning of this year.

estimates over time due to lower sale prices or fewer unit sales, (4) 
worse-than-expected earnings contribution from GATX’s 50/50 aircraft spare engine 
leasing joint venture with Rolls Royce, and (5) higher-than-expected expense

and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
