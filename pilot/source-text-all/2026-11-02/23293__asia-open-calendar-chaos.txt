Asia Open - Calendar chaos
By Preston Pagli
Markets navigated a busy cross-current of central bank decisions, earnings volatility, and geopolitical noise. A more
dovish-than-expected Fed outcome helped push UST yields lower and weighed on the dollar, while a marginally hawkish
Lagarde following the ECB's hold kept December tightening expectations intact. Headline US equity indices moved
lower, led by Alphabet's (-13%) sharp post-earnings decline, though underlying market internals remained far more
constructive. Elsewhere, hopes for de-escalation in the Middle East waned, helping keep energy prices elevated.
Looking ahead, the primary event will be the BoJ rate decision where a hold is fully priced. Our strategists will be
watching whether Governor Ueda lays the groundwork for the next rate hike, though potential hawkishness could be
limited given the recent equity correction.

In Focus‌
Equities: Headline US equity indices traded lower following a mixed slate of earnings across tech and healthcare. Alphabet
(-13%) weighed on the broader market after posting a miss in search growth (+12%), though weakness in tech was partially
offset by strength in healthcare following results from Eli Lilly (+7%) and Merck (+4%). Despite the headline weakness,
breadth remained strong with SPX equal-weight rising ~1.2%. Beneath the surface vol remained subdued with spot VIX
contained and VVIX lower, from 110 last week, however skew remains steep.
Amazon quickly fell following earnings with AWS +39% short of expectations while Intel slumped over 20% as guidance
was well below consensus at $15-$16bn vs. Our analyst Ron Josey will be holding an Amazon debrief at 21:45 ET/01:45
GMT. 
FX: The dollar weakened further following Wednesday's Fed hold with DXY trading -0.30% to 101.88. EURUSD dropped as
much as 0.4% to 1.1105 immediately after the ECB hold, a 17-month low before quickly paring losses. GBPUSD traded 1.338
a strong session against broader USD weakness. BoJ is expected to hold tonight, while there is room for a hawkish surprise
with 20bps priced for December. Our trader notes we could revisit recent lows while there have been several occasions where
a perceived dovish BoJ outcome sparked a rally, only to be followed by official action. On the eFX trading desk volumes were
well above average post-ECB across EURSEK, EURNOK, and EURUSD. Net flows were skewed towards NOK and SEK
while the leveraged segment were buyers of JPY and MXN. Outflows tilted to CHF and DKK. 

Rates: USTs extended gains on equity weakness and a more dovish than expected press conference from Fed Chair Warsh.
Front-end 2y yields trade 4.60%, falling nearly 10bps The 5s30s spread widened to ~55bps from ~50bps at the prior close. A
deluge of US data included GDP, which rose 3.4% QoQ with AI-related investment a strong driver of the growth, which was
partially offset by surging imports. Core PCE inflation rose 0.32%MoM, almost in line with our economist's 0.31% forecast. In
Europe, the desk saw better buying in 30y OATs vs 10y and selling of 15y versus 20y OATs, almost exclusively from HFs,
US Treasury Auction Recap: $44 billion of 7-year notes. The auction stopped on the screws to yield 4.845%. The bid/cover
ratio came in at 2.33 which is at the 36th percentile of the past 12 auctions.
ECB held rates steady though price action was far from steady. A hawkish tilt from Lagarde drove a reversal in EUR with a
clear message: if inflation continues to firm, Dec hike remains in play. Beyond that, policy remains gradual and data
driven.
Commodities: WTI and Brent crude rose towards the end of the session with Brent to $116. Iran said they received a letter
from US VP Vance, which President Trump said was meant to assist Iran with "medical supplies and various things". Iran's
response was Tehran’s positions on ending the war will not be compromised. Shell CEO Wael Sawan said today that the
current high oil price "no longer looks like a temporary spike" given how depleted global inventories are. US natural gas
futures fell late in the session after a government storage report showed inventories rising more than expected.
Elsewhere‌
MXN was the weakest performer across LatAm, our trader sees the market vulnerable ahead of Friday's GDP, as pressure of
a Banxico cut builds. Our trader continues to gold a core long USDMXN position into the data and next week's rate decision.
USDMXN topped out at 18.89, above the one-month high. 
Ahead:
Client call at 21:45 ET / 01:45 GMT: AMZN Post Earnings Debrief
JPY: BoJ Target Rate Decision, along with its latest forecasts for Core CPI. The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate
unchanged next week. Our economists expect Takata to once again dissent, voting for a hike, which would not be surprising.
The characterization of underlying inflation could also advance from "approaching 2%," but the assessment that financial
conditions remain accommodative is unlikely to change. We maintain our base case of a 25bp hike in Dec.
EUR: HICP releases for October for France (07:45 GMT) with state-level German CPI printing ahead of the national print at
13:00 GMT. Spain CPI due at 08:00 GMT. Inflation is expected to rise in most countries where the gas-to-HICP passthrough is
faster (e.g. Italy) but will rise further in the others in early 2027 (e.g. Germany). German HICP is expected at 2.9% YoY vs
3.3% prior. For now, our economists expect Eurozone HICP at 3.9% YoY with core unchanged at 2.5%.
Q3 GDP releases in France (06:30 GMT), Spain (08:00) and Eurozone (10:00). Eurozone growth will have likely slowed, from
0.6% QoQ in Q2 to a likely 0.2% QoQ for Q3 (Citi e.), but excluding Ireland, the underlying pace will lie remained roughly
unchanged (and marginally softer than the ECB’s forecast of 0.3%).
GBP: Monitor trends in DMP survey results for 1-Year CPI Expectations, 3M/1-Year CPI Expectations, and Output Price
Expectations for October at 09:30 GMT.
HUF: GDP data for Q3 P at 07:30 GMT. Our economists forecast the GDP growth to improve in Q3 to 1.9% YoY from 1.7%
recorded in Q2. Monthly activity data is pointing towards further recovery, though impact of EU funds flow may be not
reflected until Q4.
CZK: GDP QoQ for Q3 A at 08:00 GMT. Bloomberg consensus forecasts GDP to rise towards 0.5% QoQ/1.6% YoY vs
0.3%/1.8% respectively prior.
TWD: GDP YoY for Q3 A at 08:00 GMT. Our economists expect another strong quarter, with GDP growth accelerating by
13.4% YoY vs 12.93% prior, supported by net exports and investment amid broadening AI demand. Watch consumption
strength to gauge effects of positive wealth effects from stock market gains.
PLN: Preliminary CPI data for October at 08:30 GMT. Our economists estimate that headline inflation declined in October to
3.8% YoY from 4.1% prior. The government measures to lower fuel prices is set to be the main driver of decline in CPI, as our

proxy points to 10.7% MoM decrease in fuel prices at the pump.
