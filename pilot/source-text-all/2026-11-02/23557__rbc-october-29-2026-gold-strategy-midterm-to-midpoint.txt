Gold Strategy: Midterm to Midpoint? 
What comes next after the midterms for gold? Based on current polling, statements, and 
prediction markets, it’s likely legislative gridlock, higher uncertainty, and shifting macro hurdles. 
Thus, we think our mid-to-high scenario view still holds post-midterms, implying higher prices 
versus current levels despite macro headwinds. We still think a case can be made for achieving 
that range after the midterms, largely driven by elevated uncertainty. While we view our middle 
scenario as a cleaner macro take on where gold should end up given the mix of macro headwinds 
currently blowing and the existing uncertainty at play, our high scenario reflects a more 
significant pricing of uncertainty into gold. We have observed the price discovery process evolve 
during Trump’s second term, but overall, given the types of uncertainty percolating and potential 
post-midterm dynamics we envision as relevant to gold, we think uncertainty could be even 
more elevated and should help get us to at least our middle scenario price, if not higher. 
• 
Elections alone do not have a consistent impact on prices, so what matters is analyzing 
outcomes against the context of gold trading. We think uncertainty is key here, though there 
are some relevant macro headwinds that could be affected too. Uncertainty has been a 
material pillar of performance 2025-to-date, though it remains a quantifiably fickle driver since 
various measures work only intermittently. Regardless, it can’t be ignored. 
• 
Democratic gains (likely, based on polling and prediction markets) would likely create 
legislative stalemate, pushing Trump toward more executive action and increasing the very 
types of policy uncertainty that have already supported gold. Additionally, uncertainty could 
intensify further through congressional investigations and noise. 
• 
Iran reescalation risk post-midterms could drive oil higher, shifting gold investors’ view on 
inflation and yields, creating a stronger yield headwind for gold. Regardless of midterm 
outcomes, elevated policy uncertainty should support gold, making the persistence of 
uncertainty the key driver to monitor, although there are still macro considerations.

Election Impacts and Types of Uncertainty 
While you tend to see more US bar and coin demand under Democratic administrations, there 
is not really a consistent notable impact on gold’s price performance on the back of elections. 
Thus, it is important to analyze each election independently, weighing the resultant policies 
and political environments against the context of current gold trading. In that vein, we weigh 
the relevance of uncertainty and the potential drivers of that uncertainty based on probable 
outcomes, against some potential high-level macro implications. While President Trump is not 
on the ballot in name, the uncertainties that have dominated his second term are deeply 
intertwined with gold and markets at large. 
Uncertainty has long been a key pillar of our view on gold, and particularly so in recent years 
when we have seen it as a material driver of gold interest and prices. Over President Trump’s 
second term, much of the gold-relevant uncertainty has emerged in the form of trade and 
tariffs, Fed independence concerns, US government shutdowns, geopolitics and armed 
conflict, etc. Much of this is linked to the administration, and we have continued to observe a 
strong correlation between gold prices and Trump’s approval rating (the correlation has 
deteriorated but is still at -77%, see Figure 2). The Iran conflict has added to uncertainty, 
earning gold a geopolitical risk premium, but it has also filtered into inflation concerns and 
yield expectations from gold’s perspective. It has been difficult to identify a single quantifiable 
measure of uncertainty, as various uncertainty indexes have worked well at various points, 
only to inevitably fall away. This makes it a fickle driver, regardless of how much weight we 
place on it analytically. 
Outcome, Uncertainty and Gold’s Macro Perspective 
This brings us to the potential midterm outcomes and resultant levels of uncertainty. 
Democrats appear likely to take control of at least the House of Representatives based on 
prediction markets (see Figure 3) and polling data. Currently, prediction markets lean towards 
significant democratic gains, which gold may view as potentially limiting deficit-expanding 
legislation. For gold, this could shift how gold markets view upwards pressure on yields. 
Democratic gains would likely limit the prospect for any significant legislative wins and imply 
a potential legislative stalemate for the administration’s legislative agenda for the remainder 
of Trump’s second term. This could push Trump towards more executive order-driven activity, 
introducing volatility, potentially adding to general market unease, and increasing the types of 
uncertainty we’ve already laid out.

Whether a split congress or a larger blue wave, legislative gridlock seems likely given legislation 
still has to get through the White House, and bipartisan legislation has been elusive. Yet, even 
with a full or partial check or balance on the executive branch, we do not think the types of 
uncertainty that are relevant to gold go anywhere. In fact, they may actually increase. Most of 
the aforementioned uncertainties we’ve already laid out are largely under executive authority. 
Thus, uncertainty in the form of trade, tariffs, armed conflict, etc., may not go anywhere. 
Additionally, in the Democrat-controlled Congress scenario, high levels of noise emanating 
from congressional investigations could add to the uncertainty. We suspect that high levels of 
noise from this type of activity could filter into gold as upwards pressure on prices. 
As we outlined in Helima’s note, the risk of a reescalation in the Iran conflict is high post 
midterms. An effort to “escalate to de-escalate” would likely drive upwards pressure on oil 
prices, which gold would view as risk for further inflation and upwards pressure on yields (as 
well as associated knock-on effects). This means an even stronger hurdle for gold to overcome. 
Beyond that, though, there are other implications to consider, beyond what we believe may 
be generally elevated levels of uncertainty. While Democratic gains in the midterms could limit 
new deficit-growing spending bills amid legislative gridlock, which could support gold by easing 
upwards expectations on bond yields among gold investors, on the other hand, a Republican 
win could heighten focus on potential further fiscal expansion and do the opposite. We suspect 
gold’s sensitivity to currency moves and yields will be the more durable prevailing macro 
impact from the midterms from gold, either way, even if uncertainty remains paramount in 
our view.

Conclusion 
The midterm election outcome reinforces our mid-to-high scenario for gold, in our view. A 
Democratic win could constrain deficit spending via legislative gridlock and limit upside yield 
pressure in the eyes of gold investors — a macro positive— but could simultaneously elevate 
uncertainty through potential executive action and congressional investigations. Conversely, 
a Republican outcome could accelerate fiscal expansion and push yields higher, creating a 
steeper macro headwind for gold. Critically, the sources of uncertainty most relevant to gold 
over the last two years — namely, trade policy, geopolitical tension, Fed independence 
concerns, etc. — remain largely under executive authority and appear unlikely to dissipate 
regardless of midterm outcomes. Combined with elevated Iran conflict reescalation risk post-
midterms, and congressional investigations, we believe uncertainty itself will likely remain
