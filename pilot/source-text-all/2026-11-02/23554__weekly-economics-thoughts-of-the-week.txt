The strength of the US economy during the third quarter was hardly a surprise. We expected economic
growth of 3.5%, and the first estimate came in only slightly below that at a 3.4% quarter-over-quarter,
seasonally adjusted annualized rate. Consumer spending and investment tied to the AI and data center
buildout remained the primary drivers of growth. However, the conflict with Iran also had a larger-than-
expected impact on economic activity. Federal defense spending increased 11.7% during the quarter while
nondefense spending surged 22.2%. As a result, government consumption and investment rose 5.3% after
edging down 0.1% in the second quarter.
As we have argued repeatedly in previous Weekly Economics reports, consumer demand remains the
backbone of the US economy. Not only does it account for roughly 70% of GDP, but it is also the most
stable component of economic activity. The Investment component, however, is a different story.
Although smaller than consumption, it is significantly more volatile and often plays a central role in
economic downturns. When investors turn cautious, what John Maynard Keynes called "animal spirits"
can weaken, leading to slower investment and, ultimately, slower economic growth.
This is where our concern lies.
Financial markets often assume that today's growth trends will continue indefinitely. Former Federal
Reserve Chairman Alan Greenspan famously described this tendency as "irrational exuberance." We are
not suggesting that current conditions resemble the periods Greenspan had in mind. Nevertheless, the
remarkable performance of the stock market, combined with the outsized contribution of AI- and data
center-related spending to overall investment and GDP growth, deserves attention.

We are not arguing that this trend cannot continue for several more years. However, it raises the possibility

the widening US trade deficit and the strength in capital goods imports. During the third quarter, net 
exports reduced real GDP growth by 3.32 percentage points, while gross private domestic investment added 
3.27 percentage points. 
That does not mean the contribution from investment was entirely offset by imports. Capital goods 
represent only part of total imports, which also include consumer goods, industrial supplies, and other 
products. Furthermore, as we showed in last week's Weekly Economics, domestic production of computer 
and electronic products has been growing at an exceptionally strong pace, indicating that US

Durable Orders: New orders of durable goods were in line with expectations in September after a small upward revision to 
August’s results. New orders of transportation equipment were strong; however, the strength in new orders of capital 
goods, especially nondefense capital goods, continues to point to a still strong investment in AI and data centers. Both new 
orders of nondefense as well as defense aircraft and parts were strong during the month of September. The weakest sector 
on the capital goods side has been new orders of defense capital goods, which have weakened for three consecutive 
months. This sector should start to improve in the coming months. New orders for manufactured durable goods were in line 
with expectations for September, up 1.8% compared to August of this year after increasing by just 0.1% in August, according 
to the US Census Bureau. If we exclude new orders of transportation equipment, new orders increased by 0.5% after 
increasing by 0.3% in August. If we exclude new orders of defense equipment, new orders increased by a strong 2.2%, 
month-on-month, after increasing by 0.3% during the previous month. New orders of transportation equipment surged by 
4.6% during the month of September after falling by 0.3% in August. Much of the increase in new orders of transportation 
equipment was due to a 28.4% month-on-month increase in new orders of nondefense aircraft and parts. New orders of 
defense aircrafts and parts were also up, by 5.7%, after increasing by 2.3% during the month of August. New orders of 
capital goods were up by 4.8% after increasing by 1.2% in August as new orders of nondefense capital goods increased by 
6.2% while defense capital goods orders declined by 1.8%, the third consecutive monthly decline. Strong capital goods 
orders have continued to drive new manufacturing orders as the AI and data center buildup continues.
FHFA Home Price Index: Home prices increased by 0.3%, month-on-month, and by 2.4% compared to a year ago, according 
to the FHFA Home Price Index. The year-over-year rate of HPI appreciation slowed down slightly in August compared to July 
but is stronger than earlier in the year. It is clear that while mortgage rates have increased considerably, it has still not put 
downward pressure on home prices across the country, which is further reducing home affordability. Home prices, as 
measured by the FHFA Home Price Index (HPI) increased by 0.3% in August after doing so by 0.2% in July, according to the 
Federal Housing Finance Agency. On a year-over-year basis, the HPI increased by 2.4%. There were three regions of the

West North-Central region, up 0.7%; the West South-Central region, up 0.6%; the East South-Central region, up 0.5%; and 
finally, the East South-Central region, up 2.1%. Every region of the country saw higher prices in August of this year 
compared to a year earlier. “The FHFA HPI is a comprehensive collection of publicly available house price indexes that

Consumer Confidence: After reaching its lowest level since April 2014 in September, October Consumer Confidence was 
higher than expected, up 7.5 points to 91.7. Softening gasoline prices helped boost confidence, with both the Present and 
Expectations subindices rising in the month. Despite this increase in October, the index remains on a downward trend that 
has persisted since 2022, with respondents expressing frustration with higher prices and costs. The Consumer Confidence 
(CC) Index published by The Conference Board was higher than expected in October, at 91.7 compared to an upwardly 
revised reading of 84.2 in September, according to the release. FactSet consensus expected a reading of 80.5 for the month
