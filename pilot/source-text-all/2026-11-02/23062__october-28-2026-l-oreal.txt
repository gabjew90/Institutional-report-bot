Our view: We update our forecasts following the publication of L'Oréal's
3Q 2026 results. Our adjusted present value derived price target increases
to €480, and we retain our Outperform rating.
Key points:
L'Oréal has delivered the best consistent volume growth in our consumer
staples universe for many years (Aesop's fable) and, in our view, will
continue to do so. Volume growth is best indicator of share price
performance in our opinion (Ch-ch-ch-ch-changes). Consequently, we
retain our Outperform rating.

Valuation
We believe that consumer staples stocks lend themselves
to a DCF valuation methodology owing to the relative
strength and predictability of their cash flow together with
—in some instances—a significant mismatch between capital
expenditure and depreciation charged through the profit and
loss account meaning that P&L-based valuation metrics (PE
ratio and EV/EBITDA ratio) can be misleading. We use a
derivative of a traditional DCF calculation called adjusted
present value (APV) whereby the business's operating cash
flows are discounted at its cost of equity (7% for L'Oréal) and
tax shield at the cost of debt (3%). Assuming a terminal growth
rate of 2.5% after 2040, we derive a fair value for L'Oréal of
€455 per share. Discounting the APV forward by a year at the
cost of equity and deducting our forecast dividend payment,
we obtain a 12-month price target of €480. Our price target
supports our Outperform rating.
Upside scenario
In our upside scenario, we assume the beauty market
accelerates and L'Oréal maintains its outperformance. We
assume sales growth is 1 percentage points above our base
scenario per annum declining from 7.2% in 2027 to 6.6% in
2035. We assume this culminates in a perpetuity growth rate
of 3%. EBIT margin lies 100bps per annum above our base
scenario. This generates an upside value of €590.
Downside scenario
We assume sales growth is 200bps below our base case from
2027 until 2035 due to a material slowdown in the beauty
market and tightening of consumer spending. We assume EBIT
margins also 200bps lower than our base case. The terminal
growth rate remains at 2.5%. This generates a downside value
of €370.
Investment summary
We have long admired L'Oréal's ability to identify categories/
markets with attractive growth potential and rapidly commit
resources to stimulate and capture those opportunities.
Additionally, its broad product coverage, marketing firepower
and impending ramp-up in innovation/product launches
position the company well to strengthen its market leadership
further.
Our work on hair and skin care markets reinforce our positive
view on the beauty category. The sector is well-positioned
amidst structural demographic shift, with key players in the
markets proactively innovating to support category growth. It
is also a hiding place from risks of lower product consumption
by Gen Z over the long-term - very real concerns in tobacco
and beverages.
It is clear that L'Oréal is not immune to a challenging
macro backdrop and its recent performance came in below
expectations. However, we see this as temporary and the pace
of growth recovery could surprise on the upside. Lowered
expectations and de-rating over the last year suggest risk is to
the upside.
Risks to rating and price target
• A further deterioration in consumer confidence in its key
markets (particularly China) would be negative for sales and
margins.
• More intensified price competition could negatively affect
top-line performance, particularly for the Consumer
Product division. New and effective innovation from
competitors would lead to a further slow down in
Dermatological Beauty.
• Worse-than-expected performance of the recent major
acquisition, Aesop, would be negative for the shares.
• Unsuccessful innovation could affect L'Oréal's market
positioning and ability to gain market share.
• Lower consumption of beauty products on the back of a
higher adoption of injectables would be headwinds for the
beauty sector.

Company description
L'Oréal is a global cosmetics company with expertise across five categories: hair care, hair colour, skincare, make-up, and
fragrances. It operates through four divisions: Consumer Products (37% of 2024 sales; key brands: L'Oréal Paris, Garnier, Maybelline
New York), L'Oréal Luxe (36%; Lancôme, Giorgio Armani, YSL Beauté, Biotherm, Ralph Lauren, and — following the 2025 acquisition
of Kering Beauté — Creed and beauty licences for Gucci, Bottega Veneta, and Balenciaga), Professional Products (11%; L'Oréal
Professionnel, Redken, Kérastase, Matrix), and Dermatological Beauty (16%; Vichy, La Roche-Posay, Galderma). The group derives
33% of sales from Europe, 27% from North America, 24% from North Asia, 9% from SAPMENA-SSA, and 7% from Latin America.
The Bettencourt family owns 35% of the share capital and Nestlé holds 20%.
