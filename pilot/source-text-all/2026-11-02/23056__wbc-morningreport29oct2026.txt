TODAY’S INSIGHTS
Financial Markets
It was a busy session for markets, with plenty to digest. The 
Fed held steady and the Bank of Canada hiked for the first 
time in three years, both as expected. Meanwhile, the RBNZ 
delivered a surprise by holding rates steady, though a hike 
remains largely priced by year-end. In the UK, Chancellor 
Healey unveiled a modest Budget, delivering £4.1bn in cost-
of-living relief and pledging to lift defence spending to 3% of 
GDP by 2030.
•	US equities ended the session higher following the Fed 
decision. The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq closed 0.3% higher, 
while the Dow Jones rose 0.2%. European stocks steadied as 
Chancellor John Healey’s tax increase on lenders proved less 
severe than feared. The Euro Stoxx 50 and FTSE 100 both 
closed 0.1% higher.
•	Asian equities moved higher as strong earnings revived 
optimism around AI, outweighing concerns over funding 
costs. The Nikkei 225 rose 1.1%, while the Kospi jumped 
6.6% to its highest level in two weeks. The CSI 300 gained 
0.8% and the Hang Seng rose 0.2%, while the ASX 200 was 
broadly unchanged.
•	Short-dated US Treasuries swung following the Fed 
decision. The 2-year yield initially rose after rates were left 
unchanged, before reversing to close 3bps lower as Chair 
Warsh signalled the Fed would assess incoming information 
and not prejudge the December meeting. Futures markets 
still put the chance of a hike at 85%. The 10-year yield 
rose 3bps, with markets focusing on his comments around 
financial conditions becoming tighter. The 10-year gilt yield 
fell 3bps in a vote of confidence in the Budget, while the 10-
year bund yield edged 1bp higher.
•	Australian bond yields rose following the firm CPI result. 
Yields had already begun moving higher ahead of the release 
after comments from the RBA Governor that “inflation likely 
to remain elevated for some time” and needing “a further 
period of subdued growth”. The 3-year and 10-year yields 
both gained 6bps. Swap markets are pricing an 85% chance 
of a rate hike by the RBA next week, with a hike now fully 
priced in by year-end.
•	The USD index dropped 0.2%, with the AUD (+0.5%), 
euro (+0.3%) and pound (+0.2%) all gaining against the 
greenback. The loonie swung between losses and gains, 
ultimately rising 0.4% against the USD. Meanwhile, the AUD 
rose 1.4% against the NZD and touched a 13-year high.
•	Oil prices gained on reports that the US could resume 
attacks on Iran after the midterms, but pared some of 
the advance after Iran’s Foreign Minister said the country 
“continues to give diplomacy a chance to succeed”. Brent 
closed 1.0% higher at US$115.0/bbl, while WTI gained 
2.7% to US$102.6/bbl. Gold and iron ore rose 0.5% and 
0.8%, respectively, while copper was largely unchanged.
International Data
The FOMC held at their October meeting, albeit with two 
dissents, Governor Cook and Cleveland Fed President Hammack 
preferring to hike by 25bps at this meeting. The statement was 
short and, in the press conference, Chair Warsh again refrained 
from providing definitive guidance on the near-term outlook for 
policy. Along with the two dissents, and Chair Warsh noting 
in the press conference that consumer inflation has now been 
above target for 6 years, the “accumulation of more information 
will help the Committee determine the policy stance” suggests 
another 25bp hike is likely in December, and that the FOMC will 
remain attentive to any broadening in inflation and related risks 
during 2027. Chair Warsh also announced a structural change in 
how the FOMC undertake policy from 2027. One meeting, in the 
fall, will focus on monetary policy strategy versus the current 
stance of monetary policy. Chair Warsh then intends for 2 of the 
8 meetings in 2028 to have this strategy focus.
 
US wholesale inventories rose 0.6% in September.
 
The Bank of Canada delivered to expectations at its October 
meeting, increasing the policy rate by 25bps. “The Canadian 
economy is expected to grow 1% in 2026, and 2% in 2027 and 
2028. This is a somewhat stronger overall growth projection 
than the July Report.” 
 
Domestic Data
In Australia, the September CPI came in firm, rising 0.8%mth, 
taking the year-ended pace to 4.3%. The monthly trimmed 
mean printed 0.3%mth and 3.6%yr higher, with the year-ended 
pace ranging between 3.5-3.6% for five consecutive months 
now. The policy-relevant quarterly trimmed mean rose 1.0%qtr 
and 3.7%yr (see here for full analysis).
In NZ, the RBNZ left the OCR unchanged at 2.75%. The 
committee voted 4:3 against a 25bp hike, with Governor 
Breman within the “on hold” camp (see here for full analysis).
