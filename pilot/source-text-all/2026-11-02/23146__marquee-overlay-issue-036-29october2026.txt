Oil Expression: The $27 Risk Premium 
THE VIEW 
From Daan Struyven (Commodities Research) GS Exchanges Interview on 23 October 2026 
Brent has risen to roughly $112/bbl as escalating Middle East tensions and growing downside risks 
to supply have increased precautionary buying in both physical and financial markets. Yet the 
underlying oil balance has changed less dramatically: GS estimates Brent fair value at approximately 
$85/bbl, implying a geopolitical risk premium of roughly $27/bbl. With market-price risks now 
increasingly symmetric, the path from here hinges on whether supply risks intensify or a durable 
diplomatic resolution allows that premium to unwind.

Brent's $27/bbl Risk Premium 
 
Source: Goldman Sachs Global Investment Research Division 
GS estimates Brent fair value at approximately $85/bbl compared with a market price around 
$112/bbl, implying a geopolitical risk premium of roughly $27/bbl. In Struyven’s assessment, 
essentially all of the recent increase in crude prices reflects a higher risk premium rather than an 
increase in underlying fair value. 
Brent's Forward Curve Prices a Significant Normalization 
 
Source: Marquee PlotTool Pro as of 27 Oct 2026 
Brent remains steeply backwardated, with the current forward curve declining from roughly 
$114/bbl at the front toward the high-$80s by late 2027. The curve points to substantial 
normalization from current levels even as near-term supply risk remains elevated.

Brent Remains Elevated as Headline Geopolitical Risk Recedes

Brent has remained elevated even as the Geopolitical Risk Index has retraced from recent spikes. 
The divergence highlights that the oil risk premium is not simply a function of headline geopolitical 
risk, with precautionary physical buying, supply-chain disruption and financial hedging demand also 
supporting prices. 
Oil and Front-End Yields Have Risen Together 
 
Source: Marquee PlotTool Pro as of 28 Oct 2026, past performance is not indicative of future returns, access plot HERE 
Brent and the US 2Y Treasury yield have risen materially over the past year, with both moving sharply 
higher again through September and October. The recent co-movement highlights the broader 
cross-asset relevance of elevated oil prices as markets contend with higher energy costs and tighter 
financial conditions.
