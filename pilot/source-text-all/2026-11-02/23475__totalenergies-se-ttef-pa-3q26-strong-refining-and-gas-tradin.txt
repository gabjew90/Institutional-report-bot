3Q26 First Take: Solid results and WC release drive $6bn gearing drop qoq; $2.5 bn of buybacks for 
4Q26 and 1Q27 
 
 
Results 
TotalEnergies reported a strong set of 3Q26 results on October 29, with adjusted net 
income at US$9.1 bn, slightly ahead of company-compiled consensus of US$8.9 bn 
(+2%), and OCF (pre-OWC) at US$12.3 bn, 1% above consensus of US$12.1 bn. 
Across divisions, the beat was supported by a record performance in Refining & 
Chemicals at US$3,601 mn (in line with consensus of US$3,600 mn), strong Integrated 
Gas at US$1,885 mn post-tax (+5% vs consensus of US$1,800 mn) and Marketing & 
Services at US$467 mn (+17% vs consensus of US$400 mn). Integrated Power came in 
line at US$602 mn (+0.3% vs consensus of US$600 mn). This was partially offset by 
Upstream, where operating profit came in at US$3,157 mn (-4% vs consensus of 
US$3,300 mn). 
3Q26 FCF (pre-OWC) amounted to US$7.5 bn (vs GSe of US$8.2 bn), with solid operating 
cash flow (US$12.3 bn, consensus US$12.1 bn) partially offset by higher-than-expected 
organic capital expenditures of US$4.8 bn. The Net debt/Equity ratio (GS def.) came in at 
16.5% (vs. 22.3% in 2Q26 and below GSe of 18.0%), and net Debt decreased by c.US$6 
bn qoq, supported by a working capital release of c.US$3.1 bn. 
Total authorized US$2.5 bn of buybacks for cancellation for each of 4Q26 and 1Q27, 
and the BoD announced the third 2026 interim DPS of €0.90, +5.9% yoy (in line with GSe 
and unchanged qoq). 
Guidance 
For FY26, TotalEnergies maintained its net capex guidance of c.US$15 bn (US$11.2 bn 
spent in 9M26). Looking to 4Q26, excluding the impact of the conflict in the Middle East, 
the company anticipates production growing by more than +3% vs 4Q25, in line with its 
annual guidance. The impact of the conflict is now decreasing versus 3Q26 and is 
estimated at less than 5% of the company’s total production, primarily related to limited 
LNG exports via the Strait of Hormuz. 
The average LNG selling price is guided to be above US$14/mmbtu in 4Q26 (vs 
US$12.3/mmbtu in 3Q26). Refinery throughput is expected to be broadly comparable to 
3Q26 (utilization at 88% on crude in 3Q26 vs 80% in 2Q26), benefiting from the 
return-to-normal operations at the SATORP platform in Saudi Arabia, despite a 20-day 
turnaround at the Normandy platform.

Capex increase and production growth extended to 2035 at their 2026 CMD 
 
 
At its 2026 CMD, net capex guidance was set at US$14-17bn pa over 2027-2032, versus 
prior guidance of US$14-16bn pa over 2027-2030, a c.US$0.5bn/y increase at the 
mid-point and a two-year extension of the visibility window, in line with our expectation 
in our preview of gross capex moving up to c.US$16bn in 2027 and supporting 
TotalEnergies’ industry-leading upstream growth. 
The company confirmed +4% pa growth in total energy production (oil, gas, electricity) 
through 2030 and >+3% pa in Oil & Gas over 2025-2030. For the first time, it also set an 
ambition of +2-3% pa Oil & Gas growth over 2030-2035, sustaining a production 
plateau of c.3 mboed to 2035, supported by an industry-leading proven reserve life of 
>12 years and its portfolio in Namibia, Nigeria, Libya, Malaysia, Mozambique and Papua 
New Guinea. 
Our proprietary Top Projects work suggests that TotalEnergies has the longest reserve 
life in the sector and the strongest cash flow growth from new projects to 2030.  
Among EU Oils, TotalEnergies offers the most de-risked oil production growth, with the 
Tilenga/Kingfisher Uganda start-up in 2026 (+94 kboe/d net at plateau), GranMorgu 
Suriname in 2028 (+55 kboe/d) and potential FIDs for Venus/Mopane, which could start 
in 2030/32.

Best-in-class energy transition story and lower carbon intensity 
 
 
We outlined in our deep-dive analyses, Re-Imagining Big Oils - The scale and profitability 
of low-carbon activities and Carbonomics, how the low-carbon transition is changing 
the competitive landscape of the global energy sector. We expect Big Oils to play a 
critical role in facilitating this transition to cleaner, more sustainable energy provision, 
utilising a wide variety of available tools. These include, among other things, a deeper 
presence in global gas, power chains and retail, as well as greater exposure to 
renewables, bioenergy, hydrogen, natural carbon sinks and carbon-capture solutions. 
The results of our analysis are presented in the exhibits that follow. Overall, as discussed 
in our deep-dive, we estimate that the value of TotalEnergies’ Low Carbon division 
already represents 14% of its current EV on aggregate, having generated US$32bn of 
value for its Low Carbon division, the highest among Big Oils. TotalEnergies stands out 
among the group, having the most advanced and profitable energy transition strategy 
among Big Oils, with an aim for a ROACE of at least 12% in renewables and to be Free 
Cash Flow positive by 2028 in its Integrated Power division.  
TotalEnergies could deliver a c.20% reduction in absolute emissions (including all 
scopes) by 2030, on our estimates, in line with the path it has laid out to net zero 
TotalEnergies started this transition several years ago; the company has committed to 
become net-zero across all scopes by 2050. On its way to carbon neutrality, 
TotalEnergies is aiming to reduce Scope 1+2 net operating emissions by over c.50% by 
2030 (from a 2015 base), as well as reduce Scope 3 emissions in Oil by 40% over the 
same time frame. Overall, the company is targeting the reduction of its Scope 1+2+3 
emissions by c.25% by 2030 vs. 2015. TotalEnergies is building out an integrated 
portfolio of low-carbon technologies, including renewables (targeting 100-120 TWh of 
electricity from its Integrated Power division by 2030), bioenergy, petrochemicals and 
early positions in hydrogen and CCUS. On our estimates, TotalEnergies could reduce its 
well-to-wheel emissions (including Scopes 1,2,3) c.20% by 2030, having already led the 
total Scope 3 emissions intensity reduction among Big Oils, as shown in our report.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
