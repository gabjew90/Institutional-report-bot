As we move further into an eventful 4Q26 which includes the 3Q earnings season, 
the November elections, and two consequential Fed meetings, we take a closer look 
at positioning across Natural Resources over the past month of trading. This week in 
the Pulse, we ask our senior analyst team to identify their strongest and weakest 
performing mid/large cap stocks over the past month, discuss key debates for the 
stock, and provide their view from here.

Integrated Oils & Refining: 
MPC has been our strongest performer over the past month, benefiting from 
elevated refining margins, widening light-heavy crude differentials, and positive 
revisions to 2027 consensus earnings. More specifically, MPC’s monthly indicators 
highlight substantial margin momentum, with September’s blended crack reaching 
$53.81/bbl compared to $33.54/bbl in 2Q26. Additionally, surging international 
dirty freight rates have contributed to more favorable light-heavy crude differentials, 
notably for WCS and Latin American grades, which favors US refiners with coastal 
exposure. We note the company has highlighted that every $1/bbl widening in sour 
crude differentials drives an estimated $520 mn annual EBITDA impact in its R&M 
segment. Furthermore, 2027 FactSet consensus EPS estimates have revised upward 
by ~26% over the last month, ultimately reflecting constructive near term refining 
fundamentals. From here, while we are mindful of MPC’s YTD outperformance, we 
continue to see room for positive consensus estimate revisions which we expect will 
drive share performance. In addition, we continue to see value in MPC’s scale, 
advantaged Gulf Coast/West Coast exposure, low-cost operations, and track record

Energy Services: 
HAL outperformed over the past month, recovering some of the year to date 
underperformance as sentiment improved following the softer outlook provided with 
second quarter earnings. This week, the company reported in-line 3Q results and 
provided commentary on 2027 expectations which we viewed as a positive indicator of 
the company’s ability to drive earnings even in a flat North America activity scenario. 
More broadly, the company remains a key player in the US completions market, where it 
is seeing pricing improvements, and is seeing increasing activity from international 
regions such as Argentina, Africa, and the Middle East. In a base case of unchanged 
Middle East activity and flat North America, we highlight HAL’s ability to realize 
mid-single-digit revenue growth in 2027, supported by more favorable pricing in US land 
amid a higher commodity environment, as well as incremental activity additions in other 
regions. We see HAL trading at a ~2x discount to large-cap peer SLB on our 2028 
estimates, and we continue to see the stock as well-positioned to benefit from activity 
and pricing increases, both domestically and in international markets.

and Pipelines businesses. With the company guiding to lower Communications earnings

Midstream: 
KMI was our strongest performer in midstream over the past month as several open 
seasons to solicit customer interest in new gas pipeline projects boosted expectations

Connector project on SNG ($3.1b capex net to KMI for a phased 2030-2032 in-service) 
and the +2.8 bcf/d Permian Link project on NGPL ($1.6b capex net to KMI for a 3Q30 
in-service). Equally notable was commentary that both projects could be upsized, and 
that KMI is working towards FID on an expansion of their TGP line moving southwest out 
of the Marcellus. The updates not only increased the formal backlog and perception of 
the quality of the shadow backlog, but general feedback also suggests positive investor 
sentiment in KMI’s ability to compete and execute well on the commercial side, 
particularly relative to weaker investor sentiment late last year and earlier this year. The 
update from KMI, alongside several others this week in BTM power (LBRT, INIO, AESI, 
among others), should also support confidence that the broader gas/power macro 
outlook remains constructive – helpful datapoints after several months of investor 
concerns around the theme. We expect enthusiasm to stay relatively elevated for KMI 
into potential large project expansions/FIDs early next year, though we continue to 
monitor 2027 guidance coming up in December (we remain below consensus EBITDA) as 
well as RNG M&A commentary from management as the positive focus from investors 
remains on organic projects in the core nat gas pipeline segment. 
HESM was our weakest performer in midstream over the past month following the 
announced major restructuring of its contracts with its sponsor Chevron, an 
accompanying planned separation from CVX, and another guidance reduction for 2026 
guidance. Under the agreement, CVX would receive a ~50% reduction in its G&P fees per 
unit on HESM’s Bakken footprint and $200m in cash, while HESM receives CVX’s Rockies 
midstream assets and the ~40% equity stake in HESM that CVX owns; other provisions 
include a major restructuring of HESM’s minimum volume commitments in the Bakken to 
a minimum revenue commitment structure. The stock fell ~15% the day the CVX news 
was announced, with investors noting potential FCF/sh dilution, a lower growth outlook, 
and higher leverage.  That said, the stock has continued to be weak (-7-8% 
follow-through after day 1 performance) as investors look for more guidance around 
go-forward financials and the long-term strategy.  With little incremental color expected 
on 3Q26 earnings, we look to more formal 2027 guidance (typically early next year) for 
more color.

Clean Tech:  
VLTO (+7%) was our strongest performer over the past month, as investors continued to 
reward the company’s consistent execution and defensive growth profile heading into 
3Q earnings. We believe the relative strength also reflects continued confidence in Water 
Quality fundamentals and VLTO’s ability to compound earnings through a combination 
of organic growth and capital deployment. From here, we remain constructive, with the 
upcoming print the next key catalyst and continued earnings delivery important to 
sustaining the recent outperformance. 
OKLO (-27%) was our weakest performer over the past month, with higher interest rates 
weighing on the stock given the capital intensity required to reach commercial 
deployment, especially relative to other nuclear business models that are not pursuing a 
build-own-operate model. The pullback has coincided with a general pullback across the 
nuclear ecosystem despite continued positive announcements supporting new reactor 
demand, both for traditional large-scale reactors as well as small modular reactors. 
From here, we remain cautious on the risk/reward setup given continued business 
uncertainties, including its core business following PJM’s rejection of its interconnection 
request. The company is also entering the medical isotope industry, while other business 
segments such as fuel recycling continue to require de-risking, in our view. Thus, we 
believe a sustained re-rating will require continued project execution, greater visibility 
into capital requirements, and further de-risking of the targeted 2028 Aurora-INL 
deployment timeline.  
Metals & Mining:

outlook, we remain cautious as the improved outlook is based on sustained higher prices 
through the entirety of the year while costs are also guided to increase and the 
company’s ongoing USW negotiations could result in a higher than expected cost 
headwind.

Focus on DTM growth expectations. We fielded inbounds on DTM this week, as 
investors work to recalibrate growth expectations. For context, recent project open 
seasons pointed to a more measured, phased development cadence than initially 
anticipated. Market sentiment across our investor conversations points to a near-term 
FID on the MIST Southbound Phase 1 expansion following both supportive management 
commentary regarding shipper demand on the 2Q call and the close of the binding open 
season in September. That said, questions remain around the ultimate sizing, timing, and 
phases for MIST Phase 2+ expansions thereafter. Across the balance of DTM’s footprint, 
investors broadly view incremental expansions on NEXUS and Guardian (G4) as

early next decade. Sentiment on Millennium also remains mixed, as the Ramapo

Millennium PRO (+500 mmcf/d for 2029) project, though the potential to further expand 
Millennium if ENB’s Beacon project moves forward could be an offset over time. All told, 
while the market works to recalibrate expectations for the sizing, phasing, and scope of 
the next expansions, most investors we speak with remain willing to underwrite still 
significant pre-FID gas transmission growth for DTM.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
