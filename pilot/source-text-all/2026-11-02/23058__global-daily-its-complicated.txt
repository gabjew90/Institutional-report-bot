Market comment 
Uncertainty about the Middle East conflict continues to linger over markets, as President Trump 
keeps the option of escalation after the midterms on the table. US officials have reportedly 
warned several allies in the region to prepare for potential military strikes after the US polls, and 
the possibility that Iran may retaliate. 
Yet, there are also reports of ongoing diplomatic efforts. According to the Wall Street Journal, 
Vice President Vance has reached out to the head of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council. 
Vance reportedly did so at Trump’s request in an attempt to restart diplomatic channels. The 
newspaper’s sources say that the vice president sought to establish a framework for negotiations, 
and that he did not present any detailed peace deal.  
Oil markets currently seem to be trading the cautiously optimistic side of this binary outcome, but 
the protracted energy inflation has global central bankers less optimistic. 
The Federal Reserve kept US monetary policy on hold, and the vote was slightly less hawkish than 
we had expected: just two FOMC members dissented to vote for a hike. Nonetheless, FOMC Chair 
Warsh did prepare markets for another hike in December.   
The central bankers upgraded their economic outlook. The formal statement notes that the 
economy was expanding at a “strong” pace. Warsh noted that unemployment is lower, and non-
farm payroll growth is higher than last year. So, the Fed’s main concern is the inflation side of the 
mandate, given that energy prices have risen again. 
However, the Committee indicated a need for more information to help determine the 
appropriate policy stance. Warsh explained that the FOMC will receive a lot more data in the 
weeks through the next meeting. As our US strategist concludes, without providing forward 
guidance, Warsh essentially made the case for a rate hike in December.  
Elsewhere, concerns about the energy shock elicited a rate hike from the Bank of Canada, which 
had held off from responding to energy price shock until yesterday. Governor Macklem explained 
that this move is to contain the risk that persistently high energy costs spread more broadly into 
consumer prices: “the combination of higher-for-longer energy prices and increased pass-through 
adds to the persistence of inflation in the projection.” 
Indeed, the Bank of Canada raised its inflation projections substantially. It now forecasts 2.9% 
inflation this year instead of 2.5%. Moreover, the central bank’s economists added a whopping 1.1 
percentage points to their 2027 forecast, lifting the inflation estimate from 2.0% to 3.1%.  
Unsurprisingly, then, the policy statement closes with the comment that “there could be a need to 
increase the policy rate further,” which suggests that this may not have been a one-and-done 
hike. Our Canada strategists have therefore pencilled in another rate hike in December.  
Meanwhile, the European Union changed its relationship status with Canada to “it’s complicated.” 
A group of countries led by Germany and France objected to the “associate member” status that 
EC President Von der Leyen had invented especially for the country. The revised draft only 
mentions “ever closer association, short of full membership.”

Day ahead 
Today, the ECB will probably confirm that it holds its policy rate unchanged at 2.50%. The ECB’s 
tightening cycle may not be over, but we see no reason for policymakers to accelerate the hiking 
cycle. Inflation risks remain the ECB’s greatest concern. Yet, there are still no signs that energy 
inflation is spreading into broader wage and price setting.  
Meanwhile, investors’ concerns about debt sustainability lessen the headroom and need for 
further monetary tightening somewhat. Yet, spreads are still far from levels that require ECB 
intervention, and current financial conditions do not prevent another rate hike in December. 
The developments in French yields mean that President Lagarde will have to walk a fine line 
between keeping the pressure on politicians to agree on a fiscal solution and the risk of 
contagion. She may remind markets that the ECB has tools available in case any spread widening 
were to become disorderly and unwarranted.  
Also on the data calendar today are Spanish retail sales, UK mortgage approvals and Italian 
consumer confidence. That’s ahead of the usual weekly jobless claims out of the US and the US 
Q3 GDP report where growth is expected to land at 3.2% annualized. US PCE figures for 
September will also be released.
