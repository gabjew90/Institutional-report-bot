Spec sales comment
Oil prices fell this morning on tentative signs of diplomatic progress in the Middle East. Reuters reports that
Iranian negotiators were in Doha for a third consecutive day of talks with Qatari mediators yesterday – Iran
reiterated both the previously articulated 2-day timeline for a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz if the US meets
Iranian demands and the readiness to respond to any new US attacks by striking US and Israeli targets in the
region. US Secretary of State Rubio said on Saturday that President Trump has not yet decided whether to
launch new strikes on Iran, but would prefer to solve the conflict diplomatically. Polls ahead of the US
midterms tomorrow are strongly indicative of a blue surge, and while recent precedent (including the Israeli
election last month) gives reason to mistrust the polls, the magnitude of the apparent swing towards the
Democrats suggests the US political landscape is likely to become more complicated. My personal view is that
neither a better-than-expected outcome for Republicans nor a blue surge are likely to lead President Trump to
lean more towards diplomacy than the military options he has preferred so far (and which recent reporting in
the WSJ and NYT suggests he is preparing for again), and that the midterms are more likely to be a catalyst
for escalation. The latest Hormuz Tracker from Henri and the team shows a bounce in both oil and LNG
exports, but I think the political risk premium rather than export volumes will be the near-term focus.
