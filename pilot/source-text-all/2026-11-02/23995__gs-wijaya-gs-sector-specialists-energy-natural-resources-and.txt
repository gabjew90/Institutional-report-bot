Conversations + Prints
FANG. One of the more debated stocks in E&P – where we think specialists are leaning more cautious
into the print. Desk has positioning score of 3/10 into the print. Four things folks are looking for on the
call: (1) Commentary around inorganic growth – debate around Delaware vs Midland position (ie where
could we see potential for more) and bolt on vs larger scale – expect this to be the most top of mind. (2)
Capital allocation – company doubled share repo authorization last quarter – ties into the first point above
– return of capital vs growth vs leverage reduction – especially with WTI at $98/b. (3) Updates on
efficiency gains + Barnett. Barnett has been extremely topical on the desk over the last 6m. (4) Gas
strategy – was called out more directly in the 2Q shareholder letter around LNG / data center opps over
time … this quote should be telling just truly on how much gas is out there in the Permian: ‘We always
talk internally that the Permian hasn’t even tried to produce gas yet, and we think that still holds true
today.’ Side note – will be watching for any commentary around cost inflationary pressures / potential for
capex grinding higher. We will get a number of Large Cap E&P prints this week – this will be in focus for
the group.
WMB. Williams will report EPS tonight after the close and host the conference call tomorrow at 9:30 AM
ET – we expect this to be one of the most watched prints in all of Energy this EPS season. Desk has a
positioning score of 9/10 into the print – and we think this is one of the most well owned equities not just
in Midstream, but in the broader Energy group. The stock has been more rangebound lately (band of
$69-$75/sh with premarket indicating $74/sh). At the end of the day – the bulls will point to the story as
one of the best ‘low beta’ ways to play the power thematic – where BTM execution + contracts have all
been trending right way for the company over the last year. The kicker here is actually the base business –
aka Transco – company surprised to the upside last quarter (mindful we also had Momentum deal
announced too) – where the company raised annual EBITDA growth target through 2030 to 11%+ vs prior
guide of 10%+ and highlighted several new gas transmission projects. Think it’s important for market to
remember the driver of the earnings here is the premier pipe business – with power being the cherry on the
top. On power – there are strong market expectations for at least one BTM announcement before YE (with
potential for two?) – highly debated if we get this update on EPS or not (does it matter?). There are some
questions around customer mix / duration – ie wanting to see a new a customer come in for a larger scale
project could see a significant re-rate in the stock. At the end of the day – while positioning indicates a
high bar – there is appetite to own the story through cycle and touches on several themes that are
resonating with investors (gas volumetric growth, BTM, power, EPS growth, less commodity exposure).

CCJ. Spent a lot of time with investors on Friday thinking through the release and the EPS call – and now
that the dust has settled – wanted to share some thoughts. The entire group was under pressure on Friday
and unsurprisingly (yet also surprisingly – more on this in a second) – the stock finished as the worst
performer on my screen in the group. FY production guidance revised lower has historically been a
tailwind for the group – ie if a premier operator is lowering guidance – generally a read for the broader
group that (A) supply is tight and getting tighter, (B) spot px bid likely follows, (C) ‘what is bad for the
micro is good for the macro which is good for the micro’ and (D) general reminder that mining is hard. I
didn’t find anything on the call re. uranium that was negative – and in fact – want to make sure this is
highlighted from the call where we usually look to Grant for updated color on the contracting backdrop:
‘So what we're seeing, our floors in the mid-to-high-70s escalated. We're seeing ceilings touching $160
escalated. And for the base escalated portion, the portion of a contract that we're willing to fix, we are at
three-digit uranium at prices in -- US dollars escalated. So we are seeing on very small volumes, a very
healthy reset of the uranium price.’ Midpoint on my math (for illustrative purposes – know contract types
are different and not a one size fits all) points to $115-$120/lb. Bears will say ‘well the floors and ceilings
haven’t changed from 2Q commentary’… this isn’t a quarters / months game on how contracting changes
– contracting moving higher takes time (years in some cases) and we have talked about a stairstep
approach to px’s moving higher. Think we need to take step back and highlight – even if the DC tailwinds
for the nuclear story becomes less relevant / hit pause / are cancelled – there’s still an underlying structural
tightness in on the supply side.
