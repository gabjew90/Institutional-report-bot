EssilorLuxottica (UBS Research Rec Buy) Privacy Risk Is Priced In, Clarity Is Not
Report by Robert Krankowski, Zuzanna Pusz, Graham Doyle, Emily Teo & Ana Gogichaishvili
Privacy concerns are real, but so is the valuation gap
• Over the past 12 months, EssilorLuxottica's share price has been driven more by narrative than
fundamentals, with volatility fuelled by concerns over rising competition in smart glasses and speculation
surrounding potential changes at its key shareholder, Delfin.
• In our view, regulation remains the final overhang before the investment case materially de-risks.
• The EDPB statement on smart glasses, alongside the proactive measures taken by EssilorLuxottica and
Meta, should help improve transparency around data privacy practices and strengthen confidence
among regulators, consumers, and investors.
• At current levels, the shares trade at an estimated ~30% discount to the standalone value of the core
business, implying that the market is assigning little to no value to the smart glasses opportunity.
Regulation through dialogue, not prohibition
• The announcement suggests that European policymakers are primarily focused on establishing an
appropriate regulatory framework for smart glasses, rather than questioning the category's right to exist.
• This is reinforced by the willingness of EssilorLuxottica and Meta to address privacy concerns
proactively, with many of the proposed safeguards already incorporated into current products or planned
future releases.
• While different markets may still introduce local restrictions or usage limitations in certain jurisdictions, we
believe the probability of an outright ban remains very low.
• In our view, this should help ease a regulatory overhang on the stock, particularly given the recent increase in
privacy-related scrutiny and negative news flow in Europe.
The regulatory bear case looks overstated
• Although privacy concerns are elevated in Europe, our analysis suggests there is little evidence that they
materially constrain consumer interest in smart glasses.
• While slower roll out relative to the US reduces the risk of significant disruption, including inventory
write-downs, even under a more restrictive regulatory outcome.
• Even in a scenario where European smart glasses adoption remains subdued, we continue to see a
~€90bn global market opportunity by 2040.
• Crucially, EssilorLuxottica does not require European smart glasses growth to support the
investment case.
• Even in an extreme scenario where smart glasses are excluded from Europe entirely (assume ~4% OSG in
EMEA), we still see the group as capable of delivering a high single-digit organic growth algorithm,
among the strongest in the sector.
Valuation: Buy, €247 price target
• ESLX trades on 17.2x and 15.2x 2027E and 2028E P/E for 3-yr sales and EPS CAGRs (27E-30E) of +8%
and +13%, respectively

EssilorLuxottica (UBS Research Rec Buy) Defin's Risks Likely Overstated; Reiterate Buy
Report by Robert Krankowski, Zuzanna Pusz, Graham Doyle & Chris Huang
Disconnect Between News Flow and Share Price
• The muted market reaction to Google’s smart glasses launch (Warby Parker’s >10% decline on the day), and
press reports pointing to a potential delay in Apple launch to late 2027, would typically have supported
ESLX's share price, especially given reassuring Meta-related news flow.
• However, the absence of any meaningful share price reaction came as a surprise.
• Attention appears to be on potential developments at ESLX’s largest shareholder, Delfin (~32%
stake), where ongoing succession-related reorganisation and potential stake transfers among heirs (widely
reported in the Italian press) could create some uncertainty around funding needs at the holding company
level.
• While the situation differs materially from the post-ESLX merger dispute, the stock’s underperformance vs.
the broader luxury sector mirrors the pattern seen in 2018–19.
• In our view, the market is overestimating the risks to ESLX, resulting in a ~13% discount to the fair
value of its core business and assigning no value to smart glasses upside, which we see as unjustified.
Buy.
Delfin Risks Likely Overstated
• Delfin does not exercise full control over ESLX’s operations, but as a long-standing shareholder it remains
a key focus for investors.
• The holding splits ownership equally across eight heirs, in this context, the potential consolidation of two
heirs’ stakes by Leonardo Maria del Vecchio could potentially streamline decision-making.
• While we acknowledge some investor concerns, including the exceptional scenario of a potential disposal of
the ESLX stake, we would highlight that:
• Leonardo Maria is actively involved in day-to-day operations (currently Chief Strategy Officer) and has
repeatedly described the stake as a “crown jewel”;
• Insiders have been net buyers in recent months (>€20m of purchases over the past month);
• A potential disposal may be the less attractive option among those available, given the current
valuation.
Broader Monetisation Options Limit ESLX Overhang Risk

7
• The discussion appears to centre on the possibility that family members may seek greater cash
liquidity, potentially prompting asset sales.
• While the market seems focused on the evolution of Delfin's stake, we view potential disposal as unlikely
as Delfin has a wider set of options at its disposal.
• Press reports have highlighted several other scenarios for Delfin, the holding
company, including:
• A potential market listing of the Delfin vehicle, similar to EXOR, which would allow the family to retain
control while monetising part of their holdings;
• The potential sale of other financial assets, some of which have been mentioned as possible collateral for
bank financing (excluding EssilorLuxottica);
• Higher dividend distributions from the holding company.
Valuation: Buy, price target €312
• ESLX shares are trading on 22.9x 2026e and 19.3x P/E on 2027e for sales and EPS CAGRs (2025-28e) of
+12% and +16% respectively

EssilorLuxottica (UBS Research Rec Buy) Q3 Preview: Staying The Course Amid The Noise
Report by Robert Krankowski, Zuzanna Pusz, Graham Doyle, Emily Teo & Ana Gogichaishvili
Our view in a nutshell: Emerging Stronger, Positioned for Growth
• The stock's year-to-date de-rating reflects investor concerns around competition and corporate governance,
neither of which has fully materialised , in our view.
• Combined with broader weakness across the luxury sector, this has weighed heavily on sentiment.
• While Q3 alone is unlikely to resolve all investors concerns, it should provide an important read-through
ahead of particularly challenging Q4 comparison and may help to determine the sentiment around smart
glasses into 2027 and beyond.
• The commentary on the product pipeline during the Meta Connect event, together with the potential upside
from lenses, makes us positive, especially given current valuation.
• With the stock now trading at below 20x P/E, expectations appear subdued, and any reassurance on growth
and execution could act as a meaningful catalyst for a re-rating.
• UBS believe Q3 has the potential to provide that reassurance. Reiterate Buy.
What to expect in Q3 and 2027?
• EssilorLuxottica is scheduled to report its Q3 results on 20 October.
• UBS forecast Q3 sales of €7,449m, implying +6.5% cFX growth.
• UBS expect DTC growth of +10% to remain the key driver, while Professional Solutions grows +2.5%,
supported by recent product launches but partly constrained by more cautious wholesale ordering.
• Regionally, UBS forecast North America +5.5%, EMEA +6.0%, Latin America +5.6%, and Asia-Pacific
>10%.

12
• Looking to FY27, UBS expect high single-digit revenue growth to continue (UBSe +8.6%).
• However, gross margin is likely to face headwinds from the growing mix of smart glasses and the absence of
tariff refund benefits, leaving adjusted EBIT (cFX) broadly flat versus FY26e.
What will be the focus?
• UBS expect investor focus to centre on: smart glasses unit shipments during the quarter and
management's expectations for the holiday season;the penetration of prescription lenses within Meta
smart glasses and the implications for EssilorLuxottica's profitability;any commentary on the 2027 cost
outlook, particularly given recent movements in the prices of raw materials used in smart glasses;the
success of the Stellest rollout and expansion in the US market;any changes in purchasing behaviour
among consumers or wholesalers amid increasing pressure on disposable incomes.
Valuation: Buy, €247 price target (€282 previously)
• ESLX trades on 16.7x and 14.8x 2027E and 2028E P/E for 3-yr sales and EPS CAGRs (27E-30E) of +8%
and +13%, respectively.
• Our PT (based on the average of our DCF and relative P/E valuations) is revised primarily due to
minor estimate adjustments and the recent de-rating across its peer group.
