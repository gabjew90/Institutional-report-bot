Taiwan: Surprise Strength Extends into Q3 GDP; Revising Up 2026-27 
Growth Forecasts
31 October 2026 | 12:56AM KST
Bottom line: Taiwan’s real GDP grew 3.2% qoq sa (non-annualized) in Q3 2026, with 
sequential momentum accelerating from 1.4% in the prior quarter. The outcome was 
well above our forecast and the Bloomberg consensus. The acceleration was driven 
primarily by stronger investment, which grew 8.4% qoq sa from the prior quarter and 
recorded its strongest momentum since Q2 2024. Private consumption growth 
moderated from a high base, while net exports’ contribution to headline GDP growth 
remained marginally negative. Following the strong Q3 GDP report, we revise up our 
2026 and 2027 Taiwan real GDP growth forecasts further to 13.0% and 6.3%, 
respectively, up from 11.7% and 3.3% previously (vs. the Bloomberg consensus of 
11.4% and 5.1%, respectively). We expect growth in 2027 to be driven by sustained 
strength in net exports (4pp), with the remainder supported broadly by private 
consumption and AI-related capex.

Main points: 
1. Taiwan’s real GDP grew 3.2% qoq sa (non-annualized; seasonally adjusted by GS) 
in Q3 2026, with sequential momentum accelerating from 1.4% in the prior quarter. 
In yoy terms, growth accelerated to 14.4% from 12.9% in Q2 (Exhibit 1). The 
outcome was well above our forecast and the Bloomberg consensus of 12.0%. 
2.  The acceleration was driven primarily by stronger investment, which grew 8.4% 
qoq sa (non-annualized seasonally adjusted by GS) from the prior quarter and 
recorded its strongest momentum since Q2 2024. This sharp acceleration explains 
most of the upside surprise relative to our forecast. While the breakdown for fixed 
investment and inventories for the current quarter is not available in the advance 
release, the DGBAS press release highlighted broad strength across machinery and 
transport equipment as well as intellectual property investment. 
3. Private consumption rose 0.4% qoq sa (non-annualized; seasonally adjusted by 
GS), with momentum moderating after having sustained a robust 1% to 2% qoq 
non-annualized pace of growth in the previous three quarters. Government 
consumption picked up to 1.8% qoq sa, up from 1.1% in the prior quarter.

4. The contribution from net exports remained marginally negative at -0.1pp, with the 
drag to headline growth narrowing from -0.7pp in the prior quarter. Exports of goods 
and services rose at a moderated pace of 0.6% qoq sa, while imports of goods and 
services grew at a slightly stronger pace of 1.0%. 
5. Following the strong Q3 GDP report, we are revising up our 2026 and 2027 Taiwan 
real GDP growth forecasts further to 13.0% and 6.3%, respectively, from 11.7% and 
3.3% previously (vs. Bloomberg consensus of 11.4% and 5.1%, respectively). Our 
previous upgrade focused on exports strength, but the Q3 GDP report suggests broad 
strength extended to domestic demand. For 2027, we continue to forecast sustained 
strength in tech manufacturing underpinned by global AI demand, as expectations for 
global hyperscaler capex have continued to rise. Although hyperscaler capex growth is 
poised to moderate from over 95% yoy in 2026, consensus forecasts imply still-robust 
momentum of more than 40% yoy in 2027 (Exhibit 2). Accordingly, from a production 
standpoint, we forecast the technology sector’s contribution to real Gross Value Added 
(GVA) growth to ease from 6.8pp in 2026 to a still-elevated 4.5pp in 2027. Most of the 
gains will come from net exports, which we estimate will contribute approximately 4.0pp 
to headline GDP growth in 2027. 
6. We expect that the remainder of growth (2.3pp) will come broadly from private 
consumption and capex/R&D spending. Private consumption is well positioned to 
sustain above-trend growth, buoyed by cash handouts, public-sector wage hikes, and 
positive equity wealth effects. The central government’s proposed 2027 budget entails 
TWD 235.7bn (or $7.4bn) in universal cash handouts—equivalent to 0.7% of annual 
GDP—of which the initial tranche is slated to be distributed in late January 2027. 
Moreover, wage growth, which has been accelerating in recent months (Exhibit 3), will be 
anchored by the approved 4% public-sector wage increase for military personnel, civil 
servants, and teacher, alongside substantial bonus distributions from leading technology 
firms. In addition, gross fixed capital formation is likely to maintain a robust pace of 
expansion at around 5%. AI-related capex remains the primary growth driver, projected 
to contribute +7.0pp to headline fixed investment momentum as TSMC continues to 
expand cutting-edge production and advanced packaging capacity (Exhibit 4). Non-tech 
fixed investment, notably construction, is nonetheless likely to pull back following three 
consecutive years of gains, weighed down by the residential and commercial property 
cooldown as construction starts have steadily trended downward since their mid-2024 
peak.
