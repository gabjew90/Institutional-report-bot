The buffer is depleted
Europe is entering the 2026/27 heating season in its most vulnerable position since 2011. EU gas
storage sits at just 65%, versus a five-year average of 82% and well below the EU's 90% target.
Unlike a pure weather squeeze, Europe is entering this winter with a structural supply problem already
in place. Disruptions at the Strait of Hormuz have effectively knocked out Qatari LNG, with exports
collapsing by roughly 96%.
ABN Amro
When storage gets dangerous
The physical risk becomes increasingly nonlinear as inventories fall. Once storage drops below ~30%,
internal pressure declines and withdrawal rates begin to fall.
If a cold snap pushes inventories toward those levels, TTF could violently reprice toward €100–
150+/MWh as the problem shifts from expensive gas to actual physical scarcity.
Europe doesn't need €150 gas as the base case. The probability of getting there just needs to rise.
ABN Amro
The gas trade doesn't stop at gas
A €100–150/MWh TTF scenario transforms an energy problem into a European macro problem.
Europe would be forced to import expensive LNG at a premium to the US just as industrial demand
destruction accelerates. The result is a classic terms-of-trade shock: higher inflation, weaker growth
and renewed pressure on the euro.
That is a particularly ugly mix for the ECB. Energy pushes inflation higher just as the growth outlook
deteriorates, leaving the central bank caught between fighting inflation and supporting the economy.
Gas starts the problem. The euro and European volatility may end up expressing it.

Remember 2022?
The 2022 gas shock didn't stay in gas. Euro-area inflation spiked above 10%, while EUR/USD went from
around 1.13 to parity before briefly trading near 0.95 as Europe's terms-of-trade shock spread across
the macro complex.
The setup today doesn't need to become another 2022. Markets just need to start assigning a higher
probability to one.
Eurostat
Where is the stress?
TTF is already pricing considerable stress. The question is whether the same tail risk is being priced
across European macro assets.
EUR and STOXX volatility remain relatively subdued. If the probability of €100–150/MWh gas continues
to rise, that disconnect becomes increasingly difficult to ignore.
Gas already smells the problem. European vol doesn't.

The Europe tail
Europe's depleted gas buffer leaves very little room for things to go wrong. A reopening of the Strait
and a mild winter would take much of the pressure off. A closed Strait and a cold winter are a very
different story.
Another 2022 is not the base case. It doesn't need to be. With inventories already low, the upside tail in
gas is large, and another physical squeeze would quickly spill over into the euro, inflation, growth and
European vol.
The gas problem can become a Europe problem very quickly.
