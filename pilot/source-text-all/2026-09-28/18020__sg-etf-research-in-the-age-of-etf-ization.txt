WHERE PHYSICAL ADDS VALUE
• Stoxx Europe 600 Banks / Stoxx Europe Select Dividend 30
• EU pressure on unequal WHT regimes has gradually resulted in a convergence of tax treatment 
between domestic and foreign funds, often making them much lower than market level
• 0% Div WHT: Ireland, Luxembourg, Netherlands, France, Belgium
• Spain (1%), Italy (1.2%), Austria (1-2%)
• Germany (15%)
• MSCI India: Return comparison is misleading!
• Apparently, physical outperforms synthetic significantly
• Market local taxes and capital gain taxation makes Indian equities costly to access
• Capital gain taxation are embedded in the redemption fees of the physical ETF, thus affecting 
the seller but preserving the NAV and hence the return of the ETF. 
• By contrast, these taxations are built into the swap pricing and impact the NAV of synthetic 
ETFs.
• Net of redemption fees, performance remains comparable between both replication structures

INTERESTING SITUATIONS
• Qualified US Indices vs Non Qualified US Indices
• IRS 871(m) exempts derivatives on qualified broad-based indices (“Qualified indices”) from 
dividend equivalent taxation
• Enables synthetic exposure to US equities with no dividend WHT (0% outcome)
• Creates a structural tax advantage for eligible index derivatives
• Synthetic ETFs outperform on qualified indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, Russell 2000), but lose 
their edge on non-qualified benchmarks (such as NYSE Arca Gold Bugs, S&P 500 Scored & 
Screened+ and MSCI USA ESG Selection P-Series 5% Issuer Capped)
• Topix vs MSCI Japan
• Topix derivatives benefit from domestic hedging flows, enabling near full dividend 
pass-through with no WHT drag
• MSCI Japan derivatives are largely hedged by foreign investors, embedding dividend WHT 
(15.315% or treaty rates)
• Result: Synthetic are structurally stronger for TOPIX but not for MSCI Japan
