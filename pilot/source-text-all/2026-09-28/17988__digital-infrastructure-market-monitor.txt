Key takeaways 
• Spreads were unchanged for CMBS data centers and ABS was slightly wider. 
Comparable corporates were slightly wider w/w. 
• Four deals priced this week for $3.0bn across all of digital infrastructure. YTD 
issuance totals $635bn across digital infra. 
Digital infrastructure market monitor 
Spreads were unchanged for CMBS data centers but widened 5bps for ABS data centers. 
Across the broader corporate credit market, spreads were generally wider. Year-to-date, 
SASB data center spreads remain wider than at the start of the year, whereas ABS data 
center spreads tightened in the first half of the year but widened since. Corporate credit 
spreads are also modestly wider relative to year-end levels. 
This week, four deals priced, totaling $3.0bn. Year-to-date issuance in the project 
finance market, including both investment-grade and high-yield transactions, stands at 
$84.2bn. Across the high grade corporate market, $281.6bn of US-denominated bonds 
have been issued year-to-date in 2026. There has been $30.7bn of Securitized Products 
digital infrastructure issuance YTD; $24.1bn of ABS and $6.6bn of CMBS. 
This week in digital infrastructure news, Oracle claimed a force majeure notice over the 
development of a New Mexico data center project related to Project Jupiter. The news is 
not a standalone credit event so much as a confirmation that the market is beginning to 
price meaningful execution and permitting risk into AI infrastructure projects that had 
previously been viewed as quasi-corporate risk due to hyperscaler sponsorship. The 
notice stems from power-delivery delays tied to pipeline permitting and backup 
generation approvals, and while Oracle remains committed as tenant, the filing 
preserves its right to defer rent if completion slips beyond the current 2028 target. 
More importantly, the development comes as the $18 billion Jupiter construction loan 
has reportedly traded in the low-90s/high-80s cents on the dollar, suggesting investors 
are demanding a significantly wider risk premium despite the presence of an 
investment-grade-adjacent tenant. Against a backdrop of rising leverage, negative free 
cash flow and substantial data-center capex commitments, the project loan market 
appears to be reassessing risk. 
Additionally, Meta's Muse launch this week has been a genuine breakout—#1 on the App 
Store, outpacing ChatGPT's early download curve, retail partnerships (Walmart, Best 
Buy), and analysts raising price targets, pushing META's market cap toward records. 
Positively, it validates agentic AI's consumer monetization path via commissions rather 
than ads. But the twist: agents need heavy CPU support (4:1 to 40:1 CPU-to-GPU ratios 
according to Yahoo News), not just GPUs—already lifting AMD, Intel, and Arm, signaling 
the next buildout phase spreads capex beyond Nvidia.
