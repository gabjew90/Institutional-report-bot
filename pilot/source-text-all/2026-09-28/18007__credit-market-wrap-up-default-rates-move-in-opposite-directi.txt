Market Wrap-up  
Default rates move in opposite directions, but does it matter? 
 
iTraxx  
 
Default rates remain relatively high on both sides of the Atlantic, but credit spreads 
seem to be ignoring the figures.  This has been going on for well over a year, pointing 
to a disconnect between spreads and defaults.  However, we note that double-Bs 
remain solid and the damage is happening in the lower rating categories. 
Market highlights 
The tone in the markets was markedly better today, and there are hopes that the US-
China summit will prove positive, at least on the AI front.  Sovereign bonds remained 
volatile but this time for the good reasons as yields dropped quickly and gained back 
almost all the ground lost on Friday. The 10y Bund yield dropped by 7bp to 3.45% and the 
10y OAT outperformed with the yield dropping by 12bp.  The spread between the two, is 
now just below 100bp.  Equity markets jumped higher and the iTraxx indices rolled on to 
the new series, with the new Main trading around 59bp (some 4.5bp wider than the 
previous series) and the X-Over around 288bp (some 30bp wider than the previous series). 
The primary markets had a healthy start to the week as we had bonds from every major 
market segment totalling at least €5.1bn (two deals yet to announce final terms).  This 
follows last week’s €30bn tally and we believe we’ll continue to see strong volumes 
through the week.  The bonds today pushed the total for the month up to €78.8bn, and 
we would not be surprised to see the overall figure for September well above €90bn.

Market thoughts 
Last week we had Moody’s publish its latest default report and it showed that the Global rate 
inched a little higher in August.  The default rate came at 4.4% in July but rose to 4.5% in August 
and the main reason was the US rate which rose to 5.3% in August, from a previous 5% reading.  
At the same time, the European rate improved gently, falling from 4.5% in July to 4.4% last month.  
The moves are mild as we can see in the chart below, but the direction of travel between the 
European and US rates headed in opposite directions.  Moreover, the chart also shows how both 
default rates, remain relatively high.

Of course, default rates are a lagging indicator and the better spread levels could indicate that 
the market expects the default rate on both sides of the Atlantic to fall quickly over the coming 
months. 
But, in our view, this is not a good explanation as spreads have been tightening since 2023 and 
default rates in the US have been rising since then, and in Europe since late 2024.  Default rates 
may be a lagging indicator, but they don’t lag that much. 
Moreover, when we look at the latest default rates by rating, we can see that the better rated HY 
bonds remain very solid, and the damage is mostly happening in the weaker names and bonds.

The data above shows that there were no defaults in the double-B category in August (the rate 
has been 0% all so far this year in both Europe and the US), only a few in the single-B rating but 
triple-Cs and lower took the brunt of the pain. 
In our view, the market is now more discerning and makes a clear distinction between double-
Bs, which are the largest part of the HY market (65% in the euro market, 62% in the USD market) 
and the lower rating categories.  In that sense, we believe that double-Bs on both sides of the 
Atlantic remain solid and are the better investment alternative compared to single-Bs or lower 
rated bonds and companies.
