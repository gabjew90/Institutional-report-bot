The market starts the week with little sign that last week’s real rate repricing has run its course. 30Y UST 
yields reached fresh cycle highs near 5.53% on Friday before stabilising into the close. The worrying point is that 
the market is struggling to find duration buyers even after this major technical break. The overnight session has 
done little to challenge that narrative. USTs were back under pressure in Asia, with 10Y UST yields back around 
5.21% and the front end leading the sell-off as oil resumed higher. Brent is back above $107pb after Iran reiterated 
conditions around reopening the Strait of Hormuz and last week’s UN conference brought little progress, reinforcing 
the market's concern that the energy shock remains a live risk. For now, it is difficult to argue for a sustained 
rally in duration ahead of a packed US labour market week. JOLTS, ADP and Friday’s payrolls will determine 
whether the market feels comfortable with current pricing of further Fed tightening (92-94bp priced for the year). 
We continue to respect the risk that any near-term rally proves tactical rather than structural unless labour data 
show clearer signs of slowing and energy prices stabilise. Above current levels, we see the 5.25%-5.35% area 
as the next resistance for 10Y UST yields. In FX, the DXY index has opened the week better bid after paring 
gains last Friday and is currently trading around 101.15, consistent with the market’s renewed preference for higher 
real yields and energy-linked inflation hedges. 
Last week’s move in EUR rates was not just a US story. While global real-rate dynamics remain the dominant 
driver, domestic factors such as Germany's fiscal shift, stronger growth expectations and the shrinking ECB 
balance sheet increasingly help explain current levels. The result is that valuations no longer look as stretched 
against German fundamentals as they did earlier in the year, although another leg higher in global real rates would 
still leave EUR duration vulnerable. Besides the potential move in global real rates, the other near-term focus 
will be this week’s inflation data, starting with Spain tomorrow and culminating with Friday’s Eurozone 
CPI release. With Bunds already following UST yields higher, another upside inflation surprise would make it 
difficult for the market to fade the tightening priced in front-end pricing. Today’s ECB speakers and a heavy week 
of supply should also keep concession pressures elevated. We continue to think EUR duration remains 
vulnerable, with global real-rate dynamics still driving the bus. In FX, EUR/USD has resumed its decline and 
is trading around 1.1375, reflecting both higher US real yields. 
GBP rates outperformed on a cross-market basis last Friday (higher-beta nature), but that provides limited 
comfort when outright yields remain close to their highest levels since 2007. This week’s focus is split 
between global and domestic drivers. Internationally, the UK will struggle to decouple from higher US and German 
real yields if the global duration sell-off resumes. Domestically, Labour conference announcements around 
industrial policy, infrastructure and social care funding reinforce the broader fiscal backdrop that remains 
challenging for long-end gilts. We have BoE speakers today, including Ramsden. 10Y Gilt yields remain 
uncomfortably close to the recent 5.40% highs. Unless energy prices stabilise or US data disappoint materially, 
the market may struggle to sustain any rally into month-end. In FX, GBP/USD continues to trade within last Friday’s 
range near 1.3230, with relative rate differentials increasingly offset by broader dollar strength. 
Mixed start of the week for Asian equities, after President Trump rejected Iran’s latest ceasefire proposal 
and energy prices rose. In Japan, JGBs bear-flattened following the small beat in PPI services while USD/JPY 
is slightly up on the day, but not able to hold onto the 157.50 level.
