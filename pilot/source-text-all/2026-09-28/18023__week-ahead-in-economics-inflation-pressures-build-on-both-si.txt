Week Ahead in Economics 
Inflation pressures build on both sides of the Atlantic 
 
 
 
Key Themes: 
US: PCE and labour report to take centre stage next week 
CPI/PPI mapping points to core PCE inflation of around 0.25% m/m, while super-core PCE likely 
remained broadly in line with July at 0.25%-0.30% m/m, centred around 0.27% m/m, indicating 
continued resilience in non-housing services inflation. Estimates are subject to greater than-
usual uncertainty due to forthcoming methodological changes, as well as the upcoming annual 
benchmark revision. Meanwhile, September’s non-farm payroll figure is likely to remain firm, but 
probably will not repeat August’s blowout gain. 
 
Euro area: Rising inflation to boost rate hike expectations 
The past week saw strong survey data boosting bond yields and rate hike expectations, despite 
remaining risks linked to energy prices. Next week, September HICP inflation is widely expected to 
rise further due to higher energy prices, to 3.7% we think, while core inflation could be stable. Key 
to watch will be developments in core and food prices and whether they are starting to see any 
indirect and second round effects. The September ESI survey is likely to point to resilience despite 
cost and supply pressures, with both industrial and services confidence rising. In Germany, the 
unemployment rate was likely stable in September, while in France consumer spending might have 
dropped in August after a few strong months. 
 
Central Bank Watch: RBA to hike, while BanRep to remain on hold 
We expect the RBA to hike rates by 25bp as inflation and economic growth have come in stronger 
than its forecasts, reinforcing concerns that upside inflation risks are materialising. Policymakers 
have increasingly signalled support for a pre-emptive tightening approach, and markets have 
largely priced in a hike. Our base case is for another rate increase in November or December if 
inflation pressures, particularly from energy prices, persist. Meanwhile, in Colombia, with a slim 
majority of BanRep policymakers in agreement that the current rate is restrictive, the committee 
should vote to remain on hold. Further tightening would require material evidence that inflation 
expectations are becoming unanchored or that demand-side pressures are building.

Euro area: Rising inflation to boost rate hike expectations 
 
The past week was a humbling experience for anyone concerned over a weaker outlook for 
growth and inflation, due to rising energy prices and interest rates. With the flow of positive global 
data and surveys continuing, there was little in the way of stopping the bond market sell-off, with 
markets convinced more rate hikes and growth are in the pipeline.  
In the euro area data, the key signal this week came from the flash September PMIs, showing a 
stabilisation in manufacturing, broadly as expected, at an elevated level (52.7). More surprising 
was the large increase in services (by 1.4pts to 53) despite continued weakness in consumer 
confidence, which implied that the composite PMI now has been on a rising trend since May, 
seemingly unhindered by the energy price shock from the war in Iran. In Germany, the September 
Ifo survey also beat expectations comfortably, while the Insee survey in France disappointed 
slightly, along with euro area September consumer confidence.  
Expectations on ECB rate hikes kept rising, helped by strong US survey data which drove long-
term interest rates higher. The market pricing for an October ECB hike rose to as much as 60% on 
Thursday but has since eased back, with another three hikes following over the coming year, 
implying a policy rate of 3.5% in a year’s time. We only expect one more hike in December 
currently, but see risks for more, especially in the near term. One complication for the ECB 
ahead of the 29 October meeting might be that there will be few data releases before the meeting. 
The key data will be next week’s September inflation data (see below) and another set of monthly 
surveys for October. The ECB might get some early information about the contents of the 3Q GDP 
report which will be released the day after ECB’s meeting, but we expect it to be on the soft side 
due to this summer’s weather-related transport and production disruptions. Instead, the 
developments of energy prices until end-October are likely to be decisive. While we think the ECB 
will want to remain on the front-foot in countering non-linear indirect and second round wage 
effects, there is still a risk that growth might slow more materially in response to higher energy 
prices and interest rates. And as much as the ECB will want to cap the peak of inflation, expected 
at the start of next year, it will also need to avoid any undershooting of inflation after the energy 
impact fades next year.  
Other news coming from the ECB was the announcement of Isabel Schnabel to resign in early 
January 2027, before the end of her mandate, and instead take up a position at the IMF in 
Washington. This puts pressure on President Lagarde to clarify her intentions with regards a 
rumoured early departure. We would thus not be surprised if there would be a package-deal late 
this year or early next to fill the three positions of Lagarde, Lane and Schnabel which expire next 
year. Given the intricacies of agreeing on key EU positions, we believe the easiest solution will

EC survey to signal resilience despite cost and supply pressures 
 
For September, high-frequency indicators point to continued expansion in euro area 
manufacturing output, while services activity is also showing signs of strengthening, suggesting 
that a broader-based recovery is taking hold across the bloc. Supply disruptions and elevated 
energy costs remain key risks, a reality reflected in this month's deterioration in consumer 
confidence. The interaction between these two factors, namely firm growth but wavering 
expectations, should be reflected in the Economic Confidence indicator with the headline 
reading projected to remain roughly stable at 98.3 (z-score: -0.3). 
The EC survey explicitly incorporates three‑month ahead expectations for production and selling 
prices and attaches a greater weight to them relative to the PMIs. This is likely to moderate any 
improvement in confidence as households and firms contend with the prospect of persistently 
elevated energy costs. Still, the overarching theme remains one of resilience, with the survey 
expected to show that underlying fundamentals are supportive and the recovery remains on 
track.

Manufacturing is expected to maintain its momentum despite ongoing cost pressures. The 
September flash manufacturing PMI surprised to the upside, as industrial firms reported 
improving production and new orders. Outperformance in Germany alone cannot account for the 
resilience, implying broad-based growth in output across the euro area. While energy prices will 
likely weigh on expectations, we still expect the outlook to remain optimistic. This dynamic 
should result in a rise in the Industrial Confidence indicator to -4.5 from -5.3 (z-score: -0.1).  
Recent EC survey results suggest that the recovery in the energy-sensitive services sector has 
lagged that of manufacturing. For September, however, we expect improving output and new 
business in the core economies, together with continued resilience in the periphery, to support a 
broader rebound in the services outlook. This should be reflected in an improvement in the 
Services Confidence index to 6.9 from 5.8 (z-score: -0.2).  
We also anticipate that the release will confirm the preliminary Consumer Confidence estimate, 
leaving it unchanged at -16.5 (z-score: -1.1) following the flash reading. This result underscores 
the difficulties faced by consumers as higher-for-longer energy prices begin to weigh on 
household budgets and stands in stark contrast to the otherwise positive outlook of firms.

Final euro area manufacturing PMI to confirm industrial recovery extended into September 
 
Euro area manufacturing firms continue to show resilience in the face of continuously rising 
energy costs. The final PMI, which we expected will be revised down slightly, is nevertheless 
expected to be an impressive 52.6 (z-score: 0.2). Although it represents a marginal drop from last 
month, this result is in some ways more striking: firms reported a continued easing in delivery 
times, implying that the strength in the index is increasingly being driven by gains in production 
rather than supply-chain bottlenecks, which have mechanically raised the PMI value in recent 
readings. Consistent with this view, both the new orders and output components reached their 
highest levels since 1Q22. This signals that the underlying cyclical recovery is showing signs of 
improvement, with growth being enjoyed across the core and periphery alike.  
Manufacturing performance in Germany continues to act as an anchor for the euro area. Output 
remains elevated, and firms are reporting strong external demand. We thus expect the flash 
estimate of 53.8 (z-score: 0.3) to be confirmed in the final reading. Meanwhile, performance in 
France remains stable although the index is nearing contractionary territory. The negative 
outlook reported by firms in the flash report underscores the headwinds caused by high input 
prices and stiff external competition, which likely weighed on performance in September. In light 
of this, we expect the French index to be revised up slightly to 50.4 from 50.3 (z-score: 0.0), leaving 
the value just above the historical average, but below its peak from August. 
The final release contains new estimates for the periphery. In Italy, we expect a recovery in 
manufacturing following an August lull, with the index projected to rise to 50.6 from 49.6 (z-score: 
-0.1). Meanwhile, the outlook in Spain is expected to remain subdued as firms report pressure 
from rising energy prices. Still, some improvement in new orders is likely, and we thus expect the 
index to increase modestly to 50.0 from 49.5 (z-score: -0.2).

A new post-crisis high of 3.7% for inflation in September 
We forecast headline inflation rising by 0.5pp to 3.7% yoy in September, while core inflation 
remains unchanged at 2.4% yoy. 
As in August, energy prices are set to be the main driver of the increase. We forecast energy 
inflation rising by 4.7pp to 18.9% yoy, with almost all of the acceleration coming from a 7% mom 
increase in fuel prices, which alone should add 0.33pp to headline inflation. Even in Spain, where 
the government increased fuel tax relief for diesel, fuel prices still rose by around 6% mom. A 
further 0.06pp contribution to headline inflation is likely to come from electricity and gas, 
reflecting the recent rise in wholesale gas prices. 
Elsewhere, we forecast services inflation edging up by 0.1pp to 3.1% yoy, while goods inflation 
remains at 1.2% yoy. With the summer sales period ending in September, Spain's unusually 
elevated clothing inflation could correct, which would subtract around 0.1pp from euro area 
goods inflation. On the other hand, core inflation may begin to show the first signs of upward 
pressure from the energy shock, given the time it takes for higher energy costs to feed through 
supply chains. Resilient economic growth should provide a sufficient backdrop for firms to pass 
through these higher energy-related costs to consumers. 
Finally, we expect food inflation to rise by 0.3pp to 1.4% yoy, reflecting unfavourable base effects, 
recent heatwaves, and the gradual pass-through of higher energy-related costs. Risks are tilted 
to the downside, however, given food inflation has continued to trend lower over recent months 
despite these headwinds.

Labour market outlook improving, but employment still dropping 
In September, we expect the number of unemployed to have decreased by 4k, leaving the 
unemployment rate stable at 6.4%. The trend for industrial jobs remains weak but has improved 
in recent months on the back of resilient exports and fiscal spending. In addition, with a high

manufacturing sector to be relatively muted, while they should be stronger in the service sector.

Consumption likely to slow down in August after three strong months 
From April to July, real goods consumption increased strongly in France by 1.7% in total. A big 
part of that was due to higher non-fuel energy consumption (mostly electricity) which increased 
9% in total, driven by high temperatures. But even ex-energy consumption increased 1.0% over 
those three months. Car spending was pushed up by demand for electric vehicles and the World 
Cup spurred more purchases of electronic products. All those trends should start to moderate in 
August and we see consumption declining 0.6% mom. With fuel prices on the up again in 
September, further moderation is likely and the pace of growth could return to a lower level in 
4Q.
