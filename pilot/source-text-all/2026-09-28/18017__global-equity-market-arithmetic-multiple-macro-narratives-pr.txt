Global Equity Market Arithmetic 
Multiple macro narratives providing diversified sources of stock returns 
Cast your mind back to the late 1990s, the last time market concentration reached extremes. 
Most remember the TMT bubble, but there was much more going on beneath the surface. Asia 
was recovering from crisis, Europe had a new currency and oil, having fallen into single digits, 
was about to embark on a powerful rally. While market cap indices were dominated by TMT, 
stock returns were being driven by multiple macro and thematic forces. Today's market looks 
surprisingly similar. 
Sector performance (page five) tells the same story. Energy and AI-related sectors are pulling 
away from more consumer-facing areas of the market, creating meaningful dispersion beneath 
the headline indices. Joe Hay has applied our "dimensionality" measure to sectors using both 
market cap-weighted and equal-weighted covariance matrices, and demonstrated that sectors 
are once again providing diversification rather than moving in lockstep. 
We see the same pattern in our Momentum indices, where pairwise stock correlations are at 
record lows. Plenty of stocks are rising, but not for the same reasons. For systematic investors, 
that matters. Greater dispersion in return drivers means more opportunities to extract alpha 
from relative stock performance. Encouragingly, our multi-factor long/short models have 
navigated the latest bout of macro noise reasonably well. 
Concentrated markets can have significant divergence in stock 
prices (S&P 500 breadth vs pair-wise correlation) 
 
Global sector “dimensionality” is at high levels on a non-
market cap-weighted basis, yet very low on a market cap basis. 
 
Going up, but for different reasons: correlation of stocks in our 
Momentum indices is also at historically low levels! 
 
Systematic market-neutral equity long/short models continue 
to exploit cross-sectional stock divergence (including costs)
