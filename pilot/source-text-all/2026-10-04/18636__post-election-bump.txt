Post-Election Bump?  
With the 2026 midterm elections approaching, investors are weighing what the results could mean for taxes, 
spending and regulation. Before drawing conclusions, it is worth asking what history tells us about how stocks 
have fared before and after midterm elections.  
 
 
 
Average S&P 500 Returns Before and After Midterm Elections 
  
 
 
 
 
 
• 
Since 1950, the S&P 500 has delivered relatively weak returns in the three months leading up to 
midterm elections, as shown in the chart above. However, returns following the midterms have 
historically been much stronger as uncertainty fades and potential policy changes (or the lack thereof) 
become clearer.   
• 
Campaign season produces a stream of competing proposals, and this year affordability, including the 
electricity costs voters increasingly associate with data centers, has become a central issue for both 
parties.1 However, campaign rhetoric often differs from enacted policy, and markets typically respond 
favorably as uncertainty gives way to greater clarity. If history is any guide, we believe pre-election 
weakness may offer long-term investors a potential buying opportunity.  
• 
Regardless of the outcome, we caution against repositioning portfolios around election results. 
Congress changes hands every few years, while the forces driving corporate earnings play out over 
decades. Artificial intelligence (AI) offers a timely example. Demand for AI workloads continues to 
outstrip the infrastructure to support it, and political opposition that slows data center construction may 
extend the buildout rather than end it, reducing the risk of overbuilding along the way. In our view, 
companies with durable competitive advantages at the forefront of such innovation may be well 
positioned to deliver long-term shareholder value, whichever party controls Washington.
