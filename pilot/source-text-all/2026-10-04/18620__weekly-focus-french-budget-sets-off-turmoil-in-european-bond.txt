Macro Research 
 
  
Bond market swings continue to be at centre stage in world markets. On 
Thursday the French government presented its 2027 budget bill that aims to 
curb the booming deficit back to 5% of GDP. Following the announcement the 
spread between French and German 10Y yields rose to a new post euro crisis 
high. Uncertainty around French bonds spilled over to other markets as 
investors are growing increasingly worried about European public finances. 
On Thursday the 2Y EUR swap saw its largest decline since April as the market 
pricing for an October rate hike decreased to just 20%. EUR/USD continued to 
slide below 1.13. 
Anxious mood in the markets curbed hike expectations also in the US. The 
probability of the Fed hiking also in October has declined below 30%, down 
from around 70% just a week ago. Along with market sentiment, data releases 
from the week provide support for the Fed holding rates at level in October. 
On Wednesday the August headline PCE came in at 3.4% y/y (cons: 3.7%) and 
core PCE at 3.0% y/y (cons: 3.3%). On Tuesday, JOLTS job openings data was 
softer than expected. We continue to forecast the next hike for December.  
Inflation picked up momentum in Europe. Euro area September headline HICP 
inflation rose more than expected to 3.8% y/y (cons: 3.7%) driven by higher 
energy prices. Core inflation rose as expected to 2.5% y/y. The decrease in 
expectations for an October hike together with hotter than-expected inflation 
prints towards trouble ahead for the ECB. The markets seem to expect that 
ECB will not be hiking rates into a turbulent bond market – regardless of what 
the data indicates about the inflation outlook. 
This week brought mixed signals on how long the energy shock will last. 
Various outlets have now reported that crude exports from the Middle East 
rebounded to pre-war levels in September. Despite the rebound in exports, 
Brent traded above the USD 100/bbl mark consistently throughout the week. 
A key actor pushing crude supply lower is national strategic oil reserves. 
Drawdowns from the US crude stockpile have slowed down significantly and 
there are hints that China has become pressured on its reserves, possibly 
reflected in China's rising crude imports. An upcoming build-up of crude 
reserves back towards normal levels will take time; meaning that status quo 
in the oil market is far away regardless of Middle East exports normalizing.  
Wednesday next week minutes from the Fed’s September meeting will be 
released. With the turmoil in the bond markets, investors will be eyeing the 
minutes closely for any comments on inflation outlook. On Monday US ISM 
services index for September will be released. We will keep an eye on whether 
the ISM index confirms the strong readings from September flash PMIs. On 
Tuesday euro area retail sales and German industrial orders should give 
insight into how the European economy is fairing with higher energy costs. On 
Friday University of Michigan survey of consumer confidence for October is 
due for release. In September confidence fell to 48.1. Apart from May 2026, it 
was the most negative assessment on record, dating back to the 1950s.

Market Movers Scandinavia 
 
• In Denmark, Industrial production for August will be released on Tuesday. 
The July figures showed a 5.5% m/m decline in industrial production (SA), 
erasing most of the relatively strong 6.4% increase in June. Industrial 
production remains volatile, but other indicators still point to solid 
business confidence. We will be looking out for whether this is reflected in 
a rebound in industrial production in August. 
The unemployment indicator from the Danish Agency for Labour Market 
and Recruitment will be released on Monday, providing the first indication 
of unemployment in September. While unemployment has edged slightly 
higher, there are no clear signs of a broader slowdown in job growth, with 
businesses still indicating stable hiring intentions. The increase in 
unemployment mainly reflects more people being registered as available 
for work rather than weaker labour demand. Overall, the labour market still 
appears to be moving steadily forward. 
 
• Next week in Sweden, the services PMI will be published on Monday, flash 
CPI on Wednesday and the Production Value Index (PVI) and monthly GDP 
figures on Friday. The services PMI rose to 55.8 in August from 54.2 in July, 
with the broad-based increase mainly driven by higher business volumes, 
while the price component declined and is beginning to approach a more 
normal level. For the CPI flash estimate, we expect September inflation to 
rise quite significantly, with core inflation at 0.75% y/y, CPIF at 1.59% y/y 
and CPI at 1.27% y/y. The higher headline figure is driven by higher energy 
prices, but core inflation is also expected to rise across both goods and 
services, albeit more modestly. For more details, see our Sweden - forecast 
update, 1 October. The Production Value Index (PVI) will provide an update 
after the July print where industrial production fell by 3.6% m/m and 
construction declines by 3.8% m/m, while services increased by 1.8% 
m/m. GDP figures are also due, where last month’s figures saw a 0.8% m/m 
decline for July. 
 
• For Norway, we expect core inflation to rise moderately from 3.0% y/y to 
3.1% y/y in September. There are no particular reasons, other than 
inflation generally being marginally lower than normal in September last 
year. We expect somewhat lower price growth for food, but higher inflation 
for both domestically produced and imported goods as well as domestic 
services. We expect the annual rise in rents to be unchanged. If our 
estimate is correct, this will be 0.2pp higher than Norges Bank's estimate 
from MPR 2/26 and could thus lift rate expectations somewhat. 
The government will publish its budget forecast for 2027 on Wednesday. 
We expect the budget indicator to point to a slightly expansionary budget 
plan of around 0.1% of GDP, but keep in mind that this proposal will also 
need to secure a majority in parliament. In recent years, the budget 
balance has cracked by NOK 5-6bn (0.1pp of GDP) during these 
negotiations. 
Also keep an eye on central bank Governor Bache's annual lecture at BI 
(the Norwegian business school) on Thursday.

Scandi Update 
 
Denmark – strong revision of GDP growth 
In Denmark, the revised national accounts paint a stronger picture, with 
quarterly GDP growth for Q2 revised up from 0.3% q/q to 0.9% q/q and growth 
versus the same period last year now at 5.7% y/y. Exports, private 
consumption and investments were all revised higher. Still, the underlying 
picture is more nuanced, as households appear to have responded to higher 
prices by holding back spending rather than reducing savings. This leaves 
continued potential for consumption growth if confidence improves, 
supported by record high household savings relative to income. 
 
 
 
Sweden – Lagging, but soon leaving expansionary rates 
The Riksbank minutes were released on Wednesday and showed that all 
governors moved in a hawkish direction. The common denominator was that 
strong and broad-based growth, in combination with persistent supply 
shocks, has lifted inflation risks and removed the need for expansionary 
policy. Most governors highlighted that rate hikes should be seen as a 
normalisation of rates. The minutes were well in line with expectations, and 
we stick to our forecast of three hikes (November, February, March) to 2.50%. 
Tuesday’s NIER survey showed sentiment improving for the fifth consecutive 
month, driven by higher consumer confidence. The positive outlook was 
supported by retail sales reaching an all-time high and the consumption 
indicator showing solid growth. However, strong household demand may 
make it easier for firms to pass on costs. Wage growth was 2.9% y/y in July 
according to the Swedish National Mediation Office, though momentum 
slowed. 
Manufacturing PMI rose to 58.1 in September from 56.1, driven by a rise in 
new orders and production. Longer delivery times and higher inventories also 
supported the reading, which may reflect supply disruptions, so the print 
should be interpreted with caution. 
 
Norway – the labour market remains tight 
The unemployment rate was unchanged at 2.0% in September, and gross 
unemployment rose by 450 persons. New vacancies seem to be stabilizing 
but are still well above pre-pandemic levels. The main picture is that the 
labour market remains relatively tight, and that demand for labour may be 
stable. The figures are marginally stronger than Norges Bank's projections 
from MPR 3/26.
