September's sharp reset 
The Treasury curve flattened sharply as markets repriced a higher-for-longer Fed path. 
The 2yr rose 53bp to 4.87%, the 5yr rose 57bp to 5.07%, the 10yr rose 53bp to 5.27% 
and the 30yr rose 39bp to 5.62%. Softer core PCE revisions at month end provided little 
relief as long-end yields remained under pressure. 
HY OAS widened 49bp to 312bp in Sept, of which 32bp came in the final 4 trading days, 
while YTW rose to 8.3%. Monthly total return (TR) fell to -2.49%, its weakest month 
since Sept 2022 (-4.02%), reducing YTD TR from 2.6% through August to +0.04%. 
The selloff marked a break from most of 2026, when carry offset modest spread 
volatility. In Sept, the rates and spread move overwhelmed carry.  Loans returned 
+0.21% vs -2.49% for HY, -2.63% for IG and -3.65% for the 10yr Treasury, pointing to 
duration rather than a broad shutdown in credit as the main driver. 
Two catalysts drove the reset 
Was the selloff warranted or an overreaction? We start with the 2 key catalysts in the 
final week of Sept: rates and the Paramount/WBD financing. 
Rates: A series of macro data releases reinforced the higher-for-longer Fed policy path. 
Firm PPI supported expectations for additional Fed tightening, while Sept PMI surprised 
sharply to the upside and pointed to resilient activity\, supply-chain pressure and firm 
input costs. PCE data out at the end of the month was softer than expected but included 
methodological changes and larger-than-expected downward revisions to core inflation.  
While we saw those revisions as dovish for near-term policy, we note that upward GDP 
revisions imply stronger productivity growth and a higher neutral rate (see report 
Revisionist history). Therefore, the curve failed to meaningfully re-steepen and long-end 
yields remained under pressure. Rates volatility also rose to 101, its highest level since 
March 30, 2026.  
Supply: Paramount completed $52bn of financing for its WBD acquisition. 8yr HY bonds 
fell to ~95 from par, while 10yr IG tranche widened to roughly T+280bp from T+262.5bp 
at issuance, as investor concerns centered on leverage, deleveraging execution and 
higher rates.  The fact that recent new issues outperformed the broad HY index by 
1.33% in Sept is an indication that Paramount trading pattern appears to have been 
largely deal-specific and not evidence of broader demand impairment.

Overreaction or justified repricing? 
These events highlight 3 possible sources of renewed investor concern, along with 
important offsets. 
Cost of capital 
Higher base rates raise borrowing costs, with the greatest pressure on issuers with 
weaker balance sheets or nearer-term refinancing needs. The spread move was broad by 
rating: BBs widened 42bp, Bs widened 44bp and CCCs widened 137bp. However, sector 
performance was more differentiated. Cable returned -4.22% in Sept, followed by 
Telecoms at -3.44%, Real Estate at -3.04% and Travel at -3.02%, while Technology (-
1.58%), Services (-1.67%) and Healthcare (-1.76%) held up best. The larger CCC move is 
consistent with greater refinancing sensitivity, while weakness in higher-quality HY also 
points to rates contagion rather than a purely fundamental repricing. 
Technicals  
The timing and size of the Paramount financing added pressure during an already 
volatile week, and secondary performance was weak. Still, HY technicals remain more 
supportive than IG technicals, while broader new-issue performance suggests the 
indigestion was localized rather than market-wide (Exhibit 1).

Rates contagion 
When rates volatility rises sharply, even BB bonds come under duration pressure. IG had 
already weakened under rates uncertainty; Sept suggests that pressure has now reached 
HY. The HY and loan CSI changes provide additional evidence. 
 
More volatility than distress 
HY CSI rose 2pts WoW (Exhibit 2) driven primarily by rates volatility.  Distress 
contributed less, while improving primary access provided an offset. ETF flows returned 
to positive after a brief period of weak or sideways demand.

Dispersion beneath the index argues for selectivity. The highest-stress CSI decile 
underperformed HY by 3.2% in September, while the lowest-stress decile outperformed 
by 0.34%. 
Why we're not turning bearish 
September looks more like a rates-led valuation reset than a broad fundamental break. 
Aggregate fundamentals remain constructive: as of end of Sept, HY EBITDA grew 9.0% 
y/y against 3.1% debt growth, while gross leverage was 4.31x, net leverage was 3.62x 
and interest coverage was 4.26x. Stronger growth is supportive for risk assets, although 
a higher neutral rate and elevated rates volatility create near-term pressure on duration 
and weaker balance sheets.  
The widening also normalized several relative-value relationships. HY OAS moved back 
above 300bp from a low base, while bond-vs-loan yield relationships returned closer to 
neutral after bonds had traded at an unusually deep yield discount.

Could the pressure continue? Yes, if rates volatility remains elevated or 3Q earnings 
show signs of inflation or consumer-led deterioration. Otherwise, supportive 
fundamentals and HY technicals still argue for tighter spreads over time. We favor liquid, 
non-distressed credits that have underperformed, while loans retain a near-term 
advantage as long as duration constrains bond demand.
