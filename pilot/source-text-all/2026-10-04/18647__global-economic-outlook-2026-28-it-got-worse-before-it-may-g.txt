GLOBAL ECONOMIC OUTLOOK 2026-28
Outlook 2026-28: It got worse before it may get 
better (if three key assumptions hold)
The Great Unwind: all three chokepoints unwind rather than escalate - if three assumptions hold.
1
Energy
• Conflict stays contained; rerouting and alternative supply ease prices.
• Breaks if further escalation like the Saudi production outage runs past a month.
2
Bonds
• Inflation moderates without second-round wage effects; markets absorb heavy issuance.
• Breaks if US mid-term, French or Italian elections add fiscal risk: a self-reinforcing yield–fiscal loop.
3
AI
• Capex above USD1tn in 2028 keeps supporting growth; value shifts to adoption.
• Breaks if power and chip bottlenecks or weak earnings trigger an investment retrenchment.
•
Three risks we were watching have moved into the base case: an energy shock, a bond sell-off spreading from the US to Japan and Europe, 
and renewed doubts about the AI boom.
•
The real economy has held up: Q2 growth strong globally despite the energy shock, European manuf. PMIs are at four-year highs
• Global GDP: 3.0% (2025) → 2.6% (2026) → 2.8% (2027) → 2.6% (2028)
• Inflation stays 1–2pp above target until H2 2027 in Europe and US
• One more Fed hike to 4.00–4.25%, two more ECB hikes to 3.00%, cuts no earlier than end-2027
• Capital markets: Bond yields to gradually move lower, equity returns to decelerate from double to single digits amid valuation normalization, credit spreads to widen 
mildly amid tech issuance, private markets remaining in selective phase

GLOBAL ECONOMIC OUTLOOK 2026-28
Chokepoint #1: The Iran war is reshaping 
global energy markets
The net hit to Hormuz flows still stands at about 4.3 Md/d 
vs pre-war levels but could rise to >8 Mb/d if the Saudi 
pipeline remains halted
Supply gap estimate, Mb/d
Sources: LSEG Datastream, Allianz Research
Note: * additional flows versus pre-war flows
What we expect
•
A mild scenario could see oil flows in the Saudi pipeline resume at lower 
volumes (3 Mb/d) before ramping up.
•
Refined-product markets are running out of buffers, and persistently high 
prices are increasingly forcing demand destruction, with global oil 
demand expected to fall by 2.5mbpd in 2026.
•
We forecast Brent prices to average 87 USD/bbl in Q4 2026 before 
easing to 76 USD/bbl on average in 2027 as Gulf export flows gradually 
normalize both in and around Hormuz. 
•
European gas prices should stay elevated through year-end, with TTF 
averaging around EUR65/MWh in Q3–Q4 2026, before easing to about 
EUR36/MWh in 2027 as Hormuz LNG flows normalize and new US supply 
comes on the market. 
What could go wrong
•
A deeper or more prolonged Saudi export disruption would raise the 
probability of Brent staying around 100 USD/bbl for longer.
•
A cold-winter gas squeeze that pushes TTF above EUR120/MWh  could 
tip the Eurozone into recession given today’s weaker demand and more 
constrained fiscal buffers.
•
Financial markets would react with higher yields as inflation would stay 
higher for longer on second round effects, forcing central banks to 
maintain a stronger and longer lasting restrictive stance.

GLOBAL ECONOMIC OUTLOOK 2026-28
Chokepoint #2: Rates are reaching peak levels, 
but the equilibrium remains fragile
Global bond yields reached multi-year highs on 
energy prices, fiscal deficits and AI
Sources: LSEG Datastream, Allianz Research
What we expect
•
Higher oil and gas prices have boosted inflation and policy rate 
expectations, driving bond yields higher. Under our assumption of 
easing energy market conditions, yields should gradually decline. 
•
Political risk notably around upcoming elections in France and Italy, 
has also contributed to higher yields. We expect non-polarized 
outcomes, which should support eurozone spreads.
•
Real yields have risen amid stronger growth prospects linked to the AI 
boom. We believe markets have become overly optimistic, leaving 
room for real yields to decline as expectations are reassessed.
What could go wrong
•
A renewed energy supply shock, political risks after US mid-terms or 
European elections, or growth overheating could reinforce the rates 
sell-off leading to unstable financial conditions. 
•
Debt-sustainability concerns could then intensify, creating a self-
reinforcing sovereign yield–fiscal loop in which higher risk premia push 
yields even higher. 
•
If the loop becomes disorderly, market turmoil could force central 
banks to slow QT or to apply curve control policies to preserve 
market functioning.

Chokepoint #3: The race for AI is still on
What we expect
•
AI capex cycle is at half-way : yearly investment to peak in 2028 at over 
USD1trn then stabilize at around USD 900bn/year in 2029-30.
•
AI value migrates from compute to adoption. Infrastructure captured the first 
wave of returns, particularly battery, memory chips and IT servers. The 
second wave would come from productivity gains in data-intensive services 
and autonomous devices. US tech and EM semiconductors lead initially, 
while European sovereignty and Chinese self-sufficiency ambitions are 
under-invested segments and broaden the AI opportunity set.
•
AI delivers: US-led productivity gains, manageable labor-market adjustment 
and reverse price trajectory toward disinflation in mid-term.
•
More control at national level would shore up confidence toward AI and help 
democratizing further that technology, boosting in return adoption pace.
What could go wrong
•
Power availability, grid connections approval, water reserve, datacenter lead 
time,  memory chips supply, AI-enabled hardware are key operating factors 
that could put brakes on the AI race, raising costs, delaying technology 
deployment and impact business investment cycle.
•
Tighter monetary conditions could impact IT budget and innovation, slowing 
down adoption pace among corporates and impacting datacenter returns.
•
Financial forces abate in return to slow delivery: valuations fall, AI capex 
retrenches and tighter credit conditions pushes growth sharply lower, with 
strong disinflation and aggressive rate cuts.
At half-way, current AI expansion looks already 
bigger than past investment cycle but not over-sized
