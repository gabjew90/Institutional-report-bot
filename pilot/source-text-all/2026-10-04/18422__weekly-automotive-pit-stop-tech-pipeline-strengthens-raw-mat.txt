Automotive Industry 
Weekly Automotive Pit Stop: Tech pipeline 
strengthens, raw material risk builds 
Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Technician pipeline grows as enrollment picks up  
We think an improving technician pipeline should support auto dealers parts & service 
business as the industry continues to work through a persistent technician shortage. 
Universal Technical Institute end-of-period full-time students declined -6.0% QoQ to 
14.6k in fiscal 3Q (June), a smaller decline than normal seasonality (3Q24: -7.3%, 3Q25: -
6.7%) ahead of the typical 4Q peak from summer starts. YoY enrollment growth picked 
up to +5.2% in 3Q from +3.8% in 2Q as YoY growth has now been positive for 12 
straight quarters. Separately, IPEDS data shows degrees and certificates awarded for 
Mechanic & Repair Technologies/Technicians rose +13% YoY in 2025, adding to entry-
level technician supply after several years of strong trade-school enrollment. Dealers 
have also called out technician-focused initiatives, including ABG's internal Asbury 
Automotive Institute, GPI's focus on technician hiring and productivity, and Sonic's 
efforts to grow technician count and add service stalls.  
Raw material costs increase as 2027 risk builds   
We estimate the total value of raw material inputs for an average vehicle rose +1% MoM 
in September to $4,845, up +32% YoY. Steel was the largest incremental headwind, 
while aluminum was ~flat but still elevated YoY and oil-based products continued to 
pressure costs. At the company level, recent supplier conversations cited cost inflation in 
memory, oil-linked inputs, and steel, with commercial recoveries typically lagging by a 
quarter or two. Near-term direction is unclear, with oil prices down 13% since mid-
September but still volatile. If commodity prices hold or move higher, we think costs 
could pressure 2027 earnings for both OEMs and suppliers especially through 1Q27.  
Incentives tick up in September as OEMs diverge  
Industry average incentives in September ticked up sequentially to $3,360 per unit 
(+2.9% MoM), though they remain down -8.4% YoY. Ford backtracked after recent 
progress toward industry, with spend rising +18.4% MoM to $4,154 (-5.2% YoY), and its 
premium to industry widened to +23.6% from +7.4% in August. GM incentives fell -3.3% 
MoM and -5.6% YoY to $3,035 and now sit -9.7% below industry, a wider gap than last 
month's -3.9%. Toyota remains low and continues to reflect tight inventory levels, with 
spend rising +12.9% MoM to $1,452 but down -19.8% YoY and -56.8% below average.
