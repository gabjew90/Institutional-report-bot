Sep ’26: A big rate selloff 
     
   
 
 
Sep ’26: A big rate selloff 
The combination of strong US data, lack of resolution of the Iran war, and a hawkish Fed 
resulted in a big selloff in rates, with the Treasury yield curve bear flattening in 
September (Exhibit 1). Although the rate selloff was global, Treasuries (10yr +53bps) 
significantly underperformed both bunds (+26bps) and JGBs (+11bps, Exhibit 5). 
The implied interest rate volatility remained stable initially but jumped from about 80 to 
over 100 during the week of September 21st. By the end of September, rate vol was 
near the levels last reached at the peak of the Iran war volatility in March 2026 (Exhibit 
2). Equities remained resilient, supported by the big tech. Ex. the Magnificent 7, stocks 
were down about -2% on the month, while small caps underperformed (-5%, Exhibit 3). 
The September data was hawkish. August payrolls report was much stronger than 
expected (see our report, US Watch: Aug jobs: The good, the strong and the stable). The 
CPI inflation report was hot (see our report, US Watch: August CPI inflation: All clear for 
a hike). The September FOMC was hawkish, with Chair Warsh stating that financial 
conditions were accommodative (see our report, US Watch: Sep FOMC review: Hike first, 
ask questions later). The US Services PMI came in much stronger than expected on 
September 23rd (Exhibit 6), triggering a bout of volatility in the markets (see our report, 
Situation Room: Mini rate shock). 
The parties did not reach an agreement on Iran during the UN General Assembly. Brent 
oil traded above $100/bbl during the month, and our strategists increased their oil price 
forecast for 2H-2026 to $95/bbl (see our report, Global Energy Weekly: A deepening 
geopolitical schism). US diesel prices climbed to over $6/gallon (Exhibit 4). 
Returns: A loan-ly win 
Loans (0.21%) delivered the best return, followed by Stocks (-0.35%), Treasury (-2.29%), 
HY (-2.49%), and IG (-2.63%), while Mortgages (-3.27%) underperformed most. 
ICE BofA IG index spread +3bps in September 
The ICE BofA US IG index spread was +3bps in September for an excess return of -7bps. 
The Sep monthly rebalancing added 1bps to the index spread, bringing it to 83bps.  
Technology (+11bps) and Telecom (+9bps) delivered the highest excess returns in 
September, while Metals & Mining (-23bps) and Automobiles (-24bps) underperformed. 
Supply 
September supply was $198bn. We look for October 2026 IG supply at $110bn. 
Fund flows 
Net inflows to US IG bond funds and ETFs were +$20bn in September (based only on 
funds reporting daily) vs +$47bn in August (based on all funds).

IG October supply: $110bn 
We expect $110bn in US IG supply for October, down from $162bn issued in October 
2025. Our forecast is close to the five-year average supply for the month of October 
($109bn, Exhibit 11), but significantly below $160bn implied by our $2.1tr 2026 annual 
forecast and typical seasonality. The lighter supply volumes imply stronger IG index cash 
technicals. IG index net supply less coupon income should be close to flat in October, 
down from $20bn we are tracking for September (Exhibit 12).  
Everything points to lower supply 
We expect slow October supply due to 1) the big jump in yields and rate vol, 2) slower 
M&A and hyperscaler volumes, 3) less bank supply. Supply duration should remain short. 
Higher rates. Historically, companies slow the pace of issuance following a sharp rise in 
borrowing costs. We estimate that translates into $10 to $20bn per week for 2026 (see 
our report, IG Credit Strategist: More rate vol = wider spreads). Higher rate vol. 
Demand creates supply. Demand should be weaker in October following the jump in rate 
vol (Exhibit 13). Less M&A supply. M&A is a big driver of issuance volumes in 2026 
(Exhibit 16), but most M&A transactions will likely come after earnings in November. 
Same goes for hyperscalers. Less bank supply. We look for subdued bank supply post 
3Q earnings, as our analyst Tom Curcuruto expects US bank supply to slow to $124bn in

Returns

summarizing a number of sector statistics such as size, performance and rating. At the 
issuer level, the top 25 names gained 34bps in terms of excess return in September

Ratings trends  
Net upgrades (upgrades less downgrades, including Fallen Angels) for credits in ICE BofA 
US IG corporate index C0A0 increased to $34bn in September from $12bn in August. 
Net LTM upgrades however declined to+4.4% of index notional in September from

the overall index negative rating actions in September (including watch (75% weight) 
and outlook (25% weight) changes), while Banks/Brokers contributed the most to 
positive rating actions. 
Fund flows
