Valuation Multiples Should Include Off-Balance Sheet Commitments
We argue that off-BS commitments should be added to EV before calculating valuation
multiples for the hyperscalers because:
1.
Equity value is "residual value accruing to shareholders after debt and other contractual
obligations are paid." By implication, rising obligations and commitments (regardless of
accounting) are headwinds to share price appreciation;
2.
Hyperscaler CapX should grow from ~$400B in 2025 to ~$800B in 2026 to ~$1.1T in
2027, so tracking all sources of financing (regardless of accounting) best predicts equity
appreciation (our view);
3.
META is increasingly funding its AI infrastructure through off-balance-sheet leases and
project-finance structures; and,
4.
Adding off-BS obligations materially impacts relative valuation multiples, potentially
adding 35% to META's traditional EV multiples compared with only +3% for GOOGL and
+9% for AMZN, as shown below.
META: More Off-BS Obligations than Peers

expensive than its hyperscaler peers when off-balance sheet commitments are included in its

So What?
■
All 3 of the hyperscalers we cover are adding off-balance sheet (BS) commitments called
"Contracted Leases Not Yet Commenced." As of 6/30/26, the PV of these commitments were
$91B for GOOGL (representing 44% of total on-BS + off-BS obligations), $137B for AMZN
(36%), and $278B for META (38%). At META, this category was >2x higher than its total on-
BS debt + leases at 6/30/26 ($278B vs $112B), and "consists of data centers, co-locations,
and certain network infrastructure," according footnote 9 of META's 2Q26 10Q.
■
In addition, META reported an additional $350B of off-balance sheet "Non-Cancelable
Contractual Commitments" described as "commitments related to third-party cloud
capacity arrangements and investments in servers and network infrastructure, data
centers, and consumer hardware products in Reality Labs" as of 6/30/26.
■
By implication, META's off-balance obligations of $628B were >5.6x higher than its on-BS
debt + leases of $112B at 6/30/26.
■
Additionally, in July 2026 (ie, post-2Q26), META added $68B (up >10%) of "Contracted
Leases Not Yet Commenced", implying rapid growth of its off-BS obligations in 3Q26.
Investment Conclusions
Using traditional EV calculations understates hyperscaler valuation multiples, we believe. In
META's case, traditional EV valuation calculations miss 5.6x higher off-BS obligations than
those disclosed on META's balance sheet. By implication, META seems to be much more
expensive for equity holders than traditional EV multiple calculations suggest. We recommend
that investors add META's off-balance sheet obligations ($626B at 6/30/26) to META's EV before
calculating its valuation multiples.

At a Glance
OUR INVESTMENT THESIS
Our Hold is based on META’s strategy diffusion, which is very costly. That is, META is simultaneously talking about expanding into
custom chips, data-center infrastructure, enterprise AI software, business agents, model APIs, compute sales, advertising tools, consumer
assistants, smart glasses and other hardware. Each of these require different products, customers, sales capabilities and competitive
advantages. By not concentrating its capital and FCF on the highest-return products and services, it raises the risk that management
attention, engineering talent and shareholder capital are spread across too many things, and lowers the likelihood that META succeeds
at any of them, we believe.

POTENTIAL UPSIDE DRIVERS
Muse and META's new Connectors platform represent new products with potentially material revenue upside, over time, we believe.
META's core family of 4 apps had the largest unduplicated reach in the world, at 3.58B DAPs in December 2025. Walled Garden economics
and GenAI integrations are driving margin expansion. Also, Direct Response (DR) ads (ie, rev sharing) acts as a hedge to falling CPM-
based ad revs, which lowers META's revenue volatility and risks, in its core business.
RISKS TO TARGET
META
The Bear Case for META includes strategy diffusion, regulatory and litigation processes and fines, rising OpEx and capital spending,
growing competition, structural cost disadvantages (since META doesn't own Cloud assets), and the potential for economic, consumer
or ad demand weakness.
