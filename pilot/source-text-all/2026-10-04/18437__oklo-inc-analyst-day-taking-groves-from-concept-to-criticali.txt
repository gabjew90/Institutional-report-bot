Analyst Day: Taking Groves from Concept 
to Criticality 
Maintain Rating: BUY | PO: 80.00 USD | Price: 36.11 USD   
   
 
 
Groves Shows Progress from Development to Operations 
Oklo hosted an analyst day in Lockhart, Texas, that included an isotope-market teach-in, 
management discussions, and a visit to the Groves-1 facility. Groves-1 progressed from 
groundbreaking in Sept. 2025 to first criticality in Aug. 2026, requiring Oklo to develop 
capabilities across construction, procurement, DOE authorization, commissioning, and 
operations. While this provides a tangible example of Oklo executing a nuclear project, 
Groves-1 is a test reactor and is not directly comparable with the scale, design, capital 
requirements, or commercial objectives of an Aurora powerhouse. We therefore view the 
project as a useful execution reference point rather than validation of Oklo’s broader 
commercial power model. 
Isotopes Offer Opportunity, but Validation Is Ahead 
The isotope teach-in provided greater detail on Oklo’s plans to develop a third business 
alongside power and fuel, initially focused on healthcare applications and potentially 
extending into industrial, semiconductor, space, defense, and research markets. Oklo 
plans to combine dedicated irradiation capacity with processing, quality control, product 
finishing, and logistics, which could address a fragmented supply chain and provide a 
more integrated offering to customers. Groves-1 primarily demonstrated reactor 
deployment and startup, while Groves-2 is expected to serve as the more relevant test 
of commercial production and customer delivery. We would need greater clarity on 
customer commitments, product mix, production capacity, pricing, and project costs 
before assigning a more meaningful value to the isotope opportunity. 
Aurora Economics and PJM Remain Key Topics 
Other topics discussed included the timelines for the Aurora Fuel Fabrication Facility and 
Aurora reactor facilities at INL. We expect a more substantive outlook on Aurora pricing 
and costs as Kiewit breaks down FOAK economics and the pathway to savings from 
FOAK to NOAK, potentially in spring 2027. Management also discussed the PJM denial 
and highlighted that Oklo has multiple other similar sites in the queue that it could 
pursue and that remain under PJM evaluation. 
Aurora Read-Through Is Positive but Limited 
Groves-1 provided Oklo with operating experience and project data across suppliers, 
construction, authorization, startup, and project controls that could inform future 
facilities. However, the degree to which these capabilities translate into lower costs or 
shorter schedules for Aurora remains uncertain, given the differences in design and 
project scope. In our view, the visit provided evidence that Oklo is developing an internal 
execution organization, but it did not resolve the central questions around Aurora capital 
costs, licensing, financing, construction risk, or the timing of commercial deployment 
Maintain Buy 
We maintain our Buy rating on Oklo Inc. (OKLO). While execution remains uncertain, we 
believe Oklo’s vertically integrated build-own-operate model and sizable customer 
backlog position the company to be a leader in the early stages of the SMR market.

Price objective basis & risk 
Oklo Inc. (OKLO) 
Our price objective of $80 is premised on a DCF-based valuation framework, which we 
believe better captures the elevated volatility and longer-dated cash flow profile of 
Oklo's equity. 
 
Our cost of equity is derived using a risk-free rate of 4.6% (10-year U.S. Treasury), a U.S. 
equity risk premium of 8.42%, and a beta of 2.07, resulting in a cost of equity of 
approximately 22%. This reflects the company's early-stage development profile, 
regulatory uncertainty, and heightened equity volatility. We assume a 6.5% cost of debt, 
which together, based on a 65% of debt to capital, imply a weighted average cost of 
capital (WACC) of 11% 
 
We anchor our valuation to 2030, aligning with our expectation for initial commercial 
deployment, contracted capacity ramp, and a meaningful cash flow inflection as Oklo 
transitions toward scaled operations. 
 
We apply a 5% terminal growth rate, supported by long-duration demand for firm, 
carbon-free power, particularly from data center and hyperscale customers. 
 
Upside risks: 1) Lower capex advantage versus peers drives adoption, 2) HALEU 
availability and price results in higher returns, 3) Recycling and radioisotopes businesses 
provide revenue upside, 4) Incremental wins from hyperscaler customers accelerated 
deployments. 
 
Downside risks: 1) regulatory delays, 2) competition from other energy sources, 3) 
emerging SMR competitors, 4) manufacturing challenges, 5) fuel supply constraints, 6) 
safety risks, and 7) high upfront capital requirements.
