Editorial 
Opportunities from the bond sell-off  
Strategy summary: The global bond sell-off is creating tactical 
and medium-term opportunities. In the US, September’s yield 
surge has improved the risk-reward in 10-year government 
bonds, presenting an attractive entry point for investors with a 
6-12-month horizon. We believe bond and money markets are 
overly hawkish about the Fed outlook, given disinflation is likely 
to return as impact of oil prices and tariffs ease next year. 
In equities, we stick to quality, especially sectors riding the AI-
led earnings boom. The upcoming Q3 earnings season is likely 
to show AI-investments are powering sectors beyond the 
technology and communications services, such as financials, 
materials and power and electrification. We remain constructive 
on the semiconductor and memory segments. We also expect 
the US Dollar to peak in the next few weeks along with short-
to-medium-term US bond yields. This creates opportunities for 
AUD corporate bonds, Emerging Market USD bonds and gold. 
Attractive risk-reward in US 10-year government bonds. 
The US 10-year bond yield rose more than 50bps in September 
(biggest monthly rise since 2022) and 115bps this year, amid 
expectations of economic resilience, higher Fed rates, fuel-
driven inflation, AI-related capital demand and fiscal concerns. 
Valuations appear close to fair value, improving the 6-12-month 
risk-reward. Bond arithmetic suggests losses of less than 1% if 
the yield rises to 6%, vs. more than 10% gains if it falls to 4.5%. 
Resilient US economic activity, but softer-than-expected 
inflation. September data showed robust US employment 
(jobless claims, ADP) and core capital-goods orders. However, 
consumer confidence remains depressed, real disposable 
income is flat and households are drawing on savings to sustain 
spending. Against this divided backdrop, core PCE inflation - 
the Fed’s preferred gauge - was softer than expected at 0.2% 
m/m and 3.0% y/y. A revised method to measure PCE inflation 
also indicate that annual core PCE inflation was 35bps lower 
than previously reported, narrowing the gap with CPI inflation. 
Markets appear overly hawkish on the Fed. Money markets 
price about 85bps of tightening over the next 12 months. A 
strong labour market (September’s non-farm payrolls data is 
due tonight) may justify one or two further hikes, but aggressive 
tightening after the sharp rise in bond yields – which has 
already tightened financial conditions - could threaten financial 
stability. US small- and mid-cap equities have already fallen 8–
9% from recent peaks over the past couple of months, 
materially underperforming large caps, as energy and 
borrowing-cost concerns intensify. While some Fed speakers 
remain hawkish, we agree with New York Fed President 
Williams’ that there is no urgency to tighten. We see little 
chance of an October Fed rate hike and expect inflation data 
for Sep-Nov determining whether the Fed moves in December. 
Stay with quality equities and AI beneficiaries. US large 
caps have absorbed the rise in bond yields relatively well, 
supported by expectations of robust earnings growth led by AI-
related investment. We continue to favour quality companies 
and sectors benefiting from this AI cycle. Semiconductors and 
memory remain preferred exposures: structural AI demand 
should extend the memory upcycle, while long-term supply 
agreements provide better revenue visibility. The opportunity is 
also broadening beyond technology and communication 
services into financials, materials, power and electrification. 
Opportunities in AUD corporate bonds and gold. The USD 
rebound alongside higher US yields offers a tactical opportunity 
to add higher-yielding Emerging Market USD and non-USD 
bonds. We remain positive on AUD corporate bonds, which 
offer a yield premium over comparable US credit. Our 
constructive medium-term AUD/USD view could add returns for 
USD-based investors over the next 6–12 months. We would 
also use gold’s recent pullback to add exposure, supported by 
sustained Emerging Market central bank demand. We continue 
to view USD 4,000/oz as important technical support for gold.  
—  Rajat Bhattacharya

Top client questions 
  Do you expect more RBA rate hikes after its less hawkish 
guidance? What is your view on the AUD and AUD bonds? 
Our view: We expect the RBA to leave rates unchanged, but 
retain the option to hike again should inflation intensify. We 
maintain our medium-term constructive AUD view and maintain 
our opportunistically bullish AUD corporate bonds idea. 
Rationale: The RBA’s forward guidance remains data dependent, 
explicitly allowing further tightening if needed as inflation remains too 
high.  At 4.60%, Australia’s cash rate is currently 60–85bp above the 
Fed’s 3.75–4.00% range. Thus, even with our two additional 25bps 
Fed hikes expectation, Australian rates would hold a modest positive 
rate differential. We expect the AUD to strengthen once the Fed 
signals that US rates are near their peak, which should allow resilient 
Australian fundamentals to reassert themselves. Key risks are 
persistently high US yields, weaker China growth or a sharper 
deterioration in global risk sentiment. 
We believe risk-reward has become more attractive for AUD bonds 
following the bear-flattening of the AUD yield curve. Markets are 
already pricing in one more RBA hike through end-2027, presenting 
an attractive opportunity to earn a yield even as increasingly 
restrictive financial conditions reinforce disinflation over time. 
Meanwhile, the positive interest rate differential with the US supports 
the currency outlook, supporting the case for AUD bonds. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
— Cedric Lam, Senior Investment Strategist 
— Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist 
  What does the incoming US data tell us about the path for 
US 10-year bond yields from here? 
Our view: The risk-reward in US 10-year yields has turned 
attractive following their move higher amid incoming US data. 
Rationale: Recent US data has indeed started to show softer-than-
expected inflation. However, market technicals are likely temporarily 
delaying a move lower in bond yields, with reports suggesting the 
rise in yields is creating forced selling amongst hedge funds and real 
estate investment trusts. Nevertheless, we do not expect this to be 
an extended selloff. We have initiated an opportunistic bullish idea 
on US 10-year government bonds this week and continue to believe 
this idea offers an attractive risk/reward on a 6-12-month horizon as 
yields become attractive enough to entice buyer flow to return. On a 
broader allocation basis, we retain our structural preference for 3-7-
year maturities in bonds. 
— Cedric Lam, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  How should investors position in the GBP ahead of the UK 
budget announcement next month? 
Our view: We maintain a medium-term constructive GBP view, 
but keep near-term exposure measured ahead of the 28 October 
Budget. We would view any pre-Budget weakness as an 
opportunity to gradually build GBP exposure. 
Rationale: The government’s decision to adjust the pension triple 
lock from 2030 is an early indication of greater willingness to tackle 
longer-term spending pressures, reinforcing Chancellor Healey’s 
commitment to fiscal discipline and meeting the fiscal rules. Markets 
are likely to scrutinise the Budget closely for evidence that this 
discipline extends to near-term tax and spending decisions. A 
credible Budget should help contain pressure on UK government 
bond yields and support GBP. Any fiscal slippage remains a key risk.  
Near term, we prefer measured GBP exposure as elevated US yields 
and repricing towards further Fed tightening could continue to 
support the USD. Over the medium term, we expect GBP to rebound 
as the Fed eventually signals the peak of its tightening cycle. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist 
  How would different US mid-term election outcome 
scenarios affect the outlook for US equities? 
Our view: We believe election outcomes will have a limited 
impact over the next 12 months. We remain positive on US 
equities driven by fundamental earnings growth.  
Rationale: Over the past 50 years, the ruling US President’s party 
has retained control of both chambers of Congress on only two 
occasions (1978 and 2002). All other elections resulted in an 
opposition sweep or a divided government.  
Based on the latest prediction markets, it appears that an opposition 
sweep (both the House and Senate flips from Republican to 
Democrats) is more likely than a divided government (in which the 
House flips to Democrats, while the Senate remains under 
Republican control). A scenario where Republicans retain a majority 
is least likely per prediction markets. 
While mid-term elections may introduce near-term volatility, we 
caution against overreaction. Historically, the average return for the 
S&P 500 following an opposition sweep is 17% over the next 12 
months, but the average return following a divided government is not 
significantly worse, at 15%. We prefer to base our investment views 
on fundamental earnings growth, where investor attention should 
focus on the upcoming Q3 earnings season. 
—  Jason Wong, Senior Equity Analyst

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What is the outlook for the memory chip and broader 
semiconductors industry? 
Our view: We remain positive on semiconductors and memory 
chips. Structural AI demand should prolong the memory 
upcycle while long-term agreements improve revenue visibility. 
Rationale: Recent industry commentary points to tighter memory 
supply-demand conditions through 2028, reinforcing our view that AI 
demand is likely to extend the memory upcycle. Furthermore, adoption 
of long-term agreements improves revenue and earnings visibility. 
Despite debates around “Pacing the Frontier”, we have seen a flurry 
of new model releases, along with the introduction of new consumer 
AI agents capable of comparing prices online and making 
purchases. These are early days, but the path is open for a business-
to-agent (B2A) economy where businesses increasingly interact with 
AI assistants acting on customers’ behalf. As these applications are 
increasingly inference intensive, wider adoption should support 
demand for memory, with its share of global semiconductor 
revenues projected to rise from 30.7% in 2025 to 66.7% in 2027. 
Against this backdrop, we maintain our Opportunistic idea on Global 
Semiconductors. Given memory chips’ cyclical nature, investors 
should avoid overconcentration in memory names and maintain a 
diversified exposure across Big Tech and semiconductors. 
—  Ryan Goh, Investment Strategist 
  Does a US bond yield stress test raise the risk to your 
constructive view on US equities? 
Our view: We have a target for the S&P 500 index of 8400 over 
the next 12 months. A scenario of a sustained rise in the US 10-
year government bond yield to 5.6% could lead to negative 
returns from the current S&P500 level (7,666). 
Rationale: Our base case 12-month target for the S&P 500 is 8,400, 
which assumes 2027 S&P 500 EPS of USD 420 and a 20x forward 
P/E multiple. A rise in bond yields would have an impact on equity 
valuations, as a higher discount rate reduces the present value of 
future earnings. We stress-test our target under different 10-year 
yield and earnings scenarios.  
A rise in bond yields would arguably be associated with a stronger 
growth environment and hence, even higher earnings. However, 
assuming unchanged EPS at our base case of USD 420, a 
(sustained) rise in the 10-year bond yield to 5.6% would lead to 
negative returns from the current level if not offset by a further 
improvement in earnings growth. However, in our base case we 
expect the 10-year yield is more likely to fall from here. 
—  Fook Hien Yap, Senior Investment Strategist
