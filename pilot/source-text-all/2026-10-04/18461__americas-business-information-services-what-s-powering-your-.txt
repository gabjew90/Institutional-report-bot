 
This week in Business & Information Services, the FHFA’s move to a unified pricing 
grid for FICO Classic and VantageScore 4.0 increased regulatory and competitive 
uncertainty for FICO and the credit bureaus. We believe the change creates 
near-term FICO share risk since VantageScore produces higher average scores and 
costs less, which could encourage adoption once both models receive the same 
pricing treatment. Investor feedback skewed negative, with investors citing potential 
FICO share loss, EPS estimate reductions and further multiple compression. The 
FHFA subsequently approved FICO’s Mortgage Direct License Program, which 
expands FICO’s distribution and pricing flexibility, enables performance-based 
pricing and could mitigate pressure from waterfalling and bi-merge adoption. 
Debate remains active given uncertainty around F2027 pricing, FICO 10T timing and 
DLP’s ability to offset VantageScore competition. Separately, August consumer credit 
trends improved, led by prime borrowers, as delinquencies declined across 
bankcard, personal loan and auto categories and originations remained constructive 
outside auto. FDS reported mixed F4Q results, with revenue and EPS above 
consensus offset by below-Street F2027 EPS guidance. AI generated 10%+ of new 
organic ASV, but we expect growth to moderate as competition increases and early 
AI differentiation narrows. Finally, the BLS this morning reported a decline in temp 
staffing penetration with minimal temp headcount growth, reinforcing our cautious 
outlook for RHI.

Americas Business & Information Services: September temp staffing trends remain 
subdued, reinforcing our cautious stance on RHI. US temp staffing conditions 
weakened in September, reinforcing our cautious outlook as penetration approached a 
six-year low and headcount growth remained muted. US temp penetration declined to 
1.56% from 1.57% in August, representing the second-lowest reading in six years and a 
three-standard-deviation decline from 2.09% in February 2022. Temp jobs increased 
just 0.6% y/y, and August headcount was revised 21.8k lower, leaving total temp 
employment of 2,487k well below the January 2020 level of 2,917k. We believe cyclical 
softness is being compounded by structural pressure from AI, with ~55% of US temp 
roles white-collar in nature and increasingly exposed to AI-driven disintermediation.

Americas Business & Information Services: August credit trends show continued 
improvement led by prime consumers. US consumer credit trends improved in August, 
with y/y declines in delinquencies across non-mortgage categories, though lower-tier 
performance remains mixed. The 60+ day bankcard delinquency rate declined to 2.71% 
from 2.73%, personal loan delinquencies decreased to 3.17% from 3.25% and auto loan 
delinquencies fell to 1.32% from 1.54%, with card and auto delinquencies above 
long-term averages. Subprime and near-prime delinquencies increased in cards and

whereas subprime auto delinquencies improved and near-prime auto delinquencies 
were stable. Credit supply remains constructive, with data showing card originations up

Americas Business & Information Services: FHFA approves mortgage DLP, 
expanding FICO’s distribution and pricing flexibility. On 10/1, FHFA Director Bill Pulte 
approved FICO’s Mortgage Direct License Program, enabling mortgage resellers to 
calculate FICO scores using bureau-provided credit report data and pay associated fees 
directly to FICO, or continue sourcing bureau-calculated scores. FICO has signed 
agreements covering ~73% of reseller volume and could reach ~90% if it completes 
negotiations with another large reseller, establishing a meaningful alternative to the 
bureaus’ score calculation and distribution channel. Strategically, DLP enables FICO to 
introduce performance-based pricing that could lower its upfront score cost to a level 
more competitive with VantageScore, mitigating potential market-share pressure, and 
link incremental economics more directly to funded loans. FICO could also avoid 
per-score or per-bureau fees, reducing potential revenue pressure from waterfalling and 
bi-merge adoption. The bureaus remain critical providers of underlying credit data, 
though FICO noted that the bureaus cannot use penalty fees or differential credit file 
pricing to disadvantage reseller-calculated scores. FICO has not disclosed F2027 pricing 
or made pricing commitments to the FHFA Director, and the FHFA has not committed to 
a FICO 10T implementation timeline, though the FHA has confirmed January 1 FICO 10T 
implementation for FHA-insured mortgages. Link to our 10/1 note here.

Americas Business & Information Services: FHFA moves to single LLPA grid for FICO 
Classic and VantageScore 4.0, raising FICO share risk. This afternoon, FHFA Director 
Bill Pulte announced that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will replace separate FICO Classic 
and VantageScore 4.0 mortgage pricing grids with a single grid. No implementation 
timeline was provided. We believe the unified grid creates near-term market share risk 
for FICO Classic since VantageScore 4.0 averaged 13.7 points higher based on a 
September 2026 study by Urban Institute, which could place ~20% more borrowers in 
the highest and cheapest pricing band and increase lenders’ incentive to adopt 
VantageScore. We expect share erosion to be gradual given switching costs, lenders’ 
ability to pass score costs to consumers and continued secondary-market reliance on 
FICO. FICO 10T should restore competitive parity once launched since its scores 
averaged only 1.2 points below VantageScore 4.0 and demonstrated stronger risk 
differentiation. Meanwhile, FHFA’s potential move from tri-merge to bi-merge could 
reduce mortgage revenue at FICO and the credit bureaus, the effects of which could be 
mitigated by continued tri-merge usage, higher pricing and lenders using different 
bureau combinations. We reduce our price targets for FICO, EFX and TRU to reflect 
increased regulatory and competitive uncertainty. Link to our 9/28 note here.

Chart of the Week 
Mortgage rates move back above 7%. The 30-year mortgage rate increased to 7.3% as 
of October 1, reinforcing affordability pressures and limiting a recovery in mortgage 
activity. The current rate compares with a high of nearly 8% in November 2023 and a low

weighing on both refinance and purchase volumes. Lower mortgage activity is a negative 
read-through for FICO and the credit bureaus since it reduces mortgage-related 
credit-score and credit-file transaction volumes.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
