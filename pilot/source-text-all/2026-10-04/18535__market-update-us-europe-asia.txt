U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.19% | Nasdaq +0.04% | Dow +0.04%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher in Thursday trading, ending a bit off best levels, as the market started Q4 on a stronger note. The equal-weight
S&P outperformed the cap-weighted index but is still on track for its seventh straight weekly decline. Semiconductor and memory stocks
fared well while big tech was mixed. Outperformers included energy, software, networking/communications, tech hardware, E&Cs, multis,
trucking, healthcare distributors (MCK-US), P&C insurers, payments, retail/apparel, homebuilders and auto suppliers; laggards included
larger-cap banks, life insurers, managed care, pharma/biotech, MedTech, media, casinos, HPCs and food. Treasuries were firmer with
the curve steepening, and short-end yields were down 6-9 bp; 2s had their strongest day since mid-July after a 72 bp run-up in yields for
Q3. Long-end yields moved to fresh 24-year highs after the September ISM manufacturing report reflected continued cost pressures, but
then retreated, and the day's volatility was consistent with recent attention on the divergence between an elevated MOVE index and a
flattish VIX. The dollar index was up 0.6%, gold finished up 0.4%, silver was up 1.0% and bitcoin futures were up 1.3%. WTI crude settled
up 2.7%, near session highs, after reports more troops are being sent to the Mideast; yields provided some offset for higher oil amid

Macro

Middle East buildup and diesel: The Pentagon is deploying a third aircraft-carrier strike group and additional Marine Corps assets, adding
9K to 10K troops to the Middle East by late November, according to media reports. Trump told Time Magazine on Thursday he could
resume strikes after the midterms if no deal is in place, following the rejection of a ceasefire proposal that would have reopened key oil
transit routes; Axios reported this week's talks with Qatari mediators have yielded little progress, and both sides increasingly see renewed
military conflict as more likely. A Reuters source said Iran has received the US response to its proposal to revive the ceasefire; the two
sides mostly agree on the steps but sequencing remains the sticking point. Iran's foreign minister said Iran is open to the return of nuclear
inspectors in exchange for sanctions relief. According to Kpler, Hormuz seven-day crude volumes are averaging 13.5M bpd, matching pre-
war levels, and flows through Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline have recovered to around 5.5M bpd, while Iran loaded no crude onto
tankers in September. Refined fuels are the greater concern, with Hormuz seven-day average refined volumes of 677K bpd compared
with 3.6M bpd pre-conflict. Trump said he is still thinking about a ban on US diesel exports but signalled skepticism after warning it could
hurt gasoline prices, and the administration is asking Germany and France to draw down their own diesel inventories or face US export
restrictions. China is suspending exports of refined products, though it is unclear whether this is a short-term move over a holiday period.

Treasury yields at multi-decade highs: US Treasury yields continue to mark new highs, with the longer end of the curve at its highest since
2002 and the 10Y now above 5.30%, up over 55 bp since the start of September. Cited drivers include higher global rates, fiscal deficits
and debt, yen and BoJ dynamics, global oil and energy product prices and competition for capital from AI borrowing, despite cooler inflation
data (core PCE) and a flatter near-term Fed hike path. Goldman Sachs noted the 1m real-rate move on the 10Y is among the worst since
the 2013 Taper Tantrum, exceeding the 2 standard deviation mark that has historically led to an equity drawdown of over 4% over the
next month, though its economists argued rates are not much of a brake on growth because financial conditions are little changed on net
since January. Bloomberg's index showed global bonds had their worst quarter since late 2024, the FT reported Treasuries posted their
worst month in four years, and the average 30Y fixed-rate mortgage jumped to 7.28%, the highest since Nov-23.

Fedspeak leans cautious: Following the cooler August core PCE reading, Governor Jefferson said the Fed "may take more time" to decide
its next rate move, adding that inflation remains above target with upside risks. Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari said inflation remains too
high and it is uncertain how high rates need to go, while Boston's Collins and Kansas City's Schmid said more work is needed on inflation,
with energy prices a key challenge. Governor Bowman said there is no urgent need for more rate moves this year. A September recap
noted the Fed's first rate hike in three years triggered more hawkish-leaning takeaways.

Data: ISM prices jump, labor market subdued: September ISM Manufacturing edged down to 54.5 versus 55.0 consensus and August's
54.6. New Orders rose 1.6 points m/m to 55.3, Backlog of Orders jumped 4.6 points to 56.4, Employment increased 1.5 points to 52.7
and Prices jumped 6.8 points to 77.9, returning to levels seen at the start of the Iran war. Final September S&P Manufacturing PMI came
in at 55.9 versus 57.0 consensus. Initial claims were 197K versus 200K consensus and the prior week's revised 198K, and continuing
claims were 1701K versus 1730K consensus. Challenger reported 43,281 US cuts in September, down 18% m/m and 20% y/y, while
September hiring plans fell 23% y/y to the lowest September total since 2011. September nonfarm payrolls are due Friday (2-Oct, 8:30
ET), with headline consensus of +94K versus August's +162K, private payrolls of +89.5K, annual average hourly earnings expected up
0.1 pp to 3.2%, and the unemployment rate expected to hold at 4.1%. Previews show wide dispersion, including Jefferies +175K and
Deutsche Bank +45K.

Micron, Gemini 4 and Anthropic IPO: Micron's fiscal Q4 revenue beat by nearly 6% and guidance exceeded consensus by just over 7% at
the midpoint. More than 75% of 2027 output is already committed, Strategic Customer Agreements increased to 26 from 16 and buybacks
are set to resume in early December, though the company flagged a high bar and scrutiny around durability versus peak, the capex ramp
and slowing pricing momentum. Google released Gemini 4 Aragon, which it claims matches or beats Anthropic and OpenAI in areas in-
cluding coding and cyber security, after skipping the earlier Gemini 3.5 Pro; Alphabet shares are down ~15% since mid-May. Bloomberg
reported Anthropic is targeting a mid-November IPO at a valuation between $1.8T and $2T, with formal marketing starting as soon as the
week of 9-Nov; Reuters reported it posted a $42B net loss in 2025 and revenue grew 12x to $4.6B.

EU Market Recap
Germany -1.03% | France -1.62% | UK -1.68%
Synopsis

European equity markets ended sharply lower, near worst levels, following a downbeat Wednesday. Technology, industrials and autos
were the relative outperformers, while financials and healthcare lagged. The FTSE and Stoxx 600 both posted three-month lows before
recovering, and September was the first monthly decline in the past six months. Sentiment was downbeat, with the focus on the backup
in yields; European benchmarks are at multi-year highs, and press highlighted drivers including resilient growth, higher oil, upside inflation
risks, hawkish monetary policy expectations, a ramp in AI-related issuance, fiscal deficit concerns and forced selling by hedge funds. Gilts
and Bunds were steady overall. The dollar index was up 0.5%, gold was flat, silver was up 0.6% and bitcoin futures were up 0.8%. WTI
crude was up 2.3%, with the lack of diplomatic traction in the Middle East still seemingly overshadowing better flow data. On the macro
front, UK Nationwide house price data underperformed expectations and followed a weak BoE mortgage approvals update on Wednesday,
Swiss inflation was also on the agenda, and the final manufacturing PMI in the Eurozone was revised up slightly while the UK reading was
revised lower. ECB President Lagarde said she did not rule out leaving her role a few months before her term ends, and UK PM Burnham
said rejoining the EU is an option for the UK. On the corporate front, European chipmakers benefitted from MU-US results, SAN-FR ex-
panded its Regeneron immunology alliance, COLR-BE lowered FY26/27 operating profit guidance at its AGM, FRE-DE agreed to acquire
the remaining 45% of mAbxience for up to €750M, and MBG-DE plans a new voluntary layoff round from December.
Macro

Energy, diesel and eurozone inflation: Oddo flagged September exports from major Gulf producers at over 80% of pre-war levels, and
Goldman Sachs, including "dark" barrels, sees total Persian Gulf flows back at 23.3 mb/d. Refined products have not followed: Goldman
said Gulf diesel, gasoline and jet are still about half of last year's rate, and Middle East diesel shipments to Europe are heading for a six-
year low (SPGlobal). The IEA's Birol said Europe is the most exposed diesel importer heading into winter, EU Energy chief Jørgensen said
the EU has spent more than €100B extra on energy imports since the Iran war, and Oxford Economics warned a US export curb could lift
European wholesale diesel by 40-50%. For the ECB, a crude recovery is disinflationary at a one-year horizon, but near-term inflation and
household expectations are set by diesel, heating oil and freight, so the bar for further cuts may remain higher. September inflation was
hot in Germany (3.3% versus 3.1% expected and 2.9% in August), France (HICP 3.4% from 2.6%), Italy (4.1% versus 3.3% prior) and Spain
(5.0%, highest since Feb-2023). For Friday's eurozone flash HICP, consensus sees 3.3% headline and 2.5% core, while Goldman Sachs
lifted its forecast to 3.8% headline and 2.5% core; another 3.7%+ headline print would keep an October hike in play. ECB's Schnabel said
the focus is on second-round effects. Swiss inflation accelerated to 1.0% from 0.8%, as expected and the highest in two years, with core
CPI at 0.5% versus 0.4%; the SNB is widely expected to leave policy unchanged through 2027.

France, the UK budget and political risk: France's 2027 budget reached parliament with the OAT-Bund spread at 126 bps, up 40 bps since
mid-September, and inflation unexpectedly at 3% in September. PM Lecornu said France is borrowing at 4.8%, a rate not seen since 2008,
and public debt is near a record €3.6T. The draft claims €54B of "efforts" and aims to hold the deficit at 5%, with about 40% from higher
taxes and levies and 60% from spending cuts, including €5.5B on pensions. Non-residents hold 57% of French debt, and UBS still favours
shorting 10-year France against EU bonds, with Moody's review on 23-Oct the next test. In the UK, the 28-Oct budget will show how much
the government still needs to borrow, and Chancellor Healey is expected to address high street taxes with a tax break, after BRC analysis
found retail and hospitality pay the equivalent of 72p and 82p in business taxes for every £1 of pre-tax profit. German Chancellor Merz
said Europe is bracing for major Russian hybrid attacks on NATO soil, and Ukraine's funding gap remains a European fiscal and defense
story (Politico).

UK activity and financial stability: The Institute of Directors' measure of business optimism over UK economic prospects fell to -54 in
September from -49, following the Lloyds Bank business barometer at a 17-month low; 65% of respondents were pessimistic and more
than 70% plan to freeze or cut investment over the coming year. The Nationwide house price index fell 0.2% m/m versus consensus 0.0%,
with prices up 0.8% y/y versus consensus 1.3%, the weakest annual growth rate since December 2025. The BoE FPC warned the chances
of financial risks occurring simultaneously have increased since July, citing renewed Middle East uncertainty, vulnerabilities in sovereign
debt markets and a rapid increase in AI-related debt issuance, while keeping its view that the financial system is resilient and noting
hedge fund positioning in the Gilt market remains elevated. EY forecast UK bank lending growth of 2.9% this year before falling to a three-
year low of 2.2% next year.

UK politics and utilities: UK PM Burnham said a future Labour manifesto could promise a new in-out referendum on the EU, though not
now, and pledged to explore closer ties with the bloc at a summit later this year; options range from staying as is to a Canada-style
relationship or rejoining the customs union. JPMorgan remains positive on UK utilities after his speech, seeing the new public networks
vehicle GB Grid, with initial funding of ~£4B against over £80B of power-network capex over the next five years, as too small to crowd out
listed operators; it remains overweight SSE-GB, CNA-GB, NG-GB and DRX-GB and neutral on water names.

Sector calls: chips and bank deposits: ASML-NL, ASM-NL, BESI-NL and IFX-DE were among the top gainers on the Micron read-across;
Oddo said the results confirm the AI memory supercycle is intact and sees a positive read-across for European equipment, while UBS is
constructive on ASML, ASM and STMMI-IT and BofA prefers Infineon and STMicro. Banks were on course for their largest one-day decline
since 8-Jul as yields rose, UBS-CH faced a call from Artisan Partners to leave Switzerland over stricter capital requirements, and Partners
Group's Global Value Fund reportedly told investors it will not meet redemption requests in June, July or August. Citigroup argued fears
that AI agents will drain EU bank deposits are overstated, while Apollo's Slok warned agents could sweep household cash from checking
accounts paying about 0.1% into accounts paying 3.3-5%.

Asian Markets
Japan +3.30% | Hong Kong closed (holiday) | China closed (holiday)
Synopsis

Asia equities ended mostly higher on Thursday, with the Kospi +1.95% and Taiex +0.86%, though markets were mixed overall: Australia's
ASX underperformed and fell 2% to a more than three-month low, there were small losses in India and Southeast Asia, and Greater China
was closed for a holiday. Japan's Nikkei 225 was sharply higher as its chip stocks rallied, and the Topix was also higher. Kioxia (285A-
JP), Advantest (6857-JP) and Tokyo Electron (8035-JP) all surged following Micron's strong outlook statement after the close on Wall
Street, while Samsung Electronics (005930-KR), SK Hynix (000660-KR) and TSMC (2330-TW) were all higher on the same report. There
was no definitive catalyst for the ASX decline, though local press cited the lack of chip exposure and recent pressure from yields, inflation
and the threat of another RBA rate hike. The US dollar strengthened, with the DXY index closing in on 102, and Asia currencies were weak;
JGB and other Asia sovereign yields were higher, crude oil futures were higher with Brent nearing $100/bl once again, precious metals
were stronger but off highs, and base metals were mixed. South Korea's September exports rose more than 100% y/y to a record high
$120.9B on global demand for AI products, regional PMIs showed Asia's factories still expanding almost everywhere, and President Trump
announced $200B in investments from South Korea, although local news was skeptical, saying only the Texas power plant had been
agreed. The BOJ's summary of opinions said the bank's policy has shifted to preventing inflation from overshooting its target, pivoting
from a 13-year policy of gradually lifting price growth, and the BOJ Tankan survey for September was slightly softer than expected. China's
finance minister Lan said Beijing is mulling additional fiscal policies to reach its own annual growth target. Nidec (6594-JP) shares fell
sharply again after its auditor declined to give an opinion on its financial statements, and the company confirmed the sale of its Compo-
nents unit to Carlyle Group for ¥103B. PHC Holdings (6523-JP) confirmed it had received a takeover proposal from Toho Holdings (8129-
JP) but has not decided on its merits, SoftBank (9984-JP) said it had completed the final $10.0B tranche of funding into OpenAI paid for
by proceeds from a foreign currency-denominated bond sale, and Tencent (700-HK) struck a deal to lease 100K chips from Oracle via its
Southeast Asia datacenters to accelerate its AI program. SK Hynix said it has not decided on the future of its Solidigm unit following
reports earlier this week that it was looking to list it.
Macro

BOJ signals fall short of a consecutive hike: The latest BOJ signals were notably dovish, with the yen down some 0.5% against major
crosses. Reuters discussed the mixed picture from the Tankan, which dampened prospects for a back-to-back rate hike in October, and
Nikkei's intraday yen wraps cited views that the Summary of Opinions for the September meeting was not hawkish enough to signal a
follow-up hike. The headline large manufacturers business conditions DI was 24 in September versus consensus 25 (22 in June, the sixth
straight improvement), the large nonmanufacturing index declined to 35 from 37 versus consensus 36, and large firms' FY capex projec-

Asia manufacturing and the Korea deal: S&P Global PMI readings showed few signs of a meaningful slowdown, with South Korea at 53.9
and Taiwan at 56.7, their strongest expansion in new orders, new export orders and output since the early recovery from the pandemic.
The ASEAN index was 52.1 from 52.3, with Thailand at a nine-month high 54.3 and Indonesia at 52.4 from 49.8. Trump declared $200B
in US investments from South Korea, the first set of major projects under the $350B package committed last year, comprising $120B for
eight large-scale nuclear power plants, $54B for an LNG Alaska pipeline and $22.3B for a 6 GW gas power project in Texas; Seoul has
only confirmed the Texas project as finalized, and the broader agreement tied the investments to US tariffs falling from 25% to 15%. For

the cash rate, with the market fully pricing in another by early 2027. In India, Nomura, Deutsche Bank and ANZ expect the RBI to raise
rates by 25 bp on 7-Oct, its first increase since Feb-23, after a record $133B FCNR (B) inflow between June and August left banks awash
with cash. The rupee is down 6.8% YTD, 5Y IGBs capped their worst quarter in more than four years, and India stocks are set for eight

policy change in 11 years. Rebates for EV battery exporters were reduced to 6% from 9% in April and will be eliminated in January, and
the 10% levy waived for NEVs from 2014 through 2025 was raised to 5% in January and is due to increase to 10% in January 2028;
domestic NEV sales already fell 10% in H1. Chinese President Xi said the economy is resilient and urged efforts to reach full-year targets,
and the White House is taking steps to slow export licensing for aircraft parts to China as it seeks to add pressure (Reuters).

supervision' in a step towards eventual delisting.
