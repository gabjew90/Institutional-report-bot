Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Cycle risks rising: Wall Street and Main Street 
A consistent theme from our meetings across Europe this week has been that investors 
remain structurally constructive on U.S. (and EU) banks but are becoming more cautious 
around the next leg of the cycle. Discussion centered on all things AI, ranging from 
whether the recent slowdown in capital markets activity reflects delay rather than 
destruction to the disruption risks versus productivity opportunities for the sector.  BofA 
Research: Reading the Tea Leaves: Peak Main Street? Peak Wall Street? 
Rates, AI break the wrong way... Elections watched 
Two of the three themes we identified as critical drivers of bank stock performance 
have broken the wrong way in recent weeks. Interest rates have moved sharply higher 
since mid-August (coinciding with the YTD high in the bank stock index), while AI has 
once again shifted from a tailwind to a headwind as IPO activity slowed and disruption 
risks re-emerged. Elections remain the third variable hanging over the group. BofA 
Research: Earnings & Beyond: AI, Rates & Elections…what matters for bank stocks 
Rates: How many hikes are we talking about... 
The key question is whether this represents a mid-cycle rate adjustment (1-2 additional 
hikes) or the beginning of a more prolonged tightening cycle. The latter would likely 
amplify concerns around the sustainability of growth and earnings expectations. While 
the 2s/10s curve has bounced over the last two days, a return to inversion would 
inevitably revive recession concerns, given its long-standing reputation as a harbinger of 
economic downturns. Oil prices could prove to be the critical swing factor, influencing 
inflation expectations, Fed policy assumptions and ultimately bank valuations.  BofA 
Research: Reading the Tea Leaves: Beginning of the end? / Interest Rates Matter: Bear 
Flattening and Implications for Bank Stocks 
AI: Financing opportunity back to disruption risk 
From EPS tailwinds tied to financing AI-capex, the debate has shifted to focusing on 
delayed IPOs, questions around AI capex durability (pushback on data centers, risks tied 
to rogue AI agents) and renewed concerns regarding disruption risk from Agentic AI. 
Investors with whom we’ve spoken remain broadly optimistic on the long-term 
implications, with banks viewed as among the biggest eventual beneficiaries. The 
challenge is in pinning down when those benefits begin showing up in earnings and 
other key operating metrics. BofA Research: Reading the Tea Leaves: Musing: Watch 
deposit betas, not app downloads 
Our View: Too soon to declare an end to the cycle 
The bad news is that both rates and AI have moved in the wrong direction over the last 
six weeks. The good news is that both headwinds, for now, appear reversible. What 
changes the narrative? Greater confidence that the Fed is not entering a prolonged 
tightening cycle. Stabilization in energy prices and inflation expectations. Evidence that 
AI capex, and investor appetite to finance it, remains durable. If these pieces begin 
falling into place, bank stocks look increasingly ripe for a bounce. Continued on page 2.

Home is where the heart is... but valuation matters 
Many investors with whom we spoke acknowledged that the best long-term AI, 
productivity and capital markets stories remain in the U.S. At the same time, relative 
valuation support, less demanding expectations and fewer perceived AI-related risks 
make European financials increasingly attractive. Canadian banks also came up 
repeatedly given their defensive characteristics and improving visibility around the 
idiosyncratic "investment supercycle" story. That said, premium valuations have long 
limited their appeal among global financials investors. BofA Research: Canada Banks Day 
Takeaways: Supercycle Ahead, but Watch for Speed Bumps 
Stock ideas: JPMorgan top idea, money centers preferred 
We continue to favor quality franchises with greater leverage to higher rates, 
idiosyncratic drivers of growth and profitability improvement and limited AOCI 
(accumulated other comprehensive income) risk that could impede flexibility around 
capital return.  
Within the money centers, we see the most attractive risk/reward in our top pick 
JPMorgan (JPM), followed by Citigroup (C) and Wells Fargo (WFC). Goldman Sachs (GS) 
and Morgan Stanley (MS) are likely to remain challenged until evidence emerges of a 
sustained pickup in capital markets activity. Trust banks remain well positioned given 
higher rates, stable-to-rising asset prices and issuance activity, with BNY our preferred 
idea. Among regionals, we highlight Fifth Third (FITB), Citizens (CFG), PNC Financial 
(PNC), East West (EWBC) and Frost (CFR).  
Stock valuations undemanding is EPS estimates hold with the group trading in a tight 
range, 10-11x 2027 P/E, vs average annual EPS growth of ~10% in 2027 and 2028.

Price objective basis & risk 
BNY (BNY) 
Our $175 PO is based on 50% FY27e P/E and 50% mid-year 27 P/TBV. Assign 17.0x to 
FY27e EPS, 4.6x mid-year 27 TBV. vs. the bank's 5 year pre pandemic median of 
13.1x/3.1x given an attractive combination of a capital light, high ROE business with 
limited credit risk and sticky client relationships. 
 
Risks to the upside is stronger equity/bond markets. Risks to the downside are a severe 
selloff in equity/bond markets that that could put downward pressure on fee growth and 
M&A that could temper capital return. 
Citigroup Inc. (C) 
Our $176 PO is based on 50% FY27e P/E and 50% mid-year 27 P/TBV. Assign 14.0x to 
FY27e EPS, 1.6x mid-year 27 TBV. These multiples are above historical averages (10x 
P/E, 1.0x P/TBV) given our expectations for an acceleration in earnings growth. 
 
Downside risks to our PO are execution risk tied to management's franchise 
transformation efforts, an economic downturn or a macro-economic shock, increased 
costs tied to the regulatory consent orders, regulatory changes. Faster pace of share 
buybacks, better-than-expected operating leverage. 
Citizens Financial Group (CFG) 
Our $82 PO is based on 50% FY27e P/E and 50% Mid-year 27e P/TBV. Assign 12.5x to 
FY27e EPS, 1.9x Mid-year 27e TBV. vs. the bank's 5 year pre-pandemic median of 
13.2x/1.2x given reduced uncertainty surrounding EPS/ROTCE outlooks. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: a greater than expected decline in interest rates (FF sub-2.5%) 
that would weigh on our NIM forecast, deterioration in the macro-economic outlook that 
raises concerns around a credit cycle and would temper appetite to own regional banks. 
Cullen/Frost Bankers Inc (CFR) 
Our $176 PO is based on 50% FY27e P/E and 50% Mid-year 27e P/TBV. Assign 16.0x to 
FY27e EPS, 2.5x Mid-year 27e TBV, above the bank's 5 year pre-pandemic medians of 
15.3x/2.3x. Downside risks to our PO are worse-than-expected credit losses, greater-
than-expected net interest margin compression, and slower-than-expected economic 
growth. Upside risks to our PO are credit resilience, better-than-expected margin 
performance, and stronger-than-expected economic growth. 
East West Bancorp, Incorporated (EWBC)
