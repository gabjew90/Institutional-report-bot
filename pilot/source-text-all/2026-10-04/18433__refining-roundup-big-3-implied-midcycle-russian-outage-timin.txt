Big 3 pricing in $16-17/bbl long term midcycle margin, +$3 
Following our recent valuation note which estimated that VLO was pricing in $16-17/bbl 
on midcycle crack in the 2029-50 period (+$3-4/bbl higher vs pre-war, we think), we 
have done similar analyses for the other 2 large independent refiners MPC and PSX, and 
reached a similar conclusion. To reach current valuations for the Big 3, the FCF from the 
crack forward curve step up from 2026-28 explains ~55-70% of the market share gains, 
and backing into the required long term midcycle for the remaining 30-40% suggests a 
+$3/bbl midcycle crack, about $16-17/bbl vs the long-term average of $13/bbl. This 
stepped up midcycle crack is suggesting, perhaps, that some of the downed 
Russia/Middle East capacity never comes back which we explore in our second topic… 
..Russian refinery drone-related downtime lasts ~1mo 
Russia has 32 major refineries and 6.5mbd of capacity. In recent months, due to drone 
strikes, refinery output has fallen by 30%, or 2-2.5mbd offline. YTD, the IEA estimates a 
refinery was hit once every three days. Some refineries, particularly those near Ukraine, 
have now been attacked up to 15 times since 2022 – only 5 of Russia’s refineries have 
remained untouched by Ukrainian drones (all in the East). We examine Bloomberg data 
showing that the majority of hit capacity (for which there are clear outage/restart 
records) returns in under a month. Most of the 2-2.5mbd down currently has been 
recently struck, likely able to return in a few weeks. For some refineries, e.g. one in 
Moscow, repairs and outage could last into 2027. While it’s of course hard to tell when 
the war will ease and the strikes will end, the fact that the vast majority of Russian 
refining capacity offline at any given time has been hit in the last few weeks buffers our 
view that midcycle margin post 2029 has probably not moved up materially and we 
reiterate hesitance to buy into a +$3/bbl LT midcycle crack valuation.  
Could any US refineries really come back? Or demolished? 
The White House is reportedly weighing the use of the Defense Production Act to 
expand capacity, one angle being restarting shut-down US refineries. Of the 11 major 
refineries closed/retrofitted since 2020 (a total of 1.8mbd of CDU capacity), we 
estimate that 6 (VLO Benicia, PSX LA, MPC Martinez, PSX Rodeo, MPC Dickinson, DINO 
Cheyenne) still have the equipment in place to even contemplate a crude tpt restart 
(some still produce diesel, with bio feed). The others have demolished major equipment 
such as CDUs, FCCs or cokers. How long could this take? The restart of the Limetree Bay 
refinery, such as it was, is an informative case study – shut down in 2012 on mgn 
pressure / Clean Air Act penalties, announced in Nov 2018 plans to reopen by YE 2019. 
This was delayed to Jan 2021 (for a total of ~2+ yrs after announcement though 
including the pandemic). However the refinery had to shut down again, ultimately 
permanently, after six months due to a fire/pollution issues. This suggests 1.5-2 years to 
restart a refinery with all its equipment is probably possible, but that the longer it idles 
the harder it may be to bring back. 
PO small adjustments: Raise VLO to $414, MPC to $426 
We slightly raise our VLO PO from $395 to $414 and MPC from $410 to $426 on small 
positive adjustments to long term refinery capture, supported by recent widening in 
heavy crude discounts.

Price objective basis & risk 
Marathon Petroleum Corporation (MPC) 
Our $426 price objective is based on a DCF analysis of sustaining FCF built up from 
segment EBITDA models, accounting for demand declines in Refining and growth in 
Midstream. The forecast period extends out to 2050, and we use a 10% cost of equity 
(average of Big 3 refiners). 
 
Downside risks: 1) crack spreads deteriorate, causing R&M segment performance to 
weaken and the stock to underperform, 2) EBITDA / distribution growth at MPLX slows, 
which implies slowing MPC dividend capacity growth, 3) L48 gathering activity slows 
which also is negative for Midstream outlook, 4) buybacks slow due to falling FCF, 5) 
cash draws, implying that the buyback is unsustainable, 6) geopolitical or macro demand 
risks 
 
Upside risks: 1) crack spreads rise, 2) Midstream EBITDA growth / distribution growth 
beats, 3) buybacks and net debt improve or beat 
Phillips 66 (PSX) 
Our $265 price objective is based on DCF analysis on Sustaining FCF aggregated from 
forecasts of individual segments' EBITDA. EBITDA forecasts account for a variety of 
demand forecasts, sector-wide and covered company production cost curves, and 
government policy guidance. The model's forecast period goes out to 2050, and we use 
a 10% cost of equity. 
 
Downside risks: 1) higher cost of equity in our DCF framework, 2) falling refined product 
margins, 3) disappointing petchems margins, 4) falling Marketing/Retail peer transaction 
/ market multiples, 5) lack of demand for LCFS / RINs credits as set by government 
policy as it pertains to the Renewables segment, 6) buybacks and dividend growth slow 
going forward. 
 
Upside risks: Asset sales surprise to the upside, segment market margins surprise to the 
upside, Chems outlook improves, Midstream outlook improves, Refining tpt gains and 
integration into other segments drives margins higher due to operating leverage. 
Valero Energy Corp. (VLO) 
Our $414 price objective is based on a DCF analysis sustaining FCF built up from 
forecasts of segment EBITDA. We use a 10% cost of equity (average of Big 3 refiners), 
and forecast segment EBITDA/FCF out to 2050. VLO is a lead beneficiary of widening 
heavy crude price differentials, which drives up FCF generation in our DCF model. 
 
Downside risks: 1) crack spreads decline and drive down margins / estimate revisions / 
share price, 2) differentials narrow which hurts VLO's complex refineries in particular, 3) 
policy risk around fossil fuels but also renewable diesel and ethanol, 4) capital returns 
disappoint. 
 
Upside risks: 1) crack spreads rise, causing outperformance vs diversified peers, 3) diffs
