The Four Ps of the AI Economy 
A fascinating week lies ahead with much of the focus upon the four Ps of the AI economy—power, profits, policy and productivity. 
The 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) Leaders’ Meeting will unfold in Shenzhen, China from Wednesday through Thursday 
and may be the week’s key source of market risk. Trade and technology will be top of mind and particularly in terms of whether US-China 
relations continue to stabilize, further de-escalate tensions, or spark renewed escalation of tensions. The potential spy scandal at Nvidia 
may create US-China tensions and a heightened sense of rivalry. Canadian PM Carney will also be in attendance with possible 
developments in US-Canada and China-Canada relations.  
A big week for tech will extend beyond policy spies and policy risks. Nvidia’s earnings (Tuesday) and Anthropic’s IPO pricing on Wednesday 
and first day of trading on Thursday may extend tech jitters. A common pattern involves getting in and out of IPOs fairly quickly. 
No firm answers are expected as the Federal Reserve’s task forces begin their public communications this week. Canadian CPI, a pair of 
central bank decisions (BI, SARB), FOMC minutes, a post-CPI BoC speech, and a round of international data across the US, UK, Australia and 
Latin America will make for an active calendar. 
Also shared are views on whether the BoC will hike again in December, the risk that the FOMC upsizes rate hikes in December, and where 
current thinking sits on AI’s impact upon productivity and jobs in the US. Once again, tech influences will be a recurring theme this week. 
CANADIAN INFLATION—UNLIKELY TO BE A TREND BUSTER 
Canada reports CPI for October on Monday and producer price figures for the same month on Thursday. It’s the last pair of inflation 
readings before the Bank of Canada’s December 9th decision and could be impactful. 
I’ve roughly estimated a rise of 0.4% m/m NSA that would lift the year-over-year rate to 3.3% 
from 3.1%. If this estimate is in the right ballpark, then it would be roughly compatible with 
the Bank of Canada’s smoothed quarterly projection that expects CPI to 3.4% y/y in Q4 from 
3% in Q3.  
After seasonal adjustment, CPI would be estimated to rise by a similar 0.4% m/m SA. 
Key, however, will be measures of core inflation. 
It would take a lot to alter the recent smoothed trend in underlying inflation. The three-
month moving averages for traditional core CPI (ex-food and energy), weighted median CPI 
and trimmed mean CPI have been 2.6%, 2.7% and 2.6% respectively. Traditional core was 
3%+ for four straight months until it slowed to 1.5% in September. The other two measures 
have been above 2% in each of the past three months. The year-over-year TM and WM 
measures are not helpful since they turn very slowly around inflation shocks, given they are 
calculated as m/m weighted, compounded price changes summed up over a year. 
It’s unclear that the Bank of Canada will be so data sensitive that these figures will matter to 
the December 9th decision. It is forecast oriented as argued in the section on whether December is a ‘go’ for the BoC.  
As for producer prices, key is the trend in core industrial prices—ex-food and energy—that have been on a strong upswing as a leading 
indicator of core CPI inflation (chart 1). This is part of our narrative for volatile but persistent gains in underlying core consumer price 
inflation. 
BANK OF CANADA—IS DECEMBER A ‘GO’? 
I still lean toward the BoC going in December but will offer the following arguments for a skip and a hike to 2.5% while flagging several 
uncertainties that will be monitored. There is no doubt in my mind that they should hike again in December. The arguments for a pause 
invoke a very low bar set in favour of mild, backward-looking data disappointments that are tangential to the inflation mandate.

A BoC skip case: 
• 
Oil is down $15-20 from the peak in terms of WTI and Brent while Western Canada Select is about $23 off the peak. That means less 
income being imported into the country than at the peak and hence less trickle down through activity readings. 
• 
Jobs have disappointed for three months with about 115k lost jobs. 
• 
Recent GDP has disappointed, both in terms of monthly and quarterly tracking. Instead of around 2%+ growth in the third quarter, we 
think we’re tracking about 1½%. 
• 
The BoC said in the October statement that they would 'proceed carefully' and some take this to mean policy adjustments may not be 
in a straight line as the BoC adopts a gradual pace. 
A BoC hike case: 
• 
Inflation, inflation, inflation. All measures are either well above the 2% target—like total 
CPI over 3% y/y—or roughly on 2%—like the main core gauges in y/y terms. In month-
over-month terms, all of these measures have trended toward being well above the 
BoC’s 2% inflation target at annualized rates (chart 2). Let’s see Monday’s update.  
• 
Getting wigged out after just a quarter point hike would do nothing to contain 
inflationary pressures into 2027. A pause after meaningful tightening may be 
appropriate. 
• 
It's not Warsh. The BoC's forecasts call for a sustained overshoot of their inflation target 
and there is a high bar for a handful of backward-looking short-term readings to knock 
them off track. 
• 
The BoC’s refined five-year framework agreement placed sole emphasis upon inflation 
control and downplayed the prior agreement’s references to fully inclusive maximum 
employment. 
• 
Oil is still massively higher on the year with lagging cost pass through ahead. WCS 
remains almost US$20 higher than at the start of the year while Brent is about $35 higher and WTI is over $30 higher. The Brent 
futures curve has moved only a little lower through 2027 than where it was on October 15th—the date stamp used for the BoC’s 
forecast assumptions in the October MPR 
(chart 3). Future risks to oil prices are 
uncertain but we’re sceptical toward 
prospects for a sustained abatement of war 
pressures. Tight refining capacity will also 
keep energy prices high for the foreseeable 
future.  
• 
The FOMC remains on track to hike in 
December. The Fed-BoC policy rate spread is 
already historically wide (chart 4). To not 
follow the Fed given this starting point would 
widen the negative hot-money disadvantage 
to holding CAD and therefore potentially add 
to imported inflation. USDCAD has recently 
depreciated back to 1.425.  
• 
Commodities still support investment and spending into 2027 and not just energy prices. 
• 
There are more costs to be passed through than just oil and other commodities.

NO WIDESPREAD SUPPORT AT THE FED FOR UPSIZING 
Federal Reserve Governor Waller was responsible for spiking short-term market yields around policy expectations into the December 
meeting when he said the following just before the latest CPI release for October: 
“But if the October CPI inflation data out later today are similar to the past two monthly readings—with core CPI inflation around 0.3 
percent or even higher—then a stronger policy move may be needed. In that case, I believe a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be 
considered at the December meeting. Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk, a risk I am not eager to take.” 
Core CPI did indeed land at 0.3%, causing markets to begin pricing not only a full quarter-point hike in December but a chance at 50bps. 
Nevertheless, the Fed’s preferred core PCE inflation measure is tracking on the bubble between 0.2% and 0.3% m/m SA and we’ll find out 
on November 25th. Adjusting core CPI for weighting differences and adding an estimated flat contribution from relevant categories of the 
producer price index reveals a figure closer to 0.2%. 
Does Waller have company for upsizing? We’ll never know what Chair Warsh thinks because he prefers the silent treatment, but so far, it 
seems as though Waller is on his own in terms of upsizing. There is still a significant amount of data, geopolitical and market risk ahead 
before the next decision, but at this point it seems that markets are overpricing the policy rate path in the very near term. 
• 
Dallas President Logan (voting) supports at least 50bps of further tightening and possibly “more rapidly this winter” but did not weigh 
in on upsizing. 
• 
Governor Bowman (voting) argued further tightening is needed but that the drivers of inflation should abate in 2027. 
• 
Governor Barr (voting) said nothing directly on monetary policy, and instead spoke about regulations, how AI is driving inequality etc. 
• 
NY Fed President Williams (voting) said “We need to take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2% goal in 
a timely manner, and on a sustained basis.” He warned about inflation, would seem to support hiking in December, but “over time” 
suggests a gradual pace versus upsizing on the size and pace of tightening.

THE FED’S PRODUCTIVITY AND JOBS TASK FORCE—STILL MORE QS THAN AS 
The first public glimmer of progress on Fed Chair Warsh’s five task forces may begin to arrive on Monday. Recall that he announced the task 
forces at the June meeting. That’s when the San Francisco Fed hosts the ‘Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference’ which is designed 
to examine the impact of new technologies—like AI—on the labour market.  
Chair Warsh will deliver opening remarks that are to last 15 minutes and then it’s onto panels 
with the agenda available (here). The event is to be live streamed from the same link if you’ve 
got 3–4 hours to spare. I’ll likely run it in the background notwithstanding the low likelihood of 
market effects. The tone of the conference is likely to further distance Warsh from his earlier 
dovish remarks on AI’s possible influences that are now moot. 
Warsh had set this date back on October 28th during his press conference following the 
decision to hold perhaps not coincidentally just days before midterms. At the time, he teased 
that the hearing would invite a broad range of people and address matters like whether the 
US is in the midst of a productivity miracle or whether it may happen slowly in future. He 
noted that the task force requested this particular event to cast a wider net in terms of 
opinions. That could be good, or a sign they’re unlikely to reveal any great strides forward in 
our understanding of the matter. My hunch is the latter as the hype has oversold the ability to 
generate answers.

is AI-driven or not is likely a stretch at this point, though AI-related investment remains on a

CENTRAL BANKS—DRAW, PARDNER  
Two regional central banks will offer updated policy stances alongside sundry other communications from others, notably including FOMC 
minutes and a BoC speech. 
Bank Indonesia—I Love a Good Western 
Indonesia’s central bank has earned a reputation for being a bit of a wild west frontier. Its policy actions are often erratic and volatile. 
Wednesday’s decision may be no difference. 
Consensus is drawn right down the middle again, with half expecting a hold at 5.75% and the other half expecting a 25bps hike. It’s a replay 
of the prior meeting on October 21st when BI sided with the hold camp. The bank holds rupiah stability near and dear to its heart. That 
makes the depreciation of the currency since the last rate decision when it held and after political instability a bit of a concern in favour of 
raising the policy rate. Volatile oil prices may prevent BI from prematurely declaring a case for lowered inflation risk.  
SARB—They’ve Already Guided a Hold 
The South African Reserve Bank has employed a gradual approach to monetary easing. Having just hiked in September, it may decide to 
hold at a repo rate of 7¼% on Thursday. Volatile oil prices and only modest recent progress toward slightly lower inflation could maintain a 
hawkish stance. The September statement noted that their projections have “the policy rate broadly stable through the remainder of this 
year. The model shows cuts later in the forecast, as inflation fall to 3% and….moves to a more neutral policy stance.”  
BoC Speech Reaction to CPI 
Bank of Canada Deputy Governor Gravelle speaks on Wednesday. It is billed by the venue as a ‘state of the economy’ speech. The formal 
announcement states that the title is “Scenario Analysis and Risk-Management Approach to Monetary Policy.” There will be no press 
conference. Gravelle generally leads the financial markets and stability roles at the bank. He may react to Monday’s CPI report.  
Note that Governor Macklem speaks the following week on November 24th at the Economic Club of Canada. 
FOMC Minutes to be Stale on Arrival 
Minutes to the October 27th–28th FOMC meeting are stale before they even arrive on Wednesday. They don’t talk less in the Warsh era, for 
one thing. A massive download of Fed-speak has hit markets from across all officials except the Chair. Fresh warm core CPI and a solid 
nonfarm payrolls report paired with Fed-speak has raised pricing for the December meeting from about 20bps to approaching 30bps now.

coming off two consecutive dips. After a string of four straight gains. Housing starts could accelerate based on permit volumes (Tuesday). 
Retail sales during September had been guided to be up by 0.7% m/m by Statcan but the refreshed estimate may be subject to revision 
and we’ll get the breakdown in terms of volumes and sectors. 
How is US Consumer Spending Doing? 
US markets may primarily focus upon the Fed’s conference with otherwise light data due out. Tuesday’s retail sales figures are estimated to 
be flat based upon what we know about weaker auto sales, gas prices and coming off a strong prior month for sales ex-autos. Industrial 
output (Tuesday) is probably poised to register a softer gain of just 0.1% m/m after a strong prior gain and based in part upon ISM-
manufacturing production signals and expected utility output. Housing starts during October (Wednesday) may rebound from the prior 
month based on weather and permits. 
Tracking LatAm Economies 
Updated takes on the health of several Latin American economies will be provided by GDP readings. Peru’s monthly economic activity 
reading—a GDP proxy—is expected to advance to over 3½% y/y in September (Sunday) and ahead of the November 23rd release of Q3 
GDP. Colombia’s economy is expected to decelerate with growth slowing to 1% y/y during Q3 from 3.5% the prior quarter (Tuesday). Chile’s 
economy has been in a rough spot for quite a while as marked by contractions in three of the past four quarters and with Q3 GDP due out 
on Wednesday. Brazil will also refresh its monthly GDP proxy on Monday with growth expected to dip to under 1% y/y. 
UK and Aussie Job Markets 
Both UK and Australia are scheduled to update their labour market readings this week, however, with the RBA and BoE increasingly focused 
on broadening inflationary pressures, the labour market data will take a secondary role.  
Australia’s October employment report on Wednesday is expected to show a modest rebound (+28K) following September’s 40K decline, 
while unemployment rate is forecast to remain unchanged at 4.7%. Meanwhile, Q3 wage growth is expected to remain elevated at 3.4%, in 
line with the RBA’s forecast.  
The UK releases September and October labour market data on Tuesday, followed by October inflation on Wednesday—one of the final 
two readings before the BoE’s December 17th meeting. Employment is expected to show signs of recovery amid resilient economic activity. 
However, attention will remain on elevated wage growth and the updated inflation readings for further signs of second round inflationary 
pressures taking hold. 
One of Two UK CPI Reports Before the Next BoE Decision 
The UK also updates CPI figures for October on Wednesday. Core CPI is expected to hover close to 2¾% y/y which probably would not 
materially dent the 20bps of a hike priced for the December 17th decision. There will be another CPI report released one week before that 
next decision.

FOMC: The FOMC will release the minutes from its October 28th monetary policy meeting on Wednesday at 2 pm EST. The minutes are expected to be somewhat 
stale, given how much we have already heard from Fed officials over the past week and the busy speaking calendar ahead. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is also scheduled to 
deliver opening remarks at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference on Monday at 12pmEST. With no Q&A and his preference for avoiding forward guidance, 
there are low expectations in the way of new policy signals.
South African Reserve Bank (SARB): The South African Reserve Bank is expected to hold rates on Thursday, supported by softer inflation, with core easing to the 
upper end of the 2–4% target range and energy prices broadly stable since the last meeting in September. At its last meeting, the bank hiked 25bp amid rising upside 
inflation risks, with both a 50bp move and a hold also discussed. However, the bank’s projected rate path pointed to policy remaining broadly stable through the 
remainder of 2026. Since then, softer inflation and continued fiscal consolidation following the midterm Budget have strengthened the case for a hawkish hold, 
although the bank is unlikely to rule out further near-term tightening, particularly if energy prices rise again.
Bank Indonesia (BI): Consensus once again appears divided between a 25bp hike and a hold at Bank Indonesia’s policy meeting on Wednesday, following a similar 
split ahead of its surprise October hold. While the macro backdrop heading into the November meeting has strengthened the case for a hold, renewed weakness in 
the rupiah has kept the risk of another hike firmly in play. Oil prices have retreated from above $100 per barrel, core inflation eased in October, and Q3 GDP surprised 
to the upside, expanding 1.6% q/q. However, the rupiah has weakened further amid heightened domestic political uncertainty following the Vice President’s 
resignation, potentially increasing some pressure on Bank Indonesia to tighten policy to support the currency. As always with Bank Indonesia, the potential for a 
surprise remains.
