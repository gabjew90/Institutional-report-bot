Earnings Season Pulse 
Strong deliveries don’t offset pipeline risk 
 AI strong delivery takes a backseat to the US mid terms. AI-related companies continued to 
report exceptional growth. AMD’s data-centre revenue rose 97% year-on-year, Super Micro raised its 
annual sales outlook to $72–82 billion and, Palantir beat adjusted EPS estimates by 7%. This wasn’t 
enough for the stocks to hold up against the increased regulatory risk brought on by the US mid term 
elections as NASDAQ fell 5% on 5th November. 
 European order books are not translating reliably into earnings. Alstom cut its adjusted EBIT 
margin guidance from about 6.5% to ~1% and shifted to a €1.5bn free cash outflow, while Rheinmetall 
lowered its sales target for a second time in 2026 due to programme delays. Although company-
specific, these warnings underscore the challenge of converting large infrastructure and defence 
backlogs into margins and cash flow. 
 Japan's HEV story is driving a re-rating. This week produced one of the more significant positive 
results in Japan. Toyota Motors raised operating-income guidance by 15% to ¥3.9 trillion and 
expanded its buyback to ¥1.3 trillion, despite lowering its vehicle-sales forecast. The shares rose 17%, 
their largest one-day gain since 1987. The result suggests that hybrid mix, cost control and currency 
support are driving the profitability.  
 China consumer sectors cloud the earnings picture. Chinese banks, which account for more than 
half of onshore earnings, reported resilient profits, supported by loan growth, stable margins and 
disciplined cost control. Consumer earnings were more mixed amid sluggish household demand. 
While large companies such as Kweichow Moutai met expectations, the broader staples sector 
remained in contraction. Consumer discretionary earnings fell 11% YoY, primarily reflecting weakness 
in autos. 
 Pharma provides a defensive source of upgrades. Pfizer raised revenue and earnings guidance 
as non-Covid product sales increased 7%, while 46 of 51 S&P 500 healthcare companies beat estimates. 
Unlike technology and industrials, the sector’s earnings improvement is being driven by new-product 
launches rather than higher capital spending or cyclical demand.
