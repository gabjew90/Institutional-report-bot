China: October activity data broadly weakened, pointing to a soft start 
to Q4
16 November 2026 | 1:00PM HKT
Bottom line: 
China’s October activity data broadly weakened from September and fell short of 
market expectations, pointing to a soft start to Q4 and still-wide divergence 
between strong exports and subdued domestic demand. Industrial production (IP) 
growth slowed to 4.8% yoy in October from 5.5% yoy in September, as slower output 
growth in auto and ferrous metal smelting industries more than offset faster output 
growth in computer and other equipment manufacturing. On a single-month basis, 
the year-on-year contraction in fixed asset investment (FAI) widened to -16.3% in 
October from -7.2% in September despite a low base — this likely reflects slower 
government spending of previously raised funds, while the broad-based worsening 
appears more pronounced than steel and cement output would suggest. Retail sales 
growth decelerated to 0.4% yoy in October from 1.1% yoy in September, as the 
earlier Mid-Autumn Festival this year may have shifted some consumption from 
October into September. The Services Industry Output Index, reported in real terms 
and closely correlated with tertiary GDP growth, also moderated to 4.6% yoy in 
October from 4.8% yoy in September. We maintain our forecast that sequential real 
GDP growth will slow to 4.0% qoq annualized in Q4 from 4.9% qoq annualized in Q3. 
With this year’s growth target nearly secured, we believe some government funds 
raised in Q4 may be deployed in Q1 next year to support growth in 2027.

Main points: 
1. Industrial production (IP) growth slowed to 4.8% yoy in October from 5.5% yoy in 
September despite still-solid export growth. Sequentially, we estimate IP contracted by 
0.3% mom non-annualized in October (vs. +0.8% in September; Exhibit 1). By industry, 
the September-to-October deceleration in year-on-year IP growth was led by slower 
output growth in auto and ferrous metal smelting industries, more than offsetting faster 
output growth in computer and other equipment manufacturing (Exhibit 2). Among 
major industrial products (which differ from the by-industry IP breakdown), auto output 
growth and power generation growth both slowed in October, to -7.7% yoy and -2.1% 
yoy, respectively, from -5.7% yoy and -1.2% yoy in September. Crude steel output 
growth fell to -1.6% yoy in October from +0.1% yoy in September, while the 
year-on-year contraction in cement output narrowed to -1.3% from -5.3%. Chemical 
fiber output growth fell to -2.9% yoy in October from +0.4% yoy in September, whereas 
sulfuric acid output growth remained subdued at -8.6% yoy in October (vs. -12.4% yoy 
in September), reflecting continued disruptions from the Middle East conflict. The 
year-on-year contraction in smartphone output widened to -28.9% in October from 
-26.6% in September, while growth in industrial robot output and metal cutting machine 
output accelerated to 26.0% yoy and 19.0% yoy, respectively, from 18.9% yoy and 
12.5% yoy. 
2. On a single-month basis, the year-on-year contraction in fixed asset investment (FAI) 
widened to -16.3% in October from -7.2% in September, despite a low base and 
incremental easing measures introduced since end-September, likely reflecting slower 
government spending of previously raised funds (Exhibit 3). Sequentially, FAI contracted 
by 11.9% mom in October on a non-annualized basis, following a 3.1% mom gain in

September, based on our estimates. This brings year-to-date FAI growth to -8.1% yoy in 
October from -7.2% yoy in September. By sector, manufacturing and infrastructure 
investment growth fell to -9.5% yoy and -18.5% yoy, respectively, in October from -1.0% 
yoy and -11.6% yoy in September, property investment growth dipped further to

caution that occasional NBS “statistical corrections” to previously overstated data may 
have amplified reported FAI volatility in recent quarters — the October decline in FAI 
growth appears more pronounced than steel and cement output would suggest. 
3. Nominal retail sales growth slowed to +0.4% yoy in October from +1.1% yoy in 
September, mainly led by online goods sales and restaurant sales revenue, where growth 
fell to -3.5% yoy and +0.7% yoy, respectively, from -0.1% yoy and +2.1% yoy. The earlier 
Mid-Autumn Festival this year may have shifted some consumption from October into 
September, in our view. By comparison, offline goods sales growth rose modestly to 
+2.0% yoy in October from +1.4% yoy in September. In sequential terms, we estimate 
retail sales value fell 0.2% mom non-annualized in October (vs. +0.5% in September). 
Among major product categories, food and clothing sales growth decelerated to +0.5% 
yoy and -2.0% yoy, respectively, in October from +6.7% yoy and +1.2% yoy in 
September. Communication appliance sales growth slowed meaningfully to +6.5% yoy in 
October from +31.1% yoy in September, auto sales growth remained sluggish at -21.4% 
yoy in October (vs. -21.2% yoy in September), while home appliance sales growth rose 
slightly to +2.5% yoy from +1.9% yoy thanks to a low base. Gasoline and other 
oil-product sales growth improved to +4.4% yoy in October from -0.8% yoy in 
September, mainly on higher prices — we estimate gasoline and other oil-product sales 
volume contracted 19% yoy in October (vs. -14% yoy in September) amid a 24% yoy 
increase in domestic retail fuel prices. Separately, the NBS began publishing a new series 
this year on retail sales value for goods and services, which rose 2.5% yoy in 
January-October, with services sales growth of 4.9% yoy outpacing goods sales growth 
of 0.9% yoy. 
4. Year-on-year growth in the Services Industry Output Index—which is reported in real 
terms and closely tracks tertiary GDP growth, accounting for 58% of China’s economy in 
2025—inched down to 4.6% yoy in October from 4.8% in September. Sequentially, we 
estimate the index gained 0.2% mom sa non-annualized in October (vs. +0.9% in 
September).  
5. Property activity remained sluggish in October (Exhibit 4). Property sales fell 19.0% 
yoy in volume (floor space) terms and 18.5% yoy in value terms in October (vs. 
-20.5%/-20.0% yoy in September). New home starts, new home completions, and floor 
space under construction declined by 18.2% yoy, 39.3% yoy, and 13.0% yoy, 
respectively, in October (vs. -25.6%/-36.4%/-12.8% yoy in September). NBS and 
private-sector data indicate early signs of home-price stabilization in some large cities, 
while most lower-tier cities remain under downward pressure.  
6. Labor market conditions were broadly stable in October. The official (unadjusted) 
nationwide and 31-city unemployment rates both inched down to 5.1% in October from 
5.2% in September. On a seasonally adjusted basis, we estimate that both the 
nationwide and 31-city rate were unchanged at 5.2% in October (Exhibit 5). The 
seasonally adjusted unemployment rate for migrant workers with local Hukou edged 
down to 5.1% in October from 5.2% in September. Following the NBS definition change
