A Long and Winding Road
We provide context and perspective on research across
regions and asset classes. This week, we provide our updated
thoughts on France ahead of the 2027 election across asset
classes, present our 2027 US CRE outlook, and take stock of
Türkiye's energy transition in advance of COP31.
• The Path to the French Elections: We believe that France's domestic backdrop will be
clouded by uncertainty about its fiscal and political path over the coming year. French assets
have repriced sharply as fiscal and political risks have grown, but the road to the 2027
elections is still long and uncertain. Although much may already be priced in, the end-game
for OATs still looks skewed toward cheaper yields and wider spreads, since ECB support may
not arrive soon. The risk of renewed OAT spread widening will probably keep weighing on the
EUR until a clearer post-election path emerges. French equities already price in much of the
political risk, which leaves room for stabilization, although a lasting re-rating would need
greater fiscal clarity and less electoral uncertainty. From a credit perspective, bank
fundamentals remain resilient, while consumer & retail face demand pressure. Toll roads and
airlines are most exposed in autos & transport, and real estate remains rate sensitive. On the
other hand, utilities and energy remain fundamentally insulated despite sovereign/RV
sensitivity, as TMT offers selective value while industrials face a fragile construction recovery
backdrop. Lastly, mortgage-backed covered bonds offer greater insulation than SSAs.
• 2027 US CRE Outlook: While each of the five major CRE property types faces a distinct set of
opportunities and challenges, we expect property values to remain generally flat in the year
ahead, with NOI growth likely to offset moves in cap rates. Although GDP growth in excess of
2% should bolster demand for CRE assets, elevated vacancies will likely limit NOI growth, and
high rates will likely prevent meaningful cap rate compression. NOI growth has moderated
across the major property types, as rent growth has fallen below inflation across all of the five
major CRE sectors. Occupancy rates remain at or below pre-COVID levels, limiting owners’

• Energy Transition in Türkiye: With Türkiye set to host COP31, we take stock of the country’s
energy transition and identify opportunities as it moves to the next phase of electrification.
We note that Türkiye is accelerating electrification to reduce exposure to geopolitical and
energy shocks, which compound current-account and inflation pressure. Renewables have
scaled rapidly and largely displaced imported gas, while industrial policy has supported a
growing manufacturing base across solar, wind, batteries and EVs. Further progress will
depend on addressing transmission constraints and reducing the continued reliance on coal.
Türkiye is also among the countries most exposed to the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment
Mechanism, with its new Emissions Trading Scheme offering little near-term relief while
allowances are allocated for free. We see grids and storage as the most direct ways to gain
exposure to Türkiye’s transition. Transmission is the principal constraint on further renewable
growth, supporting activity across grid equipment, network investment, and battery
integration. Solar manufacturing and renewables operators provide additional exposure,
while EV and nuclear supply chains offer longer-dated, more-policy-dependent opportunities.

Overweight (OW):
The analyst expects the six-month excess return of the country’s index eligible bonds to exceed the six-month excess return of the Bloomberg EM USD
Sovereign Index*.
Market Weight (MW):
The analyst expects the six-month excess return of the country’s index eligible bonds to be in line with the six-month excess return of the Bloomberg
EM USD Sovereign Index*.
Underweight (UW):
The analyst expects the six-month excess return of the country’s index eligible bonds to be less than the six-month excess return of the Bloomberg EM
USD Sovereign Index*.
Rating Suspended (RS):

firm policies in certain circumstances including where the Investment Bank of Barclays Bank PLC is acting in an advisory capacity.

analyst’s view of the expected excess return over a six-month period of the sovereign debt securities relative to the expected excess return of the
