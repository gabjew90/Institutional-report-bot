Asset Management Market Flash: The 
Fed changes gear 
13 November 2026 
• 
US inflation, which came in higher than expected, reinforced expectations of monetary 
tightening and pushed up interest rates, whilst gold fell and the dollar strengthened. 
• 
Geopolitical tensions have reignited concerns over energy supplies and contributed to the 
rebound in Brent crude, despite signs of improvement in exports and maritime freight. 
• 
Equity markets recouped some of their losses, with Nvidia’s fall having no significant knock-
on effect on the technology sector, whilst CoreWeave’s results reassured investors about its 
financial strength. 
Very strong US jobs data failed to elicit much market reaction as the Fed’s focus is now on inflation. 
Investors were therefore anxious to see the CPI reading and its impact was even greater as it delivered 
a really unpleasant surprise. Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and by 0.3% ex food and energy. In 
a particularly hawkish comment, FOMC member Christopher Waller had already warned that this level 
would warrant a 50bp rate hike in December. His words carry weight as he is an important Fed official. 
Markets immediately took a December rate hike as given and 10-year US Treasury yields shot back 
above 5%. Two-year yields, which are more sensitive to Fed moves, jumped even more, reflecting 
expectations that additional tightening by end 2027 would be close to 85bp. Gold paid the price by 
falling below $4,000/oz while the dollar rose sharply, pushing the euro’s exchange rate to below 1.105. 
However, the likelihood of a half-point hike in December is still slight. It might be seen as excessive and 
likely to trigger market turbulence and for the moment no other Fed official has backed the idea. Not 
even Beth Hammack who is among the most hawkish committee members.   
 
The Fed’s shift in tone over inflation is due to persistent inflationary pressure from AI investment and 
energy prices. Developments on the international scene offer no respite and hopes for detente at the 
beginning of the week very quickly evaporated. Iran is still refusing to release its enriched uranium, 
Donald Trump says he does not need an agreement, and Ukraine peace talks in Abu Dhabi only resulted 
in a prisoner exchange. And comments from Israel’s ambassador to the UN that Washington had sent 
troops to the Middle East to prepare for another attack on Iran hardly helped ease tensions. Oil trading 
reflected these contradictory signals. Brent crude had fallen to a 10-week low but on Thursday 
rebounded towards $96. Prices had fallen since early November not only on hopes for a peace 
settlement but also due to the fact the Gulf crude exports were running at pre-war levels and that 
maritime freight rates had tumbled. But these encouraging signs are conditional on no fresh military 
escalation. 
 
Equity markets have largely brushed off this turbulence and recouped a good deal of the losses racked 
up in the previous week. But markets have not yet fully discounted the consequences of the Nvidia 
debacle. The group's founder Jensen Huang resigned after revelations that he had had two affairs, one 
with a woman suspected of spying for China. The Nvidia share sold off but without dragging down the 
rest of the tech sector. And CoreWeave’s results reassured markets on the group’s financial strength.

All this reinforces our view that the AI theme will continue to underpin global equity markets, especially 
as valuations have now fallen back to attractive levels. But we are still diversifying exposure to other 
geographical zones and to the bond market which now offers the advantage of decorrelation. 
 
European equities 
 
In an up week for European markets, indices hit new highs driven by falling oil prices and solid company 
earnings. Hopes for de-escalation in the Middle East sent oil prices tumbling, reducing energy bills for 
companies and boosting cyclicals and financials. Even so, inflationary worries persisted. Investors are 
now expecting the ECB to raise rates in December. Eurozone finance ministers began talks on who 
might succeed Christine Lagarde. France declared its support for the Spanish candidate. Elsewhere, 
eurozone investor confidence remained stable overall and the UK economy surprised on the upside. 
Tech stocks, however, had a choppy week due to AI uncertainties. European stocks were relatively 
untouched as they have less tech exposure. The rising trend in bond yields marked a pause, a reminder 
that markets are still dependent on energy prices and the ECB’s direction of travel. 
 
In company news, consumption continued to offer a mixed picture. The luxury sector gained as 
Burberry reported better-than-expected sales, a sign that the brand was still recovering. Richemont’s 
report met with less enthusiastic reactions. Strong jewellery sales failed to offset the impact of higher 
gold prices on margins. Industrials led gains this week on solid demand for data centres and power 
grids. Siemens Energy jumped after the group announced record results and a big increase in its 
dividend. Siemens raised medium term guidance on demand in its railway and digital businesses. Both 
Schneider Electric and Legrand gained from the same optimism. Tech was more volatile. Infineon 
reassured markets by focusing on data centre demand for electricity but semiconductor plays like ASML 
continued to suffer from AI doubts. In commodities, Eramet launched an increase of capital to bolster 
its financial structure. In utilities, RWE gained on a sharp increase in revenues. In healthcare, Merck 
KGaA raised guidance thanks to its pharma and semiconductor solutions and Fresenius cut its stake in 
Fresenius Medical Care. In chemicals, Brenntag’s quarterly figures missed analyst expectations. 
Financials proved resilient this week: Allianz gained on a strong showing from its asset management 
business, especially at PIMCO, and Generali beat expectations. Bank consolidation continued: Crédit 
Agricole asked Banco BPM to open talks on a tie-up with its Italian affiliate.  
US equities 
 
Wall Street rebounded from the previous week’s sell-off. The S&P 500 gained 2.9%, the Nasdaq 100 4.8% 
and the Russell 2000 1.7%. Concerns over another Fed hike returned after October inflation rose 0.4 % 
over a month when analysts were going for 0.3%. WTI shed 2% to $89 after Donald Trump made 
conciliatory comments on Iran. 
 
Tech jumped 3.8% and the SOX semiconductor index soared 12%. Nvidia (-11%) bucked the trend. The 
group announced the resignation of founder Jensen Huang after revelations about his personal life. 
Finance director Colette Kress was made interim CEO. Uncertainty is focused on who will replace Huang 
and the legal risk rather than on the business itself. Sales at CoreWeave (+22%) beat consensus 
expectations by 3%. Cisco (+9 %) had a good quarter with adjusted EPS beating expectations by 6% and 
an upward revision for full-year EPS which is 8% above consensus. Management now expects AI 
infrastructure revenues to hit $9bn.  
 
Anthropic launched its IPO road show. 825 million shares will be on offer at a price between $70-78 or 
up to $64bn in total. This values the group at between $1.25 and 1.4 trillion dollars on a diluted basis. 
The price will be set on November 18 and trading on the Nasdaq will start the day after.

Healthcare gained 1.6% but underperformed the market due to mixed results. At its investor day, 
Moderna (-9%) confirmed that sales this year would rise between 15% and 25% but triggered selling by 
not giving any forecast for 2027. Ionis Pharmaceuticals cratered 31% after cutting forecasts for sales 
this year of Tryngolza, which treats a rare form of excess triglycerides, from $100m to $50m. The 
downward revision was due to a slower-than-expected rollout.  
 
Utilities gained 2%. Constellation Energy jumped 13% after adjusted EPS came in 31% above consensus 
expectations and annual EPS guidance 5% better than expectations. Adjusted EBITDA at Vistra (+8%) 
was 11% better than expected thanks to Texas. The group raised full-year guidance and unveiled another 
$1.5bn in share buybacks.   
Emerging markets 
 
The MSCI EM had rebounded by 3.5 % in USD as of Thursday’s close. Taiwan and Korea led gains as both 
recovered more than 9 % following the previous week’s tech-led sell-off. India (+1.4 %) continued to 
benefit from diversification flows while China declined 2.9 % amid persistent concerns over access to AI 
computing capacity. Brazil also slipped 0.9 %. 
 
China: October inflation data highlighted the continued divergence between consumer demand and 
industrial pricing. CPI eased to 0.7 % YoY from 0.9 % in September, dragged down by food prices, while 
PPI accelerated to 5.7 % YoY, driven largely by higher energy and chemical prices. External demand 
remained resilient, with exports growing 35.2 % YoY (vs. 36.3 % in September), while imports surprised 
on the upside, accelerating to 41.6 % YoY from 31 %. On the policy front, authorities are reportedly 
exploring a potential 25 % income tax on government bond interest, raising concerns over bank 
profitability and bond yields. The controversy surrounding former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang intensified 
fears of tighter US restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI chips. Elsewhere, the US Department 
of Defence removed WuXi AppTec from its Chinese Military Companies List following a court ruling. 
Alibaba also announced plans to spin off its chipmaking subsidiary T-Head, which has confidentially 
filed for a Hong Kong listing. 
 
Taiwan: authorities in Taiwan stepped up market support, activating the National Stabilisation Fund 
alongside tighter short-selling restrictions following the sharp correction. Fundamentally, AI demand 
remained robust, with multiple companies reporting record October sales, including TSMC’s October 
revenue (+15 % MoM, +60 % YoY), reinforcing expectations of strong fourth-quarter sales. 
 
In Korea, semiconductor exports surged 33 1 % YoY to $16.6bn during November 1–10, accounting for 
nearly half of total exports, while passenger car shipments declined 4.3 %, highlighting the increasingly 
concentrated contribution of the semiconductor cycle to export growth. 
 
India: on the corporate front, ICICI Prudential Life reported robust October new business premium 
growth of 37.5 % YoY, one of the best results across major private insurers. Elsewhere, Adani Group 
unveiled plans to invest over $120bn over the next five years, with approximately 70 % allocated to 
energy infrastructure. 
 
Indonesia: the rupiah weakened to a record low against the US dollar, prompting central bank 
intervention in the FX market. Political uncertainty intensified following Vice President Gibran 
Rakabuming Raka’s resignation, adding further pressure to investor sentiment. 
 
Brazil: the government unveiled a spending-restraint package targeting a fiscal adjustment of BRL 70–
80 billion (~0.5 % of GDP) in 2027.

Peru raised its benchmark interest rate by 25bp to 4.50 %, its first hike in nearly four years. The move 
was designed to curb an inflation spike driven by rising food and energy prices. 
 
Corporate debt  
 
Bond volatility remained high, especially in the US due to hawkish comments1 from Fed officials who 
consider inflation to be too high. Yields on 10-year US Treasuries jumped 27bp over the week to 5.12%. 
The 2-year Treasury even gained 37bp. 10-year German Bund yields rose 9bp. Despite the rise, risk 
assets in Europe gained ground thanks to a 20bp tightening in spreads. High yield returned 0.60% and 
the CoCo index 0.35%. The Xover also tightened by 20bp over the period. Investment grade tightened 
by 4bp but ended the period 0.14% lower due to rising bond yields. Corporate hybrids tightened by 
13bp but slipped 0.03% due to rising US yields. 
 
In High Yield, UK utility Drax Finco raised €1bn at 5.875% due 2032. Italy’s Amplifon, a hearing 
healthcare specialist, raised €600m at 4.875% due 2031 and Dutch telecoms group Odido €500m at 6.5% 
due 2032. 500 millions. There were three AT1 deals in financial subordinated debt: BBVA raised €1bn at

GLOSSARY 
 
• Investment Grade: bonds rated as high quality by rating agencies. 
• High Yield: corporate bonds with a higher default risk than investment grade bonds but which pay out higher 
coupons. 
• Senior debt benefits from specific guarantees. Its repayment takes priority over other debts, known as 
subordinated debt. 
• Debt is considered to be subordinated when its redemption depends on the earlier payment of other creditors. To 
offset the higher risk, subordinated Senior debt has priority over other debt instruments. 
• Tier 2 / Tier 3: subordinated debt segment. 
• Duration: the average life of a bond discounted for all interest and capital flows. 
• The spread is the difference between the actuarial rate of return on a bond and the rate of return on a risk-free 
loan with the same maturity. 
• The so-called "Value" stocks are considered to be undervalued.  
• EBITDA: Earnings before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. 
• CTA: quantitative strategy which uses futures to invest in a wide range of financial assets, including equity indices, 
short-term and long-term interest rates, currencies, and commodities.  
• The PMI, for "Purchasing Manager's Index", is an indicator of the economic state of a sector.  
• AT1s belong to a family of bank capital securities known as contingent convertibles or “Cocos”. Convertible 
because they can be converted from bonds to shares (or depreciated entirely) and contingent because this 
conversion only occurs if certain conditions are met, such as the issuing bank's capital strength falling below a 
predetermined trigger level. 
• RT1s: perpetual bond issues with early redemption possible after 10 years. Coupon payments are discretionary 
and non-cumulative.
