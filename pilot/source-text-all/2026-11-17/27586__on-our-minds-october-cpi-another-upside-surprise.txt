October CPI: Another Upside Surprise 
 
October core CPI surprised to the upside at 0.3% m/m. Direct estimates suggest PCE 
housing inflation barely eased to 0.28% m/m, while PCE core-goods inflation likely was 
firm at around 0.25% m/m. Depending on whether monthly super-core PCE inflation 
reverts toward its recent six-month average (0.28% m/m) or remains near the August-
September pace (0.37% m/m), core PCE inflation would likely fall in a 0.27%-0.33% m/m 
range, with a central estimate around 0.30% m/m. The October PPI release will provide 
more clarity with regards to super-core PCE. 
 
The October CPI Release 
Headline CPI increased by 0.4% m/m in October vs a consensus & SG expectation of 0.3% m/m, 
below the 0.6% m/m increase in September; the year/year likely ended up at 4.1% up from 3.7% 
in September (the BLS did not publish a yr/yr number due to data issues related to the 2025 
government shutdown and our yr/yr estimate is based on interpolating the NSA October 2025 
number). Energy went up 0.7% m/m after rising 5.2% in September, with gasoline rising 1% m/m 
from a 9.1% increase previously. Food CPI increased markedly at 0.5% m/m, after remaining 
essentially flat in September.

First-Pass Implications for Core PCE 
With the October CPI we now have source data for the housing and goods components within PCE 
inflation. Directly projecting PCE Housing inflation on month/month changes in CPI OER and CPI 
Rent of Primary Residence as well as its own lags implies for October a PCE Housing inflation rate of 
0.28% m/m, which is a very modest stepdown from the 0.32% m/m pace in September. For PCE Core 
Goods the weighting of the individual components is different within PCE vs. CPI. Directly estimating 
PCE Core Goods inflation using individual CPI components for durable goods and nondurable goods 
(excl. food and energy items) suggests a relatively firm 0.25% m/m increase for PCE Core Goods in 
October compared to the 0.20% m/m pace in September.

Given the average weights for housing (~18%) and core goods (~24%) the above discussed 
projections cover less than half of core PCE. The October core-PCE forecast is now almost entirely a 
question of where non-housing core services (‘super-core’) land. Most of the source data for super 
core PCE comes from the PPI release so today’s CPI data is of no help here. If the monthly October 
super-core PCE inflation rate reverts toward its recent six-month average pace, core PCE is 
around 0.27%. If it remains near the average August-September pace, core PCE is around 0.32%. 
A central estimate around 0.30% therefore looks reasonable before the October PPI release.
