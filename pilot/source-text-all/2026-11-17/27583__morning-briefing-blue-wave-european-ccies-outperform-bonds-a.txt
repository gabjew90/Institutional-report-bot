OUTLOOK 
European currencies, gold and govies are the winners overnight following the political wind of 
change in Washington where the Democrats swept to power, winning both chambers of Congress 
after flipping seven Republican-held Senate seats the party needed to claim the majority. 
President Trump deflected blame but will accept the result and work together with the new 
Congress. This removes the threat of legal wrangling over the ballots in the courts and could be 
one of the reasons why markets are overall taking the blue sweep in their stride. S&P and Nasdaq 
futures turned positive at the European open after spending most of the night in the red. With the 
Democrats seizing control of the House and Senate Foreign Affairs Committees, the resumption 
of diplomacy between the US and Iran and reaction in oil prices will quickly become the main 
focal point for markets. Oil is holding up around $104, the lower end of the 1-month range. In FX, 
the outperformance of Europe vs dollar bloc and the Yen is interesting and could be explained 
through the lens of Ukraine (Democrats lean towards more support). It is too soon to draw 
conclusion on oil but optimism that the frozen conflict somehow can de-escalate would be a 
tailwind for the single currency and the EGB curve (lower yields), and tweak the policy context for 
the ECB in 2027. For GBP/USD the acceleration to a 7-week high above the 200dma (+2.7% since 
mid-October) demonstrates how the accumulation of short positions before the Budget last 
month are now being unwound. Further gains could lie ahead if history is any guide, helping the 
BoE in its battle to contain inflation. The blue wave should not change the direction of travel for 
the Fed, at least not in the short term. A 25bp rate increase in December remains a high 
probability event. Futures for June -27 flatten to 55.5bp (-2.5bp). The 2s/10s curve trades flat at 
33bp. The Treasury will announce quarterly refunding plans later today. With regard to the US 
fiscal trajectory, SG economics see greater risk of government shutdown (link to note, see p7). 
Elsewhere in G10, the Riksbank followed the RBA yesterday and the BoC last week by delivering 
a quarter point rate increase to 2.0%. The move was telegraphed at the September meeting. The 
policy rate remains accommodative relative to the neutral range (mid-point 2.25%) so further 
tightening could be on the cards. This is also the implicit message in the statement: if the outlook 
for inflation and economic activity holds, the Riksbank assesses that it is likely that the policy rate 
will be raised further in the coming year. The path of September still holds and implies tightening 
to 2.3% in 2027 and 2.5% in 2028. Despite the negative carry vs the Fed and ECB and the 
underperformance of the OMX vs Stoxx Europe 600, the SEK has turned in a remarkably positive 
return over the past month and ranks among the top three performers in G10 alongside the NOK 
and the CAD. EUR/SEK has retraced to the 200dma (10.94) after topping out at 11.3665 after the 
previous Riksbank meeting in late September. It’s a similar story for USD/SEK. The updated 
projection for two more rate hikes over the forecast horizon should narrow the policy rate 
differential between the ECB and the Riksbank. Separately, the preliminary inflation data from 
Eurozone this morning is forecast to show a rise in headline CPI to 4.0% yoy in October, a 3-year 
high. Core is projected to have stayed unchanged at 2.5%. This should seal the next 25bp rate 
increase in December.  
In Brazil, the COPOM meeting today comes against a markedly different backdrop following the 
surprise election victory of President Lula and repricing across Brazilian assets. The BRL, local 
bonds and equities have all come under pressure as investors reassess the fiscal outlook during 
Lula’s fourth term. BRL spot is down nearly 10% from 4.98/USD pre-run-off to around 5.50, the 
Bovespa is down 20% and the 10y BRLGB yield is up more than 250bp to 15.44%. for the BCB, 
market pricing has shifted decisively towards a pause. Most economists pencil in no change today 
after previously betting on back-to-back cuts in November and December. Our economist Dev 
Ashish is in the minority camp expecting a cut. This could be the trigger for another round of

TECHNICAL ANALYSIS 
Nasdaq Composite has experienced a steady decline after giving up its 50-DMA last month. It has reached the longer-term 200-
DMA; however, signals of a large rebound are not yet visible. The peak achieved earlier this week at 25,800 pts is the first hurdle.
An inability to overcome this level may lead to a continuation of the down move. The next potential supports could be located at
the July low of 24,400 pts and the 2025 peak near 24,000 pts. 
EUR/SEK breached a multi-month ascending trend line and failed to defend the neckline of its previous inverse head and shoulders
pattern, highlighting a lack of steady upward momentum. The pair has now reached the 200-DMA and is challenging the August 
trough at 10.91/10.87, which could act as a potential support zone. A brief bounce cannot be ruled out; however, the recent pivot
high at 11.10/11.15 may serve as short-term resistance. An inability to hold above 10.91/10.87 could lead to a deeper decline.
