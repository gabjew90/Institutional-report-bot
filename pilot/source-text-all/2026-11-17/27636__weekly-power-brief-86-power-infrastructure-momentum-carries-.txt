• With more than three-quarters of the S&P 500’s market 
cap having already reported, the 3Q26 earnings season 
is wrapping up. 3Q26 results point to healthy underlying 
demand, suggesting the recent derating has been driven 
more by macro and AI-related concerns than by a 
deterioration in earnings fundamentals.
• 3Q26 releases relevant to our strategy these past couple 
of weeks have been largely coming from industrial 
and utility companies. Across the sectors, robust orders 
and expanding backlogs continue to point to sustained 
revenue growth, while pricing power and operating 
leverage are increasingly translating strong demand into 
higher margins and increasing profitability.
• The 
breadth 
of 
the 
evidence 
is 
particularly 
encouraging. Strength extends across electrification, 
grid infrastructure, data-center power and cooling, 
automation and utilities, supporting our view that the 
investment cycle remains firmly intact and leaving scope 
for a rerating if sentiment begins to reconnect with 
fundamentals.

Our view in summary
In recent briefings, we discussed our bafflement that 
valuations for the power infrastructure universe were 
coming under significant pressure while our primary 
research signaled that earnings power for our companies 
was likely to further accelerate. Given this, we view the 
3Q26 reporting season as a key test of the market’s 
willingness to refocus on the fundamentals after a summer 
dominated by exogenous macro concerns.
The 3Q26 earnings season offers encouraging evidence 
that our thesis of accelerating growth off a high base 
is correct, and that the prevailing market view is too 
pessimistic. The trajectory of new orders remains strong, 
while rising backlogs signal rising revenue growth in coming 
quarters. Importantly, demand is translating into stronger 
profitability for our companies, supported by pricing power 
and operating leverage.
In this week’s report, we delve into earnings takeaways for 
companies within the Power and Resources list, as well as 
peers whose results are relevant to the broader theme.

Weekly performance commentary
Last week saw some earnings-related news flow, but the 
midterm outcome ultimately dominated market action, 
driving a sharp rotation away from stocks tied to the 
AI buildout. This move also weighed on our Power and 
resources strategy, which declined in absolute terms and 
lagged the MSCI ACWI Index over the week ending 6 
November. The weakness was mainly driven by equipment 
and facilitator names, while utilities provided some offset. 
From a regional perspective, US and APAC holdings 
accounted for most of the underperformance, with 
European names remaining broadly flat.
Thoughts of the week
Industrials
Honeywell (HON): Automation growth broadens
Honeywell Technologies reported 7% organic sales growth 
in 3Q, with solid momentum across Building Automation, 
Process Automation and Industrial Automation. New order 
growth of 3% was a tad light versus our expectations, but 
backlog grew 7% and management signaled that 4Q orders 
should be up high-single digits, which we attribute to the 
typical lumpiness of project timing. Management raised full-
year revenue, EPS, and free cash flow guidance.
The global buildout of energy infrastructure, including low-
carbon projects, LNG liquefaction, gas processing, and 
petrochemicals, continues to represent a key pillar of growth 
for HON. This is a key reason we added it to Power 
and Resources, and the company has received more than 
USD 1bn of incremental energy orders since 1Q26 alone, 
validating our thesis.
Johnson Controls (JCI): Strong demand and market share 
gains add to a self-help story
Johnson Controls’ fiscal 4Q26 results offered another strong 
read-through for thermal management and mission-critical 
building systems. Organic revenues grew 12% and EPS 
increased 32%, driven (as usual) by applied HVAC. Orders 
rose 44% and backlog reached a record USD 23bn, up 43%, 
with data center demand a major contributor. We believe 
the company’s new YK-HT water chiller for data centers 
(which our team viewed at the JCI Advanced Development 
Engineering Center in June) is now taking meaningful share 
from peers—essentially recovering lost business from prior 
years.
As this was the last quarter of the company’s fiscal year, 
management gave a first look at FY27, providing an EPS 
guidance range that placed consensus at the low end. 
Despite this, we see significant upside to this initial guidance 
and feel very comfortable with this position in Power and 
Resources.
Prysmian (PRY): Strong execution and another guidance 
upgrade
Prysmian is one of our largest position in our list following 
our recent increase in exposure. The company delivered a 
very strong 3Q, with adjusted EBITDA +32% Y/Y and ~7% 
ahead of consensus, organic growth accelerating to 13.1%, 
and margins expanding 200bps to 16.8%. Growth was 
broad-based, led by Transmission (+23% organic), Digital 
Solutions (+17%) and Industrial & Construction (+14%), 
with continued strength in data-center demand.
More importantly, management raised FY26 guidance 
again. The new EBITDA midpoint is ~6% above pre-
reporting consensus and FCF ~10% above, with Prysmian 
now expecting to exceed its 2028 EBITDA and FCF targets 
two years early.
The key takeaway is that strong grid investment, 
electrification and data-center demand are translating into 
both high organic growth and substantial margin expansion, 
with further upside risk to consensus, providing another 
strong datapoint for our thesis.
Vertiv (VRT): Demand and execution accelerate together
While VRT is not currently in our list, the results are a helpful 
read-across to data center electrical and liquid cooling peers.
Vertiv delivered a particularly strong 3Q, with organic 
sales growth of 55% broadly diversified across regions. 
More importantly, organic orders more than doubled 
Y/Y off a tough comp (+60% in 3Q25), indicating 
that backlog conversion is not exhausting the demand 
pipeline. Operating margins expanded 290bps, supported 
by operating leverage and manufacturing productivity 
despite continued growth investment and mix headwinds. 
Management raised full-year sales and EPS guidance, while 
the 4Q outlook calls for 50% revenue growth at the 
midpoint.
The key takeaway is that spending on data center 
infrastructure is translating into both shipments and 
earnings, with demand extending beyond a single region. 
The next test is maintaining delivery performance and 
margins while scaling capacity. Record growth also raises the 
comparison base, so the durability and conversion of orders 
matter more than extrapolating the quarter’s growth rate.

GE Vernova (GEV): A larger, more profitable backlog
While GEV is not currently in our list, the results are a 
helpful read-across to power generation and high-voltage 
equipment peers.
GE Vernova’s 3Q reinforced the strength of demand 
across generation and grid infrastructure. Orders rose 96% 
organically, including a 178% jump in Power. EBITDA 
margins expanded nearly 7 percentage points, supported 
by higher pricing, volume and productivity. GEV raised 
its 2026 revenue, margin, and free cash flow outlooks, 
and expanded its share repurchase authorization. It also 
announced the USD 9bn acquisition of high-voltage 
equipment specialist Trench Group, marking another step 
toward vertical integration.
The one wrinkle in the report was that 3Q included 11 GW 
of new contract signings, slightly below the 13 GW widely 
expected, and management signaled that bookings in 2027 
would be down Y/Y on a gigawatt basis. While this particular 
metric may have peaked, strong pricing implies that backlog 
and earnings should continue to exhibit solid growth on a 
dollar basis.
ABB (ABBN): another strong datapoint to the Power & 
resources investment thesis
ABB is currently not on our list, but its Q3 2026 results 
provide a strong positive read-across for our electrification 
names on the list: orders surged 40% y/y to a record 
USD 12.1bn, book-to-bill reached 1.24x, and Electrification 
division orders jumped 70%, driven by triple-digit growth in 
data-center orders, with US orders up 82% on group level. 
Together with similarly strong data-center demand reported 
by Schneider Electric, the results reinforce the strength 
of the structural AI/data-center power infrastructure cycle. 
After the recent derating in electrification stocks despite 
continued earnings upgrades, valuations of the stocks in 
our list look very attractive, with improving AI sentiment 
potentially providing the catalyst for a rerating.
Utilities
Iberdrola: new double-digit earnings growth guidance to 
2030
Iberdrola delivered a strong 9M 2026 performance, with 
net profit rising 10% to EUR 5.8bn and adjusted EBITDA 
increasing 7% to EUR 11.9bn. Growth was led by Networks, 
supported by a larger regulated asset base and higher 
transmission contributions. Total investment increased 13% 
to EUR 10.1bn, including EUR 6.3bn in Networks, lifting 
the regulated asset base by 14% to EUR 56bn. Reflecting 
the strong year-to-date performance, management raised 
FY2026 guidance to at least 10% adjusted net-profit 
growth. Importantly, Iberdrola also introduced new 2030 
ambitions, targeting 10% average annual adjusted net-
profit growth over the next five years, with further details 
due at its March 2027 Capital Markets Day.
Vistra Corp. (VST): Double-digit 2027 EBITDA growth 
expected
Vistra delivered record performance in the third quarter 
of 2026, highlighted by a year-over-year increase of more 
than 50% in adjusted EBITDA to company-record levels, 
driven by generation fleet optimization, strong wholesale 
market execution, and average realized power prices that 
rose by more than 10% per megawatt-hour relative 
to the prior-year period. Year-to-date adjusted EBITDA 
expanded by over 35% compared to the prior-year period, 
enabling management to narrow full-year 2026 adjusted 
EBITDA guidance around a raised midpoint, marking the 
fifth consecutive year finishing above original baseline 
expectations. Looking ahead to 2027, initial guidance 
reflects double-digit percentage adjusted EBITDA growth 
over 2026 midpoints, bolstered by contributions from 
recent generation additions and contracted nuclear off-take 
agreements.
Addressing post-midterm election dynamics, leadership 
emphasized that policy discussions across key competitive 
markets remain focused on implementing structured 
guardrails rather than halting infrastructure investment. 
Management characterized market anxieties over data 
center moratoria as overstated, pointing out that bipartisan 
leadership recognizes the economic necessity of large-scale 
capital deployment while addressing community and grid 
reliability impacts. Vistra reaffirmed its fundamental long-
term demand forecast, projecting robust annual electricity 
demand growth of 4% to 6% in ERCOT and 2% to 
3% in PJM through 2030. Management noted that 
raising interconnection standards and prioritizing high-
probability, take-or-pay behind-the-meter configurations—
such as its Comanche Peak nuclear co-location development
—positions Vistra to capture outsized commercial value as 
speculative queue backlogs are filtered.
Constellation Energy (CEG): Superior execution continues
Constellation delivered exceptional operational and financial 
momentum in the third quarter of 2026, generating 
adjusted operating earnings per share that expanded by 
over 66% year over year. Results benefited significantly 
from high fleet availability during peak summer demand, 
commercial execution across wholesale channels, and 
realized capacity pricing strength across the PJM footprint. 
On the back of this outperformance, management raised 
the midpoint and tightened full-year 2026 adjusted 
operating EPS guidance, setting the newly established 
bottom of the guidance range equal to the top of 
its previous upwardly revised target. This represents the 
company’s fifth consecutive year of beating and raising 
annual financial baselines.

Entergy Corporation (ETR): Electricity demand CAGR of 
9.5% expected through 2030
At the EEI Financial Conference, Entergy delivered a 
comprehensive strategic update reinforcing an industry-
leading rate base and earnings growth profile. Supported 
by unprecedented industrial electrification and data center 
expansion along the Gulf Coast, Entergy presented an 
updated preliminary four-year capital plan through 2030 
that drives an adjusted EPS compound annual growth 
rate (CAGR) exceeding 8% through 2030. The investment 
program is concentrated heavily in regulated generation, 
resilient transmission, and clean energy storage, resulting 
in cumulative rate base expansion of more than 55% from 
2027 through 2030.
A centerpiece of the EEI presentation was Entergy's retail 
sales forecast, projecting a total retail electricity volume 
CAGR of approximately 9.5% through 2030, led by an 
industrial sales CAGR of approximately 17%. Management 
emphasized that this massive load addition is protected 
by constructive regulatory frameworks that incorporate 
minimum take-or-pay demand charges and contribution-in-
aid-of-construction tariffs to shelter residential customers 
from cost shifting. Entergy also detailed a balanced 
financing framework targeting common equity at only 
10% to 15% of the overall capital budget, preserving 
strong investment-grade credit metrics while sustaining an 
assumed 6% annual dividend growth trajectory.
Xcel Energy Inc. (XEL): Electricity demand CAGR of 5% 
expected through 2031
Xcel Energy's EEI Financial Conference presentation outlined 
a compelling total shareholder return thesis of 11% or 
greater, composed of 9% or higher ongoing EPS growth 
through 2031 and an approximate 3% dividend yield 
growing at 4% to 6% annually. The foundation of this 
outlook is an extensive base capital expenditure plan 
through 2031, which drives a consolidated rate base CAGR 
of approximately 11% over the 2026 to 2031 period. 
Rate base growth is distributed strategically across its 
operating subsidiaries, led by Southwestern Public Service 
at an estimated 21% CAGR and Northern States Power 
Minnesota at an estimated 11% CAGR.
A core theme presented to EEI attendees was Xcel’s visibility 
into accelerating commercial and industrial load, projecting 
a consolidated retail electric sales CAGR of roughly 5% 
through 2031, with data center demand alone contributing 
approximately 3 percentage points of that growth. Xcel 
highlighted a contracted and under-construction data 
center pipeline with expected ramps through the early 
2030s, underpinned by protective tariff structures requiring 
75% to 85% minimum bills secured by letters of credit 
or parent guarantees. Management noted that every 
additional increment of growth capital above its base plan 
adds 15 to 20 basis points to its rate base growth rate, 
while average residential electric bills remain approximately 
29% below the national average, insulating customer 
affordability as the clean energy transition advances.
Materials
Freeport-McMoRan (FCX): Grasberg progresses, but 
guidance was mixed
Freeport’s copper sales broadly matching estimates, while 
realized copper prices ticked up to USD 6.54/lb, driving 
strong operating cash flow. Gold sales of 105,000 ounces 
were below the July estimate due to shipment timing in 
Indonesia—as a result, lower by-product credits contributed 
to higher unit net cash costs.
The more important operational development remains 
Grasberg, which is recovering from a mudslide last year. 
The district operated at approximately 67% of capacity in 
3Q, with management targeting around 80% by mid-2027 
and near-full capacity by the end of 2027. The Indonesian 
smelter also restarted in late August—both encouraging 
developments.
Overall, the results combined a continuing recovery with less 
favorable near-term volume assumptions.
Facilitators
Goodman Group (GMG): Leasing catalysts ahead
GMG delivered a decent 1Q FY27 update, with rising 
yield-on-cost, elevated work-in-progress and growing 
AUM supporting a constructive outlook for development 
earnings. Management reaffirmed its +9% operating EPS 
growth guidance for FY27E and the power bank was also 
increased to 6.6GW from 6.4GW. While no new leases were 
announced, management commentary suggests leasing 
negotiations are progressing well in Los Angeles, Hong Kong 
and Amsterdam. New sites are also being planned for 2027, 
and the Sydney capital partnership is expected to close in 
the coming months. Limited leasing activity to date should 
also be viewed in the context of very strong market rental 
growth, with later execution giving GMG the opportunity to 
capture higher prevailing rents. We remain constructive on

Solaris Energy Infrastructure (SEI): 2027 EBITDA expected to 
double from 2026
Solaris demonstrated accelerating operational scale in 
3Q26, delivering record quarterly revenue that increased 
55% sequentially over the second quarter, while record 
adjusted EBITDA rose 26% sequentially and more than 
doubled relative to the prior-year period. Power Solutions 
served as the primary growth engine, with segment 
revenue jumping 79% sequentially and segment adjusted 
EBITDA expanding 32%, propelled by a 20% sequential 
increase in owned generation capacity alongside accretive 
service contributions. Based on this commercial execution, 
management raised its fourth-quarter 2026 adjusted 
EBITDA guidance range by roughly 8% at the midpoint and 
increased its first-quarter 2027 adjusted EBITDA forecast by 
approximately 9% at the midpoint, projecting full-year 2027 
adjusted EBITDA to more than double 2026 levels.
Regarding regulatory and political concerns following the 
midterm elections, Solaris underscored that its unique 
contract structures insulate it from regional permitting 
friction or local grid moratoria. The company’s modular 
behind-the-meter (BTM) generation contracts feature take-
or-pay terms and built-in site flexibility, allowing hyperscale 
and enterprise clients to reallocate power generation 
assets across different geographies as state-level regulatory 
dynamics evolve. Furthermore, management noted that 
grid interconnection bottlenecks and community ratepayer 
protection initiatives serve as direct demand tailwinds for 
flexible, off-grid power solutions. Long-term commercial 
visibility expanded substantially during the period, with 
contracted backlog reaching double-digit billions across a 
weighted average duration of 11 years, anchored by a newly 
executed 15-year, 900-megawatt agreement with a leading 
investment-grade industrial technology customer.

CIO Equity Research Approach
Effective 1 November 2026, CIO does not rate or rank equity securities in the Most Preferred, Bellwether (in the US),

Bank (IB) Global Research issued by our affiliate and evaluate its implications from the perspective of Wealth Management

rating does not affect the application of any legal or regulatory requirement governing investment research, investment 
recommendations or information recommending or suggesting an investment strategy where that requirement otherwise 
applies.
CIO Selected Designation and CIO Selection Lists
CIO Selected stocks are intended to support the UBS House View (see global asset class preferences definitions) and are 
chosen based on their overall risk-return profiles and other relevant considerations. The starting universe for the CIO Selected 
designation generally consists of securities covered by IB Global Research and rated Buy or Neutral. From that universe, CIO 
Equities applies its own investment judgment, considering a variety of factors such as the fundamental outlook, expected 
performance relative to an identified benchmark, market, sector and industry trends, risk and return characteristics, downside 
considerations, diversification benefits, and other relevant factors. No single factor is necessarily determinative. Rather, CIO 
considers these factors collectively and holistically in determining which securities warrant inclusion in a CIO Equity Selection 
List.
In certain circumstances, CIO may designate a security that is not covered by IB Global Research as CIO Selected where 
CIO determines that the security presents a relevant investment opportunity and applicable eligibility, reasonable-basis and 
approval requirements for such selections have been satisfied. A security will no longer be eligible for inclusion in any CIO 
Selection List if IB Global Research changes its rating to Sell. In such cases, CIO Equities will remove it from applicable 
Selection Lists within a reasonable timeframe.
• The CIO Selected designation is not a rating.
• Inclusion reflects CIO Equities' current preference within the defined universe and objective of the applicable Selected List.
• It does not create a separate Buy, Neutral, or Sell rating, does not establish a CIO Equities price target, and does not 
supersede or modify the underlying IB Global Research rating or price target.
• Exclusion or removal of a security for any reason other than a change in IB Global Research rating to “Sell” does not 
indicate a negative investment view or constitute a recommendation to sell.
• Inclusion of a security on a selection list does not constitute initiation of coverage or imply continuous coverage. Likewise, 
removal from a selection list does not constitute termination of coverage.
• Recipients should not assume that a security remains CIO Selected after the date of this publication. The current status 
of a security should be verified using the most recent CIO publication.
A CIO Selected designation reflects CIO Equities’ current, dynamic view within the applicable list’s opportunity set. It is not a 
view on the security’s expected standalone absolute performance. The Selection Lists reflect CIO Equities’ views at a specific 
point in time and are subject to change without notice. CIO strategists will review and update the selections as market 
conditions, investment opportunities, and their assessment of relevant factors evolve.
CIO Equity Portfolios
CIO Equity Portfolios are published research model portfolio comprising stocks with specified weights that reflect each 
security’s allocation within the portfolio and, in aggregate, total 100%. These weights represent portfolio allocations and 
are not active weights or measures of overweight or underweight relative to the benchmark. Portfolios may focus on 
sectors, countries, regions, investment themes, or another stated objective. Each portfolio is constructed relative to an 
identified benchmark, which is used to define the relevant opportunity set, but the portfolio is not designed to replicate the 
benchmark exactly. The CIO view applies to the portfolio in aggregate, including how the holdings work together, rather than 
each individual holding. It is a research model, not a client-specific portfolio, managed investment strategy, or guaranteed 
outcome. CIO Portfolios do not represent actual accounts, trading or investments and performance will not be published.
Portfolio weights are not security-level ratings or rankings and do not express the relative or absolute investment merit, 
expected performance, or degree of conviction associated with any security or issuer. A higher weight should not be 
interpreted as reflecting a more favorable view, greater conviction, or higher expected return.
