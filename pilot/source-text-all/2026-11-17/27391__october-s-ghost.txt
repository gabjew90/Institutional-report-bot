October’s ghost 
◼ 
USD rates’ curve dynamics reflect fears the Fed can fall behind the curve. 
◼ 
A proactive ECB limits the passthrough, but EMU shares similar themes. 
◼ 
Fed speakers, US retail sales and ECB leadership should dominate. 
We recently made the case that USD rates face competing pressures across 
different horizons. Medium-term dovish forces are driven by AI oversight concerns, 
while data centre construction constraints are facing strong US economic 
momentum (NFP, CPI). Looking at market dynamics, last week’s bear steepening 
suggests the short-term aspect has taken over. Robust demand paired with 
persistent supply disruptions (imports and energy) amid a tight labour market point 
toward a stronger expansionary impulse than expected and increase risks that the 
Fed may be lagging in its policy response. Fed Governor Waller advocated 50bp 
insurance hikes if inflation persists, though most FOMC members prefer faster 
tightening starting in January rather than an outsized December move.  
The key theme should remain whether current levels sustain strong growth. The 
six-week rally has room to unwind further and only genuine progress in the Middle 
East or tightening financial conditions would impede further terminal rate increases 
and depricing of subsequent implied cuts. With data suggesting the Fed should 
have hiked in October, this is bound to reverberate towards the long end of the 
curve through a mix of higher Fed trajectory expectation and term premium. EUR 
curves are exposed to these steepening pressures with a beta of roughly 0.5 but 
have more proactive central banks, 2027 GDP growth back to trend and close to 
no implied easing on the curve. This limits the creation of term premium. Still, the 
reversal of prolonged underinvestment through higher capex in defence, 
infrastructures and energy echo a similar theme, albeit with a stronger headwind 
from the fiscal side.  
This week features a high number of Fed speakers, and while a hike is widely 
expected in December, we expect more scepticism from Fed members to be 
enough to put the debate around 50bp to rest. Still, US retail sales data on Tuesday 
will gauge consumer spending amid rising inflation, with modest growth expected 
outside autos and gas. On top of that, we will have some feedback on both the 
ECB and Fed reaction function on Wednesday with the Fed October meeting 
minutes and ECB Financial Stability Review. In Europe, ECB leadership vacancies 
and succession planning for Christine Lagarde should dominate headlines as a 
25bp hike in December is also a given (albeit a bit less than for the Fed), but we 
also look forward to seeing how the new European Commission forecasts on 
Tuesday will balance out the outlook between the local capex cycles and the 
breadth of fiscal situations in the bloc.
