In this note, we discuss key investor questions ahead of NKE’s 
November 17th Investor Day, frame potential five-year growth, 
margin, and earnings outcomes, and refresh our trackers across 
pricing/promotions, China, brand momentum, traffic, and digital 
engagement. 
Bottom line: We expect investor focus to center on the company’s 
five-year financial algorithm and, importantly, the timing and 
magnitude of the revenue and earnings recovery following the FY27 
reset. FY27 revenue is guided down HSD, with deliberate actions in 
Sportswear, Jordan, and Greater China expected to pressure results 
through FY27 and into FY28. With revenue expected to remain 
under pressure in the near term, we believe the cadence and 
believability of margin trajectory will be critical for the stock. 
Additionally, we believe management’s commentary on product 
innovation and the pipeline for sportswear will be a key driver of 
stock performance at the event.  
Key Debates 
Is FY27 the trough? What is the cadence and path for NKE to 
n
return to sustainable growth? We expect management to 
present the 5-yr algorithm as a series of phases, with the first 
phase in FY27 and early FY28 the reset, and then a phased 
improvement for stabilization and return to growth. However, 
key within this debate is the magnitude of anticipated financial 
pressure in FY28 as carryover impacts from the China and 
Jordan reset continue to weigh on performance, and as larger 
benefits from NKE’s cost savings program PACE scale into FY29 
and FY30. On this, we are cautious on the pace of near-term 
improvement and expect more meaningful earnings recovery to 
be in the later phases of the plan (GS FY28 EPS $1.20 vs. FactSet 
consensus at $1.49).

How long will the Sportswear, Jordan, and China resets take? Management has 
n
acknowledged these remain the key problem areas, with Jordan and China expected 
to have a longer tail. In Sportswear, Nike is broadening the portfolio beyond a 
concentrated set of franchises; in Jordan, the company is reducing supply and 
launch frequency to restore scarcity while adding innovation and newness; and in 
China, the digital and marketplace cleanup is expected to take multiple seasons. We 
look for greater clarity on the timing and milestones for improvement across each business 
at the event, including the impacts of each to profitability vs the rest of the business. 
What are the most important drivers of margin expansion (gross margin vs. 
n
SG&A), and how much will PACE contribute? FY27 EBIT is expected to decline 
faster than revenue given gross-margin pressure and fixed-cost deleverage. Against 
this backdrop, PACE represents a $2.5bn run-rate savings opportunity, with benefits 
ramping over time and the largest savings expected in FY29-FY30 before full 
realization into FY31; opportunities span both SG&A and gross margin, including 
supply-chain optimization. We expect focus on the cadence of savings, 
reinvestment, and ultimately what level of operating margin Nike believes the 
business can support. We expect NKE to chart the course to a double-digit EBIT margin 
over time at the event.  
Can improving Performance momentum broaden into a more durable brand 
n
inflection? Performance grew HSD in F1Q, with double-digit growth across Running, 
Global Football, Tennis, and Golf, and management continues to point to innovation 
and the Sport Offense as proof points that the strategy can work. However, 
Performance remains insufficient to offset pressure in Sportswear, Jordan, and 
Greater China. We look for greater clarity on how Nike plans to scale these 
performance-category successes while rebuilding lifestyle demand and brand heat, 
including the sustainability of the sportswear franchises that are currently working 
and how reliant NKE is on their continued success to deliver the plan. We believe 
product innovation and insight into NKE’s forward product strategy will be closely watched 
by investors at the event.  
Framing potential growth and margin algorithm 
Assuming Nike provides a five-year financial framework, we sensitize revenue growth and EBIT 
margin outcomes to frame potential FY31 earnings power and valuation. With FY27 revenue 
already guided down HSD, our revenue analysis assumes a -8% decline and considers a range of 
FY28 growth outcomes and FY29-FY31 revenue CAGRs. We then assess the EBIT margin 
required to achieve $2.00-$2.50 of FY31 EPS, as well as potential EPS outcomes across a range 
of revenue growth and margin scenarios. Our analysis highlights that the lower base and 
elongated recovery make a meaningful five-year top-line CAGR more difficult to achieve, 
increasing the importance of margin recovery to the longer-term earnings algorithm.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
