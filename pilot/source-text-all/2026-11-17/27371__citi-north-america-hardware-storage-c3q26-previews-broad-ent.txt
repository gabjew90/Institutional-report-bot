C3Q26 Previews: Broad Enterprise Demand & AI Buildouts Outweigh 
PC Softness; Prefer DELL, HPE, P
CITI'S TAKE 
Hardware Enterprise Infrastructure OEMs should see strong results given 
broader refresh cycle across enterprise infrastructure, corroborated by Citi’s 
3Q CIO survey, VAR checks (see inside), constructive comments from OEMs 
and supple chain (Cisco, Arista, CDW). AI infrastructure demand appears 
similarly robust per constructive commentary from SMCI, Coreweave, TSMC, 
Taiwan supply chain, Wistron conveying confident outlooks. Meanwhile, 
peak-AI-demand concerns coupled with “over-earning” fears (pull forward 
demand, memory prices stabilizing to pressure earnings) have drastically 
compressed multiples and share prices during the quarter.  PCs have also 
weakened on negative IDC datapoints and Intel earnings. We lean 
constructive on HPE and DELL noting pent-up enterprise infrastructure 
demand, healthy AI backlogs, operational/supply-chain efficiencies to 
navigate volatility.  Also constructive on P given improving profitability and 
ramping hyperscale exposure.  
DELL — Reports Nov 24th. We remain constructive on Dell shares, supported by 
continued strength in AI and traditional servers and improving storage execution, 
reinforcing our view that infrastructure demand remains durable, offsetting weak PC 
demand. With shares down more than 30% and valuation relative to growth 
expectations approaching COVID-era trough levels, we believe the stock is already 
discounting significant EPS compression in the outer years, creating an attractive 
risk/reward profile.  
HPE — Reports Nov 23rd. We remain constructive on HPE shares, supported by 
healthy enterprise infrastructure spending, strong networking demand, improving 
storage trends, while better supply availability supports backlog conversion. With 
management targeting a return to buybacks in F4Q, supported by strong FCF 
generation, we see an attractive shareholder return profile. 
NTAP — Reports Dec 1st. Positive enterprise storage demand datapoints, balanced 
with continued product margin pressure and limited share gains. Maintain Neutral.  
HPQ — Reports Nov 24th. Expect 4Q rev below normal seasonal patterns with Print 
inline seasonal and PC segment below. Despite conservative PC guide, we 
contemplate more cautious PC outlook, while cost mitigations, op-efficiency 
improvements, and ongoing mix-to-premium are offsets to component inflation. 
Maintain Neutral.
We separately previewed Everpure, reporting Nov 18th, with a Positive Catalyst 
Watch on demand momentum in enterprise storage spending, improving 
profitability plus ramping hyperscaler opportunity. 
– We engaged Citi’s Research Innovation lab to collect data for our reseller survey 
which helps gauge spending trends on OEM hardware offerings.

3Q26 Citi VAR Survey Recap 
Intra-quarter, we conducted our reseller survey (N=54) in collaboration with Citi’s 
Innovation Lab to gauge customer spending trends on OEM hardware offerings. 
VAR sentiment continues to indicate a mixed and generally cautious hardware 
environment (albeit moderating from prior qtr), where we note overall quota 
performance sentiment in the Sept qtr indicated selective slight shifts to improved 
sales performance vs June qtr responses.
n AI servers are consistently viewed as the strongest area of demand, with ongoing 
momentum and positive expectations from VARs.
n Traditional server demand is holding steady, supported by routine refresh cycles 
and increasing AI‑related workloads applied to general-purpose servers. We 
note NTM segment performance expectations have again improved slightly vs 
prior qtr survey on the rise of CPU usage for agentic AI workloads, however, like 
other HW segments, it is still sensitive to rising component costs – however we 
note, vs prior qtr, respondents indicate a relatively less negative impact to NTM 
sales expectations due to pricing impacts.
n Storage demand continues to track inline-to-slightly-positive vs prior qtr, with 
VARs describing it as relatively stable despite some component‑driven pressure, 
while pricing impacts to NTM sales expectations appear to have moderated 
slightly from prior qtr as company data modernization efforts progress. We also 
note a slightly improved NTM spending preference for Cloud and Subscription 
storage solutions vs onprem.
n PC demand is broadly in line with expectations as commercial refresh activity 
and the Windows 11 cycle keep volumes steady, however component cost 
inflation continues to weigh on demand and purchase delays, resulting in an 
overall cautious PC outlook inline with our view.
We note continued improvement of spreads between NTM and LTM across the 
board which suggests overall improvement in VAR expectations, while the sample 
size in this round is +30% higher, at 54, up from 41 in prior qtr. The current NTM 
spending view for all our surveyed product brands points to 1-5pts of improvement 
vs the LTM spending view. Overall, VARs continue to characterize the environment 
as one where refresh cycles, AI buildout, and ongoing underlying infrastructure 
needs are providing the support. This is partially offset by customer caution (albeit 
slightly moderated from 2Q levels) given persistent price increases and the pressure 
it places on IT budgets, where the palatability of ongoing pricing increases continue 
to be tested vs demand.

3Q26 CIO Survey Recap 
Citi’s latest CIO survey (Global Technology & Communications - Citi CIO Survey: IT 
Budgets Improve; AI Continues To Gain Wallet Share) highlighted that CIOs expect 
IT budgets to grow +3.4% over the next twelve months, improving slightly from 
June and remaining above historical averages. The key change this quarter was 
regional divergence, with U.S. expectations rising to +3.9%, while EMEA declined 
to +1.8% amid worsening macro sentiment. Encouragingly, 41% of respondents 
reported upward budget revisions over the past three months, up meaningfully 
from 28% last quarter, while the average revision among companies adjusting 
budgets increased to +2.7%, suggesting spending trends continue to improve 
despite macro uncertainty.
According to survey respondents, on an NTM vs LTM basis spending across all 
hardware categories is trending higher on a NTM basis. Pricing is driving a 
meaningful impact on all hardware category spend, including PCs where we expect 
meaningful unit declines in the next few quarters. 
As it relates to Gen AI impacts on hardware budgets, the survey notes only a 
quarter of respondents are not expecting it to impact IT Hardware budgets, (down 
from 45% prior) while 54% noted an increase in their hardware budgets from GenAI 
impacts compared to 33% previously. 22% noted a decrease, largely flattish 
compared to 21% prior. To us, this suggests GenAI is emerging as a more 
meaningful impact to IT hardware budgets.
In terms of pricing adjustments impacting NTM budgets, weighted-average 
revisions increased across all hardware categories, led by storage and networking.  
We note that AI server spend may be increasingly funded through other 
AI/modernization budgets rather than core IT allocations, which likely understates 
the full extent of pricing uplift in the survey’s responses.
54% of survey respondents reported they are likely to run their LLM workloads on 
public cloud followed by 25% Hybrid and 21% Private cloud/On Prem.  This 
compares to prior results of 53% of survey respondents reported they are likely to 
run their LLM workloads on public cloud followed by 27% Hybrid and 20% Private 
cloud/on-prem. For those preferring to shift workloads to private dedicated 
servers, cost consideration and performance increased meaningfully vs 2Q26 
survey. To us, this suggests that while public cloud retains its structural offsets 
around scale efficiencies, costs and performance/latency are increasingly top of 
mind.

External Enterprise Storage Market Outlook
According to IDC data, the External Enterprise Storage market grew +34%yy to 
$10.3B in 2Q, marking the second consecutive qtr of sharp acceleration. Dell 
continued to take top share at 24% (down from 31% in 1Q and up from 22% a year 
ago) with +43%yy growth, followed by NetApp at 10% share (flat from 1Q and up 
from 9% a year ago) and +36%yy growth in the qtr. Everpure rounds out the top 3 at 
10% share (flat from 1Q and up from 9% a year ago) with +50%yy growth. Three 
factors continue to drive the recent strength: delayed enterprise storage refreshes, 
rising AI-related storage demand on AI transition to inferencing, and customers 
pulling forward purchases ahead of component cost increases. All-flash arrays are 
the biggest beneficiary, as AI inference, analytics, and unstructured data workloads 
increasingly favor flash over hybrid and HDD-based systems. However, given 
significant pricing headwinds, many customers are choosing more  cost-effective 
hybrid solutions in the meantime as pricing begins to stabilize.
IDC maintains a constructive outlook on enterprise storage, with storage spending 
expected to benefit from: 1) continued AI-driven demand for storage as AI projects 
mature, 2) a recovery of storage investments that were earlier deferred while 
companies prioritized AI compute spending, and 3) higher storage system pricing, 
which is providing a significant revenue tailwind. 
Similarly, our recent checks point to improving enterprise storage demand, while 
revenue growth is being driven more by pricing than by capacity growth. Customers 
continue to need storage, especially as AI workloads move from infrastructure 
buildout toward real-world deployment and inference, though budget constraints 
have limited how much capacity they can purchase. As a result, vendors are 
increasingly emphasizing storage efficiency technologies such as deduplication 
and compression to help customers do more with less capacity.
Lastly, we note software-defined storage (SDS) is seeing increasing adoption as 
organizations pursue hybrid cloud strategies that require unified data management 
across on-premises and cloud environments, as addressed by NetApp and 
Everpure (also note our recent mgmt mtgs) at their respective recent investor 
events. The market is growing rapidly, at roughly 27% CAGR, according to IDC, 
although we note much of this expansion occurs outside traditional enterprise 
storage system (ESS) tracking and therefore is not fully reflected in reported 
storage market growth.

Networking Market Outlook
Overall networking demand remains exceptionally strong and is increasingly being 
driven by both AI infrastructure buildouts and a broad-based enterprise refresh 
cycle. Arista Networks (ANET) reported record revenue growth of 58.5% year over 
year in Q3 2026, citing robust demand for AI networking fabrics alongside 
accelerating enterprise campus spending, while highlighting growing needs for 
secure, highly automated networks capable of supporting AI and agentic 
workloads. Meanwhile, Cisco Systems (CSCO) delivered its strongest revenue and 
earnings growth in more than 15 years and raised its FY27 outlook, reflecting 
sustained momentum across networking markets. Cisco noted that networking 
product orders grew 28% year over year, enterprise product orders increased 15%, 
and campus networking orders rose 14%, with the campus refresh cycle still in its 
early stages and less than 10% penetrated.
Insights from Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE)'s Investor Day intra quarter 
reinforce this constructive demand backdrop. HPE expects the overall networking 
market to grow at an 18% CAGR through 2029, driven by particularly strong growth 
in data center networking and routing, while identifying more than a $100 billion 
enterprise networking refresh opportunity tied to Wi-Fi 7 adoption, AI-enabled 
network operations, and infrastructure modernization. Taken together, 
commentary from Arista, Cisco, and HPE suggests networking is benefiting from a 
multi-year upgrade cycle in which AI deployment, higher traffic intensity, security 
requirements, and campus modernization are driving sustained investment across 
both enterprise and hyperscale customers.

PC Market Outlook
Worldwide PC shipments fell 20.1%yy and 9.1%qq to 62.7M units in Q3 2026, 
marking a second consecutive decline that upended typical 3Q seasonal growth. 
The downturn was primarily caused by aggressive 1H inventory pull-in by vendors 
and channels ahead of memory-driven price hikes, combined with ongoing supply 
constraints and elevated prices spurred by AI data center expansion. Leading 
vendors Lenovo, HP Inc., and Dell Technologies all experienced steeper-than-
market shipment contractions, while worsening macroeconomic headwinds 
continue to cloud the demand outlook for late 2026 and 2027.
Datapoints from Taiwan ODMs and PC Supply chain: For NB ODMs, the Citi 
Taiwan HW Team notes that overall NB shipments saw a MoM decrease in Oct (-
27% MoM) due to the high base in Sep and the muted outlook for end PC demand. 
Given traditional slow 4Q season, slow end demand and the increasing constraint 
in components procurement, Citi observed major NB ODMs starting to revise down 
its shipment forecast into 4Q26. Citi now forecasts 4Q26E NB shipments to 
further decline -9% QoQ/-29% YoY. While the price hike effect in the NB products 
should partially offset shipment decline, the team expects increasing margins 
dilution impact to the NB ODM supply chains.

Print Demand Outlook
3Q Print industry results pointed to -7.8%yy total industry unit and -8.7%yy 
revenue decline on industry ASP decrease of approx. -0.9%yy, reflecting broad-
based weakness across consumer and office print markets. Unit share gainers yy in 
the qtr are HP Inc. (+3.6pts), Canon (+3.9pts), Epson (+0.7pt) and Xerox (+0.3pt), 
while Ricoh lost 0.1pt. Meanwhile, Canon earnings cited expectations of continued 
FY26 print unit declines, with slight improvement in laser (on improved 
functionalities) and inkjet (on ink tank), while office MFP expectations ticked down 
from prior qtr expectations.
IDC attributes recent declines primarily to the ongoing Middle East conflict, related 
energy-cost pressures, broader economic uncertainty, and vendor-specific supply 
chain disruptions that are reducing shipments below already-soft demand levels.
Looking ahead, IDC expects print hardware demand to remain weak through the 
rest of 2026, with supply chains improving but geopolitical risks, high inflation, 
elevated interest rates, and potential DRAM shortages continuing to pressure 
spending and shipments.
One recent notable bright spot was ink-tank printers, whose shipments grew 9.8% 
yy (as of 2Q latest data) and increased their share of the U.S. color inkjet market to 
29.3%, reinforcing IDC’s view that ink-tank devices will continue gaining share 
from traditional cartridge-based products.

Dell Technologies (DELL.N)
DELL is scheduled to report OctQ results on Nov 24th. We expect OctQ 
revenues/EPS of $49.7B (+84%yy)/$6.75 (+161%yy) vs Street of $49.2B/$6.56, 
respectively, vs guidance of $49B +/- $0.5B/ $6.50 +/- $0.10, respectively. We 
expect JanQ revenues/EPS of $53.7B (+61%yy)/$7.51 (+93%yy) vs Street of 
$53.5B/$7.45, respectively. 
We remain constructive on DELL, supported by sustained strength in both AI and 
traditional servers, alongside improving storage execution, reinforcing our view 
that infrastructure demand remains durable. We view management’s guidance 
posture as appropriately prudent, with potential upside driven by continued AI 
infrastructure deployment and an enterprise infrastructure refresh cycle that 
remains intact. While we remain cautious on the CSG segment and believe softer 
PC demand is largely captured in current guidance, we see incremental upside 
potential in Storage, where second-half revenue trends could exceed expectations 
based on recent commentary from storage component suppliers. We also expect 
continued strength across AI and traditional server demand, supported by positive 
server demand commentary from Intel and AMD. That said, the growing mix of AI 
servers is likely to remain a partial headwind to margins. With shares down more 
than 30% and valuation relative to our growth estimates approaching COVID-era 
trough levels, we believe the stock is already discounting substantial EPS 
compression in the outer years. In our view, current valuation implies a much 
weaker earnings trajectory than management’s long-term demand outlook and 
recent infrastructure spending trends would suggest, creating an increasingly 
attractive risk/reward profile. Reflecting broader multiple compression across 
traditional hardware peers, we lower our target multiple to 16x P/E from 18x and 
revise our target price to $550, while maintaining our Buy rating and constructive 
stance on the shares.
Server could see continued demand strength, however offset by lower margins. 
Despite general industry concern surrounding AI spend given Dell’s neocloud 
exposure, positive datapoints from Coreweave, Super Micro, Taiwan supply chain, 
Wistron highlight sustained robust AI infrastructure demand. At Citi’s Technology 
Conference in September, management cited broad-based  infrastructure demand 
supported by AI systems, traditional servers, and storage with AI deal pipeline of 
multiples of its $95B backlog. Enterprise AI is growing meaningfully (Dell noted 
increasing customer count in the last quarter  of 6500 vs 5000 the quarter prior), 
while traditional server demand continues to be driven by modernization, 
inference, security, and resiliency. As Dell aggressively shifts its revenue mix toward 
AI-optimized servers, these systems, while driving significant top-line growth, 
carry lower gross margin than traditional general-purpose infrastructure or storage.  
While management has leveraged disciplined pricing and operational efficiencies 
to offset some of this margin degradation, they aim to balance substantial AI 
demand with protection of long-term profitability.
Additionally, traditional server demand is supported by a still-meaningful installed

Investment strategy
We rate Dell shares Buy. Within the overall enterprise hardware space, we 
believe Dell’s broad exposure, size, and scale should enable it to outperform 
broader IT spending trends. We believe Dell’s leading share in commercial PCs 
as well as across several of its product categories (servers, storage 
infrastructure) should drive top-line growth at least in line with the core 
markets it participates in, with AI and AI PC offerings further strengthening 
Dell's moat in these core markets. Additionally, we expect FCF margins to 
remain at normalized levels at 100% FCF/NI conversions, which should aid in 
increasing its level of shareholder returns.

its AI portfolio as well as their disciplined execution and supply chain 
management.
