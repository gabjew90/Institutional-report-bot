Status quo
We see risks to oil prices skewed higher near term, as a
diplomatic breakthrough in US-Iran tensions remains out of
reach and the market is not positioned for a continuation of
the status quo.
• Notwithstanding the further weakening in oil prices since our last update, we maintain our
view that market participants seem a bit too sanguine about the balance of risks for oil prices
in the near term. Diplomacy is ongoing but an imminent deal seems unlikely. In addition,
Middle East oil flows have been volatile, demand continues to recover and positioning
remains subdued.
• The recent widening in WTI-WCS spread is not indicative of a surge in supply, as the location
differential remains steady, as do production and trade statistics. Shipping costs, upgrader
and refinery downtime and SPR releases have been the key factors driving the spread of late
in our view. We maintain our price forecasts and reiterate our long calendar spreads
recommendation in Brent futures.
Oil prices remain under pressure with front-month Brent and WTI futures both down ~7% since
our update last week when we argued that market participants seem a bit too sanguine about
the balance of risks for oil prices. Notwithstanding the price action, the incremental data
published since all still point in the same direction and we therefore maintain our view that risks
to oil prices are skewed higher in the near term.
First, the recent increase in attacks on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has weighed on
the recovery in Middle East oil flows. Direct net exports through the Strait averaged 8.9 mb/d in
the week-ending 31 October. Ship-to-ship transfers and pipeline bypassing helped take that
number to 17.7 mb/d, which is down 23% from pre-war levels. There was a brief period around
mid-October when Middle East flows exceeded pre-war levels despite the blockade of
Iran (Figure 1).
More importantly, on a 30-day moving average basis, Middle East oil flows stood at around 19.4
mb/d on 31 October, or 85% of pre-war levels. Yet, while inventories have stabilized, they have
not shown signs of a meaningful increase so far. In fact, ex-China inventories remain
significantly depressed (Figure 2) with the release of strategic buffers largely behind us. That
suggests to us that a continuation of the recent status-quo, all-else equal, not only comes with a
fatter right tail than in the past, but also poses upside risks to oil prices in and of itself.

about the fading of the war risk premium but also an increase in expectations that a potential
