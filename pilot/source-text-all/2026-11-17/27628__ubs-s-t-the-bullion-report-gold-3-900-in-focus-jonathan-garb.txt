The Bullion Report: Gold $3,900 In Focus
Jonathan Garber
13-Nov-2026, 09:25 ET (EST)
• Gold: Macro headwinds persist, but underlying support remains firm
• Silver: ETF demand remains a relative bright spot
• PGMs: Price gains lack fundamental confirmation
• Electronic Trading: Retail gains share as bank activity eases
Gold is still being driven much more by the macro backdrop than by anything specific to precious metals, with
the recent pullback consistent with a more hawkish Fed. For now, the setup still looks vulnerable. If the Fed
maintains that stance and the market starts to price in further tightening, including the possibility of a 50bp
move in December, there is still room for gold to move lower. The challenge is trading around Asia, where
thin liquidity means relatively small flows can produce outsized moves without much of a fundamental catalyst.
Geopolitics could change that picture, although there is a big difference between another positive headline and
an actual signed agreement. It has been seen how quickly markets can react to encouraging headlines and
then give the move back when the story is questioned or nothing concrete follows. A genuine agreement
would be different and could bring fresh money into gold. If that happens, an initial $50-$70 move may only be
the first reaction rather than the full extent of the rally. Until there is something more concrete, though, the Fed
and rates backdrop probably keeps the risk of a move through the $3,900 area alive.
Clients generally seem to want to be bullish, but there is not enough conviction yet for them to put
meaningful expensive capital to work. That makes sense given how much attention is still focused on rates,
the dollar, oil, AI and equities. What is more encouraging is that existing holders have been fairly sticky.
Gold ETFs have held up surprisingly well through another pullback, although cracks are starting to show,
with redemptions over the past few days. Meanwhile, silver ETF holdings added roughly 12.1 mn ounces
month-on-month. The real test will be what happens on this double test of $3,900. If ETF investors continue to
hold through that kind of move, it would be a meaningful sign that the underlying support is stronger than
broader institutional positioning suggests.
There are some encouraging signs in GLD and the options market as well. Two sizeable GLD transactions
stood out, including roughly 55,000 lots of a call spread, equivalent to an estimated 70,000 gold delta OTC.
One trade on its own does not change the picture, but repeated transactions of that size can start to create a
meaningful underlying bid. Some of the spot buying may simply be dealers hedging delta rather than
investors buying gold outright, which would help explain why gold has been relatively slow to react to dollar
strength but quick to give back headline-driven rallies. Even so, someone is clearly willing to pay for upside
exposure. If more of these structures appear, they could help put a floor under the market, although another
washout on a retest of the lows is still possible.
Physical demand in Asia is still there, just noticeably quieter than it was in October. OTC forward liquidity has
also improved, which feels more like a normalization than the start of a new physical-demand story. There is
still interest underneath the market, but it remains selective, with buyers looking for the right levels rather
than chasing price. Swiss physical activity is also very light, with more of the interest currently coming from
Asia.

Singapore is also becoming a story worth watching. At roughly 500 tonnes, the potential scale is large enough
to raise real questions about how a bigger Singapore gold hub could affect London, COMEX and the rest of
Asia. If Singapore and Hong Kong ultimately pull more metal out of London, that could have implications for
London liquidity and regional premiums. If more of the metal comes directly from COMEX, the effect could
look very different. There is also an open question around bar sizes. Singapore has traditionally been a kilobar
market, but if central banks and other official institutions become an important part of the hub, large bars would
make much more sense for reserve buying. Over time, one of the most useful signals will be the price
official-sector buyers are willing to pay for gold in Singapore relative to Hong Kong, London and New York.
Palladium and platinum have seen good moves off the early-November lows, but the fundamentals have not
really caught up with the price action. Palladium was up around 6%, yet the forwards market tightened by
only about 5-10bp, which is not especially dramatic relative to the move in spot. The reduction in
Sibanye-Stillwater's production forecast also looks too small to explain it, with the roughly 40koz reduction
across platinum and palladium amounting to only around 0.3% of global production. Platinum yields have
moved more noticeably, particularly in London, where they are up roughly 100bp, although lease activity may
explain some of that rather than a genuine structural squeeze. There has also been selective demand for
upside options, including a roughly four-month 1,700/1,800 call spread. The bigger issue for both metals is
simply liquidity. These are thin markets, and relatively modest flows can create very large moves, so it is
probably too early to read the recent price action as the start of a new fundamental trend.
