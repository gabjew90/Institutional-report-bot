A week with APEC consequences? 
 
Global Daily 
Market comment 
A third US aircraft carrier has arrived in the Middle East, and Israeli representatives have met with 
US officials to discuss new strikes. However, President Trump has reportedly told his advisers that 
he may not attack Iran unless there are any new provocations. That may partly have been at the 
request of Saudi Arabia and Türkiye, who have urged Trump not to resume fighting. 
Since then, the Houthis claim they have targeted an airport and financial centre in Riyadh, and 
Tehran launched a drone strike at a US base in Bahrain. Iran states that this was a response to 
hostile US actions, and Ghalibaf repeated that his country is still willing to discuss a peace deal if 
the US accepts Iran’s “logical conditions.” 
But the Iranian negotiator added that Tehran will not remain in a state of “neither war nor peace,” 
while Deputy Minister Gharibabadi suggested that the White House is using the diplomatic efforts 
to buy time for another attack. He added that Iran may “not wait for the US to attack first.” 
The Houthis, meanwhile, have forced the retreat of Yemen’s forces, which means that they now 
control the Bab al-Mandeb strait again. The Houthis have said they are no threat to international 
shipping, and that they will continue to target only Saudi Arabian vessels.   
Still, these tensions have lifted oil off its recent lows. Oil prices were also supported by reports 
that up to five ships may have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz – although the UK Maritime 
Maritime Trade Operations Centre has confirmed just one attack.  
While the world is looking at the developments in the Middle East, epic geopolitical and economic 
shifts may follow from the APEC summit, as the US and China fight for global influence. 
Presidents Trump, Xi, and Putin will hold a trilateral meeting on the sidelines of the summit. 
Comparisons with the Yalta-conference are easily made, but the meeting may not lead to 
immediate results. The three sides have reportedly not agreed on an agenda. China has so far 
avoided to take an active role in the Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations, so that topic may not be 
on top of the list of talking points. Meanwhile, US-China tensions are still high when it comes to 
trade and protectionism.  
Defence and protectionism will probably also dominate a separate, one-on-one meeting between 
Trump and Xi later this week.  
The US and China are trying to finalise a military accident-prevention agreement ahead of the 
summit in Shenzhen, but progress is reportedly slow due to differing opinions within the US 
administration. A US agreement with Japan, Australia and the Philippines to set up a defence 
coordination centre in the region both increases the necessity of such a US-China accord and 
complicates the negotiations.  
That’s not the only epic development to complicate the meeting between Presidents Trump and 
Xi. The ‘trade truce’ between the US and China is set to expire in January. The US has already 
accused China of not meeting its side of the deal. Rare earth minerals are not flowing into the US 
at the pace Washington desires. And last week, the House China Select Committee released a 
report that suggests China has undercut and monopolized various other industries.

The affair between Nvidia’s former-CEO Huang and an alleged Chinese spy only spells more 
trouble for the trade negotiations. Huang’s fall from grace accelerates US efforts to further tighten 
export controls of AI chips. The revelations are creating momentum in Congress, with both 
Democrats and Republicans throwing their support behind a series of bills that limit the overseas 
sales of advanced chips.  
Washington isn’t moving on its own. Several US allies have agreed to create a joint list of key 
technologies and AI supply lines that need more protection or diversification from China. The US 
is reportedly planning to nominate tens of companies as “national champions” to support the 
adoption of US artificial intelligence over Chinese technology. Those moves are bound to anger 
Beijing.  
But the plans could also be pivotal for equity markets, as the AI trade tries to regain footing after 
the drama that surrounds the former Nvidia executive – who is now eyeing a potential comeback 
while US Treasury Secretary Bessent calls for a “dramatic shake-up” at the company. The 
Anthropic IPO may prove to be the next test for AI appetite. 
Week ahead 
Today sees the publication of Eurozone industrial production, the US empire manufacturing index 
and Canadian inflation.  
It’s a busy week for central bank officials. Today kicks off with ECB Vice President Vujcic, followed 
by his colleagues Kocher, Zigman and –after the European close– Stournaras. The Fed’s Barr will 
deliver prepared remarks on AI and financial stability, Williams opens the FX market structure 
conference, and FOMC Chair Warsh appears in a panel and fireside chat at the Fed’s conference 
on productivity and jobs. 
Tuesday starts off with statistics on UK wages, followed by the final estimate of Italian CPI and the 
German ZEW survey of investor confidence. The US retail sales for October are the main event of 
the day. 
The ECB’s Moulin, Cipollone, and Lane speak. The Fed’s Musalem, Waller, and Hammack deliver 
prepared remarks, and Williams speaks in a fireside chat. 
On Wednesday, the UK and Eurozone publish inflation for October. Don’t expect Eurozone HICP 
inflation to deviate much from the preliminary estimate of 4.0%, but we will get more details 
about the underlying components. US housing starts and the minutes of the October FOMC 
meeting complete the calendar. 
Another six ECB officials are due to speak: Lagarde, Vujcic, Nagel, Escriva, Dolenc, and Kocher. The 
Bank of England’s Bailey, Bank of Canada’s Gravelle and the FOMC’s Barr, Williams and Kashkari 
are all also scheduled to make an appearance. 
The APEC summit kicks off today. 
Thursday’s theme is labour markets, with Eurozone labour costs and US weekly jobless claims 
statistics. The Federal reserve banks of Philadelphia and Kansas City publish their business surveys. 
The ECB’s Lagarde and Elderson, BoE’s Dhingra, Fed’s Hammack, Goolsbee, Barr (again), Barkin 
and Paulson speak. 
On Friday, Japanese CPI inflation, UK and Canadian retail sales, and consumer confidence in the 
EU and US are due. But markets will probably focus on Eurozone negotiated wages to confirm 
that inflationary pressures are not becoming more persistent yet. 
Friday sees more repeat appearances from rate setters. The ECB’s Lagarde, Cipollone and Nagel 
will all deliver remarks. The Fed’s Waller and Cook will also take to the stage.
