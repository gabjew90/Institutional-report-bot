Harry defends ams: ams-OSRAM shares were down 10% on a solid set of results, likely due to some
disappointment around the Q4 revenue guide and limited new updates on the new revenue opportunities. At
these levels we believe there is upside to the shares simply from the continued balance sheet improvements,

Results did not provide any major updates on the new revenue opportunities but confirmed ams-OSRAM's
intention to supply the complete optical solution from emitter to packaging. We believe this puts them in a
strong position from a competitive standpoint and will also enable them to capture a larger share of the bill of
materials. With InP laser optics based suppliers now booked out to 2029, we expect an acceleration in
adoption of alternative solutions, which should be supportive of faster revenue generation than expected for
ams-OSRAM. We believe this could represent an >€1bn op by 2030, with revenues as early as 2027.
On Technoprobe: Q4 guide had a €50m impact from issues with a new ERP system, which accounts for 2
weeks of sales in the quarter and bridges the gap vs. consensus. Whilst unusual, we do not see this as
unreasonable for a company that is preparing for a revenue base 4x vs. two years ago, with which there are
bound to be teething issues. Importantly this revenue is pushed into Q1'27 and should deliver a strong start to
2027.
Diversifying customer base into 2027
The custom ASIC opportunity has ramped significantly in recent quarters with Technoprobe expecting to have
majority of new platforms ramping next year and requiring vertical MEMS probes. One HBM customer has
been qualified, while the two others are still in progress. €30m revenue is expected in 2027 from this single
customer, with the opportunity increasing more meaningfully in 2028 with HBM5e and other customers
potentially qualifying.
Further capacity ramp through 2027 supports €2bn of revenue
The company announced a further €150-200m capacity expansion plan from Q2-Q4'27 which should support
revenue of €2bn. Given Technoprobe's proven ability to quickly ramp capacity, we see scope for further
increases through the year. Assuming a gradual ramp of capacity, our estimate for 2027 assumes 85%
utilisation rate through the year, which we see as very conservative given the demand environment.

At last year's CMD (link), Dassault set an ambition to grow revenues at a base compound rate of 7-8%
between 2024 and 2029. This had upside potential of an incremental €1bn in cumulative revenues over the
period from the UNIV+RSES AI suite through a combination of direct monetisation and driving more customers
to 3DExperience, notably in the cloud. Given that when the CMD took place the guidance was for 3-5% growth
that year and the actual outcome was 4%, it obviously anticipated an acceleration thereafter.
Factoring in 2025's results and the guidance for 4-5% growth in 2026, we estimate that the ambition now
implies a 9-13% CAGR over the next three years with ArisGlobal contributing about 80-90bp if it grows at the
10%pa rate anticipated at acquisition. If we assume that UNIV+RSES ramps from €200m in revenues in 2027
through €300m in 2028 to €500m in 2029, this could account for around 200bp of the growth, meaning the
underlying growth expected is about 8-10% (Figure 2). With Medidata stabilising revenue trends in Q3 (Figure
3) and expected to progress further in Q4 and head toward low-to-mid single-digit growth in 2026, this will

revenues and in 2026 shrinks at low single digits.
Notwithstanding the slight boost to growth rates that Medidata's recovery will bring, we see the 8-10% organic
pre-UNIV+RSES ambition as challenging, but believe management may leave it unchanged with future M&A
optionality potentially whittling away at the target. In any case, consensus appears sceptical on the delivery of

the base of Dassault's ambition (Figure 6).
While Dassault communicated an ambition to increase its margins by 40-60bp a year over 2024-29, 2025's
margin was stable on 2024's and notwithstanding the recently raised guidance for a 60-80bp increase in 2026
it leaves Dassault needing to deliver 50- 70bp a year on average for the next three years.
