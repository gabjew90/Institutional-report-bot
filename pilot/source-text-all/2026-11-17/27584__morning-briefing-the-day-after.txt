OUTLOOK 
If the Fed had raised rates for a second time before the midterms, it wouldn’t need to have a 
debate about tightening by half a point in December “to ensure its credibility”, to cite FOMC 
member Waller. Is that the short and long story of the CPI print yesterday? The 18bp spike in 2y 
yields to 4.73% and 10bp in 10s back up to 4.99% illustrates the gravitas of Waller’s words, 
whether they reflect the view of others on the FOMC or not. A third successive 0.3% m/m gain for 
core CPI in October nudged the annual rate up to 2.6%, the highest since June. Core services ex-
rent picked up to 3.52%, the highest since May. Chair Warsh was put on the spot at the last press 
conference on exactly the point why they didn’t hike again before the midterms after similarly 
challenging prints for September. If growth is strong, employment healthy and inflation still too 
high, why did they pause? His answer: “defeating inflation is not about an individual meeting or 
a single data point”. The information-rich period since that meeting has brought a decline in the 
unemployment rate to 4.1% and now another firm CPI print. In hindsight, a 25bp increase against 
a “single data point” may end up generating less volatility and market tension than what the 
positioning could now inevitably cause over Thanksgiving. Who else on the FOMC could be 
sympathetic to Waller’s view? In the last dot-plot, only four of the eighteen members pencilled in 
50bp to 4.375% by year end. Tilting that balance remains a long shot and staggering half a point 
over back-to-back 25bp in December (9th) and January (27th) would be cause for less drama into 
year-end when liquidity conditions are typically not great. The odds of 50bp in one go firmed to 
just over 10% and the case for further repricing may now depend on the outcome of PCE inflation 
on the 25th. SG economics maintain their call for December and March. One pertinent question 
looking ahead to 2027 is whether markets are in the process of taking another step towards 
repeating the playbook of the late 90s. Redux curve inversion, tighter financial conditions, 
slowdown in growth and earnings momentum and an even stronger dollar at least in the short 
term.  
In Hungary, headline inflation accelerated to 2.0% in October from 1.6% in September mainly on 
account of the surge in fuel prices. However, monthly data was more dovish than yearly 
suggested. Core inflation rose by 0.4% mom, below forecast. Our EM colleagues expect inflation 
to edge higher in the coming months but to remain comfortable for the MNB. A November policy 
hold at 5.5% remains our base case as the central bank may prefer to see further confirmation of 
the benign inflation outlook before acting. However, soft underlying inflation, alongside credible 
fiscal consolidation, strengthens the case for a 25bp cut in December, going against the grain of 
G10. This remains conditional on a further decline in oil prices towards $80/bbl, which would help 
contain fuel-price pressures. Earlier this week, lower energy prices caused the 10y HUFGB yield 
to sink to the lowest level since mid-July at 5.25%. EUR/HUF drifted below the 100dma (362.98) 
for the first time since early August.
