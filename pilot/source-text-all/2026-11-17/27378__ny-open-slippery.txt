NY Open - Slippery
By Rui Ding
Risk sentiment remains soft with US tech weakness still weighing on stocks, geopolitical uncertainty and catalysts
ahead. The former acted as a drag on Asia stocks. China real economy data saw broad-based weakness. Gilt yields
higher into a pivotal week of UK data whereas USTs consolidated overnight. Dollar is holding onto the highs here ahead
of key psychological resistance levels (USDJPY 160 and EURUSD 1.0950-1.1000). Crude is flat as markets await
geopolitical developments both in the Middle East and from Asia (Trump/Xi/Putin meeting confirmed Thurs).
While we monitor geopolitics, a heavy slate of Fedspeak begins with Williams today at 09:05 ET, Chair Warsh at midday,
and Hammack at 14:15. Monetary policy comments are not guaranteed though. US cash equities will weigh recent
newsflow and major tech catalysts ahead. Regionally, Poland's core CPI prints at 08:00 ET, followed by Canada's CPI at
08:30 where Citi Economics anticipates a headline acceleration to 0.4% MoM (cons. 0.3%). Local holidays for Colombia
and Mexico.

Weekly Macro Brief
In Focus‌
Rates: UST markets are in wait-and-see mode ahead of heavy Fedspeak, with the desk seeing touch better paying flow
overnight going through the belly of the curve. Chair Warsh gives opening remarks at the Productivity and Jobs Task Force
Conference at midday, though it's not clear if he will explicitly talk monetary policy. 
Williams will open a FX Market Structure conference at 09:05 ET so unlikely to cover rate views, but Hammack will speak to
Bloomberg at 14:15. Citi Economics says consecutive 25bp hikes in Dec and Jan are more likely than a 50bp move next
month but they do not expect any comments on the near-term path of policy this week. 
Gilt yields rallying into a pivotal week - BoE rate hike expectations have been reinforced by data, and this week's CPI and
labor report could further support this. Seeing plenty of Brexit headlines as PM Burnham calls for a new deal - we see limited
immediate market implications as it is early days. Client call today at 8am ET - What's Next for European Rates
FX: USD continues to hold onto gains, though we are trading at key levels. USDJPY has shied away from another test of 160
after last week's price action, while EURUSD eyes 1.0950-1.1000 support ahead. On the latter, our vol desk has seen pretty
consistent EUR downside interest across a mix of tenors/expressions. FX eTrading desk saw interbank volumes -15% vs 30d
averageaverage, with net buying of EUR from investor and leveraged names. Dovish Fed pushback and geopolitics will be
more consequential for dollar than AI concerns in our view. 
Equities: NQA contracts -1% into the cash open, as US tech sentiment remains low. Over the weekend, FT reports that
Jensen Huang is discussing a possible comeback while UST Sec Bessent said that a "dramatic" leadership shake up is
needed. ES1 losses are more modest, though airlines are heavy after Friday's grounding. 

In The Sunday Kickoff, our salesperson worries that last week's NVDA (-2.3% premarket) CEO resignation signals a possible
end of an era. Focus has been on "AI road builders" such as semis and hardware but now investors are starting to focus on
"AI toll operators", companies that can monetize AI roads. Those that benefit - hyperscalers (Google of interest), enterprise
software/cyber, fintech, biotech. KOSPI was the worst performer (-3.5%) overnight.
Commods: Iron Ore under selling pressure after a weak round of Chinese real economy data. Our economist describes the
Oct data as Broad-Based Weakening. Retail sales dipped to 0.4%YoY on payback effect and IP softened, suggesting
downside risks to Citi's GDP forecast for 2026. Crude contracts unchanged as we wait for geopolitical developments -
Putin/Xi/Trump meet confirmed for Thursday while we keep an eye on Iran headlines. Iran targeted a Bahrain air defense
base overnight, though they uggested a path to a US deal is open. 
Week Ahead Cheatsheet: Word on the street:
Headline risk will be elevated this week, with APEC and tech earnings keeping geopolitical and AI uncertainty high. APEC
summit in Shenzhen, China takes centre stage on Nov 18-19, with Trump, Xi and potentially Putin attending. US-China trade,
chip restrictions, Taiwan and Russia-Ukraine and Iran developments firmly in focus. Nvidia earnings (Tue) should beat though
questions on leadership should persist.
The macro calendar is also busy. UK jobs and inflation data will determine whether the MPC will hike in Dec. FOMC minutes
and a heavy Fedspeak (20x), including Chair Warsh will be closely watched after Waller put a 50bp December hike on the
table. Also on the radar: Japan GDP/CPI, Canada CPI, Australian wages and employment, and China’s activity data. Major
US and China retail earnings feature also.
Elsewhere‌
CAD: CPI at 08:30 ET. Our economists anticipate a slight acceleration in headline towards 0.4% MoM (cons. 0.3%) vs 0.1%
prior, core trim and median measures are most relevant. With inflation trends remaining soft, BoC will likely keep rates on hold
in the near-term (Citi house view).
PLN: Core CPI prints at 08:00 ET. Our economists estimate core inflation declined to 2.8% from 3.1% on the partial
breakdown of the flash CPI release, suggesting little spillover from high commodity prices to consumer prices. Quiet start to
the week in POLGBs. 
ILS markets attempt to look through the CPI miss amidst the geopolitical uncertainty and recent weakening of the shekel. CPI
undershoot at 1.1%YY - published yesterday. Print does leave the door open for a cut next week, but not more than that.
BRL: Bloomberg reported late Friday that Lula's government plan to address fiscal concerns further with measures that could
increase the adjustment by BRL20bn. Weekend headlines were few and far between. Flows in BRL were muted last week,
with no sustained directional positioning. The theme is the approach of seasonal year-end outflows, not an acute repositioning
by real money.
COP/MXN local markets closed on account of a holiday.
