Main

Equities
•
We are underweight equities. Recent weakness in AI-related shares highlights growing scrutiny 
of valuations, funding costs and the returns on substantial capital expenditures. Persistent 
energy-price volatility and inflation risks add pressure to margins and discount rates. Although 
earnings growth and AI investment remain supportive, we judge the near-term risk-reward 
balance insufficient to justify a neutral or overweight allocation.
Fixed income
•
We are overweight fixed income, with the preference concentrated in investment-grade credit. 
Higher bond yields have improved return potential, while high-quality issuers offer a more 
defensive alternative to equities. This is not a broad overweight in duration: Persistent inflation 
and fiscal pressures leave government bonds vulnerable to further yield increases. Recent 
credit-market volatility reinforces the importance of issuer selection. We favor investment 
grade over lower-quality credit.
Commodities
•
We are neutral on commodities. Sharp oil-price swings around conflicting diplomatic and 
supply-disruption developments underline the two-sided risks. Energy exposure can cushion 
renewed inflation shocks, but any resolution of the Iran conflict will result in a sharp fall in 
energy prices. These competing forces argue against a directional overweight or underweight.
Cash 
•
We remain underweight cash, preferring to deploy capital selectively into investment-grade 
bonds in an effort to secure income over a longer horizon. Cash still offers useful liquidity and 
competitive short-term yields, so the underweight reflects relative return opportunities rather 
than an assumption of imminent central-bank rate cuts.
Preference by asset class
Equities
US
•
We are neutral on US equities. Strong corporate earnings and substantial AI investment remain 
supportive, but the recent technology sell-off exposes sensitivity to elevated expectations and 
rising financing costs. Concentration risk and persistent inflation offset the strength of the 
underlying profit cycle.
EU
•
European equities warrant a neutral regional allocation. Relatively reasonable valuations 
provide support and lower energy prices would offer relief to corporate margins and household 
purchasing power. However, renewed oil-price volatility and persistent inflation limit the case 
for an overweight. Europe remains exposed to imported energy costs, trade uncertainty 
and tighter financing conditions. These headwinds balance valuation support and potential 
recovery opportunities.
UK 
•
We remain neutral on UK equities. Reasonable valuations and exposure to internationally 
diversified businesses provide support. However, fiscal constraints, inflation risks and 
sensitivity to financing costs temper the outlook for domestic demand and earnings. The 
market’s sizeable energy and mining exposure can offer protection during commodity-price 
increases but also creates downside sensitivity to easing supply concerns. 
Japan
•
We are neutral on Japanese equities. Corporate-governance reforms and earnings growth 
support the market, while regional diversification remains valuable. Against this, energy-import 
dependence, currency volatility and higher borrowing costs create risks. Recent volatility in 
Japanese government bonds reinforces the need to balance corporate improvements against 
the sensitivity of valuations to interest rates.

Preference by asset class
Equities
Asia ex-Japan
•
We are neutral on Asia ex-Japan. Taiwanese and South Korean semiconductor producers 
benefit from structural AI demand, but recent technology-sector weakness highlights 
concentration and earnings-expectation risks. 
REITs
•
We remain neutral on REITs. Recurring rental income and potential relief from lower bond 
yields support the asset class, but persistent inflation limits the scope for monetary easing. 
Refinancing costs and uneven property-market fundamentals offset these positives, favoring 
selective exposure rather than an overweight.
Fixed Income
Government 
bonds
•
We are neutral on government bonds within fixed income. Higher yields improve income 
and provide potential protection against weaker growth, but energy-driven inflation, heavy 
sovereign borrowing and renewed tightening risks can push yields higher. We therefore 
balance defensive benefits against duration and fiscal risks.
Investment 
grade
•
We are overweight investment-grade credit. Higher all-in yields offer the potential for 
attractive income, while stronger issuer balance sheets provide greater resilience than 
lower-quality credit in an uncertain growth environment. Recent market volatility creates 
opportunities, although spread risk and rising debt-financed AI expenditures require 
disciplined issuer and sector selection.
High yield 
•
We are neutral on high yield. Carry supports returns, but inflation, higher refinancing costs 
and potential earnings disappointments leave weaker issuers exposed. We prefer investment 
grade for additional credit allocations rather than increasing lower-quality exposure.
EM debt
•
We are neutral on emerging-market debt. Country selection remains crucial and we prefer 
developed-market investment grade credit for additional spread exposure.
Securitized
•
We are underweight on US securitized assets. Income and structural protections in higher-
quality segments are still strong. We currently prefer longer duration in investment grade. 
Note that we remain constructive on European ABS and CLO exposure due to the spread pick 
available in those markets. 
Currency
USD
•
We are neutral on the US dollar. Relatively high policy rates and defensive demand provide 
support, but changing energy prices, fiscal concerns and shifts in global risk appetites create 
two-sided currency risks.
Euro
•
We are neutral on the euro. Energy-import dependence remains a vulnerability, while 
sustained energy-price relief and the ECB’s inflation focus could provide support. Growth and 
fiscal risks offset these potential positives.
Japanese yen 
•
We are neutral on the yen. Energy-import costs and interest-rate differentials can weigh on the 
currency, while defensive demand and any narrowing of those differentials could support it. 
The balance between external energy pressures and domestic monetary policy developments 
does not justify a directional overweight or underweight.
GBP
•
We are neutral on sterling. Interest income offers support, but sensitivity to energy costs, 
weaker growth and fiscal constraints limits conviction. Changes in inflation expectations and 
relative interest rates remain important drivers.

Our asset allocation decisions—in short 
Cross asset allocation: We are underweight global equities and 
overweight fixed income. Recent AI-related market weakness highlights 
the sensitivity of equity valuations to earnings expectations and financing 
costs. Persistent inflation and energy-price volatility add downside risks. 
While corporate earnings and AI investment remain supportive, we favor a 
more defensive allocation.
The fixed-income overweight is focused on investment-grade credit, 
where higher all-in yields and relatively resilient issuers offer attractive 
income opportunities. Government bonds remain neutral within the asset 
class: Their defensive potential is balanced by inflation, fiscal pressures 
and the risk of further monetary tightening.
We are neutral on commodities. Energy exposure can protect against 
renewed supply disruptions, but any sustained improvement in supply 
conditions could reverse the geopolitical price premium. Recent sharp oil-
price swings underline these two-sided risks.
We remain underweight cash, preferring to secure income selectively 
in investment-grade bonds. This reflects relative return opportunities 
rather than an expectation of imminent rate cuts; short-term interest rates 
remain supportive of cash income. 
Within fixed income: We are overweight investment-grade credit and 
remain neutral on government bonds, high yield, emerging-market debt 
and securitized assets. Higher yields improve income opportunities, but 
recent volatility reinforces the importance of credit quality and issuer 
selection. Lower-quality borrowers remain vulnerable to refinancing costs, 
energy pressures and earnings disappointments. 
Within equities: We hold neutral relative allocations across the US, 
Europe, the UK, Japan, Asia ex-Japan and REITs. These neutral preferences 
sit within an overall equity underweight. We believe regional valuation and 
earnings opportunities are balanced by technology concentration, energy 
sensitivity, trade uncertainty and financing risks; we therefore avoid a 
strong regional tilt. 
Within currencies: We are neutral on the US dollar, euro, Japanese yen 
and sterling. Relative interest rates, energy-price developments and 
shifts in risk appetite create offsetting drivers. The dollar benefits from 
defensive demand and relatively high policy rates, while lower energy 
costs could support importing economies. Fiscal uncertainty and uneven 
growth limit conviction in directional currency positions.
Market Commentary 
Recent market developments have reinforced a more cautious cross-
asset stance. Weakness in AI-related equities has shifted attention from 
the scale of investment toward the timing of returns, funding requirements 
and valuation sensitivity to higher interest rates. Strong corporate profits 
remain a support, but concentrated market leadership leaves equities 
vulnerable to disappointments. This backdrop supports our equity 
underweight rather than a broad withdrawal from risk assets.

Energy markets remain volatile, with sharp price reversals demonstrating 
how quickly supply expectations can change. Persistent inflation 
complicates the policy outlook: The Federal Reserve held its target range 
at a range of between 3.75% and 4.00% at its meeting on October 28. 
It is therefore premature to assume a renewed easing cycle or that the 
inflation shock is fully priced into bond markets.
Higher bond yields improve prospective income, supporting our fixed-
income overweight and preference for investment-grade credit. However, 
defensive characteristics vary: Sovereign bonds remain sensitive to 
inflation and public borrowing, while corporate issuers face refinancing 
and spread risks. We favor credit quality and selective exposure rather 
than a broad duration or lower-quality credit overweight.
Across equity regions, earnings opportunities coexist with distinct 
vulnerabilities. US and Asian technology exposure increases sensitivity 
to AI expectations, while European and Japanese businesses remain 
exposed to energy-import costs. UK resource-sector exposure provides 
some diversification but introduces commodity cyclicality. These trade-
offs support neutral regional preferences within the overall equity 
underweight.
Currencies remain driven by relative interest rates, energy prices and risk 
sentiment. Defensive demand can support the dollar, while energy-price 
relief could benefit the euro, sterling and yen through improved terms of 
trade. With these drivers capable of reversing quickly, we retain neutral 
currency scores. Commodities also remain neutral, balancing supply-
shock protection against the risk of a declining energy premium.
