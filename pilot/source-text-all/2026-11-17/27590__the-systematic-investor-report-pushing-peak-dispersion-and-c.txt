◼ As sector rotation and stock dispersion continue to attract attention, we add a longer-term 
perspective, using 50 years of daily sub-sector data, to show that the case for more unconstrained 
equity investing has rarely been better. 
◼ AI and energy themes have helped fuel sector rotations and more negative correlations between 
stocks. This has created challenging conditions for long/short systematic equity strategies, while 
simultaneously increasing the dispersion that creates return opportunities in relative space. 
◼ This is not the full story, however, as high levels of speculation and concentrated positioning 
have also generated sharp unwinds in the likes of momentum and high-volatility stocks, as we 
discussed in Global Equity Market Arithmetic: A Speculative Rout, not a Rotation.  
◼ To take a long-term perspective on market dynamics we apply an information-theoretic 
approach linked to our dimensionality work that assesses the volatility and correlation structure of 
the market to determine the concentration of risk. This is based on 50 years of daily sub-sector data. 
◼ Our measure is the Effective Number of Sub-Sectors (ENSS), which takes lower values when risk 
is more concentrated. In Fig. 1, for an equal-weighted universe of 41 US sub-sectors, we see that the 
effective number has risen to its highest level since 2000 and has rarely been higher. 
◼  Applying market-cap weightings to the sub-sectors shows a very different story. Market-cap-
weighted ENSS hit an all-time low below 2 in June this year, indicating extreme index risk 
concentration and representing a loss of 12 effective dimensions relative to equal weight. 
◼ “Dimensions lost” are return streams that are inaccessible to benchmark-constrained investors, 
making the case for a more unconstrained approach. This could be unconstrained long-only equity, 
though a broader opportunity set is available when implementing long/short (Fig. 2). We can also 
trade dispersion directly and systematically, essentially taking long stock-volatility positions versus 
index volatility, which has benefited from AI and energy disruption, speculative routs, and leverage.
