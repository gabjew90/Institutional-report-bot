 
Nscale's $32 Billion Problem: Neocloud Admits It 
Has "Not Obtained Binding Commitments" To Fund 
Its Anthropic Megaproject As Debt Needs Explode 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
FRIDAY, NOV 13, 2026 - 02:15 PM 
The good news for Nscale: revenue is up 1,344%. The bad news: so is pretty much everything it 
owes. 
On Thursday, the Nvidia-backed British neocloud filed Amendment No. 1 to its S-1, adding the 
third quarter to its numbers. As Reuters noted, nine-month revenue came in at a preliminary 
$307.5 million, up from $21.3 million a year ago, while the net loss more than tripled to $1.69 
billion. Do the math, technically the subtraction, and Q3 alone lost roughly $672 million on about 
$167 million of revenue, i.e., about $4 out the door for every $1 in. 
But the headline losses are the least interesting part of the amendment. What jumps out from 
the sprawling filing is the financing: how much Nscale has borrowed this year, how much it has 
committed to spend, and how much more it will have to raise just to deliver the contracts that 
make up its $105.8 billion backlog. Which, as regular readers know, is why we have been 
warning since last October that AI is now a debt bubble too, and every incremental data point 
merely confirms this.  
From $137 Million To... A Lot 
Start with the balance sheet. As of June 30, Nscale carried just $137.4 million of debt. That 
number is about to look quaint. 
Since the start of the year, Nscale has signed or upsized a dizzying list of facilities: a ~$1.4 
billion GPU Financing Facility, a revolver upsized to $900 million, a $725 million term loan (plus 
a $65 million VAT facility) for its Kvandal South data center in Norway, a

The kicker: per the filing, these facilities "bear interest at variable rates," so every bump in

Interest Already Eats 68% Of Revenue 
And that bill is already enormous relative to the business. In the first half of 2026, Nscale 
booked $95.1 million of net interest expense against $140.6 million of revenue. That's interest 
equal to 68 cents of every revenue dollar, before the bulk of the 2026 borrowing gets drawn. 
Meanwhile, the business does not cover its own costs even before depreciation. H1 cost of 
revenue (excluding D&A) was $189.6 million, or 135% of revenue. Add $174.0 million of D&A 
 

 
and the interest, and you get a $1.02 billion first-half net loss and Adjusted EBITDA of -$199.2 
million. So the debt is being serviced from... more debt, plus customer money. 
Which brings us to the cash flow statement, where things look fantastic, until you read the 
footnote. Operating cash flow was a whopping $1.69 billion in H1, but the filing attributes this 
mainly to "upfront prepayments of $2.3 billion" from customers. In other words, Nscale's 
"operating" cash is largely its customers' money for GPUs it hasn't delivered yet. Investing 
outflows, meanwhile, hit $3.29 billion. Despite all that inflow, cash ended June at $1.48 billion, 
basically unchanged from December. 
$24 Billion Of Orders, $1.5 Billion Of Cash 
Now for the scariest number in the filing. As of June 30, Nscale had "outstanding purchase 
commitments for technology equipment not yet delivered of $24.0 billion", plus another $3.5 
billion of data center construction commitments "payable primarily throughout 2026 and 2027", 
plus $1.4 billion of leases that haven't commenced yet. 
That's almost $29 billion of commitments against $1.5 billion of cash: 
 
And no, they can't just walk away. The company warns that "some of the new capital 
commitments cannot be contractually deferred, reduced or cancelled by us," and then, in the 
line that should be on the cover of the prospectus, it concedes that it "will be required to obtain 
additional financing to meet our forecast obligations in 2027." 
 

 
Translation: the IPO, the converts and the $9 billion of facilities still aren't enough. Nscale will be 
back for more next year, and probably the year after that. 
The $32 Billion Building Nobody Has Financed 
Then there's Anthropic. The crown jewel of Nscale's backlog is the agreement to deliver 
dedicated GPU capacity at its Monarch Compute Campus in West Virginia, worth aggregate 
payments of up to ~$44.6 billion. The amended filing finally puts a price tag on what it will take 
to build just the first phase: 
●​ ~$10 billion to $10.5 billion for the Building 1 data center and power infrastructure (about 
$1 billion spent as of Sept 30); 
●​ ~$21 billion to $22 billion for the GPUs and other compute equipment at Monarch. 
Call it $31-32.5 billion for one campus (napkin math, as the filing doesn't add the two together), 
or roughly 2.3-2.4x the $13.4 billion Nscale has raised in equity and debt in its entire existence. 
And how much of that is financed? Per the S-1/A, the Anthropic agreements require Nscale "to 
use best efforts to obtain qualifying financing for the required GPU equipment", however: 
"we have not obtained binding commitments for any of the financings required to fund 
performance" ... "there can be no assurance that we will obtain any of the required financings on 
acceptable terms or at all" 
Any of them. Full stop. 
And the stakes are asymmetric: if Nscale misses a delivery timeline on any tranche, the filing 
says "Anthropic may be entitled to terminate that tranche without liability." Nscale, meanwhile, 
still owes on whatever it has already ordered. Heads Anthropic wins, tails Nscale's creditors 
lose. 
Oh, and Anthropic wants more. On Sept 29 it exercised its right to contract for additional 
compute at Building 2, and may exercise a right for Building 3 within 15 business days of 
signing that deal. Every new building is another ~$30 billion financing that doesn't exist yet.

Backlog-wise, only $6.4 billion of the $105.8 billion TCV is actually active, about 6%. The other 
94% is a promise that first has to be financed, then built, then powered, then delivered on time.

Growth That Depends On Financing Remaining Available 
Last month, Goldman's credit desk summed up the state of the neocloud trade in its "What's 
Trending Across Credit" note (available to pro subs), with a line that might as well have been 
written about this S-1/A: 
"Capital hasn't disappeared. It's become far more discriminating... the market is increasingly 
differentiating between companies that can finance growth and those whose growth depends on 
financing remaining available." 
 

 
Nscale has told us, in writing, which bucket it is in. Its revenue depends on delivering GPUs it 
hasn't paid for, in buildings it hasn't finished, funded by loans it hasn't secured, for customers 
who can walk if it's late. The bull case is that Anthropic's appetite is bottomless and lenders will 
keep showing up. Maybe. But with CoreWeave's unsecured bonds at 15%, Microsoft walking 
from two Nscale deals, and Nvidia's own leadership in flux, our bet is that the 2027 "additional 
financing" comes at a far higher price than the roadshow will admit, or - more likely - that the 
IPO slips into 2027 alongside it. 
Either way, the borrowing has barely started: TWENTY-FOUR BILLION IN EQUIPMENT 
ORDERS AGAINST ONE AND A HALF BILLION IN CASH!
