Thoughts from Q3/26 On-Quarter Software Earnings
Bifurcation continues with a generally disappointing Q3
Our view: Looking back at Q3/26 on-qtr earnings, q/q revenue outperformance moderated slightly to
2.5% vs. 2.6% in Q2. ARR/billings beat expectations at the lowest levels since Q1/25 at a 1.5% beat
vs 3.4% last quarter. In general, Q3 earnings were disappointing as bifurcation continued between
AI leaders/laggards despite elevated expectations coming into the Q3 earnings season and investors
looking to expand exposure to tier 2 AI beneficiaries were generally not rewarded. Q3 reared further
data points of AI spending taking away budget or delaying decision-making. Given this, we are not
banking on a typical Q4 budget flush, though this could be a source of upside in Q4. When thinking
about off-qtr earnings, given the recent selloff, we see a better setup than the on-qtr cohort. We look
forward to hosting our TIMT conference this week where we look to dig into CY/27 drivers as investor
look for new ideas heading into next year. We come out of on-quarter earnings feeling better about
trends for AKAM, BSY, DBX, DDOG, FROG, GEN, HNGE, MSFT, NET, RNG & TEAM and have incremental
questions about CHKP, COUR, EVCM, FTNT, HUBS, NABL, NOW, PEGA, PLTR, QLYS, & TDC.
What happened with results and guidance:
• The mean revenue beat for 55 on-quarter software companies for Q3/26 was 2.5% and the mean
revenue guide for Q4/26 was up 1.0% vs consensus. As a point of reference, the average on-quarter
software company beat Q2/26 revenue estimates by 2.6% and the average off-quarter software
company beat Q2/26 revenue estimates by 2.1%. Beat levels are still below 2025 levels and the modest
step-down in Q3 could be indicative of further bifurcation in software results as we see potential AI
crowding out. The biggest Q3/26 revenue beats came from Ad-Tech, Observability, and Vertical while
the smallest beats came from SaaS and Front Office.
• ARR/billings beat expectations on average by 1.5%, lower than the revenue beat at 2.5% and the
lowest since Q1/25, though we note the bifurcation in ARR/billings as Back Office/Front Office missed
expectations. As a point of reference, the average on-quarter software company beat Q2/26 ARR/
billings estimates by 3.4% and the average off-quarter software company beat Q2/26 ARR/billings
estimates by 2.8%. The biggest Q3/26 ARR/billings beats came from Vertical and Security while Back
Office and Front Office missed expectations.
• The mean profitability beat vs consensus was 23.0%. As a point of reference, the average on-quarter
software company beat Q2/26 profitability estimates by 21.2% and the average off-quarter software
company beat Q2/26 profitability estimates by 27.8%.
• Q4/26 guidance was mixed across sleeves, moving up 1.0% on average while profitability estimates
moved down -14.1% as profitability guides likely contemplate flowing through in-qtr beats.
• CY/26 guidance on average was up 1.2% vs. consensus for revenue and up 4.8% for profitability as
guides were generally better than expected on the top-line.
• The avg. return 1-day post earnings was -2.3% while the avg. return from earnings to 11/13 was -2.1%.
• A week before earnings (10/16), our RBC All SaaS index was at 5.9x FTM EV/S vs. 5.7x as of 11/13.
Additional thoughts (RBC covered companies in bold throughout report):
• Our focus pivots to our 2026 RBC Capital Markets TIMT Conference we are hosting this week, where
we will explore key themes from companies including: 1) a potential product launch or cycle that
could accelerate growth; 2) increased AI relevancy; 3) market share-shift opportunities and if the
competitive landscape has changed; 4) a more durable end-market; 5) new go-to-market initiatives or
partners; 6) pricing or packaging changes; 7) hiring while others are pulling back; 8) margin expansion;
9) additional disclosures for investors that can help the stock's narrative; and 10) M&A opportunities.

Thoughts from Q3/26 On-Quarter Software Earnings
• We also pivot to off-qtr earnings kicking off with PANW on Wednesday. While on-qtr results
have underwhelmed the market relative to the upside that was expected including some
suggesting deal cycles are slowing, the off-qtr cohort has several tier 1 software AI winners
as we look for solid beat/raises off more attractive valuations. Our off-qtr checks were bullish
for cyber as we found resellers incrementally more optimistic. While this most recent sell-off
has been difficult, tier 1 software AI winners look attractive. Our focus remains on ideas that
could accelerate growth, benefit from AI trends/be insulated from AI narrative risks, and have
idiosyncratic drivers.
• The macro remains stable but multiple companies called out complex environments that could
suggest an intensifying AI crowding out effect impacting deal cycles with some slippage across
companies (DT, HUBS, NOW & TEAM all made macro comments). Further proof of this could
lay in companies beginning to roll out FDE motions to help implement AI solutions (NOW &
TEAM).
• Investor sentiment coming into Q3 earnings was positive as much of the group had a solid
run from Q2 across cyber, data, and infra, especially as investors looked to get more exposure
beyond tier 1 AI beneficiaries, though tier 2 names generally disappointed, and we could see
a reversion to crowding into tier 1 names.
• In general, investor patience is running thin on qualitative or cherry-picked AI metrics/progress
that are not showing up in top-line results and companies must ultimately show AI is causing
an upward inflection in the business.
• On the positive side, Fed strength was notable for the Fed year-end, and we expect this to
continue for the off-qtr cohort.
• Key themes from infra, data, and cyber earnings (as we wrote about previously) included: 1)
AI monetization is becoming real, but companies remain at different stages in this journey; 2)
Agentic AI governance is gaining increasing focus; 3) Customer budgets are being restructured
around AI; 4) AI is elongating deal cycles and creating new pipeline dynamics as customer
decisions become more complex; 5) The shift towards consumption and hybrid pricing models
is continuing; 6) The cybersecurity threat environment has fundamentally changed; 7) Rising
memory costs and supply chain pressures are creating headwinds across hardware-exposed
software companies; 8) Internal AI adoption is reshaping workforces and operating models;
9) The broader macro environment remains stable, but uneven; 10) U.S. Federal government
spending remains robust and increasingly AI-oriented.
• Regarding volatility, Q3 was generally more volatile to the downside post-print than Q2 as this
qtr FROG, APP, LFTO, MGNI, PCOR (not covered) were all up more than 20% while FFIV, FTNT,
COUR, FSLY, NOW, & APPS were down more than 20% post earnings.

A recap of on-quarter software earnings (sorted by day 1 post earnings stock

• JFrog - System of Record, Record Results; Q3/26 review - The stock traded +35% the day
after results as the quarter was impressive with outperformance and acceleration across key
metrics. Highlights included revenue growth of +30.9% vs. +28.8% q/q that outperformed by
8.1% vs. 5.3% q/q, Cloud revenue of $101.5M, +60% y/y vs. $87.5M, +59% q/q while NRR was
124% vs. 121% q/q and 118% y/y. Cloud growth was driven by the conversion of customer
overages into larger annual and multi-year commitments, and accelerating adoption of JFrog's
security and governance solutions. Other positives included: 1) Cloud mix shift acceleration
without margin degradation, 2) NRR inflecting higher with visibility into sustainability, 3) the
continued emergence of security as an important growth driver, 4) a large $76M telco deal, 5)
continued success around a favorable consumption- to-commitment conversion dynamic, 6)
further penetration of Enterprise Plus at 61% of revenue vs. 56% y/y, and 7) RPO +60% y/y to
$812M as an indicator of revenue visibility. With Q4/26 guidance implying +26.6% growth, we
think another quarter of acceleration could be likely as revenue outperformance would need
to be 3.5% for +31% revenue growth. Overall, FROG remains our favorite midcap growth idea
as a system of record for the AI era accompanied by rapidly growing security and governance
offerings. Maintain OP rating and increase PT to $121 from $118 on higher estimates.
• AppLovin - Bounce back; Q3/26 review - The stock traded +31% the day after results as
AppLovin delivered a convincing rebound, beating the high end of guidance on both revenue
and adj-EBITDA. Revenue of $2.18B, +55%, beat consensus by 5.3%, the largest beat since
Q1/25. Adj- EBITDA of $1.84B had an 84% margin, up ~190bps y/y. Results aligned with
management's commentary coming out of a softer than expected Q2 with nearly 50% growth
y/y in gaming despite only a partial model roll-out. Management noted no change in the
competitive environment, but re-iterated their belief that companies can succeed in parallel.
Despite increased training and compute costs, profitability outperformed due to infrastructure
optimization and revenue outperformance.
• Liftoff Mobile - Strong trends into year-end; Q3/26 review - The stock traded +31% the day
after results as in its second quarter as a public company, LiftOff showed additional revenue
upside outperforming consensus by 8.4% while raising Q4 by 6.1%. Revenue grew 32% y/y to
$237M vs consensus of $221.7M while core advertising revenue grew 33%, as management
plans to report a single revenue line starting in Q1/27 given that non-core advertising revenue
becomes immaterial. Adjusted EBITDA margin was 61%, +100 bps q/q as profitability again
grew faster than revenue showing leverage in its largely fixed cost model. Looking ahead,
FY/26 guidance also was raised for both metrics now reflecting 33% revenue growth and
60% margin. The quarter benefited from multiple model improvements with one allowing for
better predictions around users who generate ad revenue for apps and a second improving
the volume of data the Cortex engine can learn from. Growth was broad-based with over 50%
of revenue again coming from non-gaming with no individual vertical driving the upside in
the quarter. Additionally, management noted that they see 30% better prediction accuracy
in gaming from utilizing non-gaming data. Both the revenue upside and the flow through to
profitability were positive indicators for the power of the model as we maintain our OP rating
and raise PT to $38 on higher estimates.
