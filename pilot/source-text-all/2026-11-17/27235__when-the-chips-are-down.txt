 
 
 
                  
When the Chips Are Down 
Following the midterm elections, investor anxiety about the pace of the artificial intelligence (AI) data center 
buildout has weighed on AI-related stocks. The selloff has pushed valuations of semiconductor companies, 
which sit at the center of the AI buildout, well below their historical norms. Where does that leave the 
technology stocks that play a critical role in powering AI services?

Semiconductor companies design and manufacture chips that power artificial intelligence. The 
Philadelphia Semiconductor Index is a representative basket of these stocks, including companies 
whose chips perform AI computations, store and rapidly retrieve data, manage and convert power, and 
connect servers across data centers. Together, these companies supply much of the hardware within 
the data center. 
• 
In the chart above, semiconductor valuations, as measured by the price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple for 
the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, were more than one-third below their 10-year average1 and at 
their lowest level relative to the S&P 500 in at least 25 years2, as of November 5, 2026. That marked a 
notable shift from June 2024, when the group traded at a premium of more than 60% to the broader 
market. T   i d x’s P/E had also fallen nearly 50% since the beginning of 2026.   
• 
While valuation multiples could fall further and earnings estimates could decline, we believe a 
significant amount of bad news could be reflected in these stocks. In our view, cloud providers, 
enterprises and governments continue to expand data center capacity, and the need for faster, more 
efficient computing is likely to grow as AI models grow more capable and adoption broadens across 
industries. Given the strong fundamental outlook we see for AI computing demand over the next few 
years, we believe many semiconductor stocks appear attractively valued.
