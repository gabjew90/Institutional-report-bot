 
Dollar Is Now An AI Trade "In Good Times And 
Bad": Goldman Sees One Last Greenback Push 
Before The 2027 Turn 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
SATURDAY, NOV 14, 2026 - 09:00 PM 
Remember the death of the dollar? 
After a 2025 spent hedging, diversifying and writing obituaries for American exceptionalism, the 
greenback is ending 2026 right at its highs, with EUR/USD back at levels not seen since 
Liberation Day, the DXY at its highest since April 2025 and USD/JPY knocking on 160. Or, as 
we put it this morning: 
So what happens next? According to Goldman's FX strategy team led by Kamakshya Trivedi 
and Michael Cahill, whose just-published 2027 Global FX Outlook, "Timing the Turn" (available 
to pro subs), the answer is a little more dollar strength first... and then the bill comes due. The 
reason is the same thing that has propped up the dollar all year: 
"We believe markets will gradually see the sources of recent Dollar support—stubborn inflation, 
relative resilience to higher energy prices, and surging AI-related investment expectations—turn 
from a boost to a drag. However, this will likely be a gradual shift given the recent data trajectory 
and the time required for these factors to decisively turn." 
In other words, the dollar has become an AI trade, and Goldman thinks AI capex expectations 
are about to peak. Which is something we have been warning about since the AI rout started in 
October. 
Back In "Overvalued" Territory 
Start with the scoreboard. 2025 did a lot of damage to the dollar's valuation premium against 
Europe and the Americas, and 2026 has quietly undone much of it. On Goldman's misvaluation 
gauges, the trade-weighted dollar is back to roughly 13% overvalued, up from around 7% as 
recently as September, while against Asia it never really cheapened at all (still roughly 25% 
 

 
rich). As Goldman puts it, "strong macro fundamentals and firm currency management have 
merely been enough to tread water." 
 
Yet if you only looked at the headline FX vol, you'd think nothing happened this year. Goldman 
notes that G3 FX vol lagged far behind rates vol "throughout the energy shock," a backdrop of 
low recession risk and limited policy divergence that made carry king and everything else 
frustrating. Perhaps the most stunning datapoint in the whole report: 2026 saw "the largest 
swing in FX positioning in history against some of the narrowest trading ranges." Diversification 
at its finest. 
 

 
 
And Goldman doesn't expect the thaw to come quickly. Near term, the bank sees the "frosty 
mix" continuing: 
"Solid activity, heightened inflation risks, and lingering political uncertainties argue for further 
Dollar upside, particularly against the G10, while limited divergence and a strong starting point 
mean that FX is likely to continue to play only a supporting role for now." 
The Dollar's "Situation-chip" 
Here is the core of Goldman's 2027 case, and it is one ZH readers know well. The Liberation 
Day "sell America" trade reversed in 2026 as cross-border demand for US assets returned to 
trend, and Goldman's sector-level analysis says the driver was demand for AI-linked assets 
(plus the US's relative isolation from the energy shock). 
 

 
 
The problem is what foreigners are buying: equities now account for an increasing share of 
portfolio inflows to the US, with private purchases of US corporate stocks (the red in the chart 
below) exploding to the top of a 40-year range. Goldman's conclusion: 
"While the Dollar is still very much a safe-haven currency, it has also become increasingly 
cyclical. We have found that Dollar performance looks linked to AI-related equity 
performance—in both good times and bad." 
 

 
 
Goldman's economists "expect AI-related capital expenditure expectations to show signs of a 
peak before too long," at which point "investors might demand greater price concessions 
(including via the exchange rate) to continue to fund the US's growing capital account balance." 
Translation: when the AI story stops paying for America's deficit, the dollar will have to. Longer 
term, the bank adds, a more cyclical dollar could also weigh on reserve managers' allocations. 
Goldman is hardly alone. Two weeks ago, Deutsche's George Saravelos warned that "The AI 
Dollar" is here, estimating that roughly 90% of the $20+ trillion in US equities owned by 
foreigners is price appreciation rather than actual buying (for every $1 bought, there is $10 to 
sell), all of it barely hedged. We summed it up the same morning: 
And as MUFG flagged in today's "The Dollar Is Dead. Long Live The Dollar", foreigners bought 
a record $1.47 trillion of US equities and corporate bonds in the year to August, almost double 
the pre-GFC peak. That's the tailwind; it is also, as MUFG notes, the reversal risk. A shift in 
hedge ratios alone could be enough to sink the dollar, no exodus required. 
Fed Hikes: The Floor Under The Dollar (For Now) 
So why not sell the dollar today? Because the Fed keeps getting more hawkish. Rising 
expectations for tighter policy have "played an important role in the Dollar's rise over recent 
months," and after Tuesday's red-hot CPI (only one of 70 forecasters was higher), Goldman's 
economists added a third hike: 
 

 
 
Goldman's baseline still has inflation moderating and "a much smaller Fed tightening cycle than 
the market currently prices," which should weigh on the dollar. But the bank concedes the 
near-term risks remain "skewed towards a stronger Dollar, especially if there are more signs that 
global spillovers from tighter US financial conditions expose vulnerabilities in the rest of the 
world (especially Europe)." With the 10Y yield back above 5% and this morning's PPI at a 
4-year high, that's hardly a tail scenario. 
Hence the euro call: Goldman sees EUR/USD dropping to 1.08 in three months as the 
terms-of-trade hit from the energy shock works through with a lag and France flatlines in the first 
half (a below-consensus call), before rebounding to 1.15 by end-2027. French spreads have 
come in since early October, but credible deficit steps may not arrive until after June's 
parliamentary elections and, as Goldman dryly notes, "credible fiscal tightening steps often 
require market pressure for political passage." Spoiler alert: France won't do a damn thing 
unless a bond crisis forces it to. Goldman still likes downside EUR/USD tails, cheap thanks to 
"the prolonged period of low realized FX volatility." 
The Yen: 30% Cheap, And Finally Getting Help 
If the dollar is the AI long, the yen is Goldman's AI hedge, and the bank isn't backing down after 
it barely flinched on the Nasdaq's worst day since Covid. On Goldman's preferred fair value 
 

 
blend, the yen is roughly 30% undervalued against the dollar, and the domestic policies that 
crushed it are shifting: Japan's large intervention in late July/early August (the record 
intervention which, as we reported, Tokyo funded by dumping almost $90 billion in Treasuries) 
and the BoJ's move to quarterly hikes halted the inflation premium that had been building since 
PM Takaichi's surprise win in October 2025. 
 
Goldman's economists now have the BoJ at 2% by June 2027 (three more 25bp hikes), versus 
about 1.75% priced. And beneath the frustrating monthly numbers, the hedge has worked day 
by day: since early October, the yen has been the best G10 performer on the days the Nasdaq 
lagged the S&P by more than 1%, up roughly 0.3% on average (sample of just five days, 
Goldman admits). 
 

 
 
"The likelihood of greater AI-related volatility further improves the case to hedge tech exposure 
with the Yen... we have already seen more instances of equities and yields falling together over 
recent weeks—the backdrop for JPY outperformance." 
The near-term risks are a Fed that out-hawks everyone (sound familiar?) and disappointment 
over Japan's long-awaited reallocation trade, at a time when speculators are already net long 
yen near the top of their three-year range. Goldman's GPIF box concedes the rotation remains 
"more possibility than reality," with spring 2027 the earliest a change could be implemented. 
Regular readers will recall we pinned the relentless Treasury selling on Japan back in 
September; a GPIF shift would be the mother of all repatriation signals. 
 

 
 
Goldman's path: USD/JPY at 158, 155 and 150 in 3, 6 and 12 months, or a roughly 6% decline 
from today's ~160, though "the adjustment should be much sharper if markets more significantly 
challenge AI expectations." In the meantime, USD/JPY is back "just shy of 160," Japan's FX 
chief Mimura is calling the moves "excessively rapid," and BTIG's Krinskysays the Bloomberg 
dollar index is breaking out of a multi-year range. Tokyo's trigger finger may get itchy long before 
Goldman's 3-month target. 
Winners And Losers When The AI Music Slows 
Goldman's full 2027 scorecard reflects four themes: i) a reversal in commodity-sensitive crosses 
as energy normalizes, ii) continued AI spillovers into Asia, iii) a still-solid (but smaller) carry 
contribution, and iv) the yen finally getting durable policy support. High-carry BRL and ZAR top 
the projected total-return table at roughly 15% and 10%, while ILS, NOK and the dollar itself sit 
at the bottom. 
 

 
 
A few standouts: 
●​ The Shekel is the purest AI short. ILS is the most sensitive currency in Goldman's 
universe to the Nasdaq: a 10% drop implies a roughly 2.7% ILS selloff (and a gain for 
the yen). Goldman forecasts USD/ILS at 3.35 in 12 months. 
●​ Asia's AI "haves" keep winning. Korea's current account should stay at an extraordinary 
+18% of GDP (vs +20% this year and an average of 4% over the prior five) and Taiwan's 
at 23-24%. Long-term equipment contracts mean "much of 2027 demand is already 
committed," so KRW, TWD and MYR should outperform. 
●​ China Shock 2.0 means a stronger yuan. Goldman sees USD/CNY heading to 6.30 by 
end-2027, matching the 2022 lows, with the yuan still roughly 22% undervalued. 
●​ Energy relief flips the Scandis. Normalizing energy prices imply "a meaningful unwind of 
this year's gains in NOK and losses in SEK," with Goldman eyeing EUR/SEK and 
NOK/SEK downside once the supply picture clears. 
●​ Careful funding in Swiss francs. The SNB is the only G10 central bank not to hike after 
the energy shock, and at 0% it has the lowest policy rate of any central bank on the 
planet. CHF funding still carries risks, from Europe's "election supercycle" to a gold 
rebound to SNB intervention. 
 

 
 
 

 
Finally, for the quants: after a record year for swings in speculative dollar positioning, Goldman 
finds CFTC positioning now works as a contrarian signal, and a rules-based strategy that fades 
extreme positioning does best when FX vol is low, which is exactly the regime Goldman 
expects. Worth remembering with euro shorts at their most extreme since March 2020, per 
MUFG. 
 
Timing The Turn... Or Turning On A Dime? 
Goldman's own summary of the year ahead is about as hedged as FX outlooks get: "relatively 
limited FX returns," but "value in hedging against wider tails given historically low levels of 
implied volatility and risks to both sides of our baseline outlook." And the bank is candid that the 
dollar's decade-long premium has been a function of America's superior returns: 
"We think the data demonstrate, and the experience of 2026 helps corroborate, that the Dollar 
has been able to sustain its high valuation over the last decade because of strong relative return 
 

 
prospects. However, this remains a high-wire act—signs of more balanced global returns should 
weigh on the Dollar from current levels." 
The technicians disagree with the destination, at least for now: The Market Ear sees "little 
meaningful resistance" for the DXY until its trendline around 105, and JPM's FX strategists have 
nailed this leg of dollar strength. 
Our take: Goldman is right about the direction and probably too gentle on the timing. With the 
dollar now an AI derivative, foreign holdings 90% paper gains and hedge ratios at the floor, the 
"gradual" turn depends on the AI capex story deflating politely. It won't. With AI now a debt 
bubble too (as we've been warning since last October), Nvidia without Jensen and the 
hyperscalers flooding the IG market with dollar paper, the first real crack in AI will be the catalyst 
that resets FX vol higher, and when that happens, the dollar's "safe haven" will look a lot like the 
rest of the AI complex. Liberation Day, when Deutsche warned of "a simultaneous collapse in 
the price of all US assets", was the preview. 
Then again, every dollar bear in 2026 got carried out on the very Fed hikes and AI inflows 
Goldman says will eventually turn. Being early isn't the same as being wrong... it just costs a lot 
more carry.
