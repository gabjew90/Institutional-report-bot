SAS MARKET STRATEGY 
Market Pulse 
Macro Views 
Growth: Global activity remains supported by solid corporate 
and household balance sheets, AI investment, and fiscal 
cushions, which offset the dampening effect of tighter monetary 
policy and higher commodity prices. We expect 2027 growth 
near 2% in the US, and above 1% in Europe and Japan.  
Monetary Policy: The FOMC has telegraphed a pace for 
potential rate hikes of every other meeting. We expect them to 
raise rates in December, but the move in March will be highly 
dependent on the data. If inflation continues to normalize, with 
impulses from energy and tariffs rolling off, we may see them 
hold at 4.0-4.25%; if pressures broaden, they may have to hike. 
We expect the ECB to deliver one more hike in December to a 
peak policy rate of 2.75%. 
Geopolitics: In an election cycle, our focus is not on who wins 
but rather what a win may change. In the US, a divided 
government likely means gridlock and more of the same. 
Upcoming elections in Spain, France, and Italy put a spotlight on 
fiscal risk, with fractured parliaments less likely to address debt 
burdens. Government bond yields may stay elevated, as seen 
recently in French OATs.

Market Views 
Equities: Earnings growth has been exceptional in 2026, 
boosted by AI investment and higher energy prices. We expect 
this growth to decelerate in 2027, but to remain supportive of 
equities. Historically, markets can rally for years past peak 
earnings growth, though they do become more idiosyncratic. As 
the AI cycle continues, we would expect focus to be on value 
capture, quality revenues, and balance sheet strength. 
Fixed Income: Higher rates have led to scrutiny around the 
funding requirements for AI capex, leading to greater dispersion 
and spread widening across the AI complex. We remain cautious 
on IG tech but see scope for some near-term reversal in HY given 
the scale of the recent selloff. Credit fundamentals are 
supportive and supply is expected to slow in Q4. 
Commodities: Our base case is for energy prices to moderate as 
supply/demand normalizes and risk premium recedes. However, 
upside risk remains from possible re-escalation in the Middle 
East or a colder than expected winter. We also see upward 
pressure on metals such as copper, where demand from 
electrification, data centers and energy security is facing 
constrained mine supply; and gold, where central bank demand 
remains robust.

Equity securities are more volatile than bonds and subject to greater risks. Foreign and emerging markets investments may be more volatile and less

Investments in fixed income securities are subject to the risks associated with debt securities generally, including credit, liquidity, interest rate, 
prepayment and extension risk. Bond prices fluctuate inversely to changes in interest rates. Therefore, a general rise in interest rates can result in the 
decline in the bond’s price.  The value of securities with variable and floating interest rates are generally less sensitive to interest rate changes than 
securities with fixed interest rates. Variable and floating rate securities may decline in value if interest rates do not move as expected. Conversely, 
variable and floating rate securities will not generally rise in value if market interest rates decline. Credit risk is the risk that an issuer will default on 
payments of interest and principal. Credit risk is higher when investing in high yield bonds, also known as junk bonds. Prepayment risk is the risk that 
the issuer of a security may pay off principal more quickly than originally anticipated. Extension risk is the risk that the issuer of a security may pay off 
principal more slowly than originally anticipated. All fixed income investments may be worth less than their original cost upon redemption or maturity. 
International securities may be more volatile and less liquid and are subject to the risks of adverse economic or political developments. International 
securities are subject to greater risk of loss as a result of, but not limited to, the following: inadequate regulations, volatile securities markets, adverse 
exchange rates, and social, political, military, regulatory, economic or environmental developments, or natural disasters. 
When interest rates increase, fixed income securities will generally decline in value. Fluctuations in interest rates may also affect the yield and liquidity 
of fixed income securities.
