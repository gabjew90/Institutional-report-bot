Avishek Biswas  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Vice President, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
Sun 15 Nov 2026, 7:57am ET
My broad framework remains similar to my previous Sunday note – “Important to differentiate between
risk and uncertainty”. Last week’s price action had that flavor with headline credit spreads finishing tighter
vs previous week while realized volatility remained high (iTraxx Main 3.2bps tighter while realizing
~1.3bps/day move). In my view, the ingredients for the continuation of that backdrop remain in place; into
year-end I remain moderately constructive on the direction of credit spreads while being mindful that
uncertainty (= realized volatility) could remain elevated. Long story short: I would still be long risk credit
spreads (especially IG credit) and own gamma against it.
Credit delta-1 in a reasonable place
Credit technicals continue to strengthen: Yet another week of inflows into credit with each of EUR
IG/USD IG/EUR HY/USD HY seeing inflows. The fund flow picture now looks pretty constructive
(Graphic 2) and combined with manageable net issuance, the supply vs flows picture is tilting into
constructive territory (Graphic 2). Also worth keeping in mind that December is seasonally the month of
lowest issuance in credit.
Credit positioning a tailwind: Aggregate non-dealer positioning across iTraxx and CDX indices screens
around the most defensive levels (less long) over a multi year history (Graphic 3). The spread sell-off in
Sep/Oct saw a significant amount of de-risking flows in iTraxx/CDX – and even with some non-dealer
risk addition last week, we are still a long way off the earlier 2026 positioning.
Preferred credit longs: Similar as previous weeks – iTraxx Main 5y5y fwd ( Graphic 4), EUR IG SnrFins
cash (GSLCIF5Y basket ), EUR IG corp hybrids.
But keep owning gamma (= uncertainty hedge)
Continuing with my argument from last week, what risk assets are going through in my view is a period of
elevated uncertainty (rather than a period of high downside risk). It’s particularly apparent in the US rates
market where realized volatility remains elevated (Graphic 5/Graphic 6 drive home that point). The AI
sell-off in the earlier weeks translated into a meaningful rally in US rates as market started to get
concerned with the impact on US growth; that rates rally completely reversed in a period of 1 week post
the NFP and US CPI prints (Graphic 7).
I expect continuation of the high realized volatility environment across assets as market wades through
this period of elevated macro-economic uncertainty (especially with the AI sector remaining choppy and
dispersive).

While I think owning gamma across assets makes sense, what I particularly prefer is owning iTraxx Main
gamma/vol. It looks cheap within the X-asset space (Graphic 8), it looks low relative to SX5E implied vol
for instance in the context of current level of iTraxx Main spread (Graphic 9). As a proposal, I would
recommend buying iTraxx Main Dec expiry 62.5 strike payer; it’s a 1-month to expiry 40% delta payer
option – Graphic 10 shows the price history of a 1-month 40% delta iTraxx Main payer.
Two relative value trades
Firstly, the trade idea I recommended last week (short risk CDX HY vs long risk iTraxx SnrFin index)
ended up performing reasonably well with iTraxx SnrFin 4bps tighter on the week vs CDX HY just 8.8bps
tighter. While the broader thesis behind the trade remains in place (reach out to discuss), I would look to
monetize some of this trade given the performance last week (Graphic 11).
EUR IG corps cash/CDS basis: Long risk EUR IG corps cash vs short risk iTraxx Non-fins index screens
as a decent year-end trade. Owning the IG corps cash/cds basis (long cash vs short CDS) has a good local
entry point given the past couple of months’ cash underperformance (Graphic 12); additionally the
expected supply slowdown in December (along with the improving fund flow picture) should be
supportive for cash performance to the end of the year..
