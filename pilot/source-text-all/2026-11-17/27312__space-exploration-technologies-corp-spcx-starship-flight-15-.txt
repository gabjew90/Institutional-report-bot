Starship Tower Catch Reinforces Longer-Term Reusability Goals

On the back of Starship Flight 15 (on 11/13-11/14), we take the opportunity to 
address the operational performance of the vehicle and mission. In our view, the 
launch provided positive proof points around driving full and rapid reusability for the 
Starship launch vehicle. Within this report, we recap key milestones achieved and 
areas of focus going forward.  
Against the broader goal of driving increased reusability, Flight 15 continued to show 
progress across key areas (beyond developments/milestones achieved with Flight 14, 
outlined here), including: 
Starship recovery. Given investments made into Starship over the last several 
n
years, we continue to view SpaceX as being an industry leader with respect to 
achieving historical milestones through technological innovations. With the 
recovery of the spacecraft on 11/14, we believe that the concept around 
Starship reusability will become somewhat de-risked given the positive proof 
points achieved with the vehicle recovery (and continued operational execution).  
Continued iteration of Starship hardware & software. From a technical 
n
perspective after the launch of Flight 14, investor focus remained around engine 
performance. Relative to Flight 14, we would note that engine performance 
improved, as highlighted by the successful launch and initial ascent phase. 
Beyond improved engine performance, we would also note that with the launch, 
Starship (Ship 42) remained in orbit for ~22h and completed 14 orbits around 
Earth - marking the longest orbital flight for Starship. In our view, the ability to 
establish orbital endurance & maintain communications is supportive of 
continued execution against SpaceX’s long-term launch goals.   
Additional satellite deployments. Flight 15 resulted in the successful 
n
deployment of another 26 operational Starlink V3 satellites, driving further 
capacity unlocks. Relative to Falcon 9 launches, we would note that the capacity 
unlock, both from a mass-to-orbit (Starship vs. Falcon 9) & satellite (V3 vs. V2) 
perspective is significant - with each of the last 2 Starship flights having deployed 
~10 Falcon 9 launches worth of capacity. Going forward, we continue to view 
Starship as being a key vehicle to increase capacity and support the further 
scaling of SpaceX’s connectivity business. While our operating estimates do not 
include orbital compute ramping until 2029+, we believe that Starship could play 
an important role in scaling compute capacity longer-term.

Flight Recap & Time Stamps 
 
 
Starship Flight 15 was conducted Friday (11/13) and Saturday (11/14) with Super 
Heavy Booster 22 (B22) and Starship 42 (S42). Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase, TX 
on Friday at 5:47pm EST, and S42 was caught by the launch tower at Starbase, TX on 
Saturday at 4:20pm EST. Flight 15 achieved significant operational and technical 
milestones including better engine performance compared to Flight 14, a 14-orbit 
Starship mission around Earth, the successful deployment of another 26 operational 
Starlink V3 satellites, and the successful tower catch of S42, the first time SpaceX has 
ever caught the Starship vehicle. While the mission plan called for B22 to also perform a 
tower catch, B22’s boostback burn ended early due to low fuel mass, and B22’s GNC 
triggered an automated tower catch abort, leading to an offshore landing. 
Key milestones & operational items achieved: In our view, Flight 15 was largely 
successful, improving operationally compared to Flight 14, and achieving new 
milestones.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
