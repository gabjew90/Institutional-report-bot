October Retail Sales: We expect a soft start to 4Q
We forecast headline retail sales were flat in October, with retail control rising 0.2%m/m. 
Labor market income growth is the main support in our estimate, while several other 
factors weigh on our forecast: transactions data for October indicates broad-based 
softness, with the control-equivalent measure down 0.2% and holiday-sensitive spending 
down 0.4%.
In the retail sales data, holiday spending categories were flat in September, with 
categories such as miscellaneous stores and sporting goods declining on a m/m basis. 
Despite this, holiday categories are up 5.4% this year, relative to 1.8% in 2024 and 2.1% in 
2025. So we do not expect a big uptick in holiday related spending in October. Warmer 
weather is also likely to have dented clothing stores sales.
Among the other components, we expect sales of autos were up a tenth in October, sales 
at gasoline stations fell 2.2%, sales of building materials were flat. We expect another 
solid month for restaurant sales (up 0,.4%m/m), though this is entirely due to price this 
month. 
This would be a soft start to 4Q real consumer spending: No m/m change in total real 
spending and a 0.2% m/m decline in real goods spending. It is consistent with the slowing 
we expect: our underlying forecasts have consumption growth slow from its 3.8% q/q 
annual rate in 3Q to 1.6% in 4Q, with 4Q GDP up at a 2.2% pace.

"Control" Retail Sales & GDP Tracking
Control-group retail sales, which is retail sales excluding food services, autos, gas, 
and building materials, includes all of the retail sales components that feed 
through into the spending estimates for goods consumption in GDP. Also, food 
services affects our services consumption tracking. Revisions to retail sales data 
also feed through into our current-quarter tracking of consumption.
