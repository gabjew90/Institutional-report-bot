C.O.T.W:  Waller the Dollar
Highlights
Sources: Bloomberg, World Gold Council.
*Data as of 13 November 2026. Commodities represented by Bloomberg’s BCOM index TR. Oil is Brent, Dollar is DXY index. Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council 1 Top Federal Reserve official considers largest rate rise since 2022.
•
Last week was dominated by leadership turmoil at Nvidia and the resulting volatility in global AI 
stocks. On the macro front, in the US, elevated CPI and PPI prints, alongside hawkish Fed 
comments, raised expectations of a December rate hike. Labour markets remained resilient, but 
housing weakened. Growth strengthened in both the Eurozone and UK, while China saw mixed 
inflation signals and stronger exports as Beijing extended its trade truce with the US. In India, 
inflation rose and the trade deficit widened.
•
Major global equity markets ended the week higher, while bond yields and the US dollar rose and 
oil prices eased.
•
The US dollar rose sharply last week, supported by hawkish Fed commentary, including Governor 
Waller raising the prospect of a 50bp hike in December, stronger-than-expected CPI (0.4% m/m 
vs 0.3% expected), and resilient US growth and equities (rotation rather than outflows). As a 
result, the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index (BBDXY) broke what we view as a cluster of key technical 
resistance at 1,229/1,233 (C.O.T.W). A sustained hold above here would suggest a large USD base 
has formed, turning the core trend higher. Alongside rising yields (10-year TIPS yields are up 
~30bp in November), this presents a possible “double whammy” headwind for gold for the 
foreseeable future.

•
Gold fell, with the LBMA Gold Price PM closing Friday at US$3,891/oz, down 3.9% w/w and at its 
second-lowest level of the year. The decline brought its y-t-d loss to 10.9%.
•
Stronger-than-expected October US CPI and hawkish Fed commentary raised expectations of 
further rate hikes, lifting yields and weighing on gold. Shanghai futures traders trimmed longs, 
while options positioning turned increasingly bearish in the near term. Yet ETF flows diverged: 
European and Asian investors continued to sell, while US flows turned positive, driven largely by 
Friday’s sharp equity sell-off and renewed safe-haven demand.
•
Gold has held key technical support at US$3,858/oz, the 38.2% Fibonacci retracement of the 
entire 2015–2026 rise, and, with the market also seen at a 'typical' downside extreme (10% below 
its 200-day average) our bias remains to look for a floor here (p6 & appendix).
•
AI equities remain a key driver of broader market sentiment. Recent turbulence around Nvidia 
has weighed on its share price and fuelled volatility across the AI sector. Its earnings this week 
(Tue), alongside ongoing leadership discussions, could therefore be important for broader AI 
sentiment. Any resulting market volatility could spill over into other assets, including gold.
•
The unprecedented Xi-Trump-Putin meeting in Shenzhen this week (Thu) is also vital to follow. 
Current conflicts in various regions, trade relationships and other topics may be discussed.    
•
Investors may also focus on the October FOMC minutes (Wed) for further insights, alongside Fed 
officials’ remarks. Hawkish Fed commentary in the past week has already led investors to price in 
more hikes – further hawkishness’ marginal impact on gold remains to be seen.

Gold strength has proved to be short-lived with the rally capped well ahead of a its 55-day average for a sharp fall to leave the 
market undergoing a concerted retest of key technical support from the 38.2% retracement of the entire 2015 – 2026 rise at 
US$3,858/oz. With the USD breaking major resistance to warn of a potential large base and with the trend for US real and bond

year. We would be alert to a positioning “washout” on such a fall if seen.

Recap of the week  
US
•
Retail inflation in October exceeded expectations and rose 0.4% m/m, while core gained 0.3%. The y/y 
rates were unavailable as October ’25 data is missing due to the government shutdown last year. 
Producer inflation also remained elevated, with PPI rising 6.5% y/y,its fastest pace since Nov’22. The 
high and persistent inflation coupled with the hawkish comments by Fed officials over the week has 
raised bets for a Dec Fed hike.
•
Labour markets remained resilient, with initial unemployment claims for the week of Nov 7 at a 
relatively subdued 200k and continuing claiming declining 20k to 1.69mn. 
•
Existing home sales fell 5.5% m/m in October to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.75mn, a 16 
year low.
Europe
•
UK growth strengthened in Q3, with GDP expanding 0.7% q/q, the fastest quarterly pace 
since early 2024, raising expectations of further BoE tightening. 
•
Eurozone growth edged higher, with GDP rising 1.3% y/y in Q3, with better industrial 
activity in Italy and lower unemployment in France.
Asia
•
 China’s inflation trends remained mixed, with CPI easing to 0.7% y/y in October from 0.9%, 
highlighting still-soft domestic demand, while PPI accelerated to 5.7% from 5.0% amid 
higher energy costs. Meanwhile, trade remained strong with exports rising 35.2% y/y and 
imports 41.6%; Beijing also extended the trade truce with the US by 2 months to Jan’27.
•
India’s retail inflation rose above the RBI’s 6% upper tolerance level with October CPI rising 
6.09% y/y in October, driven by food inflation. The trade deficit also widened to US$39.9bn, 
a one-year high.

Gold Drivers - The USD has broken key resistance 
to warn the core trend may have turned higher
12
The Bloomberg DXY (BBDXY) (which has a lower EUR weighting than the “classic” DXY) has closed above what we have continually flagged as important resistance from the top of the range 
from last summer, 38.2% Fibonacci retracement of the 2025/2026 fall and long-term 200-week average at 1229/1233. A sustained hold above 1233 would be seen to mark the completion of 
a large base to warn of a significant change of trend higher for the USD. Resistance is seen next at 1242 ahead of the 50% retracement of the 2025/2026 fall at 1249. Whilst we would look 
for this latter level to cap at first, we would see no technical reason not to look for a move above here also in due course, with resistance then seen next at 1261. Support is seen at 1227 
initially, with the immediate trend for the USD seen staying higher whilst above 1217.
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council

Gold Drivers –10yr US Real Yields maintain their 
large “triangle” continuation pattern
13
10yr US Real Yields have surged higher after holding well above support from the 38.2% retracement of the rise in yields from March at 2.47% to put the market back above its 55-day 
average. More importantly this is seen to reinforce the bigger picture scenario that a large “triangle” continuation pattern remains in place to suggest recent yield weakness has been a 
temporary decline only and the core trend remains higher. Resistance is seen next at the 2.975% y-t-d high and then the 3.00% psychological barrier. Whilst this should again be respected, 
we see no technical reason not to look for a break in due course with next key resistance then seen at the 3.16% notable high of 2008. Support is seen at 2.79% initially, with the immediate 
yield trend seen staying higher whilst above 2.72%/2.68%.
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council

A stronger USD and higher yields are likely to prove a 
major headwind for Gold
14
If we are indeed seeing the core trend for the USD turning higher and US real and indeed bond yields also remain in uptrends, then this “double whammy” is seen likely to keep gold 
prices under pressure. This would then likely leave the risk for gold to see a clear and sustained break of key Fibonacci retracement support at US$3,858/oz (see page 6) for an extension 
of the downtrend that has been in place for much of the year, with support seen next at US$3,605/oz, then US$3,500/oz.

Glossary of Technical Analysis terms
26

Technical Analysis Glossary
27
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Advance/Decline Line 
A popular type of Breadth Indicator (see below) which represents the cumulative number of individual stocks in a broader index that have risen during a session, 
 
against those in the index that have fallen.
Bar chart 
 
A bar chart shows the open, close, low and high of the price of an instrument over a specific time-period. A vertical bar shows the low to high move, with the open 
 
a small horizontal bar to the left of the vertical line and with the close a small horizontal bar to the right.
Bollinger Bands 
Shows bands that represent 2 standard deviations above and below a central move moving average, typically a 20 period average. The bands are expected to 
 
 
typically capture 95% of price action under normal conditions. 
Breath Indicators 
Breath indicators describe a range of indicators that aim to show the internal strength of a specific equity market index (see Advance/Decline line).
Candlestick chart 
A method of representing open/high/low/close data, originally from Japan. The candlestick (or candle) is formed of a rectangle which represents 
 
 
the open to close move, called the real body, with this shaded different colours depending on whether a higher or lower close was seen for the session. The low 
 
and high are shown as vertical lines above and below the real body/rectangle.
Continuation Pattern 
A pattern that indicates a consolidation phase which is a pause within the direction of the current prevailing trend.
Divergence 
 
When two separate measures behave differently. For example, when a new high or low in price is not confirmed/matched by a corresponding new high or low in
 
 
a momentum indicator, hence showing a divergence.
Double Top/Bottom 
A Double Top is a type of Reversal Pattern (see below) formed during an uptrend when two price highs occur at approximately the same level. Completion of the
 
 
pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern (see below) is broken. A Double Bottom is the exact opposite setup.
Fibonacci retracements 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found when a market retraces following a trending move. The percentage 
 
 
value shown is how much of the prior trend the price has retraced. The Fibonacci retracement levels typically shown are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%.
Fibonacci projections 
Horizontal lines that can indicate where support and resistance can potentially be found in the direction of the current trend. The percentage values are applied 
 
to the prior trending move, projected off the low/high of the subsequent corrective counter-trend move. The Fibonacci projection levels typically shown are 50%, 
 
61.8%, 100%, 150% and 161.8%.

Technical Analysis Glossary
28
Source: Bloomberg, World Gold Council
Flag 
 
A Flag pattern in a classic continuation pattern, characterised by a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole) followed by a short-lived counter-trend move (the flag). They are
 
 
expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
Head & Shoulders Top/Bottom A Head & Shoulders price pattern is a classic trend reversal pattern that appears with three peaks, where the outside two are seen closer in height and the
 
 
middle peak is the highest. Completion of the pattern is signalled when the “neckline” to the pattern is broken (see below).
Measured Objective 
Most technical patterns, regardless of whether they are reversal or continuation patterns come with a “measured objective”, which is typically based on the size
 
 
or height of the pattern. The objective is a potential indication of where the price may move to after a pattern has been completed.
Momentum 
 
Momentum is the rate of acceleration or velocity of the underlying instrument/security. It is thus the speed at which the price of the security is changing.
MACD 
 
Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) is a trend-following indicator, often also used as a momentum indicator. It shows the relationship between two
 
 
exponential moving averages of a security’s price, know as the MACD line, with an exponential average then taken off this line (the Signal line).
Moving Average 
A classic statistical moving average of the underlying price data of the security to give a guide to the direction of the prevailing price trend. Different periodicities
 
 
are used to define short-, medium- and long-term trends. Also used to identify potential areas of support and resistance.
Moving Average Envelope 
Shows bands which represent the percentage distance from a selected moving average, which can be used to identify potential support and resistance.
Neckline 
 
A trendline which marks the point where a reversal pattern is confirmed, typically found by connecting the lows/highs of the pattern.
OnBalanceVolume 
A cumulative volume indicator constructed by comparing the amount of volume traded seen on positive sessions to those on negative sessions.
Overbought 
 
An overbought condition occurs when a price rally has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Oversold 
 
An oversold condition occurs when a price decline has extended too far to fast and is seen unlikely to extend further and a pause is likely to be seen.
Pennant 
 
A Pennant pattern is a type of continuation price pattern, formed when there is a sharp rise or fall (the flagpole), followed by a short consolidation period within
 
 
converging trend lines, similar in shape to a small triangle (the pennant). They are expected to be resolved in the direction of the prevailing trend.
