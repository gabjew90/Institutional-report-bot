EU NATURAL GAS - we have reduced our bullish bias as prices have rallied despite LNG flows ticking
up. Middle East exports are now at ~40% of pre-conflict levels, and as they track towards 50%, the picture
for European gas looks more balanced. European weather has been colder than seasonal average, although
this is forecast to move closer to normal over the next two weeks. The JKM/TTF spread has weakened
which is indicative of a diminished Asia physical bid.
Speculative clients remain positioned net long, particularly looking at VaR metrics rather than simply net
positioning data. The upside tail, from cold weather or a reduction of flows, is keeping price above our
modal outcome. However, this tail remains a significant risk and makes holding short positions very
difficult without a view on mild weather or Middle East export flows.
Further on the left tail, an edge case which clients have been increasingly querying is a resumption of
Russian flows with US talks seemingly increasing in frequency and intensity. While we view this as a very
tail risk (<20% probability), prices would fall quickly if the market began to price a higher likelihood of
Russian flows, even if they do not materialise. However, €7 intraday moves make delta difficult to hold
(particularly when they occur without a headline, such as was the case Friday). Geopolitical headlines
provide two-way gap risk, so we prefer optionalising delta, with any length being in call spreads in the
very front of the curve (so as not to be short a higher call for a prolonged period if we do get a cold
winter).
