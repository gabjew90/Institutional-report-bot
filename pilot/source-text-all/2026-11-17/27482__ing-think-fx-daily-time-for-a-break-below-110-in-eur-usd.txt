USD: Strong momentum looks harder to unwind
The macro backdrop makes it risky to call for a dollar correction just yet. Two-year USD swap
rates have risen 35bp since the Federal Reserve's Christopher Waller mentioned a 50bp hike
and core CPI overshot on 10 November, while other Fed speakers have done little to quell the
hawkish repricing. We’d be more bearish on the dollar if markets had jumped to pricing a 50bp
move in December, which seems unlikely. Instead, the Fed funds futures curve favours back-

We therefore do not see this week’s long list of Fed speakers as a significant downside risk for

minutes from the October FOMC meeting are due on Wednesday. The recent hawkish turn in
Fedspeak and hotter core CPI have probably raised the bar for a market-impactful surprise.
The larger risk of a USD correction comes from US equities, which have shown great potential
to drive dovish repricing. The AI trade faces two major tests this week: Nvidia reports quarterly
earnings tomorrow, while Anthropic is expected to make its stock market debut on Thursday.
So far, tech stocks have held up remarkably well despite Nvidia’s idiosyncratic selloff, and
futures currently point to a modestly positive start to the week.
Today’s Empire Manufacturing index is one of the few notable data inputs this week. Given the
size of the front-end rates move and the arguably high bar for further hawkish surprises, we
tend to favour dollar stabilisation rather than another leg higher. But for now, we think
markets will tend to fade dollar corrections.

EUR: 1.100 test coming
With the exception of tomorrow’s ZEW release, the eurozone macro calendar is mostly centred
on central bank speakers this week, including outgoing ECB President Christine Lagarde on
Thursday and Friday. While the European Central Bank's rhetoric has remained broadly
hawkish-leaning, the contrast with Fed members’ comments is stark.
That divergence is mirrored in the EUR/USD 2-year swap rate differential, which has widened
by nearly 35bp to its widest since September 2025. EUR/USD was some 5-6% higher back then,
but the backdrop couldn’t be more different. The dollar was still suffering from a material post-
Liberation Day risk premium. Now, it is the euro facing some premium due to heightened
eurozone fiscal risk. More importantly, the real front-end rate differential is significantly lower.
Adjusted for 2yr inflation swaps, it is at its widest since December 2022.
Our macro team still thinks markets are overestimating US inflation risks and will face large
dovish repricing, but that doesn’t seem a very near-term story. Given the lighter US calendar,
we feel EUR/USD can hold the line at 1.100 this week, but risks of a break lower are significant,
especially as that would be only a small stretch relative to short-term fundamentals. For now,
EUR/USD optimists like us should be content with some stabilisation in the 1.10-1.11 range.

CAD: Inflation shouldn't endorse a December hike
Canadian inflation data for October is released today. Headline CPI should accelerate by 0.3%
month-on-month, as in the US, taking the yearly rate slightly higher from 3.1% to 3.2%. That
won’t matter too much unless the so far extra-stable core measures start to climb.
Expectations are for only a small 0.1ppt tick higher in median and trim CPI, with both staying
anchored around 2.0%.
Don’t expect the Bank of Canada to turn particularly more hawkish on this inflation backdrop.
December remains a risk for a surprise hike (15bp priced in), but we think January is more
likely.
The widening rate differential has pushed USD/CAD close to the October highs (1.430 intraday).
That is despite the Canadian dollar outperforming all other G10 currencies over the past week,
in line with its lower negative correlation with front-end USD swings. While not our base case, a
break of 1.430 would technically pave the way for much more upside, with 1.450 the next key
resistance.
Francesco Pesole
CEE: Vain wait for sustainable relief
The second half of the month is typically quieter in the CEE region. Today, Poland’s core
inflation for October will be released; we estimate it has fallen to 2.8%. In the Czech Republic,
October PPI figures will be published – a metric that has consistently surprised on the upside in
recent months.
Tomorrow, the National Bank of Hungary decides on interest rates; we expect no change, with
the rate remaining at 5.50%. Inflation has rebounded, and we anticipate further upside.
Although the headline figure should remain near the inflation target, global uncertainty will
likely lead the central bank to delay rate cuts and focus instead on maintaining a stable
exchange rate. Throughout the remainder of the week, we will see more secondary data, such
as wage figures in Hungary and industrial production in Poland.
On the global front, we have seen little change in recent days. If anything, the situation for the
CEE region has deteriorated further. The US dollar is testing new highs, and global energy
prices have seen a rebound. Meanwhile, central banks in the region remain on the dovish side.
Overall, this leaves our view – which has been bearish on CEE FX since the end of the summer –
essentially unchanged looking forward.
EUR/CZK has likely absorbed all the dovishness from the Czech National Bank's last meeting.
We believe the repricing is complete, and current levels around 24.400 appear fair. On the
other hand, EUR/PLN and EUR/HUF likely have more upside. However, the forint could receive
some support from the NBH tomorrow, if our hawkish view that the central bank will not rush
