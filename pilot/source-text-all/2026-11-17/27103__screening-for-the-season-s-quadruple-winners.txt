Screening for the season’s ‘Quadruple Winners’
Written by
Trading Strategy, Pictet Trading & Sales

The Q3 US earnings season has delivered another set of robust results. With most of the S&P 500 now having
reported, earnings and sales growth remain strong, beat rates are elevated and company guidance is generally
improving. Revenue growth also suggests that earnings gains have been supported by resilient demand, rather than
cost control alone.
Growth has also been broadening: all 11 sectors are recording positive sales growth, around three-quarters of
companies are reporting positive earnings per share (EPS) growth, and the earnings-growth gap between the Mag-7
and the rest of the index has narrowed. However, a large share of the total increase in S&P 500 profits continues to
come from a small number of AI-linked industries, particularly semiconductors and market rewards for positive
surprises vary across sectors. The earnings cycle is broadening, but the AI investment theme remains a significant
driver.
As reporting season draws to a close (albeit with several big names still to report including Nvidia and Home Depot
next week), today we discuss the main takeaways from Q3 results, and screen for ‘quadruple winners: companies that
have delivered positive EPS and sales surprises while also raising their full-year EPS and sales guidance.
Approaching the end of Q3 reporting
The Q3 earnings season is well advanced, with most S&P 500 companies having already reported. As shown in the
weekly calendar breakdown below, roughly 90% of constituents, representing approximately 80% of the index’s
market capitalisation, have released results. The figures available therefore provide an increasingly representative
picture of the quarter.
Several important companies are still due to report, including Nvidia, which is expected to report after the close on
Tuesday. However, the season is sufficiently advanced to draw some initial conclusions. At the headline level, these
remain clearly positive: US companies continue to deliver strong earnings and sales growth, with results holding up
well against already elevated expectations.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Another quarter of robust underlying earnings and sales growth
The chart below represents the year-on-year growth rates of sales and EPS on a quarterly basis since Q2 2022. So far,
S&P 500 earnings growth reached 29.9% YoY in Q3, remaining exceptionally strong despite moderating from Q2,
when reported growth was boosted by significant one-off “other income” at Alphabet and Amazon. Even after
adjusting for these effects, the underlying earnings picture remains robust and among the strongest seen in recent
years.
The strength is also visible at the top line. Sales grew 14.5% YoY, compared with 16.1% in Q2, pointing to continued
strength in underlying corporate activity and providing a solid foundation for earnings growth. Taken together, Q3
results therefore continue to depict a very supportive fundamental backdrop for US corporates.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Beat rates are still elevated - Companies continue to clear a demanding expectations hurdle
The strength of the season is also evident at company level. The proportion of S&P 500 companies beating both
earnings per share (EPS) and sales expectations remains above historical averages, suggesting that strong aggregate
growth is not simply the result of a handful of outsized results.Despite increasingly demanding consensus
expectations, companies continue to beat forecasts for both revenue and earnings, pointing to relatively broad-based
resilience beneath the headline index figures.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
The strong beat rates however mask a more moderate EPS surprise
The magnitude of surprises, shown in the chart below, provides a more nuanced picture. 
Despite the strong beat rates, aggregate EPS surprise (in beige) has fallen below its historical average, marking a clear
normalisation from the exceptionally strong Q2 reporting season. Companies are still beating earnings estimates
frequently, but typically by a smaller margin. At the same time, however, sales surprise remains above its historical
average, and this is more encouraging: top-line delivery remains resilient, even as earnings beats become less
pronounced against an increasingly demanding consensus backdrop.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Sector results confirm broad fundamental strength
The detail below illustrates how strong aggregate Q3 results have been supported by positive growth across a wide
range of sectors. Technology remains the principal earnings-growth engine, but several cyclical and other more-
economically sensitive sectors have also been delivering solid earnings growth: All 11 S&P 500 sectors are recording
positive year-on-year sales growth.
The revenue picture (bottom) is particularly encouraging. Broad-based sales growth suggests that earnings growth
continues to be supported by resilient underlying demand, rather than relying solely on margin improvement or cost
control. Cyclical sectors are also growing faster than defensive sectors, reinforcing the constructive message
emerging from the quarter:

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Earnings breadth remains historically strong
Broadening remains one of the clearest messages from the Q3 earnings season. Approximately 75% of S&P 500
companies are reporting positive year-on-year earnings per share (EPS) growth, indicating that the current earnings
expansion extends well beyond a small number of mega-cap companies.
Earnings breadth has moderated slightly from the exceptionally strong levels recorded earlier in 2026, but remains
historically elevated. This provides further evidence that the underlying corporate backdrop is healthy across a large
proportion of the US equity market.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Earnings growth is broad, but exceptional growth remains concentrated
Previous analysis shows positive earnings growth across sectors and at a large majority of S&P 500 companies.
However, broad participation does not mean that aggregate profit growth is evenly distributed.
The industry contribution to aggregate earnings growth, shown in the chart below, provides a more nuanced
picture. S&P 500 net income increased by approximately 30% year on year in Q3, with positive contributions from a
range of industries, including Hardware, Capital Goods, Software and Health Care Equipment & Services. Earnings
growth is therefore not confined to just one or two areas of the market.
However, growth remains highly concentrated. Semiconductors contributed 13.4 percentage points to S&P 500 net
income growth, while Energy contributed a further 6.6 percentage points. Together, these two industries accounted
for roughly two-thirds of the aggregate increase in net income. Other technology and AI-linked areas, including
Hardware and Software, also contributed.
The distinction is important: earnings breadth is improving, but the exceptional strength of the current cycle
remains heavily dependent on a relatively narrow group of industries. In particular, the AI investment cycle
continues to play a significant role in driving US earnings growth.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Earnings growth is broadening beyond the Magnificent 7
To assess the health of the US earnings cycle beyond its largest technology companies, we compare the aggregate
earnings growth of the Magnificent 7 with that of the remaining 493 S&P 500 constituents, referred to here as the
S&P 493. These figures show each group’s standalone earnings growth, not its contribution to overall S&P 500
earnings growth.
The Mag-7 continue to deliver stronger earnings growth, but the gap with the rest of the market has narrowed
considerably. In Q3, earnings grew by 28.4% for the Mag-7 and 23.8% for the S&P 493, a sharp convergence from
previous quarters.
This points to an evolving US earnings cycle, with the rest of the market now delivering growth rates close to those
of the mega-caps. However, broader participation does not necessarily mean the cycle has moved beyond AI. As the
industry analysis shows, semiconductors and other AI-linked businesses remain important drivers of aggregate
profit growth. Earnings are broadening across companies while remaining heavily influenced by the wider AI
investment cycle.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Earnings calls: AI is broadening beyond Technology and the Mag-7
Earnings-call commentary suggests that the AI theme is being discussed across management teams far beyond
Technology and the Mag-7. Mentions by companies outside both groups have accelerated, reaching new highs in
2026. This points to AI becoming a broader corporate theme, extending beyond semiconductor producers,
hyperscalers and major technology platforms.
Meanwhile, tariff-related mentions have declined substantially from their 2025 peak, suggesting that trade concerns
feature less prominently in corporate discussions.

this does not necessarily signal a shift away from AI. Rather, AI adoption, investment and monetisation appear to be

Positive guidance supports the earnings outlook
The positive Q3 reporting backdrop has been accompanied by generally constructive company guidance. Among S&P
500 companies for which both current and previous FY2026 earnings per share (EPS) guidance midpoints are
available, 67% have raised their full-year guidance, suggesting that positive earnings momentum is extending beyond
the reported quarter.
The improvement is most pronounced in Information Technology, where 91% of companies in the available sample
raised guidance, followed by Health Care (80%) and Industrials (77%). Consumer Discretionary also remains
constructive, at 68%. By contrast, guidance has been more subdued in Energy, Utilities and Real Estate.
Overall, the data suggest that strong Q3 earnings delivery is accompanied by generally improving full-year
expectations. However, guidance trends vary significantly by sector, with the strongest momentum concentrated in
Technology and selected cyclical and growth-oriented areas of the market.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Strong earnings surprises are being rewarded, but not uniformly
The strong Q3 earnings season has been accompanied by a particularly positive market response.
As illustrated below, companies that beat expectations have recorded a median share-price reaction of +4.09%,
among the strongest responses to positive surprises in recent years. Companies that missed expectations saw a
median decline of 3.18%, while the median reporting company gained 0.8% around its results.
However, and as we discussed above, the sector breakdown shows meaningful variation. Strong results in Technology
and Energy have been met with more challenging market reactions, suggesting that investors may have set a higher
bar in sectors where earnings growth and expectations are already elevated.
Overall, Q3 has combined strong fundamentals with positive market validation, but good results have not been
rewarded equally across the market. Where expectations are highest, the threshold for further share-price gains
appears higher.

Source: FactSet financial data and analytics; Pictet Trading Strategy; as of 12/11/2026.
Quadruple winners: raising the bar in a strong earnings season
The strength and breadth of the Q3 earnings season are also reflected in our initial screening results. Almost 25% of
S&P 500 companies have qualified as “Triple Winners”, combining positive earnings per share (EPS) and sales
surprises with an increase in full-year EPS guidance. While this high proportion underscores the strength of
corporate earnings, it also calls for a more demanding screen to identify the season’s most complete fundamental
profiles.
We have therefore this season extended the methodology to identify “Quadruple Winners”: companies that delivered
positive EPS and sales surprises while also raising their full-year EPS and sales guidance. This additional criterion
identifies companies that are not only outperforming current expectations but also signalling increased confidence
in future revenues and profitability.
The resulting list spans several industries, although technology, electrical equipment and other areas linked to the AI
infrastructure investment cycle feature prominently. Companies are ranked by the size of their EPS guidance
increase, with Bloom Energy, Zebra Technologies and Qnity Electronics recording the largest upgrades among those
shown. Overall, the screen highlights a select group combining strong current results with an improved forward
outlook.

The Equity quantitative grades
Our equity quant process is one by which we rank and score
the stocks within our equity universe according to various
short-term (i.e. EPS growth) long-term (i.e. quality) and
positioning (i.e. technical) characteristics, and on relative
and/or historical basis as appropriate. The various scores
make up a stock’s total or ‘global' grade’:Growth Grade: The
Growth Grade is a proprietary formula made up of earnings
revision momentum, past earnings growth, earnings stability,
and current and long-term earnings growth.  A grade above 55
is considered bullish on a 3-month basis, bearish below 45 and
neutral between 55 and 45.
EPS Grade: The EPS Grade is a proprietary formula consisting of
current and forward EPS growth, change and surprise data.  An
EPS grade above 60 or below 40 is considered predictive for
future out/under performance.Sales Grade: The Sales Grade is a
proprietary formula made up of current and next year’s sales
momentum, past sales growth, sales stability, and current and
long-term sales growth. A grade above 55 is considered bullish on
a 3-month basis, bearish below 45, and neutral between 55 and
45.
Value Grade: The Value Grade is a proprietary formula made up
of estimated P/E, P/B, P/S and P/CF ratios. 40% of the grade is
based on historical values and 60% on current market data.  A
grade above 55 suggests a stock is cheap, below 45 expensive,
and neutral between 55 and 45.
Quality Grade: The Quality Grade is a proprietary formula that
focuses on the balance sheet (i.e. change in accruals, change in
free cash flows and profitability). A grade above 55 suggests a
healthy balance sheet.Credit Grade: The Credit Grade focusses
on the passive side of the balance sheet.  It is divided into three
sub-components to assess both short and long-term solvency.  A
grade above 55 suggests a strong capital structure, while a grade
below 45 suggests a weak one.
Money Flow Grade: The Money Flow Grade is a proprietary
formula that gives the accumulation/distribution based on the
volume flows of a stock. A grade above 55 indicates good money
flow and a grade below 45 suggests weak money flow.Smart
Sentiment Grade: The Smart Sentiment grade is a contrarian
indicator based on investor positioning measures such as the
days-to-cover ratio, the put-call ratio, and the short-interest
ratio.  A weak grade suggests ‘too much’ optimism.

Relative Strength (RS) Grade: The RS grade measures the price
momentum of a stock over its 1-year price performance.
Global Grade: The Global Grade is a weighted average of the
Growth, EPS Sales, Value, Quality, Credit, Money Flow and Smart
Sentiment Grades.

The Exposure Optimisation Model
The PTS Exposure Optimisation Model (EOM) is a proprietary
model designed to give tactical signals on the prevailing
short-term regional equity market regime. The model is
applied to each market on a mutually-exclusive basis, with
output expressed by way of exposure scores (US; Europe;
Japan and EM); in each case an average of underlying factors
scores with dynamic weighting applied according to the
prevailing structural market volatility regime. Exposure score
ratings are: very bullish (80-100%); bullish (60-80%); neutral
(40-60%); bearish (20-40%); and very bearish (20-0%). Factors
are as follows:
Regime: A combination of technical indicators, moving averages
and a Constant Proportion Portfolio Insurance (CPPI) strategy,
the regime score is designed to reflect short-term market
momentum in the region;
Valuation: Built around the forward price-to-sales ratio, the
dynamic valuation score is based on a calculation that is re-set on
bull/bear market regime changes and calibrated to facilitate
regional comparison;
Economics: The economics score draws on real economy activity
data (i.e. heavy truck sales and shipping traffic), economic signals
from financial markets (i.e. yield-curves) and medium-term
economic indicators (i.e. PMIs and unemployment data); 
Flows: The flows score draws upon both active and passive
weekly EPFR fund flows data giving us an indication of risk
appetite going into the week ahead; 
Market Sentiment: The market sentiment score is built around
classic regional market risk indices (i.e. VIX; ARMS) as well as soft
survey data (i.e. U.of Michigan Consumer Sentiment). The scoring
system is applied in a similar fashion at both regional and market
level, while at sector level we refer only to the Regime, Flows and
Valuation factors.

Technical Analysis
The technical analysis used in this presentation combines
traditional technical tools: graphical analysis (trend lines,
support lines, continuation and reversal patterns) which
determines the tendency, mathematical indicators (moving
averages, RSI, MACD) used as numeric filters and Elliot wave
theory which allows us to build a scenario with target levels
and invalidation points. We also occasionally refer to
Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis. 
Elliott Wave Theory: According to Elliott Wave Theory, markets
move in impulse waves – with five sub-waves (numbered 1-5 or I-
V) following the direction of the main trend, followed by three
corrective sub-waves (A-B-C) (example below).  These waves
follow a set of specific rules and are linked to each other by target
and retracement ratios based on the Fibonacci sequence, and the
characteristics of each wave form an integral part of the
reflection of the mass psychology it embodies.
Relative Strength Index (RSI): A momentum oscillator that
measures the speed and change of price movements. It ranges
from 0 to 100 and is typically used to identify overbought or
oversold conditions in a market. An RSI above 70 suggests that a
stock may be overbought, while an RSI below 30 indicates that it
may be oversold.
The Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD): A
trend-following momentum indicator that shows the relationship
between two moving averages. It is calculated by subtracting the
26-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) from the 12- period
EMA. The result is the MACD line. A nine-day EMA of the MACD,
called the signal line, is then plotted on top of the MACD line,
which can function as a trigger for buy or sell signals. When the
MACD crosses above the signal line, it is typically considered a
bullish signal, and when it crosses below, it is considered bearish.

Ichimoku Cloud Technical Analysis: Ichimoku Cloud is a popular
technical analysis tool used to identify potential trends and
support/resistance levels in financial markets. It consists of
several components, including the Kumo (cloud), the Tenkan-sen
(conversion line), the Kijun-sen (base line), and Senkou Span
(leading span).
