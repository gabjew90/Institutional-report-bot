Refuses to die
Despite all the bearish narratives, the dollar refuses to roll over. The latest squeeze has caught plenty
of bears off guard, with little meaningful resistance until the big 105 level.
And there is more to this than just price action. US yields are moving in the dollar's favor, the Fed is
turning increasingly hawkish, and the AI boom is quietly reshaping global capital flows. The dollar has
plenty going for it, but its growing dependence on the AI trade is creating a new set of risks.

The dollar has some catching up to do
US yields have moved sharply in the dollar's favor, but the dollar itself hasn't really kept up. JPM points
out that US yield spreads versus other developed markets are now at their widest in 15 months. The
gap between rates and FX is becoming hard to ignore. JPM sees room for the dollar to play catch-up.
JPM
Forget oil, watch the Fed
Oil has been falling, which would normally be bad news for the dollar. But the dollar doesn't seem
particularly bothered. JPM shows that the USD has become much more sensitive to Fed expectations
than to energy prices. The oil connection has faded, while the Fed is back in the driver's seat. As long
as markets keep pricing a more hawkish Fed, the dollar has a reason to stay strong.

The dollar likes this curve
There is another reason not to fight the dollar. The US yield curve is bear flattening, meaning short-
term yields are rising as markets price in more Fed tightening. JPM notes that this has historically been
one of the best environments for the dollar. It is almost the exact opposite of the setup that hurt the
USD last year. The bond market is increasingly sending a dollar-bullish message.

Swedish sucker
Not all currencies suffer equally when US yields rise. JPM highlights SEK as particularly vulnerable, with
USD/SEK gaining roughly 6.3% for every 100bps rise in US two-year yields based on the past year's
relationship. The yen would normally be an obvious candidate to short, but intervention risks
complicate that trade. JPM prefers SEK as a funding currency in the current environment.
JPM
Eleven yen of thin air

USD/JPY is back near 160, but the fundamentals tell a different story. JPM's rate-based model puts fair
value around 148, roughly 11 yen below spot. The gap has persisted since Japan's political shift in
October 2025, with policy concerns adding a significant risk premium to the yen. Add the growing
threat of Japanese intervention, and the risk/reward of chasing USD/JPY higher is looking increasingly
questionable.

The rise of the chipdollar
Deutsche Bank's Mallika Sachdeva highlights a major shift in global capital flows. Asia's booming chip
trade is generating enormous surpluses, but unlike the petrodollar era, these dollars are not being
recycled into US Treasuries. Instead, they are flowing into US equities. Foreign equity inflows have now
surpassed $1 trillion annually, with Asia driving much of the recent increase. The AI boom is
increasingly becoming a key source of US deficit financing.
DB
The AI-dollar feedback loop
There is a catch to the chipdollar boom. As Deutsche Bank points out, equity inflows are not only
supporting the dollar but also financing the very companies driving the AI buildout. This creates a
potentially fragile feedback loop. US equities are three times as volatile as Treasuries, while tech
stocks can be up to ten times as volatile.
The irony: The AI boom is helping finance America's deficits and support the dollar, but an unwind in
the same trade could expose just how fragile that funding model has become.
