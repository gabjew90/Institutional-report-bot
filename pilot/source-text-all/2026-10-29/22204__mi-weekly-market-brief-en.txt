Thought of the week
US consumer spending has remained robust this year, which partly explains
the resilience of domestic economic growth. One important tailwind has been
the wealth effect from buoyant equity markets. US households invest a relatively
high share of savings in the equity market, meaning stock market gains can
increase their willingness to spend. Household net worth relative to disposable
income has indeed risen, with the latest data suggesting that most of the
increase over the past year has come from gains in financial assets. This has
helped support spending even as some measures of inflation-adjusted wage
growth have turned negative amid higher energy prices. While the US
consumer still faces other headwinds, including elevated interest rates,
spending is likely to remain supportive for economic growth, particularly as the
household savings rate has further room to fall.
