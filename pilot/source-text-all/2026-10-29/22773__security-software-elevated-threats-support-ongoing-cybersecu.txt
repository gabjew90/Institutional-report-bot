Rising critical vulnerabilities should drive ongoing cybersecurity 
spending. We have seen a continued elevated threat environment in recent 
quarters, with AI-driven discovery surfacing more severe vulnerabilities across 
widely used software at a faster pace (Figure 1). We highlighted the recent critical 
PHP vulnerability, which affects roughly 70% of websites globally, in our note 
Black October Is Haunting C-Suites (link here). We think vulnerabilities of 
comparable severity will become recurring events rather than isolated incidents as 
frontier models continue to improve. Cybersecurity has moved from an IT concern 
to a board-level business continuity priority, tied to protecting critical 
infrastructure, customer data, and operations. We see potential for security budgets 
to accelerate into 2027 even as other IT spending areas face closer scrutiny. Partner 
conversations indicate that enterprises are willing to invest more in security, as the 
post-Mythos era has brought a sense of urgency to migrate off of legacy Network 
Security, Endpoint and Identity environments to get the latest versions of 
hardware, software, and operating systems into their networks. Considering 
typical sales cycles of 6-12 months, we expect more of the upside will come 
through in 4Q and 2027 rather than in 3Q results. We think vendors with exposure 
to the following will benefit most: 1) Next-generation platforms with robust 
detection and response capabilities as the window between discovery and 
exploitation compresses (CRWD, PANW), 2) software supply chain governance, 
as more agent-generated code and binaries move into production (FROG), Next-
generation idenity platforms to help govern the wave of agentic identities on the 
horizon (OKTA, SAIL, PANW), 3) exposure management, as security teams sort 
through a rising volume of vulnerabilities (TENB, QLYS), and 4) software supply 
chain governance, as more agent-generated code and binaries move into 
production (FROG). We expect investor focus will center on forward-looking 
demand indicators, pipeline conversion, and early views on 2027 budgets as the 
clearest signals that threat urgency is converting into revenue. Among those 
reporting over the next few weeks, we remain Overweight FROG and CLBT, 
Neutral CHKP and QLYS, and Underweight RPD.

CheckPoint (CHKP)
CHKP is scheduled to report 3Q earnings on November 2nd before market open
Execution in focus as FY26 guidance relies on a stronger 4Q. We expect 3Q26 
product revenue to decline at a similar rate to 2Q (14% y/y), as CHKP anticipates 
several large appliance deals pushing into 4Q. The company took a prudent approach to 
3Q guidance, assuming those deals push along with a lower win rate vs. last year, and 
we see modest room for upside if some close in the quarter. Unchanged FY26 guidance 
implies roughly 6.5% y/y revenue growth in 4Q at the midpoint, which we think sets a 
high bar with little margin for additional deal delays. CHKP noted that its 4Q pipeline is 
significantly higher than the same period last year, including late-stage deals, though 
channel conversations point to greater use of discounting and retention rebates to protect 
the installed base. Focus remains on pipeline conversion, particularly win rates as the 
sales organization stabilizes after the go-to-market reorganization earlier this year. We 
remain Neutral rated, as we view risk/reward as balanced until we see a sustained 
recovery in sales performance.
Pricing actions and emerging products set up FY27. Partner conversations indicate 
that CHKP’s firewall and SASE growth has trailed larger platform peers this year, which 
we think puts more weight on emerging products to complement the core business. 
Combined ARR across Email Security, CTEM (continuous threat exposure 
management), and AI Security grew more than 40% y/y in 2Q, and we expect the 
elevated threat environment to sustain demand for CTEM as customers look to prioritize 
exposures. We view the new AI Network Firewall, which embeds AI protection directly 
in CHKP’s appliances, as an AI security entry point into the company’s large installed 
base, though adoption remains early. We see pricing as another lever for growth, with 
CHKP implementing a 12% increase on SMB and branch products effective October 1st 
and more than 20% for enterprise. Guidance does not include the October increases, 
which should also help offset elevated memory costs and support the FY26 operating 
margin outlook. Higher discounting largely offset the two 5% increases earlier this year, 
and channel checks indicate that CHKP had not raised prices at the partner level as of 
mid-October. We think discounting could again absorb a good portion of the uplift. The 
Analyst Day during Check Point Experience (CPX) in February could represent the next 
non-earnings catalyst for the stock, and the event should offer an opportunity for an 
update on the multi-year outlook.

JFrog (FROG)
FROG is scheduled to report 3Q earnings on November 5th after market close
Consumption momentum supports potential upside to cloud guidance. We expect 
strong 3Q26 results driven by AI-related binary volume flowing through the platform. 
Cloudflare (covered by Samik Chatterjee) recently announced that 10 million 
developers are now building on its platform, up from 7.4 million in the prior quarter, 
with daily requests from AI agents on its network up more than 1,700% y/y. We view 
the data as a positive read-through for FROG, as faster AI-driven application 
development should translate into more binaries flowing through Artifactory, the 
company’s binary repository. FROG’s guidance continues to de-risk large deals for 
timing and excludes any benefit from cloud usage above contractual commitments, 
which we think leaves room for results to exceed the outlook. We expect investor focus 
to remain on consumption durability, as partner conversations indicate that enterprises 
plan to place guardrails on AI spend over the next 6-12 months, starting with token 
caps. We think token caps pose limited risk to FROG, which monetizes binary traffic 
rather than AI prompts. The company’s sales team is focused on converting usage above 
contracted minimums into higher annual commitments. We remain Overweight rated, as 
FROG continues to deliver consistent Rule of 40 execution with multiple levers for 
outperformance across DevSecOps, AI, MLOps, and governance markets.
Security, governance, and SaaS migration add growth levers. Security has become a 
key driver of FROG’s largest deals, as more than 80% of customers that crossed $1mm 
in ARR in 2Q added security. We expect security attach to keep expanding deal sizes 
and support more multi-year contracts. We think the Network-Layer Protection (Traffic 
Controller) and new AppTrust governance capabilities introduced at the company’s 
swampUP user conference in early September extend FROG’s reach from developer 
teams to security and compliance teams. FROG now counts four frontier AI labs as 
customers, and the wins reinforce its position as the neutral system of record for binaries 
across an increasingly fragmented toolchain. The recent zero-day vulnerability in self-
hosted Artifactory could also accelerate migration to FROG’s SaaS platform, as more 
customers view SaaS as the more secure environment.

Qualys (QLYS)
QLYS is scheduled to report 3Q earnings on November 9th after market close
Vulnerability urgency supports installed-base upgrades. We expect healthy 3Q26 
results as accelerating vulnerability volumes support upgrades from VMDR, the 
company’s core vulnerability management product, to Enterprise TruRisk Management 
(ETM), its risk management offering. Channel conversations indicate that customers are 
setting aside new budget to address vulnerabilities and accelerating upgrades of 
outdated systems that no longer receive security updates. We think this trend could 
benefit QLYS’s asset inventory (CSAM) and Patch Management offerings, which help 
customers find unpatched systems and fix them at scale. ETM, CSAM, and Patch 
Management each rose as a share of the bookings mix in 2Q, and rising remediation 
urgency should sustain the shift. We see the larger opportunity in 2027, as fixing AI-
discovered vulnerabilities becomes a multi-year budget priority rather than a single-
quarter project. We expect investor focus to center on whether new-product attach 
continues to build and on early adoption of the Risk Operations Center (ROC). We 
remain Neutral rated, as we look for the tailwind to extend beyond renewals into new 
business.
Remediation-led upgrades anchor the ETM opportunity. We expect TruRisk 
Eliminate, the company’s autonomous remediation capability, to strengthen the case for 
upgrading to ETM. Newer launches such as InstaScan and Agent Val add faster 
detection and exploit validation ahead of remediation. Management’s guidance implies 
7-8% y/y current billings growth in 2H, which we view as conservative after 16% y/y 
growth in 2Q. We expect 2H billings to be weighted toward 4Q, the company’s 
seasonally heavier renewal quarter, as some 3Q renewal customers still evaluating new 
products could push decisions into 4Q. Management indicated that 9.5-10% y/y current 
billings growth is a starting point for FY27, assuming net dollar expansion steady at 
105% and a modest contribution from new business. We view the framework as a 
reasonable floor, with potential upside if upgrade conversion continues under the new 
ETM leadership.

Rapid7 (RPD)

the reset framework outlined last quarter, with 3Q ARR guided to roughly $812mm as

driven tailwind while the new leadership team works through product and sales changes.

Cellebrite (CLBT)
CLBT is scheduled to report 3Q earnings on November 12th before market open
Execution against a reset outlook in focus, with the US redomicile adding longer-
term upside. We view 3Q26 results as the first test of the updated FY26 outlook, after 
several large deals slipped on new administrative requirements tied to CLBT’s foreign-
entity status. CLBT lowered its FY26 ARR and revenue guidance on softer pricing, 
Inseyets conversion (the company’s upgraded digital forensics suite) and more prudent 
deal-timing assumptions. We think the updated outlook is conservative, as guidance 
removes potential upside from larger, more complicated deals. CLBT noted that none of 
the delayed deals were lost and that $4mm had already closed at the time of the 2Q call. 
The federal fiscal year-end in 3Q should help CLBT recapture more of the remaining 
government business, now that the company has secured a master permit to expedite 
federal orders. We expect questions on large-deal timing with government customers, 
Inseyets conversion economics, and early priorities under the new CEO. The board 
recently authorized a reorganization under a new US parent company, moving the 
domicile from Israel to the US (expected by 1H27). We think the move will ease 
procurement friction tied to foreign-entity status on US federal deals and position CLBT 
for potential S&P and Russell index eligibility, which could broaden its shareholder 
base. We remain Overweight rated, as we view valuation as undemanding, though 
consistent execution over multiple quarters is needed to rebuild investor conviction.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
JFrog (Overweight; Price Target: $115.00)
Investment Thesis
JFrog is a software company that offers a DevSecOps platform that helps software 
developers to efficiently manage, secure and distribute binaries (compiled source code, also 
called artifacts) in an automated way, meaningfully reducing software development cycle 
times, which is a key consideration as businesses race toward digital transformation. JFrog’s 
Artifactory product is considered the “gold standard” for artifact repository, but it has 
gradually developed more into a complete DevSecOps platform, adding integrated security 
capabilities that can scan compiled code for security vulnerabilities before the software 
“build process.” Additionally, the company has developed CI/CD capabilities that help in 
the integration of the various binary packages as well as deployment of the code into 
runtime. Our Overweight rating reflects our strong fundamental positive bias toward JFrog 
given plenty of tailwinds in the DevSecOps space, and we see its expansion into security, 
solid cloud growth and its evolving cloud-aligned go-to-market motion, with extending 
DevSecOps into the edge as a potential future catalyst. We are also impressed by the 
financial model driving high-90s% gross retention, and over 30% revenue growth and being 
sustainable FCF positive since 2019, achieving a “Rule of 40” consistently since its IPO. 
Valuation
Our CY27 price target of $115 is derived from 60.5x EV/CY27E FCF ($232.6mm for 
CY27E FCF). The multiple represents a premium to our high-growth coverage (>20%), 
which is justified in our view as FCF growth could approach 50% y/y next year.
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Downside Risks
Execution Risk: We believe execution is one of the key risks to the story. In the case of 
JFrog, management is looking to scale a fast-growing business. While the company 
primarily has had a bottom-up, community focused approach to driving adoption of its 
product, JFrog is leaning into a high-touch strategic sales team focused on top-tier accounts. 
While we think it's executing pretty well so far, it is difficult to predict execution as we enter 
a potentially choppier macro backdrop. 
Competitive Pressure from Microsoft (GitHub), GitLab, and Others. The three main 
external competitive risks come from Microsoft, Sonatype and other DevSecOps platform 
players like GitLab. Microsoft’s acquisition of GitHub positioned it as the clear leader in 
VCS (version control systems), the single source of truth in the development phase of 
DevSecOps and precursor to the artifact repository that is the single source of truth for the 
operations phase. It could make sense for a single vendor to offer products in both 
categories, and that might mean Microsoft enters the space through development or 
acquisition, and that would be a risk. Similar to GitHub, GitLab is also a competitor and has 
expanded into adjacent areas of the DevSecOps platform, with strong capabilities in CI/CD, 
but lacking in areas such as artifact repository and security, with the latter having breadth 
rather than depth. There are also private companies that are attempting to provide a complete 
DevOps suite of products that include the artifact repository, which if successful could eat 
into FROG’s market share. Finally, JFrog’s primary competitor is Sonatype, which we think 
is moving more into security but if it attracted strategic interest by the likes of a GitLab or 
Microsoft, that could pose a challenge for JFrog.

General Risks 
Security Breaches and Outages. JFrog provides its software as a self-managed as well as 
a SaaS subscription. With respect to the SaaS solution, service can be provided over the 
internet, which involves transmission of its customers’ data. Additionally, JFrog’s 
applications are hosted, and the data is stored in third-party data centres. Any unauthorized 
access to the data due to security breaches or any outages that might impact the uptime of 
the systems could negatively impact JFrog’s business. 
Macroeconomic Gyrations. Any persistent macroeconomic headwinds could prompt 
buyers to more carefully consider their purchase or delay purchasing altogether, which 
could adversely affect JFrog’s business prospects. For example, the current Israel-Hamas 
conflict escalating further could impact business operations as close to half of JFrog’s 
employees are based in Israel.

with other companies in our coverage universe, which could also represent a drag to top-line 
performance in the event macro conditions continue to deteriorate.

think the discount is justified considering muted growth as we wait for signs of better sales 
execution and product traction.

(+) Margins inflect for expansion faster than expected, RPD gains faster traction outside of 
core VM offerings, ARR per customer benefits from increased upsell to existing customers.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
Cellebrite (Overweight; Price Target: $16.00)
Investment Thesis
Investigations by government agencies, police, and enterprises are going through the same 
digital transformation that we are seeing across industries. These customers are moving 
away from manual processes and point products toward a more comprehensive suite of 
digital solutions that drive better efficacy and efficiency across the investigatory process. 
Cellebrite has established itself as a leader in this industry with a presence in most of the 
major relevant customers, and now the focus is on expanding its presence through cross-
selling and up-selling. In addition, Cellebrite is in the process of transitioning to a 
subscription-based business model that we believe will improve revenue visibility, growth, 
and profitability/cash flow. These dynamics, combined with what we view as an attractive 
valuation that is at a discount to the overall software universe, could lead to share price 
outperformance as investors become more aware of this uniquely positioned company.
Valuation 
Our Dec 2027 price target of $16 is derived from a 17.8x EV/CY27E FCF multiple (FCF 
of $197.1mm for CY27E), which is a discount to the average across our Security Software 
coverage universe. We think the current multiple is warranted given the FY26 outlook reset 
and CEO transition, as we look for consistent execution against the revised framework over 
the coming quarters.
Risks to Rating and Price Target
(-) New technologies make collection much harder or too easy, complexity and structure 
make valuation tougher to understand, Cellebrite already has a presence in most of the target 
customers, headline risks on control and usage of product.
