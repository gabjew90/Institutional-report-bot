Buy on mid/longer-term strategic value of memory 
Following MU’s Tech and Product webinar, we hosted COO Manish Bhatia, CTO Scott 
DeBoer, CFO Mark Murphy, and IR CVP Satya Kumar for investor meetings in New York 
and Boston.  Overall, we are incrementally more positive on memory after the meetings, 
with mgmt increasingly seeing CY27/28 to be tighter than CY26.  Key takeaways: 1) 
Memory remains the key enabler of AI perf and power efficiency; 2) De-specs reflect 
shortages; more memory = higher token/$; 3) SCAs greatly increase supply flexibility, 
spanning 3-5 years as take-or-pay with financial commitments; 4) Tech leadership spans 
HBM, DRAM, NAND; 5) Stronger capital returns begin on Dec-9.  Maintain Buy and a top 
pick. 
De-spec reflects memory constraints, not less significance 
Memory is increasingly becoming the most critical architectural pillar in AI, the key 
enabler of better perf, token/$, and power efficiency.  Mgmt views current memory de-
spec as evidence of memory constraints, not other constraints – compute, land, 
power and shell.  Currently both GPU vendors and hyperscalers have incentives to de-
spec memory, given attractive compute economics and relatively better availability of 
logic/CoWoS which they want to fully monetize, even with less memory initially.  Over 
time if memory becomes available, system builders could re-spec memory again as it is 
the largest contributor of token/$ and ROI (adding compute alone requires more power). 
SCAs add flexibility in 3-5+ year supply, reduce volatility 
Importantly, SCAs provide memory vendors much longer time to balance supply together 
with their customers.  Current SCAs span ~5 years, extending beyond CY30 and with 
CY31 discussions already underway, vs. historical LTAs’ limited visibility of 1 year or less. 
As such, it has now become easier for memory vendors to forecast bit demand growth 
3-5 years out and have the flexibility to reduce capex/tool spent in advance if demand 
slows.  Moreover, all SCAs are take-or-pay with committed bit volumes and financial 
commitments, with ~35% of total sales expected to be covered under SCAs (~50% 
target by CY30) with pricing framework whose price floor is well above prior cycle high. 
Roadmap confidence across HBM, DRAM and NAND 
MU flagged the ability to support HBM 4-hi through 16-hi, custom HBM, vHBM (a 
potential scalable alternative to SRAM) and vEIM (for edge applications).  MU expects to 
have four more planar DRAM nodes after 1-gamma, with 1-delta’s volume ramp targeted 
for 2H27.  Meanwhile, mgmt downplayed 4F² DRAM and believes capacitor scaling 
matters more than the cell footprint itself, with MU’s access to advanced litho extending 
MU’s scaling runway – no customers have shown interest in 4F². In NAND, G11 targets 
400+ layers and twice G9’s bits by CY27-end.  Adoption of SLC and TSV-enabled 
NAND may eventually depend on customer interest, and MU is developing both techs. 
Capital-return message clear: increase returns from Dec-9 
Mgmt reiterated its intent to increase repurchases using prior quarter FCF (we est. 
~$150-200bn/yr FCF in CY27-28), beginning Dec-9 CHIPS restriction deadline.  Currently 
~$35bn is authorized, with further authorization remains subject to board approval.

Price objective basis & risk 
Micron Technology, Inc (MU) 
Our $1550 PO is based on a sum-of-parts valuation that values: (1) traditional cyclical 
memory business at $1,127/sh at 3.0x CY28E P/B, toward the high-end of MU's long-
term range 0.8x-3.1x as we are potentially in a memory upcycle, and (2) AI HBM business 
at $422 at 21x CY28E PE, in-line with AI compute peer group median. 
 
Downside risks: (1) larger than expected memory ASP decline, (2) greater competition 
from China newcomers, (3) share loss to large competitors, (4) softening of demand 
across major end markets such as data center, smartphones, or PCs.
