Silver: The great washout
Silver is getting crushed. The metal is trading at its deepest discount to the 200-day moving average
since the Covid liquidation, speculative longs have disappeared, and technical support is coming into
focus. But this isn't just another positioning unwind. A stronger dollar and weakening industrial demand
are adding to the pressure. The technical dislocation is extraordinary, but extreme oversold conditions
alone won't fix a deteriorating fundamental backdrop.
Covid-level dislocation
Silver is now trading 25% below its 200-day moving average, the widest discount since the March 2020
liquidation. Before Covid, you have to go back to 2013 to find a comparable dislocation. One of the
most extreme technical washouts in silver's history. The question is whether this is exhaustion selling or
a market repricing a fundamentally weaker outlook.

Silver longs have disappeared
The silver positioning reset continues. According to JPM, managed money net length has fallen to its
lowest level since Q1 2024. With silver trading 25% below its 200-day moving average, positioning
looks increasingly washed out. But a positioning reset and a fundamental bottom are two very different
things.

Silver's fundamental problem
The silver selloff isn't just about positioning. Solar demand, one of the key structural growth drivers for
silver, is expected to fall by roughly 30%, equivalent to some 60 million ounces, this year, with
weakness extending into 2027. That is a significant shift for a market accustomed to structural deficits
according to JPM. The risk is that silver is moving toward surplus just as speculative positioning gets
washed out.

The dollar connection
