In this note we preview Dollar General’s 3Q26 earnings, providing 
key charts on consumer sentiment, pricing and value perception, 
and recent third party data trends. We found that NPS and NPI 
metrics have improved in recent weeks, though at a moderating 
pace. Pricing and value trends remain mixed, with metrics higher y/y, 
but slightly lower than metrics seen in 2Q. Third party data trends 
also continue to show continued growth, but at decelerating rates 
compared to 2Q.  
As we approach 3Q, we note that Dollar General will likely see 
continued top-line momentum as its extensive store footprint and 
delivery capabilities serve as a beneficiary of higher fuel prices. 
Rising inflationary trends have also recently supported the stock, 
given the company’s strong value proposition and large 
consumables mix. Dollar General is currently trading at a 16.1x P/E, 
compared to its 3-year/5-year average of 16.2x/17.3x, likely 
reflecting these tailwinds. However, key investor questions still 
center on FY27, including whether the CEO transition could lead to 
changes in the company’s long-term algorithm and whether the 
recent benefits from elevated fuel prices are temporary or 
structural. Additionally, we remain cautious on the company’s 
potential margin expansion in FY27 amidst higher fuel prices. We 
reiterate our Neutral with a price target of $141.

Improving consumer sentiment, though pricing and value perceptions are mixed 
We analyzed Dollar General’s consumer sentiment and pricing trends through HundredX 
and Leah Jordan’s 3Q Supermarkets survey linked here. The survey questions ~2,000 
consumers belonging to different age, income, education, and employment backgrounds 
on various parameters and preference metrics relevant to grocery shopping, covering 
pure-play supermarkets and big-box retailers with exposure to grocery. 
Consumer sentiment continues to improve, though at a moderating pace 
As shown in Exhibit 1, more consumers continue to shop at Dollar General, with 35.0% of 
survey participants shopping at the company in Sept’26 compared to 31.8% in Jun’26 
and 25.8% in Sept’25. We note that these are the highest shopping levels for Dollar 
General in our survey over the past four years, with the exception of Sept’23. Consumer 
sentiment metrics continue to improve this quarter as well, with NPI at -5.3% and NPS at 
10.4 as of 10/11. Dollar General’s NPS has improved meaningfully YTD from a low of 3.2 
in Jan’26, with the strongest acceleration between March and June, likely reflecting 
improved store conditions and greater convenience amidst higher gas prices. While 
sentiment continues to improve, the pace has moderated more recently, with early 
October’s NPS increasing +0.2 points and NPI +1.2% since August. We also note that 
improving trends are evident across other discounters, with Walmart’s NPS in particular 
showing a similar acceleration.

SensorTower (+/-) 
We also analyze Dollar General’s app downloads on Sensor Tower, as higher oil prices 
could drive increased engagement with delivery and digital coupons. Monthly 
downloads and active users (MAU) growth peaked in Jun’26, at +46.1% y/y and +21.0% 
y/y, respectively. Since then, monthly downloads have moderated, reaching a decline of 
-4.6% y/y in July, though downloads have started to increase since then at +3.0% in

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
