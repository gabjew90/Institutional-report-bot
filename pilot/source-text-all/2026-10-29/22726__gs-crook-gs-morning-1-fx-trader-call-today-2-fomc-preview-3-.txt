2) US ECON (MERICLE) – October FOMC Preview: Hiking at a Slower Pace –
BOTTOM LINE: Comments from FOMC participants since their last meeting point to a compromise to
raise the funds rate every other meeting. This means that the FOMC is set to skip raising rates at its
October meeting but is likely to deliver a second hike in December. The Fed’s hawkish pivot has
contributed to a large increase in interest rates that has driven a moderate tightening since September in
broad financial conditions, which before then had been little changed on net this year. But the tightening is
only a bit more than is usually associated with one rate hike and much less than is usually associated with
the four hikes the market has now priced, meaning that the FOMC is unlikely to see it as excessive or a
replacement for hikes.
Communication: A key question for the October meeting this week is how the FOMC will modify the
sentence in its statement that refers to the hike at the September meeting. We think it might adapt language
from Vice Chair Jefferson’s recent speech to say, “Any further adjustments to the target range for the
federal funds rate to support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal will depend on trends in
the data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks.” We do not expect significant new information
from the press conference. The seemingly strong support for hiking every other meeting means there is
likely a high bar to deviate from that plan through the end of the year, which should limit the impact of
Fed communication for now.

Risk of a March 2027 Hike: If the FOMC sticks to an every-other-meeting pace in 2027, it could consider
hiking in March, by which point we will have five more months of inflation data. We do not expect a hike
in March because we expect the inflation trend to improve as the effects of tariffs, the energy shock, and
AI price pressures diminish. We also suspect that some FOMC participants share our views that the
economy is not overheated and that the inflation overshoot caused by past supply shocks will largely have
to be waited out, and they might prefer to limit the total size of the hiking cycle. If concerns about growth
or employment emerged, that would likely bring hikes to an end too.

3) ADAM CROOK – 2 Mins Read: How Could Major CB's Surprise This Week –
Fed, ECB, BoJ ahead this week – how could they surprise the markets? Thoughts from around the floor
summarised below – full piece here.
Fed: Warsh echoing Waller's "hikes don't need to come at consecutive meetings" plus a clear "no pre-set
pace" message -> Likely keeps hike-premium priced into 2027. Clearest misstep risk is a repeat of July,
when Warsh’s most dovish answers came when explaining why the Fed wasn't hiking; therefore, a risk he
focuses on the current decision, rather than the path, and the market reads dovish. More airtime on
inflation likely extends curve flattening + keep the front end selling off; more discussion on tighter
financial conditions likely re-steepen the curve; with ~75bps priced through Q3 2027, a 15bp rally more
likely than a selloff; in FX, AUDUSD likely moves most on a surprise hawkish Fed.
ECB: Statement not expected to mention sovereign spreads - Lagarde will likely call France a political
problem rather than a monetary one; will probably add that the ECB has tools to contain contagion + keep
policy transmission working. Bigger risk for EUR-FI/Bonds is a dismissive, or very hawkish, tone on
French spreads that would leave markets feeling an ECB backstop is far away. Fewer hikes would be
bearish for EUR + bullish for Bunds; focus on credit easing or transmission tools would be bullish for
EUR + the periphery.
BOJ: Mildly dovish pause as base-case, underlying inflation converging toward 2%. Board members will
likely cut FY26 core inflation forecast + raise FY27 on energy. In Rates, short covering largely concluded
following the recent JGB rally, we expect a gradual post-meeting sell-off, leaving the medium-term
expectation of a 2.00%+ terminal rate intact. BOJ will not push back against a December hike (~80%
priced), mindful of the risk of USDJPY rising back above 160. However, with the market already priced
for this outcome, and Western Core CPI running at 4% annualized over the last 3m, the market could have
a higher-bar for what constitutes a hawkish meeting.

4) RBNZ Recap

NZ ECON (BOAK): The RBNZ surprised expectations by keeping the OCR unchanged at 2.75% in
October, following a 4-3 split vote of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC). Ahead of the meeting,
financial markets had priced in 24.4bp of tightening and 23 of 25 economists surveyed by Bloomberg
expected a rate hike (including ourselves). The RBNZ framed the split vote as reflecting "how much
weight (MPC members) placed on various risks and the timing of the monetary policy response", with
MPC members who voted for a hold reflecting on the two OCR hikes to date and wanting "further time to
assess their effects and economic developments". RBNZ Governor Breman voted for a hold at today's
meeting - even so, the Governor flagged nine times during the press conference that we are "likely to see
further OCR increases" (i.e., plural) and noted the discussion at today's meeting meant that future
tightening is "likely to maybe be a bit more than what we said in September" (when the RBNZ's OCR
track was projected to reach a 2027 peak of 3.15%). Looking ahead, we continue to expect a 25bp at
December's meeting to 3.00% and now expect a final 25bp hike in March to 3.25% - around the mid-point
of our/RBNZ neutral rate estimates. Our forecast 3.25% peak in the OCR remains well below current
pricing in financial markets (3.75% by August 2027). Full piece here.
G10 FX OPTIONS TRADING (CAO): We went into the meeting with an implied gap of ~50bps in
NZDUSD and AUDNZD, and this dovish hold drove AUDNZD through the 1.26 level (1.6% higher low
to high) and outperformed the implied gap by more than 3x. Market saw a round of AUDNZD topside
demands from the past few sessions so with spot higher dealers will trade shorter. Market trades short
gamma with AUDNZD o/n TK highest paid at 11v (5v higher to roll) and as spot settles down 1m trades
0.4v above roll. Franchise wise, we saw some interests in AUDNZD to roll their existing positions further
and higher, and we saw fresh NZDUSD downside demands to own NZD downside over the election.
5) EM FX OPTIONS TRADING (SUNNY WANG) – USDCNH Trade Idea: Own the Underpriced Break
Lower
3mnth USDCNH 6.575 Digi Put
19.70% USD Offer
~5x Max Leverage
Fwd Ref: 6.6190
Max Loss: Premium Paid
CNH has shown notable resilience over the past month. USDCNH has continued to grind lower even as
the DXY retained a firm footing through the first half of October and risk sentiment deteriorated amid an
AI/tech led equity sell-off. That divergence matters – CNH is no longer relying solely on a weaker dollar
or a risk-on backdrop to outperform.
The fixing trend is constructive, and CNH still appears under-valued on a medium-term basis. Trade
dynamics and exporter conversion should become more supportive into year-end. We see a compelling
opportunity to position for a further CNH strength going into year-end via buying 3mth 6.575 Digi puts,
with a preference to leverage low vol to run short USDCNH delta.

Vol is one-way down to a historical bottom 10% percentile, driven by corporate structured supply and low-
vol environment. However, the option market is being too complacent about the forward path: 1m implied
vol is 1.3, which implies straddle breakeven at 195pip. Yet over the past month, USDCNH spot has moved
~475pip lower and fixing moved ~234pip lower. The 3m 6.575 digi put price implies <20% probability of
a ~300pip per month move lower over the next 3month horizon, which screens conservative given all the
fundamental arguments mentioned above.
