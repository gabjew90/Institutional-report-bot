Addressing Key Debates: User Growth, Margins and Gen AI 
 
 
Following a strategic shift at the beginning of the year toward prioritizing long-term 
user growth and teaching efficacy over near-term monetization, we see the investor 
debate increasingly shifting from whether the company can reaccelerate engagement 
toward the pace at which stronger user trends can translate into improving bookings 
growth and monetization over time. Q2’26 results provided encouraging evidence on the 
former, with DAU growth accelerating to +23% YoY and management highlighting record 
retention. Against this backdrop, we see the following key themes as top of mind for 
investors heading into Q3: 
1) the trajectory of user growth & the conversion of engagement into bookings 
n
growth - following the acceleration in Q2 DAU growth, we expect investors to 
remain focused on the sustainability of these trends as well as the timing with which 
improving user engagement flows through to paid subscriber and bookings growth; 
2) progress scaling newer monetization initiatives without compromising the 
n
company’s user growth strategy - management has outlined several potential 
levers including advertising, longer free trials, subscription tiering and broader 
access to AI-powered experiences, with key debates centered on the magnitude and 
timing of the potential revenue contribution from these initiatives; 
3) the balance between continued growth investments and underlying margin 
n
efficiencies - while 2026 remains an investment year, improving AI-related cost 
efficiencies contributed to management raising its profitability outlook following Q2, 
providing an incremental offset to investments across product and marketing; & 
4) longer-term, whether generative AI serves primarily as a product & efficiency 
n
tailwind or represents a source of competitive disruption for traditional 
learning platforms. DUOL has continued to deploy AI across content creation and 
speaking experiences while also realizing lower costs to deliver AI-powered features, 
increasing the potential to broaden availability across subscription tiers. At the same 
time, we continue to see the longer-term competitive debate around the 
proliferation of increasingly capable general-purpose AI products and the extent to 
which they could alter how consumers approach language learning.

Utilizing Data To Analyze Operating Performance 
To provide a quarterly health check of Duolingo’s key performance indicators (user 
growth, monetization, engagement), we leverage SensorTower data to track Duolingo 
iOS & Android app usage & engagement trends both globally and in the US. We believe 
these data points are useful for flagging inflection points in overall subscriber trends. 
In terms of user growth, we’d note the following key takeaways: 
User Growth Reaccelerated Beginning in Q2’26 Across All Regions. According to 
n
SensorTower, user growth trends inflected positively in Q2’26, following a period of 
deceleration through 2025 and into Q1’26. Global MAU growth improved to +13% 
YoY in Q3’26 (from +9% in Q2’26, and +10% in Q1’26), with global DAU growth also 
accelerating to +18% YoY in the quarter (vs. +12% in Q2’26 and 17% in Q1’26). 
Emerging Regions Continue to Grow Users Faster than the US. In Q3, while Asia 
n
MAUs grew +28% YoY, US MAUs grew +8% YoY (vs. +1% YoY in Q2’26 & ~flat YoY in 
Q1’26), signaling a continued user mix shift toward lower monetizing regions. 
DAU / MAU Ratio Remained Strong in Q3’26. In line with improving user trends, 
n
DAU/MAU ratios improved across most geographies in 2026, increasing by ~2% pts 
compared to 2025. The improvement suggests users continue to engage with the 
platform more frequently, supporting healthier engagement trends.  
Reported User Metrics Have Increasingly Deviated from SensorTower Data. 
n
While third party data is helpful for drawing directional trends, we’d note that the 
gap between company reported metrics (MAUs/DAUs) for the period and 
SensorTower user counts has widened, with reported user count typically 
underperforming SensorTower estimates.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
