Now the fun starts. There is a very heavy line-up of developments starting today and 
lasting through the end of next week including midterms next Tuesday, Canada’s Budget 
next Thursday, and twin US and Canadian jobs reports next Friday. 
Markets are over 80% priced for a BoC hike today. Five of the six major Canadian banks 
expect a hike. Markets are about 90% priced for a Fed rate hold today and a hike in 
December.  
Other developments include ongoing counting of Israel’s election results with 
Netanyahu’s bloc slightly ahead thus far (chart 1). The UK Budget is also due, and there 
were market moves around surprises in Australia and New Zealand. 
BANK OF CANADA—HIKE & BIAS SCENARIOS 
It’s show time. The BoC’s statement, MPR and written opening remarks to the press 
conference all arrive at 9:45amET and will be followed by the press conference at 
10:30amET. A full BoC preview is available here. Key is chart 2 that compares oil prices 
now versus the last time they forecast. 
In terms of the bias, there may be at least three possible scenarios: 
One is that Governor Macklem is likely to be cagey and loathes forward guidance which 
could make him highly circumspect and noncommittal on the bias. 
Another is that he hikes and trades off trade and commodity shocks against one another 
while generally leaning on upside to inflation risk in a mildly hawkish stance.

And there is at least one scenario where he could come off sounding extremely hawkish. If he goes back to the staff's "illustrative scenario 
with persistently higher energy prices" and the accompanying guidance that "consecutive increases to the policy interest rate" would be 
required back in the April MPR, then look out, the front-end will heave. That scenario would spike December pricing and probably January 
pricing too. 
Here is a take on recent client discussions. I would lump them into the following categories: 
1. Totally onside (25%): They’ve subscribed to the narrative for a hike today and further hikes ahead either early on or more recently. 
2. Totally disagree (10%): They don’t buy the motivation to tighten monetary policy at all and think it should be kept flat.  
3. Somewhat disagree (40%): They don’t buy the motivation to hike as much as priced. 
4. They’re sidelined lacking conviction relative to what’s priced (25%): They missed the sell off and are evaluating. In a limited number of 
discussions bank year-end may be an added factor. 
5. No one expects more hikes than priced (0%): Which probably means there will be more hikes than priced…lol. 
6. Cuts (0%): I hear no one saying this now versus a few months ago. 
In some of the sceptical cases when I ask if they’re receiving today’s pricing no one said yes. When asked if they are receiving December, the 
answer was slightly more mixed but generally no.  
The tricky thing is that from a risk-reward standpoint, our forecast is a tougher sell to clients now because of how far the market has come 
in alignment with our view. It’s hard to advise them to heap on more risk on expected hikes at this juncture since we’re at the purgatory 
stage awaiting the evidence and tracking. It was a lot more fun in the summer and previously when markets were pricing cuts and then 
nothing or very little.  
FEDERAL RESERVE—A HINT OF NERVOUSNESS 
Today should be largely just a placeholder on the meeting calendar but there is a nervousness around the call given Chair Warsh’s style. 
The FOMC statement arrives at 2pmET and Chair Warsh’s press conference starts at 2:30pmET. No SEP is due today with the next 
forecasts and dots due on December 9th. 
Markets are priced for no change today. Consensus is almost unanimous. It would truly be a major shock if the Fed hiked and would 
probably add to December hike pricing regardless of the dot plot.  
Recall that the dot plot that was delivered at the September FOMC meeting indicated one more hike in 2026Q4 and then ending 2027 
unchanged. While Chair Warsh loathes forward guidance, other FOMC officials have generally downplayed the prospect of a hike at this 
meeting following the one last month. 
Markets took Warsh’s comments that were delivered at the IMF meetings in Bangkok the other week as relatively dovish by downplaying 
odds of a move this week. I’m still not entirely sure why. He said: 
“I don’t believe in saying now what I might do at the meetings we have in the balance of this year. It’s a new chapter at the Fed, and we’re 
not in that business anymore.” 
Markets somehow heard ‘I’m not hiking.’ The logic was that he didn’t sound incrementally hawkish or issue forward guidance which is 
curious because he says he won’t do so. 
A surprise hike could have data behind it. Core PCE inflation won’t be released until tomorrow, but I’ve estimated a rise of 0.4% m/m SA 
which would be relatively warm. That’s based off CPI, weight differences on key categories, how some producer prices flow through to PCE 
and other methodological differences. The Committee is likely to continue viewing the job market as reasonably solid and the job market 
isn’t their focus relative to inflation.

A hike would rattle markets at a fragile moment just days before midterms amid volatility in stocks and bonds. It would further fray the

RBA HIKE PRICING JUMPS ON CPI, GUIDANCE 
Market pricing for the November 18th RBA decision shot up from about 15bps of a quarter 
point move to 21bps in the wake of Australian CPI and RBA guidance. 
Australian CPI was very slightly firmer than anticipated (chart 3). Q3 CPI was up 4.3% y/y 
(4.2% consensus). Trimmed mean CPI was 3.7% y/y (3.6% consensus) and 1.0% q/q (on 
consensus). September CPI was up 0.8% m/m (0.7% consensus).  
RBA watchers had an eye on testimony before parliament by Governor Bullock and her 
colleagues last night. The RBA offers its latest decision next week that is 20bps priced for a 
hike. Governor Bullock warned about materializing upside risks to inflation, officials warned 
that the latest inflation readings were too high and hotter than expected in their August 
outlook. Hawkish guidance was therefore generally supportive of hike pricing for next week. 
RBNZ SURPRISED BY HOLDING 
New Zealand’s central bank stuffed markets with an unexpected hold at 2.75% whereas 
markets had priced almost a full 25bps hike. This follows 50bps of prior hikes. The NZ$ sank 
on the immediate headline and the kiwi front-end rallied. Some of the reaction was offset by guidance in which the central bank said that 
further tightening was probably needed. The vote was a razon-thin 4–3 decision to hold versus hike. The RBNZ may have felt it had more 
optionality at this meeting since it had already hiked twice. It had also said at its prior meeting that it would “gradually” remove stimulus 
and “respond as needed” which was more conditional than where market pricing ultimately went. 
BCCH HELD AS EXPECTED—WITH A HAWKISH STANCE 
Chile’s central bank stayed on hold as widely expected last evening at a policy rate of 4.5%. Guidance was hawkish by flagging rising 
inflation risks and a warning that the Board will be closely monitoring inflation. 
EARNINGS RISK, UK BUDGET, LIGHT DATA 
Earnings risk also intensifies today, particularly for tech stocks. Alphabet and Microsoft will release in the aftermarket.  
The UK Autumn Budget will be released this morning (8:30amET) and there is a limited take on it in my weekly but won’t be our focus with 
the BoC and Fed on deck today. There are early reports this morning that the expected hike in bank taxes may be smaller than expected. 
The US updates advance merchandise trade figures for September (8:30amET).
