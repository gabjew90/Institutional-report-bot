The Boeing Company
3Q26 Results
Resetting the bar on the multi-year FCF trajectory
Our view on BA following 3Q26 earnings: BA 3Q26 results were mixed, with strength in BDS
and BGS offset by weakness in BCA. Segment EBIT came in below consensus, while free cash
flow was ahead. BA narrowed its FY26 FCF guidance to $2bn, roughly in line with consensus, but
provided a multi-year outlook that was below consensus in FY27-29 driven by a slower than
expected 737 production ramp. With continued production rate uncertainty and a weaker-than-
expected free cash flow outlook, the stock sold off ~11% (vs. S&P 500 +0.6%). Despite today's
sell off, we remain constructive on BA's longer-term outlook. Recent -7/10 aircraft certifications
support higher commercial production rates and improved mix, while the F-47 and F/A-XX
programs drive M-HSD% revenue growth in BDS. We believe the company set a low bar for
expectations through the end of the decade and we see potential for upside to estimates, but
the company's choppy results and inconsistent progress makes this an execution story. We
remain Overweight.
Key takeaways from the quarter: 3Q26 adjusted EPS of ($0.34) came in below consensus of
($0.21). Total revenue of $24.2bn was 1% above consensus, while segment EBIT margin of 3.4%
was slightly below consensus of 3.6%. BDS and BGS margins came in above consensus, while
BCA margins were below, reflecting a less favorable delivery mix, higher projected costs for
future rate adjustments, and higher supply chain costs. BCA delivered 161 aircraft in 3Q, up 1%
YoY. Free cash flow of $464mn was above consensus of $325mn. BA noted its 737 program is
progressing towards a stable production rate of 47/month, while 787 production remains stable
at 8/month. Looking ahead, BA now expects to increase 737 production to 52/month in the back
half of 2027 and increase 787 production to 10/month this year. The 777X remains on track for
first delivery in 2027. Total backlog reached $745bn, up 4% sequentially. BCA backlog reached
$612bn, up 3% sequentially with 300 net orders in the quarter. BDS backlog reached $101bn, up
18% sequentially and not yet inclusive of the F/A-XX. On the call, BA provided a multi-year free
cash flow outlook through 2029. The company narrowed 2026 FCF guidance to $2bn (roughly in
line with consensus of $2.3bn) and expects FCF of $3bn in 2027 (below consensus of $5.5bn),
$6bn in 2028 (below consensus of $9.6bn), and $10bn in 2029 (below consensus of $12.3bn). BA
continues to see FCF growth beyond 2029.
