[GSX] AI health care - broadening well beyond drug discovery
(GSHLCAIP)
Faris Mourad
28 Oct 2026

AI's impact on drug development appears broad rather than concentrated in one
step (discovery), with reported gains in molecule design, lab optimization,
manufacturing documentation, regulatory drafting, and trial planning. While nearly all of
the evidence is preclinical or operational, and there is no human clinical data yet, AI
developers have expressed there is "no single problem to solve" in health care,
because bottlenecks run from target identification through molecule design,
manufacturing, regulatory filings, and trial design and execution. For an investor, this
implies benefits may go to healthcare companies that apply AI across both R&D and
operations, not just to specialists in AI-driven molecule design. 
We reiterate our bullish view on AI and are focused on the newly rebalanced AI Health
Care Beneficiaries basket (GSHLCAIP) to reflect the developing theme. This group
happens to exhibit low correlation to AI, the opposite of AI, rates, and oil:

We rebalanced our AI Health Care basket (GSHLCAIP) to represent the following
categories:
AI Drug Discovery – Physical Lab Demand (31%): AI dramatically increases the
number of biological hypotheses and molecules that can be generated computationally,
shifting the bottleneck from discovery to physical experimentation and validation. This
allows DHR, TMO, WAT, BRKR, DNA, CRL, A and MTD to benefit from higher demand
for instruments, analytical testing, automation and wet-lab capacity, while autonomous
labs and AI-enabled workflows can further increase throughput and productivity.
AI Diagnostics & Precision Medicine (24%): AI allows GH, TEM, NTRA, CAI, VCYT,
NEO, MYGN and CTKB to extract more clinically actionable information from genomic,
pathology, imaging and longitudinal patient data, enabling better diagnosis, treatment
selection, disease monitoring and risk prediction. This can drive higher test volumes,
new diagnostic products and greater value per patient, while their expanding proprietary
datasets can continuously improve their algorithms and strengthen competitive
differentiation.
AI Training Data – Biological Data Generation (17%): More sophisticated biological AI
models require larger, higher-quality and more diverse datasets, increasing the value of
the sequencing, spatial, synthetic-biology and immune data generated by TXG, ILMN,
PACB, TWST and ADPT. As biological foundation models scale, these companies can
outperform through higher data-generation volumes and by monetizing proprietary
datasets or becoming critical infrastructure for AI-driven research.

AI Clinical Development & Pharma R&D (15%): AI enables IQV, ICLR, FTRE, RHHBY
and MRK to compress traditionally lengthy and expensive drug-development processes,
from molecular design and target identification to trial design, site selection, recruitment
and data analysis. The result can be faster development timelines, higher R&D
productivity, lower costs and potentially improved probabilities of clinical success,
creating economic benefits for both CROs and pharmaceutical companies.
AI-Enabled Life Sciences & Laboratory Productivity (14%): AI allows RVTY, TECH,
LH and DGX to automate scientific and laboratory workflows, improve existing products
and reduce labor-intensive administrative and analytical processes. The primary
opportunity is therefore higher productivity and operating margins alongside better
product functionality, with AI enabling laboratories to process greater volumes without
requiring a corresponding increase in headcount or infrastructure.
