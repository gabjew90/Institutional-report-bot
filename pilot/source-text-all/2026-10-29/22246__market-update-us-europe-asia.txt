U.S. Markets Recap
US -0.52% | Nasdaq -0.21% | Dow -1.23%
Synopsis

US equities finished down in Friday trading, ending off session highs. Breadth was notably negative after strong equal-weight outperfor-
mance Thursday. The S&P and Nasdaq logged modest weekly declines. The AI trade stabilized after Thursday weakness, with
semis/memory largely higher, though neoclouds were down. Other outperformers included energy, software (particularly cybersecurity),
networking/communications, steel, space, ag chemicals, and tower REITs. Laggards included transports, E&Cs, staples, travel/leisure,
restaurants, insurers, credit cards, PE, retail/apparel (particularly dollar stores), and larger-cap banks. Treasuries were firmer after a big
Thursday rally, with yields up 3-4 bp. The dollar index was up 0.2%, gold finished down 1.3%, silver was down 1.9% and bitcoin futures
were off 0.2%. WTI crude settled up 3.8% and capped a third straight weekly gain; oil strength was the big story, with WTI settling back
above $100 a barrel and Brent still near $114/bl in Asian trade. Nothing was incremental on the US-Iran conflict, though expectations
are elevated for a nearer-term resumption of kinetic activity. Oil was an outsized headwind on the consumer groups and, together with
the hot inflation dynamic evident in the October flash PMIs, likely contributed to higher rates, which weighed on the broadening trade.

Macro

Iran conflict and oil: UN ambassador Waltz told the FT the conflict could last another year, though he hopes diplomacy can work before
then, coming on the heels of Trump saying he is not negotiating and reports of plans for a new round of US strikes. He said pressure will
continue until the sides reach an acceptable nuclear deal and that the US Navy can maintain its blockade for the foreseeable future. US
officials reiterated that there will be no attacks before Election Day, while Israel's Netanyahu stressed the need for joint action to topple
the Iranian regime. Overnight reporting said Iran is prepared to respond to renewed US strikes by hitting a wider range of targets, including
Gulf airports, energy pipelines and other civilian infrastructure, with undersea cables, data centers and AI facilities also mentioned.
Sources have mentioned a possible short, intensive campaign against Iranian energy infrastructure, nuclear sites and its missile and
drone arsenal, although there are doubts that another campaign would change Iran's calculus even as economic pressure builds from
the naval blockade. Middle East physical crude prices have softened as flows through Hormuz hold up and Gulf exports recover, but Citi
cautioned against extrapolating several days of improved flows into full normalization, saying the rebound could partly reflect inventory
drawdowns or the release of previously hoarded barrels, and that near-term oil-price risks remain skewed to the upside.

October flash PMIs and consumer sentiment: The flash composite PMI eased to 57.6 versus 57.3 consensus and 58.4 prior, though it
still signaled the second-fastest expansion since Mar-22. The manufacturing PMI rose to 56.0 versus 55.5 consensus and 55.9 prior, the
highest since May-22, while the services PMI slipped to 57.9, in line with consensus, from 58.8 prior. New orders growth accelerated to
the strongest in over four-and-a-half years and employment remained strong. Selling-price inflation accelerated to the highest since Aug-
22, with input costs elevated on higher oil, energy, fuel and freight costs. Final October consumer sentiment was little changed month on
month at 48.0 versus 46.3. Year-ahead inflation expectations ticked up to 4.7% from 4.6%, while long-run expectations rose to 3.5% from
3.4%, both rising for a second straight month to the highest since May. Housing data was soft, with builder confidence at its lowest since
2012 and pending home sales at a record low.

AI headline sentiment versus earnings fundamentals: A cautious-leaning batch of AI headlines fits market sentiment but contrasts with a
continued stream of upbeat compute and capex demand takeaways from earnings. Hyperscaler CDS is at record highs, and OpenAI
trimmed its valuation target in its latest fundraising round to $1.25T from $1.4T, with the round seeking to raise around $30B. Coatue
and UAE funds were in talks to lead, sources told the FT that client demand had weakened, and investors are reportedly waiting to see
how Anthropic prices its IPO before committing to OpenAI. Meta's filing for new non-voting shares noted potential capital needs in the
hundreds of billions, and Meta's Muse success means higher compute costs and monetization scrutiny. SB Energy is considering pushing
its IPO to December or later. On the earnings front, Western Digital beat, guided above, said demand remains supply constrained, noted
visibility now extends into 2030-31 and downplayed capacity addition concerns. Bloom Energy delivered a big beat and raise with positive
takeaways on backlog growth, customer diversification and capacity outlook. Comfort Systems reported a nearly $4B q/q surge in backlog
on continued data center demand, and MaxLinear results highlighted strong optical AI data center demand.

Breadth and the week's talking points: The S&P 500 logged a 1.2% decline on Thursday while the equal-weight RSP rose 0.7%, a 194 bp
differential that was the largest since July 2024 and, per Bespoke, the fifth largest equal-weight outperformance since 1990 when equal-
weight rose and cap-weighted fell. Despite SOX being down 15% MTD and off nearly 27% from its June ATH, nearly 55% of S&P constituents
are above their 50-day moving averages, the highest proportion since late August and up from ~21% on 29-Sep. BTIG observed that a
third of trading days this year have seen an S&P 500 decline but positive index breadth, the most of any calendar year back to 1996, and
cautioned that a higher-correlation selloff could happen at some time. On the bullish side, the Russell 2000 and equal-weight S&P were
up over 1% for the week, and Goldman Sachs noted the largest two-week net selling in Global Info Tech on record, a potential squeeze
facilitator, with hyperscaler earnings, midterm elections and buyback resumptions the near-term catalysts. On the bearish side, Thurs-
day's AI selloff was driven by Meta's capital-raise signals, CoreWeave failed to stabilize despite sell-side defense, and input cost pressure
was flagged by companies across a range of industries.

Treasuries and the AI trade: The 20-day correlation between Treasury returns and AI stocks has swung to -0.56, turning negative for the
first time since March, according to Bloomberg. Strategists say markets may be pricing in the probability of a growth scare from the AI
reset, which has prompted a pullback in rate hike expectations. The cost of hedging against further Treasury gains has risen to the highest
since March, with the bond rally amplified by short covering.

Synopsis

European equity markets closed lower Friday, but off session lows. Defense dragged on Industrials, while Autos and Luxury were the big
laggards. Energy, Construction and Retail were the best performers. The Stoxx 600 extended the previous session's weakness and is
down 1.4% week to date. European government bonds were weaker into the close, with yields edging higher across core markets, while
UK Gilts underperformed. The dollar index was up 0.1%, gold was down 1.2%, silver was down 1.6% and bitcoin futures were flat. AI trade
volatility was still the big talking point of the week, though the European tech sector held up somewhat after underperforming on Thursday
and Asia and South Korea held up much better than expected overnight. A flurry of headlines still focused on funding concerns and spread
widening, deal delays, monetization scrutiny, cybersecurity risks and questions over a lack of traction despite favorable compute and
capex demand. Bond flows increasingly got attention in Europe amid a big rotation out of French paper this week. The tone was supportive
on Friday, but sentiment is fragile ahead of further expected civil unrest. The German Bund has been a beneficiary, and Gilts are perform-
ing relatively well ahead of next week's UK Budget amid expectations for a lowkey fiscal event. Energy prices continued higher on head-
lines of expected renewed military strikes, reinforcing expectations of higher-for-long inflation, though the ECB is seen on hold next week.
The latest Bloomberg poll of economists highlights the likelihood of a December ECB hike and policy remaining unchanged until May
2028, and focus is turning to whether the ECB will step in to stem the ongoing backup in French yields. On the macro data front, attention
was on PMIs: eurozone activity eased in October, largely due to a slowdown in services, though manufacturing also eased somewhat,
while the UK PMI unexpectedly jumped on services sector strength. UK GfK consumer confidence saw its biggest drop since the Brexit
vote and UK retail sales were soft in September. The ECB consumer expectations survey saw one-year inflation expectations edge up. On
the corporate front, Energy and select quality cyclicals and materials outperformed on results and commodity tailwinds. Eni (ENI-IT) ben-
efitted from higher energy prices, record-low leverage and a special dividend and buyback boost, Holcim (HOLN-CH) posted a strong beat
and guidance raise, Rexel (RXL-FR) raised FY sales growth guidance to 7% from 5% and adjusted EBITA margin guidance, and L'Oreal (OR-
FR) delivered a premium beauty beat that added a consumer-quality bid. Autos, luxury and more cyclical industrials lagged: Volvo Car
(VOLCAR.B-SE) posted a sizeable miss and a China and US demand hit, Kering (KER-FR) reported Gucci weakness with no clear inflection
seen, and Safran (SAF-FR) reported spare-parts growth and FY26 EBIT guidance below forecasts.
Macro

French bond rout: French sovereign debt was the biggest macro development of the week, with 10-year yields above 5% for the first time
since 2002 and the OAT-Bund spread reaching a 15-year high on budget paralysis, political fragmentation and weekend unrest and
violence that heightened concerns over fiscal credibility. By the European close, 10-year Bund yields were down 7.6 bps to 3.340% amid
rotation into safe-haven debt, while French OAT yields extended their sell-off, up 9.6 bps to 5.090% after reaching 5.142% intraday, their
highest level since July 2002. The French-German spread widened to ~175 bps, up from around 158 bps at the previous close, returning
to levels last seen during the Eurozone sovereign debt crisis. Across OAT maturities, the 5-year yield was up 11.7 bps to 4.467%, the 2-
year up 7.3 bps to 3.783% and the 30-year up a more modest 4.4 bps to 5.550%. Some contagion spread to Belgium, where the 10-year
spread over Germany hit a 14-year high, though fears of wider Eurozone contagion eased somewhat. Economists warn that the widening
has further to run, with several flagging the risk that French spreads could widen by as much as 200 bps before finding a floor and no
immediate relief or intervention likely from the ECB. UniCredit strategists noted that foreign investors' sizeable holdings of French
government debt leave scope for further selling, with Germany expected to be the main beneficiary and the Netherlands and Austria also
potential destinations.

ECB and euro-area data: ECB messaging remained cautious, with no hints of coming to France's rescue. President Lagarde warned that
higher bond yields would weigh more heavily on growth and curb inflation pass-through, while Kazaks said further hikes remained possible
if inflation stayed elevated. Most economists expect the ECB to leave rates unchanged next week and deliver a final 25 bp hike in
December, taking the deposit rate to 2.75%, although markets continue to price a steeper path, with ~75 bp of tightening by end-2027.
The S&P Global Eurozone flash composite PMI for October fell to 51.9 versus 52.7 expected, with services easing to 51.7 versus
consensus 52.5 and manufacturing output at 52.4 versus 52.7 expected, the lowest in three months. France was the main drag, with
output returning to contraction partly linked to strikes and unrest, and business confidence fell to a five-month low. Input-cost inflation
accelerated for a second month while output-price inflation slowed. In the ECB's September Consumer Expectations Survey, median
inflation expectations over the next 12 months edged up to 3.1% from 3.0% in August and perceptions of inflation over the previous 12
months rose to 3.6% from 3.5%, while the three-year-ahead median held at 2.9% and the five-year-ahead median was unchanged at 2.5%.

UK Budget, gilts and sterling: Chancellor Healey is expected to delay difficult fiscal decisions until next year's departmental spending
review, with the focus of next Wednesday's 28-Oct Budget on doing no harm and sustaining the positive sentiment around PM Burnham.
Healey has ruled out the most contentious revenue-raising options, the banking sector is widely expected to bear most of the burden even
though UK Finance highlighted that banks already pay a record £48B in taxes, and analysts expect only approximately £5B of revenue-
raising measures to fund a cost-of-living support package. September borrowing reached £24.5B, the highest monthly level so far this
fiscal year, and UK government debt exceeded £3T. Gilts have performed relatively well, with 10-year yields down 4.8 bps to 5.336% at
the close, down ~7 bps week to date and off Monday's high of 5.45%. The pound has outperformed most regional peers in October as
investors shifted out of Eurozone assets, with GBP/USD rising to $1.34. UK inflation accelerated to 3.5% in September, cementing a 5-
Nov BoE hike, with markets discounting at least three moves by Apr 2027 versus roughly two from the ECB, and BoE's Greene flagged
further tightening. Credit Agricole is more cautious, holding targets until the Budget and the BoE decision and expecting a dovish hike
that fails to validate the four moves priced.


UK activity and consumer data: The UK composite PMI hit an eight-month high of 53.1 versus consensus 51.7 and prior 52.0, with services
at 53.1 versus 51.8 forecast and prior 52.1 and manufacturing at 52.8 versus consensus 51.8 and prior 52.0, the best level in five
months. Employment returned to growth for the first time since September 2024, AI-related investment spending and technology services
demand supported activity, and input prices accelerated at the sharpest rate since June. Consumer data was weaker: September retail
sales fell 0.4% m/m versus consensus for a 0.1% drop, and excluding fuel sales fell 0.7% versus a 0.1% fall forecast. The GfK consumer
confidence index fell 11% to -24%, well below expectations for -14%, the biggest drop outside Covid since the Brexit vote in 2016, with
every indicator GfK tracks declining including plans for big-ticket purchases.

European gas prices and winter energy costs: European gas prices reached €91-93/MWh, a fresh multi-year high and on track for a fourth
consecutive weekly gain, as disruptions to Persian Gulf LNG flows kept the global market tight ahead of winter. European storage stood
near 75% of capacity as of late October, the lowest seasonal level since records began in 2009, and the IEA's Birol warned that Europe is
particularly vulnerable and that even a ceasefire would leave costs elevated for months. Goldman Sachs argues this is not a repeat of
2022, citing higher non-Middle East LNG supply, a 78% increase in Northwest Europe LNG import capacity, higher nuclear and renewable
generation, structurally lower demand and far less forced government storage buying, and sees a base case of ~€70/MWh by year-end if
Persian Gulf flows improve, with a tight-supply, cold-winter scenario capped at ~€150/MWh. The Guardian cited official forecasts that
the UK is expected to pay some of Europe's highest energy costs this winter, and NESO forecast a power buffer of 5.9GW, or 9.5% of
demand, versus 6.1GW, or 10%, last year.

Synopsis

Asia equities ended mixed Friday. Steep losses at the open were largely pared by the close, with many benchmarks ending higher. Japan's
two benchmarks missed the rally and ended lower, and India's main boards were also down. Greater China rallied strongly after the lunch
break to end higher, and South Korea also reversed an early loss. Taiwan remained closed amid a typhoon watch. Stocks gapped lower
at the open, with the large-cap tech names in Japan and South Korea all lower, but most of the prominent chip names in Tokyo and Seoul
battled back to the flatline or better by the close. It was nevertheless a poor week for equities: the MSCI Asia Pac ex Pacific index was
2.6% lower, with outsized declines on Japan's Nikkei 225 benchmark and losses in South Korea, Taiwan and Hong Kong. The US dollar
was flat, with little movement of note in Asia forex. Treasury yields were higher across tenors while JGB yields were lower. Precious metals
were down again, base metals were mixed and cryptocurrencies were strong. In regional developments, Japan September CPI rose to
2.1%, above the BOJ target range for the first time this year, and a Nikkei report said the BOJ would keep interest rates steady at its
meeting next week, although a December rate hike was still possible. October flash PMIs from Japan showed manufacturing growth
stalling and Australia's manufacturing sector contracted again, while India's reading showed a modest acceleration in growth. On the
corporate front, Nippon Steel (5401-JP) is to invest $3B to update a 90-year-old steel mill in the US, and HD Hyundai (329180-KR) and
Kiewit Offshore Services are to form a joint venture to build ship blocks in the US as part of the US-South Korea investment deal. SK Hynix
(000660-KR) has dropped plans to list its Solidigm in the US amid market volatility and shareholder pushback, and SK Telecom's
(017670-KR) data center unit is in talks to secure funding for a $350B data center build out. Rio Tinto (RIO-AU) is to invest $1.9B to
expand a bauxite mine in northern Australia, and Jio Platforms' IPO has been oversubscribed five times amid a surge in retail demand.
Macro

BOJ and Japan inflation: A Nikkei front-page story said the BOJ will leave the policy rate unchanged at 1.25% at the October 29-30 meeting
as board members monitor the impact of the September move, while likely signaling that underlying inflation has effectively reached the
2% target. The focus will be on keeping inflation stable at 2% as the board assesses the timing of the next hike. The decision is likely to
be passed by majority vote, as those who see a risk of a sharp inflation overshoot, as Governor Ueda mentioned at last month's press
conference, remain in the minority, and one senior BOJ official source affirmed that a back-to-back hike is unlikely unless there is an
emergency. The Outlook Report is likely to describe underlying inflation as consistent with the 2% target, which would not automatically
trigger a rate rise but would give more leeway to raise rates flexibly in response to upside risks. Japan nationwide core CPI rose 2.1% y/y
in September, matching expectations, after 1.7% in the previous month, a return to the 2% range for the first time this year. Inflation
excluding fresh food and energy was 2.4% versus consensus 2.3% and 1.9% in the prior month, the highest since March. Attention has
shifted to October, which saw another wave of price hikes on thousands of items, and the latest JCER consensus looks for core inflation
to peak at 3.1% in 1Q27 before fading notably, reflecting the scheduled consumption tax cut on food.

China state buying lifts stocks: Chinese stocks swung higher on Friday afternoon as turnover in a basket of ETFs favored by state-backed
funds, the so-called national team, picked up pace. The CSI 300 ended 1.7% higher, erasing a drop of as much as 1.9% earlier and
trimming the gauge's decline from the June peak after it entered a technical bear market on Thursday. The tech-leaning ChiNext and STAR
50 both surged in the afternoon, narrowing MTD losses to 14.4% and 12.1% respectively. Bloomberg noted inflows into nine ETFs favored
by the national team hit CNY84B ($12.6B) on Friday, the most in three months, and the E Fund ChiNext ETF (159915-CN) saw inflows hit
a two-year high of nearly CNY20B. Analysts are skeptical about the sustainability of state support, saying national team buying may provide
a floor and offer a temporary solution, while fading stimulus hopes and the global AI trade unwind added to the headwinds on top of weak
domestic consumption and earnings concerns at some of China's biggest companies. Separately, the FT reported that Washington pro-
posed caps on investments into China's technology and biotech firms.

October flash PMIs in Japan, Australia and India: Japan's manufacturing PMI fell to 53.3 from 54.1, indicating a slower pace of expansion
and the lowest reading since March, with new orders, new export orders and output growth all slowing while employment increased mar-
ginally. The composite reading was steady at 52.3 as continued services growth offset the manufacturing slowdown. Australia's composite
PMI slipped into contraction at 47.7 from September's 51.3 as overall demand slowed amid higher prices and interest rates, business
confidence fell, and service activity joined manufacturing in contraction. The survey also showed inflation could remain elevated as input
costs and output prices rose. The warning comes as Australia's chancellor Chalmers said GDP growth will likely be slower than the 1.75%
forecast in May, with unemployment likely to rise. India's reading showed a modest acceleration in growth.

Yen and JGBs: A Nikkei article discussed bullish signs for the yen as the US midterm elections and turmoil in France pose headwinds for
the dollar and euro, which could break the yen out of its recent narrow range between upper 156 and 158 per dollar. The yen hit a one-
month high against the dollar, and EUR/JPY is already at its weakest since November 2025. Prediction markets are pricing in a near 70%
chance of Democrats winning the Senate and roughly 90% probability of a majority in the House, and SBI FX Trade research highlighted
a historical pattern of yen appreciation in the months following nine of ten midterms from 1986 to 2022. Superlong JGB yields have
trended lower and the curve has flattened, with the 20-year rate retreating to its lowest since July, down 40 bp since early October. Nikkei
QUICK attributed this to easing concerns about the government's proactive fiscal stance as well as haven demand, with life insurers net
buyers for the fourth straight month through September and foreign investors net buyers for three straight, while domestic investors were
net sellers of foreign bonds for five straight weeks. BNP Paribas noted the Takaichi administration is paying greater attention to fiscal
discipline since the US-Japan joint FX intervention, though another flare-up in fiscal concerns could push yields higher ahead of the budget
formulation process at year-end.
