Earnings/Capital : All eyes on whether this week can be a clearing event for the AI financing debate or if
questions being asked can’t yet be answered. Following Meta’s cash-flow disappointment, the market needs
Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon to show that cloud growth, backlog conversion and operating cash flow are
keeping pace with infrastructure spending. Importantly, last week’s selling has already tightened financing
conditions, potentially creating the slowdown investors fear. SpaceX’s reported $40bn chip-financing package
supportive against this, but reports conflict on whether it has actually closed. Likely should mean cash-rich
bottlenecks should recover first if we do clear the hurdle this week and see relief, while leveraged
capacity builders still need to prove the economics.
AI/Compute : Lots of focus over the w/e on Musk’s warning around “stranded compute” in partially complete
DC’s (X). Not meant to mean demand is disappearing, but reinforces the risk we have been contemplating that
developers secure expensive GPUs but cannot synchronise power, cooling, networking and construction,
leaving depreciating hardware trapped in incomplete facilities. At the same time, AWS says large blocks of
capacity are largely committed through 2027 and into 2028. Operational compute can remain exceptionally
scarce while poorly executed or financed capacity becomes stranded. The debate here for me is whether
this supports bottlenecks, or in fact just a reality that we cannot build as fast as previously thought.
Oil/Geopolitics : Oil remains the macro constraint despite today’s pullback. Qatari mediation gives the market
some hope, but US military preparations and Trump’s comments keep renewed strikes firmly in the
distribution, while the Houthis claim further attacks on Saudi infrastructure. With the midterms just over 1 week
away and early voting already commencing, it feels we should get some clarity soon. A credible deal would be
the cleanest catalyst for lower inflation expectations, calmer rates and broader equity participation; whilst
renewed hostilities would likely reverse all three.

Thoughts : I’m constructive that the AI financing premium can fade if this week’s hyperscaler results deliver,
particularly after substantial de-grossing and systematic selling. But this is no longer a market where a generic
demand beat is sufficient. Investors want proof that revenue arrives before financing costs and depreciation
overwhelm the return. Lower oil and sub-5% 10yr yields help, while Moody’s decision to maintain France at
Aa3 removes one immediate European tail. The tactical opportunity may increasingly sit further down the
capacity chain, especially in less crowded European industrials and infrastructure. For now, the bull case is
hyperscaler cash flow plus stable oil; the risk is stranded capital meeting renewed geopolitical
escalation.

strategy, particularly the RTD roll-out. The problem with Campari is that positioning on the long side feels
heavy, so this could become a “travel and arrive” situation. People may get more cautious into Q4 as Aperitifs
are more Q2/Q3 weighted, so Sarti Rosa performance could fade in Q4.
