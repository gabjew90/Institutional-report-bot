Natural Gas Comment: Europe Winter Tracker: TTF Pricing High Winter 
Tightness Risk
26 October 2026 | 9:45PM EDT
TTF risk premium rises further. TTF is up 8% WoW at 95 EUR/MWh, with the 
n
Dec26 TTF contract, also at 95 EUR/MWh, suggesting a 73% (vs 51% last week) 
market-perceived probability of a low-Mideast-LNG-supply winter. We calculate 
this based on where Dec26 TTF is pricing relative to our rising-winter-supply 70 
EUR/MW base case and our low-winter-supply 105 EUR/MWh scenario1. The rise 
in the TTF risk premium is consistent with an increase in perceived Mideast 
energy supply uncertainty. Specifically, although month-to-date Persian Gulf 
LNG exports have averaged at 30% of normal, above our 20% Oct base case, an 
increase in tanker attacks near Hormuz earlier this month has kept daily tanker 
crossing numbers volatile and forward supply highly uncertain. Importantly, data 
from the UK Maritime Trading Operations center shows the number of tanker 
attacks has dropped over the past 10 days, suggesting a potential rebound in 
Mideast energy exports ahead. 
Oct weather has softened European balances. Despite the volatility in Mideast 
n
LNG supply, NW European balances have softened marginally WoW primarily 
driven by warmer Oct weather. We now expect the month to end with regional 
gas storage at 64% full (vs 63% prev.). 
China shoulder-month softness is over. Asia LNG demand is firming up, with 
n
the last two weeks of China LNG imports having moved up yoy despite domestic 
demand for natural gas remaining lackluster in Sep. We think the pick up in China 
LNG imports is consistent with its push to rebuild gas storage ahead of the 
winter, with the reported Sep gas storage build beating our expectations.  
Qatari LNG deal might just signal Qatari market share growth. We think the 
n
Qatari deal to buy US LNG announced last week reflects a push for long-term 
geographic diversification of Qatari supply - and potentially an indirect increase 
in Qatari market share. We think it brings limited, if any, insight about the 
potential duration of the ongoing Mideast conflict, because (1) the timing of 
deliveries in the deal is several years in the future (2030-2054), while Qatari LNG 
exports have been able to initiate a partial rebound even under a still volatile 
Mideast environment and (2) Qatar recently announced its domestic LNG export 
capacity expansion is finishing construction, with their guidance on first LNG in 
1Q27. 
1
Our base case assumes Persian Gulf LNG exports continue to improve gradually to reach 2/3 of normal in 
Jan27, and a max of 83% of normal (given long-term damage to Qatari export infrastructure) in Feb27. Our 
low-winter-supply scenario assumes LNG flows stay at 25% of normal through winter (Nov26-Mar27).
