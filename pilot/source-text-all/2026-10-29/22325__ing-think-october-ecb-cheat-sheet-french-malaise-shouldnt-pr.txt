October ECB cheat sheet: French
malaise shouldn’t prompt a dovish turn
Markets will scan the European Central Bank announcement on 29
October for any signs that French bond turmoil has triggered a dovish
rethink. We don’t think it has, and expect the ECB to leave the door open
for a December hike, which should shield rates and the euro from a
major adjustment. As usual, we also look at three alternative scenarios
to our baseline
The ECB is expected to leave rates on hold this week at 2.5%
This is our market preview of October's European Central Bank meeting; you can find
our macro preview here. 
In September, the ECB effectively brushed aside rising bond market concerns with a hawkish
rate hike. This week, a hold is widely expected. The key question is whether the latest turmoil
in French and other European bonds is enough to trigger a dovish shift in the ECB’s message.
We don’t think we are there yet. But equally, we admit that a more dovish tone on further

Four scenarios for October
As usual, we look at four scenarios for this meeting. As in previous instances, the market
reaction will depend as much on the statement and press conference as on the now-
customary tip to the media on Thursday afternoon. In September, the message was: “ECB
officials expect more tightening, with October in play”.
A hike this week would, however, be a big surprise (close to zero chance priced in), as inflation
has remained well-behaved, France has prompted a dovish repricing and President Christine
Lagarde has sounded a bit less hawkish.
In a meeting without new projections, the wording around December will be crucial. We expect
the door to remain open for a December move, with a broadly neutral and balanced message
to markets. Signalling that a hike by year-end is “likely” would be a slightly hawkish surprise in
our view, even if markets are pricing in 20bp.

Rates look for predictability amid fragile sentiment
Rates have started to deviate from their tight correlation with oil, but global factors remain an
important driver. Risk sentiment is more fragile, and not just because of the French turmoil, but
also because of renewed AI jitters. So whilst markets will listen carefully to Lagarde, we don’t
expect this week’s meeting to trigger a major repricing. A stronger dedication to a December
hike would nudge a hawkish repricing, but with more than 20bp already priced in, the reaction
should be contained. Both the ECB and markets understand the outlook is shrouded in
uncertainty, so any forward guidance will be weighed against that backdrop.
A bigger surprise to markets would be a 25bp hike, but this would not be taken lightly by

investors, especially if December remains in play too. Without clear second-round inflation
risks, an unexpected tightening of financial conditions while sentiment is fragile could trigger a
broader risk-off move. Expect French government bond spreads to widen and equities to
continue their drift lower. We might even see 10yr Bund yields fall on the decision, as growth
concerns return.
EUR could do with some ECB protection
The euro has become less sensitive to the latest moves in French bond spreads, with
undervaluation to short-term fair value still around 1-1.5%. Still, the common currency has
been hurt not only by the build-up of a fiscal risk premium, but also by the repricing lower in
ECB rate expectations because of the French turmoil.
This week’s meeting is unlikely to have the same FX impact as September’s, but currency
investors will be looking for any cracks in the ECB’s tightening bias as a sign that bond market
concerns could prompt a dovish rethink.
That probably leaves the balance of risks on the downside for the euro. Still, our baseline is for
no pushback against lingering hawkish bets, leaving the euro broadly stable after Thursday.
If the ECB does continue to fuel bets on a December hike, we think the bar is higher for French
spreads to deliver another big blow to the euro. Our preference remains for EUR/USD to return
to the 1.120-1.130 area over the coming weeks.
