UBS STIR Weekly: Wake Me Up Before
You Hike-Hike
Yijing Zheng
27-Oct-2026, 16:32 ET (EDT)
Last week saw a broader risk-off move across global rates, though local central-bank dynamics continued to
drive meaningful divergence. USD rates rallied sharply amid weakness in AI-related equities, but payer
interest remained limited, while EUR markets were dominated by French spread and funding concerns before
finding some relief after Moody’s left France unchanged. CHF broadly followed EUR, with December receiving
looking attractive as little is priced for the SNB. In Japan, underlying inflation momentum remained strong
despite an October BoJ hold being firmly expected, while Australia stood out on the hawkish side, with
Hauser’s inflation comments driving a sharp repricing of the November RBA meeting.
USD STIR: Rates Rally, Payers Stay Sidelined
USD rates rallied sharply midweek last week, with the back whites rallying over 20bp, primarily due to
weakness in AI-related equities and in spite of higher oil. Despite the lower rate environment, there was little
meaningful payer interest in the October meeting. Further out, pricing still embeds just under one hike through
year-end and roughly 40bp of hikes into the middle of next year. Flow was relatively subdued overall. There
was some marginally better SOFR paying in the front end and interest in paying next year’s turn, but otherwise
activity remained limited.
EUR STIR: A Bit Relieved
Europe delivered some of the most notable market moves last week. Throughout the week, significant
widening in German-French spreads was accompanied by broader credit spread pressure, and EUR basis
continued to grind lower, while previously strong funding support at the front end began to fade. Then, on
Friday afternoon after market close, Moody's decided not to downgrade France, and since then, basis has
been recovering and funding has been strong again. Positioning in basis appears somewhat cleaner than it
was several weeks earlier.
ECB expectations also continued to soften. The approaching October meeting was not expected to deliver a
hike, while December pricing fell to roughly 18-19bp last week and firmed back to 21bp on Monday, before
being offered again following an article from MNI citing a "Eurosystem source" saying a December hike is less
certain, as higher bond yields are substituting for some policy tightening and buying the ECB time to wait and
see.
CHF STIR: Receiving December Looks Attractive
CHF outright has generally traded with a beta to EUR, broadly in line with the wider fixed-income move over
the past week. With the December SNB meeting now pricing around 3bp of hikes, upcoming 3m real-money
rolls starting in November and a relatively low probability of an actual hike, particularly given the French
political backdrop, receiving December looks attractive.
Further out, the market currently prices roughly one full hike by the June 2027 SNB meeting, with around 13bp
priced for March and 17.5bp for June.

JPY STIR: CPI Trend Very Strong
Japan’s nationwide CPI data released Friday showed a sharp acceleration in September, rising 2.1% y/y from
1.7% in August. BoJ research data published Tuesday, two business days after the official CPI, further
showed a persistently strong inflation trend since July. The trend was not driven by specific items but occurred
across a wide range of indicators, including ex-fresh food and institutional factors, ex-food and energy, and the
trimmed mean. Strong inflationary pressures are evident across both goods and services.
An October BoJ hold remains the widely held base case, with markets pricing almost no chance of a hike into
Friday after a series of BoJ messages via media outlets over the past two weeks. Front-end OIS up to 6m is
better bid.
On FX/OIS, JPY funding remains very strong, trading at -5 FX/OIS. The very front-end paid remains attractive,
with funding at -14 FX/OIS in 1m despite incoming month-end flows later this week. Further out, JPY XCCY
moves higher alongside risk-on sentiment in the G10 XCCY space.
AUD STIR: Front End Reprices Higher On Hauser Comments
Australia was one of the clearer exceptions to the broader risk-off theme. Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser’s
message that the inflation forecast would need to be revised higher at the November meeting prompted a
meaningful hawkish repricing.
Around 16bp was priced for the meeting, accompanied by a decent amount of paying. UBS continues to
expect a November hike. Most of the adjustment has been concentrated around the front meeting, while the
remainder of the curve has been comparatively quiet and stable. Short-dated FX swaps continue to be fairly
muted in their moves.
