Views Quick Hits on EPS - 28 Oct 2026
Adam Wijaya  ·  Goldman Sachs  ·  Associate, Global Banking & Markets, FICC & Equities
28 Oct 2026
Desk Commentary + Quick Hits on EPS
BKR. Another strong quarter, positive momentum on IET and broadly in line OFSE expectations should
alleviate some of the concerns we were hearing into the print (particularly around IET rate of change of
downside to OFSE). Ahead of the call – struggling to see what folks could be picking at here – IET
Horizon 2 order guide higher, raising FY guidance (albeit with some ME assumptions) and highlighting a
large generation and cooling order from a major US tech company. We think positioning has generally
been lighter over the last couple of weeks, though still screens as a more owned name into the print.
DINO. With the stock outperforming vs the broader Refining group over the last 1m - expecting to get
more questions on what do from here. 3Q adj EBITDA of $2.98 bn well ahead of Street at $2.11 bn with
cash flow higher (though RIN / SRE impacts are topical and magnitude of the beat is more debated). New
CEO announcement with the print today – has been widely expected there would be an announcement on
this before YE. DINO has been one of the more crowded longs into the print this morning.
OKE. Investors have generally been more cautious on OKE – and the early feedback from the print
continues to highlight generally more caution on the path forward. 2026 EBITDA guidance raised by $200
mn at midpoint generally in line with Street expectations – and would expect buyside numbers to already
be closer to $8.6 bn for FY2026. Quarter itself was quiet on headline front and there are lingering
questions around the long-term EBITDA CAGR target achievement path.
PUMP. The bar was low into the print on both frac and power – and the print was generally in line with
expectations. Service revenues ahead ($355 mn vs Street at $317 mn) – though we think most of the focus
has been on the path forward for PROPWR. Company is pointing expectations to deploy >500 MW by
early 2028 and narrowed capex guidance. The question for the market on go forward is – with limited
number of MWs to contract with customers – how does the mix shift between O&G/Industrial/DC
players?
PSX. Has been one of the more debated stocks throughout the quarter – inland vs coastal, beta vs defense,
light vs heavy, buybacks vs deleveraging vs building cash – all topical on the desk. There are some mixed
results for the quarter itself – Midstream lighter vs expectations with Chems ahead – though think the
focus here should be on Refining and use of cash. Gross margin / bbl ahead of Street and the kit ran at
98% utilization for the quarter. Recall net debt at 2Q was at $16.5 bn – the company pointed to net debt of
$12.7 bn as of 3Q end… at the end of the day – buybacks + deleveraging have generally been well
received by the market.

EXE. Expectations were low for the quarter – and the key areas of focus have been on (A) production
profile changes, (B) CEO change – any shift in strategic priorities and (C) capex – which was previewed
to come in hotter at the print. Going in reverse order – capex did come in hotter for 4Q & 2026 vs Street –
inflationary cost pressures have been topical on the desk for several of the E&Ps and expectations were for
commentary to highlight this during earnings. On the CEO change – most investors not expecting a
material change in messaging on the call – recall this will be CEO’s first EPS call. On production –
revising the production guidance range is generally in line with Street at the midpoint – so don’t think
folks will spend a ton of time on this.
