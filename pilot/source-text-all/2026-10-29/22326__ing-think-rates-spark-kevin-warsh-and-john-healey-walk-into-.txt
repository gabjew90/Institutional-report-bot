Gilts should find some relief in a boring budget, but risks
remain
Gilt markets are always a bit nervous heading into the Autumn Budget announcement, and
this year is no different. We estimate the risk premium embedded in the 10Y gilt yield to be
around 20bp, whereas this was close to zero a few months ago. Overall though, global factors
continue to be the bigger driver of sterling rates and with energy hitting new highs, we don't
expect this to change.

But we do think a bit of a relief rally could take place as the budget turns out to be more boring
than Burnham’s ambitions would imply. Also, last year the auctions just after the budget
looked slightly better and gilts rallied on the shortening of issuance maturities. An unwinding
of the risk premium would offer up to 20bp of relief for 10yr gilts. Of course, a surprise
spending splurge could turn the story quite bearish as this would compound with energy-
related inflation fears.
Broader political risks, however, are likely to stay, and early elections could be the next surprise
for markets to deal with. We’ve learnt from France that markets attach more value to political
stability than to the decimal place of the fiscal deficit. If Burnham decides to reopen the
political battleground, we can expect investors to take a more cautious approach again to gilts.
Until inflation is under control, any government spending promises would quickly translate to
more upward pressure on gilt yields.

It seems the US 10yr is waiting to tick this FOMC meeting off
before it deviates too much from 5%
A solid performance for US Treasuries in the past couple of weeks has acted to reduce the prior
tightening impulse that had gripped the market. The 10yr yield below 5% obviously contrasts
dramatically with the prior break above 5.25%. We’re not convinced that the more recent
downward lurch will persist. But its genesis lends itself to a softer approach from the FOMC at
this meeting.
Whether we remain below 5% for the 10yr yield is a whole other question. We're minded to
expect it to snap back above, as we still have inflation, a fiscal deficit, and AI-boom-related
impulses at play. The Fed will no doubt remind us of these ongoing pressures. We think it
should ultimately move back above 5%; the timing, however, is a tougher call.
It's remarkable how the 10yr yield has been clinging to the 4.99% level. It's like a comfort level;
technically below 5%, but not too far below in case it needs to turn tail and shoot back up
again. A super dovish Warsh would validate its recent break below 5%. In contrast, a super
hawkish Warsh (without an actual hike) would tempt it back above. Anything in between
(which is most likely) would leave it where it is.
We’re likely still some way from the Fed spelling out a plan to shrink its balance sheet, which
we know remains a policy objective for Chair Warsh. We expect recommendations from the
various committees to surface closer to the turn of the year. Meanwhile, liquidity
conditions are stable, with repo behaving far better than it did a year ago.
Wednesday's events and market views
The FOMC meeting will be the highlight, but European markets will be closed by then.
Data releases include US weekly MBA mortgage applications and wholesale inventories
for September. Neither are market-moving. 
In terms of supply, Germany will auction €5.5bn of 10y Bunds. The US will auction a
