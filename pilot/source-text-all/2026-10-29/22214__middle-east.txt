COMMODITIES / ENERGY 
Oil pulls back as markets await next geopolitical catalyst. Oil prices are selling off 
in early trade today after rising to nearly USD 118/b at the end of last week. Brent 
futures are trading below USD 115/b while WTI is trading close to USD 101/b with both 
benchmarks down around 2% since the start of the week. US President Donald Trump 
gave no clear indication of supporting a new offensive against Iran in commentary he 
gave to the press over the weekend, saying that “it would be very easy” to “finish off” 
Iran but that he would also consider making a deal. With no material change in the 
geopolitical balance in the last several days, oil markets will be looking for the next 
catalyst to support another push higher. Time spreads remain well supported, 
particularly the prompt spread which straddles the period after the US midterm 
elections in early November, after which a renewed US strike against Iran appears 
widely expected. 
Gold stabilises after sharp weekly selloff ahead of key Fed decision. Gold prices 
are stabilizing around USD 4,020/troy oz after dropping more than 4% last week, their 
largest weekly loss since June this year. A pull back in oil prices in early trade is helping 
to limit some inflation pass-through expectations though energy futures still remain 
elevated. The next major catalyst for gold markets will be the FOMC decision this week 
and whether the Fed shifts its tone to more overtly hawkish. The October 28 decision 
is essentially entirely priced out by markets while the December meeting has about 
21bps of hikes priced in. Gold prices may also fall victim to sell-offs in risk assets as 
investors sell down positions in AI-linked equities.

MIDDLE EAST – MACRO / MARKETS 
GCC hiring rebounds in Q3 as UAE leads regional recovery. Hiring across the 
GCC increased 2% in Q3 2026, reversing a 3% contraction in Q2, according to Cooper 
Fitch’s Gulf Employment Index, signalling a gradual recovery in regional labour market 
conditions. The UAE led the rebound with 4% growth, following a 4% decline in the 
previous quarter, while Saudi Arabia and Oman recorded modest increases of 1.5% 
and 0.5%, respectively. However, hiring continued to contract in Qatar (-1%), Kuwait 
(-2%) and Bahrain (-2%), highlighting an uneven recovery. By sector, energy hiring 
rose 7%, supported by the resumption of previously suspended recruitment, while 
strategy and senior finance positions increased 6% each. In contrast, banking and 
investment management hiring declined 4% and 5%, respectively. While the rebound 
suggests improving labour market resilience, persistent weakness in financial-sector 
hiring and uneven country-level performance indicate that the regional employment 
recovery remains fragile amid broader economic uncertainty. 
AfDB approves USD 487m to strengthen Morocco’s economic resilience. The 
African Development Bank (AfDB) approved a USD 487m financing package for the 
third phase of Morocco’s Programme to Support the Strengthening of Economic 
Governance and Resilience to Climate Change, aimed at enhancing economic 
resilience and accelerating structural reforms. The programme focuses on 
strengthening public-sector governance, promoting private-sector investment and 
improving access to financing for small and SMEs. Additional measures aim to expand 
industrial hubs, strengthen trade infrastructure and deepen the integration of Moroccan 
industries into global value chains, reinforcing the country’s position as a commercial 
gateway between North Africa and Europe. The financing should support Morocco’s 
economic diversification, private-sector competitiveness and climate resilience, while 
improving its capacity to withstand external shocks.
