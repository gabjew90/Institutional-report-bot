Treasury yields were mostly up after the Federal Reserve held interest rates steady, as expected, with policymakers choosing to 
delay changes to borrowing costs before the November US midterm elections. In a 10-2 decision, the US central bank said it 
would maintain its benchmark interest rate within the current 3.75%-4.00%. The Fed also was awaiting further data on the path of 
inflation. The Fed hiked rates in September for the first time in about three years. Benchmark 10-year notes declined 8/32 to 
yield 5.0238%. Thirty-year bonds lost 20/32, with a yield of 5.3469%. The Treasury Department sold $30 billion in 2-year notes 
at a high discount margin of 0.038%. The bid-to-cover ratio was 3.05. 
The dollar edged lower against major currencies after thes Fed delivered an expected rate decision, while the New Zealand 
dollar fell sharply after the country's central bank surprisingly left interest rates unchanged. The euro gained 0.37% to $1.1163. 
Against the Japanese yen, the dollar sedged down 0.06% to 157.84 yen. The dollar index edged 0.18% lower to 102.14. The 
New Zealand dollar dropped 0.85% to $0.5632. 
Oil prices gained on renewed concerns about additional energy supply disruptions in the Middle East due to continued fighting 
in Yemen, an attack on a refinery in Saudi Arabia and worries the US could strike Iran after next week's US midterm elections. 
"Crude futures once again pricing in nearer term tighter supplies on news the US is likely to re-start military operations soon, 
which could be more intense than in the past given the latest attacks on Saudi Arabia and other Middle East allies," Dennis 
Kissler, senior vice president of trading at US financial services firm BOK Financial, said in a note. Brent futures rose 1.08% to 
$115.11 per barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude rose 2.63% to $102.53 a barrel. 
Gold prices were higher after the Fed left its key policy rate unchanged. "Long-end Treasury yields are moving higher with 
market not quite satisfied with Warsh's explanation of the hold today; higher rates will keep some pressure on gold. I don't think 
metals will sell off aggressively but the upside may be limited," said independent metals trader Tai Wong. Higher rates increase 
the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold. Spot gold edged 0.56% higher to $4,080.59 per ounce. US gold futures for 
December delivery inched 0.05% to $4,076.70 an ounce.

US DOJ is inquiring about Nvidia's purchase of Hugging Face, sources say 
The US Department of Justice has made inquiries about how Nvidia’s $12.93 billion deal to buy open-source platform Hugging 
Face would affect competition in artificial intelligence, at the same time as antitrust enforcers pursue their years-long 
investigation into the chip designer's conduct, two people familiar with the matter said. The Justice Department's recent 
questioning covers Nvidia's acquisitions, investments, sales practices, and whether the chip designer has exerted pressure on 
its customers, the two people said. On Hugging Face, Justice Department officials have asked whether the deal would let Nvidia 
capture a fundamental distribution point for AI, the people said. The acquisition is expected to close in the first half of 2027, 
Nvidia has said, adding that Hugging Face would continue to support work with a range of chip, cloud and AI providers. 
Apple shows pricey touchscreen laptops, a first for the iPhone maker 
Apple CEO John Ternus unveiled the company's first touchscreen MacBooks, abandoning a long-held aversion to the 
technology as it caps a burst of product launches ahead of the holiday shopping season. Ternus took the stage at an Apple 
event in Los Angeles and showed a MacBook Pro model with an organic light-emitting diode (OLED) display, playing up the 
display's contrast, sharpness and brightness in front of an audience of content creators and creative professionals. He also said 
the company would return to a glowing logo on the back of the laptops. "Look, we love the Mac, and it's the reason why so many 
of us found our way to Apple in the first place. And the Mac has never been better, and it's never been stronger," Ternus said. 
The event is part of a media blitz filled with new product launches following Ternus taking over as the company's new CEO. 
Meta and Snap face major US legal test of youth social media addiction claims 
Two teenage plaintiffs who say social media caused them to develop serious mental health problems will take Meta Platforms 
and Snap to trial in Los Angeles in the latest test of claims raised in thousands of US lawsuits over social media's impact on 
young people. The case is only the second to go to trial involving individual plaintiffs who allege that social media companies 
deliberately designed their platforms to be addictive. Social media companies lost the first such trial to the tune of $6 million in 
damages in March. Plaintiffs referred to in court papers as S.J., a 16-year-old girl from Illinois, and K.D.B., an 18-year-old 
woman from Mississippi, have said they began using YouTube as early as age 3 or 4 and other platforms around age 9. They 
have alleged that autoplay, infinite scroll and other features drove them to compulsive use, and they developed depression, 
anxiety and eating disorders as a result.

Oracle eases AI spending worries with plan to turn cash flow positive 
Oracle said it expects capital spending to peak this fiscal year and next as it builds more cloud capacity, but pledged to raise no 
more than $20 billion in additional financing through fiscal 2030. The pledge is meant to reassure investors worried that Larry 
Ellison's company will need ever-larger sums of outside money to fund its aggressive AI buildout. Speaking at Oracle's investor 
day in Las Vegas, CFO Hilary Maxson said Oracle also expects free cash flow to turn positive in the second half of fiscal 2028 
and top $30 billion in fiscal 2029, when capital spending is set to decline. Oracle has become a focal point of worries about the 
soaring cost of AI infrastructure because it lacks the deep revenue base of cloud giants such as Microsoft and Amazon and 
relies heavily on a single customer, loss-making ChatGPT creator OpenAI. 
Lilly's retatrutide outshines Ozempic on weight loss, blood sugar control in trial 
Eli Lilly said its experimental next-generation obesity drug, retatrutide, outperformed Novo Nordisk's Ozempic on blood sugar 
control and delivered more than double the weight loss in a diabetes study. The data strengthens the competitive profile of 
retatrutide, which is widely expected to fuel Lilly's next wave of growth following the success of its Zepbound and Mounjaro. In 
previous studies, the drug has already shown a bariatric-surgery level of weight loss in patients with obesity, likely positioning it 
to be the most powerful drug in the booming market. Commonly nicknamed "triple-G", the once-weekly retatrutide activates 
GLP-1, GIP and glucagon hormone receptors. Triple-G drugs are expected to deliver greater weight loss than earlier 
generations by combining appetite suppression, blood sugar control and increased calorie burning. 
GE Vernova boosts grid push with $9 billion Trench deal, lifts revenue forecast 
GE Vernova said it will buy high-voltage grid equipment maker Trench Group from investment firm Triton Partners for $9 billion, 
as the company moved to expand its grid business and raised its full-year revenue forecast for the third straight time. Utilities 
are spending more on grids to replace aging infrastructure and meet demand from data centers and industrial electrification, 
driving orders for high-voltage transmission networks and specialized equipment used to move power over long distances. GE 
Vernova said the deal would expand its grid manufacturing footprint and add Trench's Charlotte facility, where production of 765-
kilovolt equipment is expected to begin in late 2027. The acquisition also strengthens its presence in Europe and Asia and 
brings a backlog of about $5 billion. GE Vernova expects to split the purchase price equally between cash and debt, and close 
the transaction in late 2027. 
Frontier swings to surprise profit on 'healthy balance' between higher fares and demand 
Frontier Airlines has a healthy balance between ticket prices and passenger volume, its chief executive said, after the airline 
posted a surprise profit on higher fares and forecast a smaller-than-expected quarterly loss. To offset higher fuel costs driven by 
the Iran war, the ultra-low-cost carrier, like most airlines, has turned to fare hikes, capacity cuts and additional fees, but has 
nevertheless seen margins take a hit as fuel prices have surged some 90% since the Iran war that began in late February. While 
larger US airline peers are favoring higher fares over fuller planes, Frontier CEO Jimmy Dempsey said the airline is taking a 
more measured approach. "At the moment, we have a nice balance between yield and volume in our business, given the 
supply-demand dynamic that exists in the environment, we feel pretty good about it," Dempsey told analysts on an earnings call. 
Phillips 66 posts record quarterly profit on refining strength 
Phillips 66 reported a record quarterly profit, topping Wall Street estimates, powered by higher refining margins on fuel supply 
disruptions due to the wars in Iran and Ukraine. Phillips 66's refining segment reported a sharp jump in adjusted earnings to 
$6.06 billion in the quarter from $430 million a year earlier, while realized margin more than tripled to a record $38 per barrel 
from $12.15 per barrel last year. Phillips 66's net income rose to $5.62 billion from $133 million a year earlier, marking its highest 
quarterly profit on record. Adjusted earnings at its renewable fuel segment rose to $191 million during the quarter, compared to 
a loss of $43 million a year earlier. Separately, HF Sinclair reported a record quarterly profit, as elevated refining margins

US FTC demands Walmart, Uber, Delta and others hand over data on personalized pricing 
The US Federal Trade Commission is demanding information from Walmart, Uber and seven other companies on whether and 
how they use consumers' personal data such as location, browsing history and income to set individual prices, wages and job 
offers, a senior FTC official said. The FTC is flexing its consumer protection powers ahead of the US congressional elections 
next week, as voters consistently rank the cost of living as the most important issue. Target, along with app services Lyft, 
DoorDash and Instacart, and airlines Delta, United, JetBlue, are also receiving demands, the official said. The FTC will use the 
information to eventually publish a study to help inform regulators and lawmakers about personalized pricing and wage setting 
practices.

Insight and Analysis 
AI giants' funding ambitions test Wall Street's appetite for risk 
The heavyweights of the AI industry are facing a reckoning on Wall Street, reflecting concerns over who will bear the cost of the 
artificial intelligence buildout that has fueled much of this year's stock market gains. Anthropic revealed paperwork for its initial 
public offering on Monday, paving the way for a share sale reportedly worth $100 billion as early as next month. Meta also 
unveiled a plan this week that could bring it as much as $97.5 billion through equity offerings over the coming months. By 
pressing ahead with their enormous funding plans, the biggest AI players are wagering that investors' fear of missing out on 
future returns will outweigh their fear of overpaying for them. 
REUTERS OPEN INTEREST-Wall Street's wider weakness keeps AI trade rolling: McGeever 
AI remains the only game in town for Wall Street, and a lack of attractive alternatives is perpetuating this reliance. That risks 
making a crash all the more likely and painful when it comes. The concentration of US equity indices in tech is well known, but 
less appreciated is how weakness in other sectors is helping drive this phenomenon. Normally, when a market boom starts to 
seem a bit top-heavy, investors begin trimming exposure. Technology and communication services, just two of the S&P 500's 11 
sectors, are expected to deliver 42% of the entire index's forecast $783 billion in third-quarter earnings, according to LSEG. That 
share is expected to rise to 45% in the fourth quarter. 
Basis trade retreat spurs shift into new rate bets 
The slowdown in one of the US Treasury market's most scrutinized trades has driven investors to other corners of the rates 
market. The basis trade, a strategy in which hedge funds buy cash Treasuries using borrowed money from the repurchase or 
repo market while selling futures against them, has plateaued after several years of rapid growth, analysts said. The leveraged 
trade typically has become less attractive as rising Treasury yields and financing costs have increased the risks of borrowing. 
Regulatory changes have also allowed banks to hold more Treasuries on their balance sheets, reducing opportunities for hedge 
funds to profit from price discrepancies between cash Treasuries and futures.

Canada, European Union officials drop 'associate member' from joint summit declaration 
Canada and the European Union agreed to ever closer association but dropped the contentious idea of associate membership 
in a draft statement seen by Reuters ahead of a summit hosted by Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney in Montreal starting on

Canadian competition watchdog warns Air Canada-United deal could raise airfares

routes between Canada and the United States and drive up ticket prices on eight of them, costing passengers millions of dollars 
annually. The proposal, which the carriers made earlier this month, would allow the airlines to coordinate prices on routes where 
they currently compete for passengers. With the two carriers already dominating nonstop flights on several of those routes, the 
watchdog warned that closer cooperation would remove an important check on fares, leaving travellers with fewer ⁠competitive 
choices. Air Canada said the partnership plays an important role in supporting connectivity between communities across 
Canada and the US. ⁠Since the partnership was established in 2022, 20 new routes have been created between Canada and the 
US, and more than 4.3 million passengers have flown jointly on United and Air Canada.

Robinhood's shares fell after a prolonged chill in crypto markets dragged the trading platform's third-quarter profit below Wall 
Street expectations. Shares of the company ended 3.31% lower at $91.43. 
IPO BETS 
Prediction market bets set the tone for Anthropic's much-awaited IPO 
Prediction markets are seeing massive trades for Anthropic contracts after the AI giant's IPO filing, even as retail stock traders 
await the official opening bell likely next month. 
GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK 
MGM Resorts amends voting agreement after People Inc withdraws takeover proposal 
Resorts International said it had amended its existing voting agreement with People Inc after the investor withdrew its previously 
announced proposal to acquire the casino operator. 
DIGITAL ASSET 
Coinbase adds Ripple's stablecoin to its exchange 
Cryptocurrency exchange Coinbase is adding Ripple's stablecoin RLUSD to its platform, expanding its slate of dollar-backed 
crypto tokens.
