Our View - US cosmetics sales in NielsenIQ (incl Amazon 1P) decelerated slightly to 
+2.8% in the latest 2-wks ending 10/17/26 (vs. +3.0%/+5.5% for 4-wks/12-wks), as 
volumes declined -2.5% (vs. -0.9%/+2.4% for 4-wks/12-wks) and pricing 
accelerated by +5.5% (vs. +3.9%/+3.0% for 4-wks/12-wks). By category, face 
cosmetics $ sales grew +6.2% in the L-2wks (vs. +5.3%/+7.3% for 4-wks/12-wks), 
despite a volume decline of -0.8% (vs. +0.9%/+5.1% for 4-wks/12-wks), which was 
more than offset by price growth of +7.0% (vs. +4.3%/+2.0% for 4-wks/12-wks). Eye 
cosmetics $ sales growth of +2.1% in the L-2wks (vs. +2.9%/+5.8% for 
4-wks/12-wks) was driven by pricing growth of +4.2% (vs. +3.5%/+3.1% for 
4-wks/12-wks), partially offset by volume declines of -2.1% (vs. -0.6%/+2.6% for 
4-wks/12-wks), while lip cosmetics $ sales grew +0.1% in the L-2wks (vs. 
+0.7%/+3.3% for 4-wks/12-wks) as volumes declined -4.0% (vs. -2.2%/+0.6% for 
4-wks/12-wks). Elsewhere, fragrances $ sales growth moderated quite a bit to 
+1.7% in the L-2wks (vs. +8.2%/+14.9% for 4-wks/12-wks), as unit volume growth 
slowed to +6.2% (vs. +11.4%/+20.0% for 4-wks/12-wks), partially offset by pricing 
declines of -4.3% in the L-2wks (vs. -2.9%/-4.2% for 4-wks/12-wks). Additionally, 
facial skin care $ sales accelerated to +10.0% in the L-2wks (vs. +7.3%/+7.4% for 
4-wks/12-wks), on sequentially higher volumes, which grew +4.7% in L-2wks (vs. 
+3.7%/+7.1% for 4-wks/12-wks), aided by pricing growth of +5.0% (vs. 
+3.4%/+0.3% for 4-wks/12-wks). Promotional levels were sequentially lower across 
all categories in L-2wks except for the fragrance category. Against this backdrop, ELF 
continues to outpace beauty peers, with sales up +13.2% in the latest 2-wk period 
(vs. +12.7%/+14.6% for 4-wks/12-wks), supported by robust volume growth of 
+12.5% (vs. +12.7%/+12.9% for 4-wks/12-wks) and pricing growth of +0.6% (vs. 
flat/+1.5% for 4-wks/12-wks). Meanwhile, COTY’s total sales declined -1.1% (vs. 
-2.1%/+0.5% for 4-wks/12-wks) as volume declines of -3.8% (vs. -3.7%/-0.5% for 
4-wks/12-wks) were partially offset by pricing growth of +2.8% (vs. +1.7%/+1.0% 
for 4-wks/12-wks). Lastly, ODD’s recent weekly credit card spending data (from 
Bloomberg Second Measure) indicates that ODD’s credit card sales were flat y/y for 
the week ending 10/18/26 (vs. -7% the prior week). Credit card data suggests ODD’s 
sales were down -11.9% in Q3, below our estimate of -5.0% and suggests Q4 credit 
card sales are down -4.7% so far, above our estimate of -10.0%. Further, e.l.f. 
Cosmetics’ credit card sales were down -43% (vs. +50% the prior week), while 
Naturium and rhode’s credit card spending growth improved, at -33%/+59% 
respectively (vs. -47%/-2% the prior week).

Credit Card Spending Data 
 
 
We use credit card spending data from Bloomberg Second Measure, which provides 
aggregated analytics from billions of credit card and debit card purchases, using a 
subset of millions of consumers from a U.S. consumer panel that includes 20+ million 
members, in order to evaluate ODD’s and rhode’s recent sales trends since the 
companies are majority DTC in the US (though we note rhode is expanding in retail). The 
following charts analyze observed credit card sales, transactions, and average 
transaction value trends. We note that this data has had a strong correlation (0.85) with 
ODD’s reported sales growth trends over the past 21 quarters that we analyzed. The 
data also has a strong correlation to e.l.f. Cosmetic’s sales growth (0.53) over the past 21 
quarters and rhode’s sales growth (0.73) over the past 5 quarters.  
ODD 
Digging deeper into the weekly data, ODD’s credit card sales growth was flat y/y for the 
week ending 10/18/26 (vs. -7% the prior week) driven by ODD’s observed transactions 
up +6% (vs. +5% the prior week) and average transaction value declining -6% (vs. -12% 
the prior week). Digging deeper into brand performance, Il Makiage’s credit card sales 
fell -36% (vs. -45% the prior week), primarily driven by observed transactions declining 
-34% (vs. -40% the prior week) and average transaction value down -3% (vs. -9% the 
prior week). Spoiled Child’s credit card sales growth was stable, up +89% (vs. +88% the 
prior week) as observed transactions grew +110% (vs. +122% the prior week) and 
average transaction value declined -10% (vs. -16% the prior week). For Q3, Bloomberg 
Second Measure suggests ODD’s credit card sales were down -11.9%, below our 
estimate of -5.0%. For Q4 so far, Bloomberg Second Measure suggests ODD’s credit card 
sales were down -4.7%, above our estimate of -10.0%.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
