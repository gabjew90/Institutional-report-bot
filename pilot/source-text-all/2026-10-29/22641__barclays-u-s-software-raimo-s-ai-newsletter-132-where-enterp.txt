rAImo's AI Newsletter #132: Where
Enterprise AI Still Falls Short
This week, we unpack two recent benchmark assessments
highlighting where enterprise AI still struggles, particularly as
tasks require more complex information, cross-source
reasoning, and consistent execution. We also cover other key
developments across AI.
Key Takeaway: Accuracy and consistency remain key hurdles for enterprise AI. Model
performance declines as tasks require reasoning across more complex or conflicting
information, while getting the right answer once does not necessarily mean getting it
consistently. For investors, we think closing these gaps will be important as enterprises look to
move AI from experimentation toward broader production use.
Studies/Benchmarks: MMEnterprise-Bench, developed by researchers at KAIST (Korea
Advanced Institute of Science and Technology) and Korea University, evaluates 10 proprietary
and open-source multimodal models across over 1,600 questions based on simulated
workplace records. Results depend on the benchmark’s retrieval and evaluation design and
should be interpreted as a controlled test of enterprise-record reasoning rather than a direct
measure of production deployment performance. APEX-Agents 1.1 from Mercor Research
evaluates AI agents on realistic professional tasks, where models must find and reason over
information distributed across files and communications.
LLM Accuracy Drops as Information Requirements Become More Complex
There is nothing particularly surprising about AI performing best when all the data required for
an answer is kept simple and clean, and worse over complex data sets. However, the magnitude
of the drop may come as a surprise to investors. Using the best-performing model in the study
(GPT-5.6), we can see that accuracy drops from ~83% for single-text questions to ~52% for
multiple-text questions, while falling further to ~28% when multiple images are introduced. The
results suggest that synthesizing information across a variety of sources and documents poses a
vastly different challenge than understanding an individual piece of information. That
distinction should not be overlooked in enterprise AI, where real-world workflows often require
models to pull from scattered documents and formats.

Problem Amplifies With Contradictory Data
The challenges associated with interpretation become greater when the underlying data
contains sources of conflicting information or information that can be changed/updated over
time. In the simulated work environment created by KAIST and Korea University, models
performed relatively well on tasks where the relevant information was readily available, with
proprietary-model accuracy ranging from ~62-77% across these categories. However, that was
not the case for tasks requiring information that seemed to be conflicting. Cross-source and
Temporal questions saw a drop to ~51% and ~54% accuracy for proprietary models,
respectively.
• Cross-Source: Imagine an employee's title is listed differently across an HR record and a
•
more recently updated internal document. The model needs to identify the conflict,
determine which source should govern the answer, and avoid simply returning whichever
record it retrieves first.
• Temporal: Imagine asking what a customer's contract value was at a specific point in time
•
after the contract has subsequently been amended. The model needs to distinguish between
the current value and the value that was valid at the date asked about.
Knowing when not to answer is another important part of the equation. The benchmark also
tests whether models can recognize when the available enterprise records do not provide
sufficient evidence to answer a question, rather than filling the gap with hallucinations.
Surprisingly, this was a relative area of strength, with proprietary models achieving ~74%
accuracy on unanswerable questions, suggesting models were considerably better at
recognizing when an answer was not available than reconciling conflicting information when it
was.

More Context Helps, But Does Not Solve the Accuracy Challenge
If complex and conflicting data compromise model accuracy, a common-thought solution could

much as one would expect. Increasing the number of retrieved enterprise records from 10 to 50
improved accuracy among all models tested, with proprietary models seeing the greatest
benefit. However, even with 5x the data, overall accuracy for the best model still remained
below 70%.

Getting the Right Answer Once Does Not Mean Getting It Consistently
Additionally, accuracy is only one part of the equation. Mercor's APEX-Agents 1.1 benchmark
adds another important consideration: whether AI agents can reliably reproduce successful
outcomes across identical, independent attempts. The results show a meaningful consistency
gap, with leading models showing a ~17-31ppt difference between tasks successfully completed
at least once across four attempts and those completed successfully across all four. Put simply,
a model demonstrating that it can complete a task does not necessarily mean it can do so
reliably every time. For enterprise use cases, we think this distinction is particularly important,
as repeatability may ultimately matter just as much as peak capability.

Overall, the results suggest that improving model capabilities alone may not be enough to
accelerate enterprise AI deployments. Models continue to struggle as information becomes
more complex or conflicting, while greater context only partially improves results and
successful outcomes remain inconsistent across repeated attempts. For investors, we think this
supports a measured view on the pace of enterprise AI adoption, while reinforcing the potential
advantage of incumbent software vendors that already sit on top of enterprise data and
established workflows.
