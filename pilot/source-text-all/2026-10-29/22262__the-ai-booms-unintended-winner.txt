The cybersecurity bull lives on
Buying dips toward the 50-day moving average and trendline support has remained a simple but
profitable strategy in CIBR. While much of the AI trade has struggled, cybersecurity continues to
display impressive relative resilience. The structural bull case remains intact, and the latest
developments suggest the fundamental tailwinds are only getting stronger.
LSEG Workspace
The spending machine
The cybersecurity spending cycle shows little sign of slowing. SG estimates global information security
spending at roughly $240 billion in 2026, growing 12.5–13.3% annually, marking a fifth consecutive
year of double-digit expansion. Security now accounts for around 13% of IT budgets, up from 8.6% in
2020. Meanwhile, the AI-amplified cybersecurity market is projected to grow from $49 billion in 2025
to $160 billion in 2029. Quite the structural tailwind.

AI's unintended beneficiary
AI may be creating its own cybersecurity boom. BofA highlights the latest PHP2Shell vulnerabilities as
another wake-up call, with AI increasingly capable of discovering and exploiting security flaws at
machine speed. The implications extend well beyond one vulnerability: faster detection, vulnerability
management, threat hunting and AI-powered security operations all become more important. The
stronger the AI capabilities, the greater the need for cybersecurity.

AI's cybersecurity arms race
AI is dramatically changing the economics of cyberattacks. According to SG, roughly 76% of malware
now exhibits AI-driven polymorphism, while AI is increasingly automating phishing, credential theft and
exploitation. Defenders have little choice but to respond with more automation of their own. Gartner
expects AI cybersecurity spending to nearly double in 2026. The faster AI capabilities advance, the
stronger the structural demand for AI-powered security solutions.

Follow the budgets
Corporate budgets are backing up the structural story. According to BofA's latest CIO survey, cyber
budgets are expected to grow 7% in 2027, outpacing overall software spending. Half of CIOs expect
growth above 7%. Against this backdrop, BofA has raised price targets across several cybersecurity
names, including CrowdStrike, Check Point, SailPoint and Zscaler. Demand is increasingly structural
rather than discretionary.
The cybersecurity spending gap
The structural cybersecurity bull case has another powerful argument. According to a new World
Economic Forum report, more than half of organizations still make cybersecurity investments primarily
in response to crises, while just 8% cite demonstrated returns as the trigger. In other words, much of
the market remains reactive rather than proactive. As cybersecurity moves from an IT cost to a
strategic investment, the potential for a more sustained spending cycle looks considerable.
