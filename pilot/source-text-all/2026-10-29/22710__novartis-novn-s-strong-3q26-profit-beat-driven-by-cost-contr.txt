Novartis’ 2026 scorecard does not match the share price YTD reaction in our

(unadjusted sales of Rhapsido $9.5bn, ianalumab $4.6bn, Leqvio $4.7bn, Fabhalta 
$5.8bn), we still believe this is not enough to drive MSD growth into the next 2 waves 
of generic pressure across 2030-34 (Kisqali $10.1bn, Cosentyx $7.9bn, Kesimpta 
$7.5bn) and 2033-36 (Scemblix $4.3bn, Leqvio $4.7bn and Fabhalta $5.8bn). 
Additionally, YTD, Novartis’ financial results have been mixed with c.2% cumulative 
implied earnings upgrades from guidance updates (4Q25 revenue miss, 1Q26 
revenue and op profit miss, 2Q26 low quality beat, 3Q26 beat driven by cost cuts), 
there have been four Phase 3 misses (HORIZON, HARBOR, VAYHIA and remibrutinib 
in gMG), two Phase 3 trials below expectations on efficacy (REMODEL 1 & 2), one 
Phase 3 trial that has delivered positively as anticipated (RemIND) and a key 
mid-stage programme highlighted at the 2025 meet the management paused on 
safety grounds (YTB323 in immunology). Despite this, Novartis’ shares have bounced 
back c.15% since the lows seen on Sept 9th (now close to all-time high levels), which 
we attribute to general market dynamics (noting ROPC being up +15% also across 
this period, and the SXDP +11% since Sept 9th), as well as the acceleration in its 
buyback (from 130k shares per day to 400k shares per day since Sept 10th), and also 
a perceived lack of alternatives in the sector. While we do believe that the catalyst 
path is less risky from here, we see this outperformance vs. the sector as unjustified 
as we roll closer to the significant waves of LoEs.

Share buyback continues to be a driver 
 
 
As noted above, Novartis expects buybacks to remain a core pillar of its capital 
allocation strategy, with the ongoing $10bn buyback (expected to complete end-27) 
being key in supporting the shares following pipeline setbacks (i.e. pelacarsen and 
del-desiran - see Exhibit 1). We note since these setbacks, Novartis has increased the 
level of its buyback from 130k shares per day, to 400k shares per day. Additionally, we 
highlight the company undertaking additional buybacks outside of this $10bn 
programme (i.e. 12.1mn shares being repurchased at 9M26 for $1.8bn to mitigate 
full-year dilution related to participation plans of employees), which per our 
calculations, currently amounts to c.65k shares per day. On this basis, should Novartis 
continue at this 400k per day rate, our analysis points to completion of the $10bn 
programme by Feb’27. In order to hit the end-27 guided completion date, the buyback 
rate could drop to c.150k shares per day (incl. the c.65k ‘other’ amount, assuming the 
current share price of CHF 128.5 per share).  
 
Forecast changes 
 
 
We update our underlying forecasts for FX, cost-lines, and product-level 
adjustments. Key updates include: (i) increasing our weighted average PoS for 
remibrutinib to 85% (from c.75%) despite discontinuation in gMG, supported by the 
Food Allergy indication (now $2bn peak vs. prev. $1bn); (ii) increasing our PoS for 
pelabresib from 40% to 65% following EU filing; (iii) accounting for Novartis’ better than 
expected cost control; (iv) removing TIN816 and VHB937 from our model; (v) including 
the $1.1bn cash outflow from the closing of the Myricx acquisition in 3Q; and (vi) slight 
FX headwinds from 2027 onwards. Net-net, this leads to limited changes to our top-line 
estimates, and +2-3% upgrades to our bottom-line.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
