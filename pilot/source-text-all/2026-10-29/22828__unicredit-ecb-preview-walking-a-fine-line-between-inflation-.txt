ECB Preview
Walking a fine line between inflation and sovereign risk 
27 October 2026 
On hold this week, low bar for a December hike 
On Thursday, the ECB will almost certainly leave interest rates unchanged. With energy prices still on the rise 
and inflation risks tilted to the upside, a rate hike in December appears very likely. The impact of sovereign 
tensions on the outlook for monetary policy is likely to be a prominent topic during the press conference.  
ECB rhetoric on recent spread tensions is key 
With no new staff projections and markets fully pricing in unchanged rates, the scope for significant moves in 
government bond yields is limited. A relatively hawkish message could put further pressure on sovereign 
spreads while supporting Bunds through safe-haven flows. Conversely, a softer tone could encourage a further 
unwinding of rate-hike expectations. In FX, whether and how the ECB intends to manage renewed debt tensions 
within the eurozone would probably be the most critical factor for the euro’s reaction to this meeting.  
The ECB will almost certainly stand pat at its meeting on Thursday, remaining on track for a further increase in 
interest rates at its December meeting, as inflation risks continue to point to the upside. The decision is likely to be 
unanimous. Rising long-term rates in several jurisdictions have tightened financial conditions, buying the ECB time 
to better assess incoming data. The December round of macroeconomic projections will likely provide the Governing 
Council (GC) with a solid rationale for raising the deposit rate into slightly restrictive territory, from 2.50% to 2.75%. 
The reference macro picture is one of intensifying energy pressure on headline inflation as both oil and natural gas 
prices are drifting further away from the ECB’s September baseline. Against this backdrop, data still point to broadly 
stable core inflation, ongoing moderate economic growth and no evidence of second-round effects through wages. 
Sovereign tensions have increased, mainly reflecting investor concerns about the fiscal and political outlook in 
France, but have not escalated, remaining far from levels that would push the ECB to seriously consider intervention.  
At the press conference, ECB President Christine Lagarde is likely to face several questions regarding the implications 
of sovereign stress on the outlook for monetary policy. In our view, a strong focus on financial conditions would serve 
the ECB well here. As highlighted in our Coffee Break, a broad-based tightening of financial conditions would reduce 
the need for additional rate hikes. At the same time, Ms. Lagarde will have to show confidence in the central bank’s 
toolbox to cope with risks to the transmission mechanism of monetary policy if sovereign market dynamics become 
disorderly and not fundamentally warranted. In the current context, the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) 
would likely be the GC’s reference tool.

In our view, a slowdown in quantitative tightening (QT) would not prove effective at calming sovereign stress. 
Reversing or suspending QT at a time of heightened inflation risk would appear inconsistent with previous guidance, 
signalling the ECB’s reluctance to deploy the TPI, which could ultimately undermine the credibility and effectiveness 
of the tool.

Market reaction 
RATES 
Since mid-September, markets have scaled back expectations of an additional ECB hike before year-end, with the 
probability now at around 80%. Over a one-year horizon, investors are still pricing in slightly more than two additional 
rate hikes, which would take the deposit rate to just above 3%. 
Among the key market moves since the ECB’s September meeting, the widening in sovereign spreads, particularly 
French OATs, stands out. The stress remains largely confined to sovereign debt, as evidenced by the relatively limited 
move in iTraxx spreads. With the OAT-Bund spread at multi-year highs and some spillover to BTPs and SPGBs, this is 
likely to be a major focus during the Q&A session. As spread widening is primarily driven by domestic political 
developments, the ECB faces a delicate balancing act: reassuring markets while maintaining that fiscal challenges 
ultimately require a national response. A key question is whether Ms. Lagarde will emphasise downside risks 
stemming from market volatility. We think such a shift would be premature, but any signal in that direction could 
significantly support fixed-income markets. 
Oil and gas prices are up strongly, yet breakeven inflation is virtually unchanged. The ECB scores a point here, with 
its pre-emptive action having contributed to keeping inflation expectations anchored. We expect the central bank to 
flag more tightening, thus providing stability to inflation expectations. Indeed, another key point in the press 
conference will be how Lagarde will square the recent increase in energy prices, which should support more policy 
tightening, with the fallout from financial market volatility.  
Despite the recent weakness in AI and technology stocks, major European equity indices are broadly unchanged since 
the September meeting and are unlikely to be a key concern for the ECB.

With no new staff projections and markets fully pricing in unchanged rates at this week's meeting, the scope for 
significant moves in government bond yields is limited. Lagarde is also likely to reiterate the ECB's meeting-by-
meeting approach. As a result, investors will focus on any clues regarding the medium-term policy outlook, 
particularly against the backdrop of widening EGB spreads and AI-related equity market volatility. In this context, a 
relatively hawkish message could put further pressure on sovereign spreads while supporting Bunds through safe-
haven flows. Conversely, a softer tone could encourage a further unwinding of rate-hike expectations. EGB spreads 
should benefit but Bund yields are unlikely to rise much in this case.

FX 
A steady ECB meeting outcome, probably preparing markets for a rate hike in December, is unlikely to provide EUR-
USD with substantial support, although the eurozone’s forward curve is no longer fully pricing in such a move on the 
back of ongoing market turmoil, which is primarily affecting French bonds.  
That said, the recent EUR-USD decline from its highs above 1.17 to the new YTD low of 1.1114 reached on 14 October 
has been mostly driven by soaring UST yields, even before any yield-spread considerations (see chart below). Hence, 
any solution to the crisis must primarily pass through the US again. If US yields ease further, the USD will be forced 
to loosen its grip, potentially allowing for some recovery in EUR-USD. However, the margin for this is capped by two 
factors: 1. as Bund yields fall alongside UST yields, the corresponding spreads are unlikely to narrow much in favour 
of the common currency; 2. political tensions across the eurozone still represent a risk factor, further weighing on 
the euro. To some extent, whether and how the ECB intends to manage renewed debt tensions within the eurozone 
would probably be even more critical for the euro’s reaction.
