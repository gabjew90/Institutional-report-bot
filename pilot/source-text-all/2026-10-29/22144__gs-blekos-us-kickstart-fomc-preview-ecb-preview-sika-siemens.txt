US WEEKLY KICKSTART - One step back from the retail trader, two steps forward from household equity demand
 
• Going forward, the trajectory of the labor market and interest rates will be key determinants of household equity
demand, which in turn will help determine the near- and long-term path of US equities. 
• Household equity demand is sensitive to employment, wealth, and the competition from yields on cash and bonds.
Our baseline forecasts suggest household demand will remain solid in 2027, although recent macro volatility is a
reminder of the risks around those forecasts
 
US ECONOMICS ANALYST - October FOMC Preview: Hiking at a Slower Pace
 
• We do not expect significant new information from the press conference. The seemingly strong support for hiking
every other meeting means there is likely a high bar to deviate from that plan through the end of the year, which
should limit the impact of Fed communication for now. 
• And even if the FOMC decides to change course in the future and wants to communicate this to markets,
Chairman Warsh’s decision not to hint at the slower pace at his September press conference suggests he might
prefer to leave that to others.
 
European Daily: ECB Preview—Measured Tone
 
• We maintain our forecast for one more hike at the December meeting. An additional hike in March looks possible if
energy prices continue to rise (especially for gas in case of a cold winter). But we see a significant hurdle to
speeding up the pace of hikes or raising the deposit rate above 3%.
 
Sika (SIKA.S) - 3Q'26 Results: Pricing power with defensive end market skew; Buy (on CL)
 
• Sika (-2% Friday) reported a resilient 3Q topline result and reiterated FY26 guidance. We believe the result
reinforces Sika's position as one of the more defensive names in the sector, supported by strong pricing power and
positive volume growth despite a challenging inflation and rate backdrop. 
• Into 2027 Sika is one of the few names where we feel that 2027 consensus EBITDA forecasts are achievable
(alongside Holcim), vs. our forecasts which are MSD below most other names (SGO, HEIG, ROCK, BZU) given
pressure from the higher for longer rate backdrop.
 
Siemens Energy (ENR1n.DE) - Capital Market Day to outline higher growth & returns of value
 
• We see the Capital Markets Day (Nov 11) as the next step in the Siemens Energy re-rating story. In this note we
detail a checklist of items for the day, including our thoughts on 2030 targets. 
• We forecast group EBITA to increase c.6x between FY 2025 and FY 2030, putting our estimate 11% ahead of
Bloomberg consensus by FY 2030. This leaves shares trading on c.5x 2030E EV/EBITDA, with visibility and

growth expanding.
 
Norsk Hydro (NHY.OL) - SepQ’26: Premium support fades as cost pressures build; down to Neutral
 
• We downgrade NHY to Neutral from Buy as we see a more challenging earnings setup into 2027, and the stock-
specific differentiation underpinning our Buy thesis as failing to materialise. 
• NHY's Q4 premium guidance implies a step down on remaining unpriced volumes and increasing Middle Eastern
units are easing regional tightness quicker than we expected.
 
ENI (ENI.MI) - Strong Q3 results support guidance and distributions upgrade; 10.3% cash returns to shareholders in
2026E
 
• ENI reported a solid set of 3Q26 results today (October 23) with adjusted net income at €2,801 mn, +13% above
consensus of €2,475 mn. 
• The FY26 buyback guidance was upgraded to €3.8 bn (vs €3.4 bn previously) and an extraordinary dividend of
€1.3 bn was announced, now resulting in cash returns to shareholders of 10.3% in 2026E.

JCDecaux (JCDX.PA) - Examining momentum in Digital OOH, with Brazil a potential case study for Programmatic
adoption
 
• Our analysis suggests digitalisation remains the primary driver of JCDecaux's medium-term growth with early
evidence of the transition to programmatic. Our base case is traditional analog revenues are likely to remain
broadly flat to declining, as digital revenues continue to outgrow the group and increase as a share of revenues. 
• Based on our sensitivity, digital revenues could grow at a ~10-14% CAGR over FY26-30E even assuming low-
single-digit declines in traditional analog revenues to achieve our group forecast. We remain Neutral-rated, with the
favourable structural outlook for digital-led growth balanced by current valuation.
