FX Daily: Abatement in AI risk helps the
dollar
Fears of an AI bust have receded slightly, and the AI story has been
helped overnight by impressive 3Q results from Korean chipmaker SK
Hynix. This has allowed the market to focus on the core story of resilient
growth, high inflation and the prospect of higher energy prices – all
dollar-positive. Look out for the Fed, the Bank of Canada and the UK
budget today
Better news from the AI sector is softening a key threat to the dollar
USD: AI risks recede, hawkish Fed to dominate dollar
In an otherwise bullish dollar environment, one of the key threats in recent weeks has been
the AI-driven equity correction, as higher borrowing costs questioned business models –
particularly those of high-yield issuers. Those fears are slowly being assuaged as the big beasts
in this space continue to deliver. Overnight, SK Hynix announced strong third-quarter earnings,
strong order books and an attractive distribution to shareholders. Nasdaq futures are up
another 0.6% overnight and continue their 3% correction from last week's low. Focus now
turns to Alphabet and Microsoft, who report 3Q earnings after the market today.

EUR: First signs of weaker lending growth
EUR/USD remains at the lows of the year as broad dollar strength and an unresolved French
budget crisis dominate. Energy prices are at their highs and look to go higher on the prospect
of the US and Israel attacking Iran after next week's US midterms. On the eurozone side,
activity remains resilient and Friday's release of 3Q26 GDP data should be relatively
encouraging. However, yesterday's ECB bank lending survey showed tighter lending conditions
and an unexpected fall in corporate loan demand.
It may be too early for the ECB to pour cold water on market tightening expectations at
tomorrow's press conference, but the risks look to be building in that direction.
This 1.1065/1110 area is quite important support for EUR/USD, below which technical analysts
will be dusting off targets at 1.0880. Even though the market is reasonably long dollars
already, the reasons for EUR/USD to bounce from these levels are conspicuous by their
absence.

GBP: Taking its budget cue from gilts?
ING's Rates Strategist, Michiel Tukker, estimates that there is a 20bp risk premium in 10-year
UK gilt yields – some of which could come out of the market during today's UK budget release.
The budget itself is not expected to have too much say on the Bank of England's tightening
prospects, although any upside surprises on minimum wage adjustments could hawkishly
ripple through money markets.

CAD: A very close call on BoC today
We think the Bank of Canada will wait until December to deliver its first rate hike. But
speculative bets on a move today have built materially over the past few days. The trigger was
the jump in inflation expectations in the 3Q BoC Business Outlook report on 19 October. Pricing
for today’s meeting rose from 9bp to 20bp and remains there as of this morning. That more
than offset a softish September CPI print, with headline inflation rising less than expected to
3.1% and core measures staying well anchored around 2.0%.
Our call for a hold today stems from those relatively muted inflation figures and softening jobs
market. But we admit there is an increasingly higher chance that policymakers will hike given
concerns about inflation expectations de-anchoring and seek to avoid an adverse reaction in
back-end yields, given how much is priced in. If they do hold, expect a very hawkish statement
signalling a move in December.
Judging by the evenly split consensus, CAD faces downside risks today given the 20bp priced
in. A hike should, however, come with indications that more tightening is coming and could
send USD/CAD back below 1.40. Beyond the near term, we don’t see USD/CAD trading
sustainably below 1.40. We think markets are overestimating BoC tightening in 2027 and we
expect lower oil prices to leave the loonie exposed to a not-so-attractive domestic backdrop. A
return to 1.420 remains a risk in our view.
Francesco Pesole
