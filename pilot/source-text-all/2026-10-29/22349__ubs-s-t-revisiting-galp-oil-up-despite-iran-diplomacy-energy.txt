Spec sales comment
It is interesting that with October oil exports from the Middle East hovering in double digits (see the latest
Hormuz Tracker from Henri and team) and signs of renewed diplomatic efforts with Iran (admittedly led by
regional states rather than by the US), oil remain not far from the 6-month high. Markets have belatedly moved
to price in the risk of higher-for-longer scenarios for the medium/long term and of significant escalation in the
near term, with the potential for fresh disruption to exports trumping higher actual volumes. Reuters reports
that Hezbollah is preparing for new attacks on Israel after receiving funds from Iran, Houthi attacks on Saudi
Arabia and vessels in the Red Sea have picked up. Israeli PM Netanyahu reportedly pushed for new attacks
on Israel before Israel’s election today and US president Trump is reportedly still considering a restart of
military action following the midterm elections next week. Diplomatic channels are now open between Saudi
Arabia, Pakistan, Turkey, Qatar, Oman and Iran, but until the US makes a decision on the path forward the
market probably won’t see a diplomatic outcome as particularly likely.
Josh published overnight on Galp after solid 3Q results were overshadowed by the election outcome in Brazil
and the collapse of talks to merge Galp’s Downstream assets with those of Moeve. Galp beat by 4% at
operating level, 7% on NI and 14% on CFFO and raised FY EBITDA guidance, but saw an 11% dop in its
share price because of the Moeve and Brazil news. The outcome in Brazil sees another term for the left-wing
Lula administration and a continuation of the export levy that has already been one of the drivers of Galp’s
relative underperformance ytd, but does not seem to bring any incremental negatives. The failure of talks on
Moeve highlight a number of new negatives, however. Guidance from Galp as recently as a few weeks ago
was that talks were progressing, making this the second time in recent memory that Galp management has

2
proven to be far too optimistic on a major commercial and strategic issue. A successful deal was not fully
priced into the shares but was framed within the upside case at UBS and most specialists with whom I’ve
spoken. Management reiterated their commitment to carving out the Downstream business, and while they
said they remain open to partnerships there are no discussions currently active. The key negative from the
collapse of Moeve talks, though, is that it reduces the perceived probability of M&A focused on the more
streamlined Upstream entity that Galp would have become following a deal. There are other catalysts for Galp
– news on the Namibia drilling programme, a potential step-up in share buybacks next year and the ongoing
tailwind from a supportive macro environment. Josh retains his Buy rating and sees the opportunity in Galp as
similar now to the period following the dramatic correction after the announcement of the disappointing
Namibia deal with TotalEnergies. With yet another major hit to management credibility, however, along with
uncertainty in Brazil and windfall tax risk in Europe, investors are likely to feel no real urgency to reengage with
Galp.
UBS trading desk comment
#Markets : EStoxx -25bps following a US session which tracked sideways into the close (S&P +0.7%, Nasdaq
+0.8%) though US futures drifting overnight. AI IPO filings did the heavy lifting for sentiment yesterday, Semis
+c2.7%, AI Winners +2% - Hyperscalers earnings due later this week. Market-cap outperformed equal weight
and Growth and Momentum recovered, though neoclouds fell on AI infrastructure debt concerns. AI
fundraising carries on regardless of the recent damage to sentiment: Meta plans to raise up to $95bn for its AI
buildout, starting with $35bn in common and preferred shares. Asia trades higher for the most part led by
Tech/Memory given the read across from the US, KOSPI +4.6%, SK Hynix +5.5% on Korean media reports of
a further 50-60tr won capital return. Samsung +4.2%, and the Nikkei gained more than 2% to ~63,500 with
Advantest +10% and SoftBank +7%. China lagged into the Party plenum, with the HSI -0.2%. Oil remains the
biggest threat to keeping the early stages of a recovery rally intact: Brent briefly traded above $117 on the risk
of renewed US-Iran escalation after the midterms, before easing back below $115 as diplomacy resurfaced.
The Fed is expected to hold on Wednesday, but the real test for the rebound is hyperscaler capex versus
monetisation from MSFT, GOOGL and AMZN later in the week. Elsewhere, SX7E futures trade -65bps, HSBC
earnings unhelpful for the narrative for the sector due to commentary around deposit competition (-5% in HK
overnight).
#Flow : Flows on Monday finished better for sale (55/45), with turnover c.15% above the HT YTD run rate.
Healthcare was the busiest sector (26% of turnover) and finished better for sale, with supply in pharma, while
Banks (15% of turnover) finished better bid on demand for French and UK names - an interesting shift
following heavy supply over the summer amid political headwinds. Industrials (11% of turnover) saw supply in
electricals and maintenance, while Technology (10% of turnover) saw supply across semis and software.
Elsewhere, Insurance, Food & Bev, Energy and Basic Resources saw supply, while Utilities and Div Fins saw
marginal demand.
