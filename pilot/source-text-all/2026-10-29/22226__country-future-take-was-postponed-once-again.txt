Last week saw a continuation of the prior week’s market trends, with risk-off drawdowns in high beta and AI-related stocks
globally, accompanied by falling yields. The 10-year US Treasury yield is now under 5% again. The stock/bond correlation
flipping back to negative demonstrates the extent to which AI and the AI build-out is expected to underpin US growth and
broad risk sentiment from here, in our view. Hyperscaler earnings remain the key hurdle in the short term, with only Meta
having reported so far. This week Google, Microsoft and Amazon will report. With capital increasingly looking like the most
pressing bottleneck to data centre expansion, reassurance around AI monetisation will likely be the key swing factor.
Meta’s numbers were positive: it raised the lower bound of future capex guidance and reported strong AI earnings. However,
its comments on the tightening of funding markets spooked investors: “Recent significant capital raises from peers like
Alphabet and SpaceX, alongside anticipated fundraising from Anthropic and OpenAI, have created an increasingly
challenging fundraising environment.” Several industry leaders have confirmed this concern. BlackRock chief executive Larry
Fink said that competition for capital was the fiercest he had witnessed in his career.
Funding stress is so far confined to the weak links in the AI investment boom, and to France. US ‘CCC’-rated spreads have
widened to 11.27%, 8.03pp above single-’B’ and the widest gap since the pandemic. Single-’B’ spreads, however, are still a
modest 3.24%, and their 1.19pp premium over ‘BB’ remains closer to its historical lows (c. 0.5pp) than its highs (c. 5.0pp). This
points to localised stress rather than systemic stress.
Even if hyperscaler earnings are bullish, upcoming US midterms, which carry implications for data centre regulation, and
ongoing uncertainty around energy prices (and hence inflation, rates and ultimately funding costs for hyperscalers) may
make it difficult for markets to regain conviction around risk assets and tech in the short term. Meanwhile, with earnings
expectations continuing to be revised higher, valuations have compressed meaningfully. On 12-month forward earnings
expectations, emerging market (EM) stocks are trading at their largest-ever discount to US stocks, even as the S&P 500
valuation has fallen from 22x to 18.5x since October 2025. Despite returning nearly 70% since early 2024, EM stocks are
now nearly as cheap as they have ever been, trading at a forward price/earnings (P/E) ratio of 8.7x, only slightly higher than
2011 lows (8.3x).
In Brazil, President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was re-elected for a record fourth term on Sunday, taking 51.76% of the vote to
Flávio Bolsonaro’s 48.24%. This was the first-time a first-round leader has lost the run-off since 1989. The runoff was
controversial, with the Supreme Court ordering polling stations in several key states to remain open beyond regular voting
hours. Bolsonaro called for an annulment of the runoff, accusing the Supreme Court of changing the rules in the middle of
the voting day. Flávio’s annulment request is unlikely to hold water. President Lula will face challenging governing conditions,
with both houses of Congress dominated by Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party, a credit crunch affecting both families and
agriculture, and unsustainable fiscal dynamics. A fast move towards pragmatic economic policies is necessary to anchor the
risk premium and the currency and allow for lower interest rates. The hope lies in a bigger role for Vice President Geraldo
Alckmin, with a clear mandate to control expenditure and rein in deficits.
French assets are getting some relief after a weekend in which protests did not lead to serious incidents, and after Moody’s
kept its rating on French sovereign debt at ‘Aa3’ – one notch above Fitch and S&P. Moody’s said “slower growth and a higher
deficit have pushed France’s debt burden somewhat higher than previously expected”. Government bond yields have risen
more rapidly than Moody’s had expected and widespread social protests “could constrain fiscal consolidation efforts”.
Moody’s said the rating (with a negative outlook) “assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted, while
financing costs and social tensions will abate somewhat in the next few months”.
This week’s key events include monetary policy meetings in the US, Europe and Japan, as well as Canada and New Zealand,
with the last two expected to hike rates by 25bps. In EM, policymakers meet in Chile, Colombia, Thailand and Egypt. Israel’s
election tomorrow will have an important bearing on the geopolitical outlook.
Geopolitics

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and the Norwegian and Finnish leaders in Oslo. Putin agreed “in principle” to three-way talks

November, and Russia released a US detainee as a goodwill gesture. The two sides remain far apart on terms. Foreign

Commentary
Malaysia:  Opposition leader Hamzah Zainuddin said Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim is likely to call a snap election in August
2027, shortly before the Southeast Asian (SEA) Games in September, rather than wait for the February 2028 deadline. He
pointed to the ‘goodies’ in Budget 2027, including a larger MYR 367m allocation for the Election Commission against MYR
167m a year earlier, and argued there are signs the ruling coalition is starting to fray.
South Korea:  The Bank of Korea held its policy rate at 3.00% in a unanimous decision, pausing after 25bps hikes in July and
August to assess their effects. It kept a restrictive stance, with CPI inflation at 2.9% yoy in September against a 2.0% target,
and warned that 2027 inflation would exceed previous projections. It also signalled that growth in 2026 and 2027 would
significantly exceed its August forecasts of 3.3% and 2.9%, on robust chip exports and investment. Markets expect another
25bps hike at the 26 November meeting.
Vietnam:  Vietnam signed a free trade agreement (FTA) with the European Free Trade Association (EFTA), comprising
Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein, its 18th FTA and the final piece of its European trade network. EFTA
members will immediately remove tariffs on more than 90% of tariff lines, covering 99.5% of Vietnam’s exports to the bloc,
while Vietnam will remove tariffs on over 86% of lines, covering 99.9% of imports from EFTA, immediately or over three to 15
years. Bilateral trade was a modest EUR 4.8bn in 2025.

Commentary
Brazil:  President Lula was re-elected with 51.76% of the vote to Flávio Bolsonaro’s 48.24%, a margin of 3.5pp versus 1.8pp
in 2022. Right-leaning parties hold a significant share of seats in Congress, making coordination between the executive and
legislative branches a likely challenge. The unprecedented extension of voting hours may provide legal grounds to challenge
the results, yet the election is unlikely to be overturned.
Peru:  Fitch affirmed Peru’s ‘BBB’ rating with a stable outlook, citing strong external liquidity, low public debt and improved
political stability, as the new Senate’s veto powers and the right-wing bloc’s blocking minority reduce the risk of presidential
removals. It said the reform agenda still lacks clarity, and that the fall in the fiscal deficit to 0.9% of GDP is largely cyclical,
with the deficit seen at 1.2% by year-end as El Niño relief spending rises. Fitch expects growth to slow to 2.9% in 2026 and
inflation to reach 4.9% by year-end, which could prompt a rate hike by the Central Reserve Bank of Peru (BCRP).
Venezuela:  María Corina Machado returned to the country. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said her return was a result
of the political talks the US sponsored in Venezuela, which he said had the “best chance in 30 years” to hold free and fair
elections. Her return comes amid negotiations between the government and the moderate opposition led by Dinorah
Figueira, who was the head of the 2015 National Assembly and just returned from exile in Spain.

Commentary
Poland:  Finance Minister Andrzej Domański survived a no-confidence vote by 234 to 204 as the ruling coalition held
together, and Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced that Domański will be promoted to deputy prime minister. The
appointment requires President Karol Nawrocki’s approval, which could open a new front in the dispute over presidential
powers, given the president’s criticism of the government’s fiscal policy. Domański is unlikely to force consolidation but
should prevent further major fiscal loosening ahead of next year’s elections, with the general government deficit set to stay
wide but unchanged next year.
Romania:  Parliament approved Luca Niculescu’s government on 20 October by 280 votes to 58 (233 needed), ending a
deadlock of more than five months. Niculescu, an independent pro-European diplomat, was President Nicuşor Dan’s fourth
nominee. The cabinet has 11 Social Democratic Party (PSD) ministers and seven independents, who hold finance, foreign
affairs and energy. The National Liberal Party (PNL) and the Democratic Alliance of Hungarians in Romania (UDMR) backed
the government in parliament but stay out of the cabinet. PNL support is conditional on the deficit targets being kept and
runs for up to six months, and the PNL can file a no-confidence motion if commitments are broken. The far-right Alliance for
the Union of Romanians (AUR) opposed the cabinet, calling it a “puppet government” and demanding new elections, with its
polling support approaching 40%. The 2027 budget is due by 1 December, with the 2026 deficit target at 6.2% of GDP. S&P
affirmed Romania at ‘BBB-’ with a negative outlook on 2 October.
Serbia:  Former President Aleksandar Vučić conceded defeat after Sunday’s snap parliamentary election to the student-led
Student List. He had stepped down as president to lead the ruling Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) list and run for prime
minister. By his own projection, the Student List will take 135-140 of 250 seats against about 105 for the SNS, ending a run
in which he was prime minister or president since 2014.
Ukraine:  President Zelenskyy met US envoy Steve Witkoff with the leaders of Norway and Finland in Oslo to discuss
ceasefire proposals, ahead of Witkoff’s meeting with Putin in St Petersburg, and reiterated Ukraine’s readiness for an energy
ceasefire. Foreign Minister Lavrov ruled out political concessions, but suggested Moscow could accept something
“beneficial to us economically and financially”, while President Trump said “Ukraine has to want to settle this war”.

Commentary
Türkiye:  S&P said Türkiye has contained the investment fund crisis that erupted in September, as fast central bank liquidity
support and regulatory action stopped the liquidation of around USD 18bn of funds from spreading. Investors largely moved
the proceeds into lira deposits rather than foreign currency, allowing the central bank to partly rebuild FX reserves, with FX
deposits steady at 38.7% of the total. S&P expects the energy shock to slow growth to 3.0% this year and disinflation to
continue only gradually, with inflation averaging 30.9% in 2026 and 21.7% in 2027.
Egypt:  The Egyptian pound fell to a record low of USD/EGP 55.3 on the official market, extending a depreciation since
August driven by costly energy imports and renewed portfolio outflows, which have intensified this month. Sentiment has
also been hit by parliament’s debate on deploying troops to Saudi Arabia. Remittances, tourism and Suez Canal revenues
have offset some of the pressure, and the central bank is expected to hold rates at its 29 October meeting.
Morocco:  Prime Minister Fatima-Zahra El Mansouri’s new government won a confidence vote by 204 to 127, approving its
2026-2031 programme. It targets average growth of 5% in 2027-2031, a budget deficit below 3% of GDP and public debt of
around 62% of GDP by 2031, alongside higher social benefits and pensions and 1m new jobs. The opposition questioned
how the pledges will be funded, making the 2027 budget, now under debate, an early test.
Saudi Arabia:  Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport reopened after being closed since 10 October, when a Houthi attack
killed at least 13 people and injured more than 300, the deadliest attack on the Kingdom in over a decade. Most foreign
airlines have yet to return, with Lufthansa suspending flights until 18 November and Cathay Pacific until 31 January, and
organisers have postponed two of the country’s largest construction exhibitions by a year, citing regional tensions.
Zambia:  Zambia joined the first group of eight countries in a World Bank Group initiative to mobilise USD 19bn of metals
and minerals financing by FY30, up from USD 3.6bn in FY21-25, although the total combines World Bank financing with
private capital and allocations have not been disclosed. Zambia’s compact prioritises copper expansion and domestic
processing through better geological data, licensing, power and transport, in support of the government’s target of 3m
tonnes of copper a year by 2031.
