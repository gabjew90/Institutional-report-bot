The Bullion Report: Structural Support,
Tactical Caution
Jonathan Garber
26-Oct-2026, 09:22 ET (EDT)
• Gold: Structurally bullish, tactically cautious, with strong China physical demand providing support
• Silver: Headline-sensitive, with limited conviction and downside risk below 56.27
• DeMark Technicals: Gold is bullish, Silver is neutral, Platinum is neutral
• Electronic Trading: Gold volumes modestly higher, Silver volumes flat; spreads tighten across both metals
Contributors: Andrew Matthews, Christine Gilfillan, Dhiraj Singh, Laurie Kirby, Michael Crimmins, Vincent
Nickel, Joe Barra, Jason Perl, Ahmed Elmogi, Matt Slater, Hannah Kench, Sebastian Buergler, Amaud
Hageman, Jeff Hui)
New York precious-metals activity remains very quiet, particularly in the afternoon, with an exceptionally
narrow trading range highlighting the current slowdown. The broad client stance is structurally bullish but
tactically cautious, while recent stop-outs across several fast-money trades have reinforced the market’s lack
of conviction. Volatility-focused participants have moved to the sidelines because current price action offers
limited opportunities for meaningful exposure. As year-end approaches, participants are increasingly focused
on protecting existing P&L rather than adding incremental risk.
Gold remains caught in a low-conviction range, supported by meaningful buying interest near the lower end
but lacking sufficient momentum for a sustained rally. The tightening monetary-policy backdrop continues to
constrain the upside, leaving the market treading water and providing little incentive for discretionary
participants to take significant positions. Longer-term structural support remains intact through geopolitical
risk, de-dollarization, non-dollar settlement demand, and continued central-bank buying, but these
themes have yet to provide a catalyst strong enough to drive gold out of its current range.
Against this subdued trading backdrop, physical demand from Asia remains strong, led by kilobar
demand destined for mainland China, alongside some large-bar activity. Buying began around the start of
October and has remained consistent, arriving earlier than in recent years despite the period’s traditionally
stronger seasonal demand. Refinery conditions are also tightening, with production orders and available
refinery slots becoming more difficult to secure. Chinese demand is contributing to front-end tightness in
gold, while some market participants are showing interest in transacting against renminbi rather than
exclusively against US dollars.
Silver appears more fragile and headline-sensitive, with recent news triggering an aggressive short-term
reaction despite little material change in the underlying fundamentals. Its industrial characteristics may partly
distinguish its behavior from gold, although neither metal is currently demonstrating strong directional
conviction.
Potential catalysts include US elections, European political developments, and the broader shift toward more
nationalist and right-leaning politics, which could contribute to geopolitical uncertainty or reinforce the
currency-debasement narrative. However, a direct connection between European political developments and
gold remains too speculative. For precious metals, developments in Washington and Beijing are more
