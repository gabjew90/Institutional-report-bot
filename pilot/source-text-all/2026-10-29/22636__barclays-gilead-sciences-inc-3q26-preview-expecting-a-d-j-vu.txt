the overall PrEP market and more favorable channel mix. In the PrEP setting, Gilead benefits

giving this figure at 2Q26 results), unless there's a meaningful change from ~70%.

2026 driven by NCCN guideline updates.

Notes from our call with the company
HIV Treatment
Market growth and the ACA transition
• The treatment market has had fluctuations before and has always returned to the same
growth rate. Once someone is infected, they have HIV for life and have to receive treatment,
and there are many layers of access, so patients move channels rather than drop out of the
market. Management expects growth of 2% to 3%.
• That dynamic continued through the first half of 2026, and it shows up in treatment as a
volume effect rather than a price effect. The whole treatment market saw an impact from the
expiry of ACA subsidies. Plans came with higher premiums, patients moved around, and
some had to find a new physician, with a gap until they establish with a new provider.
Management characterizes this as a temporary impact on the treatment market, since
patients cannot forgo treatment and will regain access. This is consistent with the public
2Q26 commentary, where management attributed the slowing to a greater-than-expected
ACA impact, said it hit volume in the quarter, and guided the market back to the typical 2% to
3% annual growth rate.
• Longer term, the market grows as HIV grows. Only a policy outcome that eliminated
transmission entirely would change that. PrEP shrinking the infected pool works in the same
direction, but it would take very large numbers to move the percentage growth rate.
Biktarvy and Florida ADAP
• Biktarvy absorbed the ADAP impact, but those patients would have switched to something
else rather than gone untreated. Management expects it to reverse over a few quarters rather
than immediately: patients receive a three-month prescription, so the switch back lags. The
product was important enough to ADAP that it was put back on.
Bixlenvo portfolio fit
• Bixlenvo (bictegravir plus lenacapavir) fits as a two-drug single-tablet regimen for the switch
population, giving a dedicated, simple option. First adopters are likely to be patients on
multi-tablet regimens.
• The differentiation is the barrier to resistance. Bictegravir and lenacapavir each carry a very
high barrier, and management's point is that the two do not overlap, so Bixlenvo clears the
same bar as Biktarvy while being a two-drug regimen. Other two-drug regimens sacrifice
some of that; this one does not.
• The value is greatest for multi-tablet regimen patients, who are moving off multiple pills with
more adverse events, more drug-drug interactions, and a frequent need for boosting, into
something simple. That population skews older and is largely protease-inhibitor based, a
class Gilead has no legacy in, its own history being in INSTIs and NRTIs. Management has
previously sized the complex-regimen population at roughly 6% to 8% of US HIV patients,
many of them taking five to eleven pills a day because they have developed resistance to
older alternatives, typically protease inhibitors or nucleosides.
• There is some cannibalization of legacy products into Bixlenvo, but those products were
already a declining share of the business and were negotiated as part of the drug pricing
agreement, so Gilead is seeing less of them regardless. Not a significant concern.

HIV PrEP
Persistency disclosure and measurement
• Management does not intend to update the Yeztugo persistency rate every quarter, so as not
to get boxed in. Probably no further update unless it changes meaningfully from above 70%.
• Persistency cannot be backed out of IQVIA. The capture rate does not cover the whole picture.
Both the six-month injection and the orals show up in the data, and specialty pharmacy ships
the orals, so orals are over-represented at any given point in time.
• The measurement is a manual cohort exercise done at the individual level: a given person is
matched to that same person six months later. It is a difficult process. The label allows dosing
plus or minus two weeks around the due date, so measured on a weekly or monthly basis the
rate fluctuates. If returns cluster into a single month you get a large apparent increase in
persistency and thinner tails on either side, which can look like the rate is jumping around.
Channel
• Buy and bill came in ahead of what management expected at the start of the year. Once
systems had been updated for the J code, physicians were able to buy and bill Yeztugo on
their own, and the economics work for the physician.
Pricing and access
• Demand growth in PrEP is strong and is being compounded by pricing tailwinds that
continue as negotiations with payers proceed following USPSTF. The reference is to the tri-
agency FAQ published around October 2024, which named the PrEP products approved at
that time as named entities under the Grade A recommendation. Management has previously
quantified the effect: Descovy access jumped about 10 points in 1Q25 on the back of those
FAQs, and step edits requiring a generic TDF before Descovy were pulled. Payer negotiations
have continued from there.
• The contrast with Descovy is deliberate. When Descovy launched into a market that was
about to genericize, substantial discounts were required to move prescribers off Truvada,
because the differentiation was TAF versus TDF. Yeztugo is a far more differentiated product,
with a high barrier to resistance and unprecedented efficacy, so it does not necessarily
require the same level of discounting.
• On where price ultimately lands, management would not be drawn. Gilead has navigated
policy effectively for a long time, HIV attracts a lot of policy attention, and there are backup
options in play that management does not expect to need. They pointed to having worked
with the first Trump administration on remdesivir as evidence they can work constructively
with this one.
Anito-cel
• PDUFA is 23 December. Gilead is well into launch preparations to move as quickly as possible
once approval comes.
• Management would not expect authorized treatment centers to be activated immediately.
Given how much has to happen after approval, activation runs through the first half of 2027.
Sales will come in 2027.
• Gilead will launch with five times the number of authorized treatment centers that Carvykti
had at its own launch.

Future CAR-T: in vivo and fast CAR
• The safety questions are on the in vivo programs. It is still quite early, but Gilead has done
considerable diligence and analysis and believes it is well placed to develop a good in vivo
CAR-T, having run the process for a long time and studied what results from CAR-T made in
the body.
• On the competitor rapid manufacturing announcements from BMS and Novartis: faster
manufacturing produces more juvenile cells and, with them, more inflammatory events.
Gilead has not seen the same auto-inflammatory events in its own autoimmune programs.
• Asset selection was deliberate, and Gilead spent a long time evaluating multiple CAR-T
constructs before committing to the next-generation portfolio. KITE-363 is not rapid
manufactured and is the one taken into autoimmune. KITE-753 is rapid manufactured and is
for oncology, a different risk-benefit profile, without the same hyperreactive cells. The point
management was making is that it understands the risk-benefit profile differs by setting and
chose the construct it thinks is right for each population.
• IEC-HS is a class warning on all CAR-T. Every product carries that risk.
Trodelvy
• Management is very happy with Trodelvy. Growth recovered after the loss of the bladder

story. That comparison lapped earlier this year.
• Favorable guidelines arrived ahead of approval, with NCCN adding Trodelvy as a preferred
regimen in first-line metastatic TNBC, and Gilead saw more spontaneous use as a result.
• The first-line approval came on 24 June 2026 and covered both studies on the same day:
ASCENT-03 for monotherapy in patients who are not candidates for PD-1 or PD-L1 inhibitor-
based therapy, and ASCENT-04 for the combination with pembrolizumab in PD-L1 positive
tumors (CPS of 10 or above).
• Last quarter showed demand growth in both HR-positive and TNBC, which management
reads as a halo effect around Trodelvy in breast cancer generally.
• The next potential step is first-line non-TNBC.
