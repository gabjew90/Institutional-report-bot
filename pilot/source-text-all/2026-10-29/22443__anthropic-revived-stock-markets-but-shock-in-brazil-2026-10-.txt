Anthropic revived stock markets, but shock in Brazil
Global key stories
Stock markets started the week in a better mood in both Europe and the US, while oil prices have fallen
slightly from recent highs. In the US, the stock market was boosted by the news that Anthropic, which
among other things is behind the AI model Claude, has submitted an application for listing on Nasdaq. At
the same time, Meta announced that it is planning a new share issue of USD 95 billion to finance its AI
investments. Asian stock markets are mixed, with rising equity prices in Japan and IT-heavy Korea, but
down in Shanghai. Stock market futures are pointing slightly up in the US but down in Europe. In Brazil,
markets reacted with shock to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva's unexpected election win over Flávio Bolsonaro,
triggering a fall of close to 8% for the currency and 14% on the stock market. The market's concern is that
Lula will continue on the path of rising budget deficits and interest rates. Bolsonaro's party has now
recognized Lula as the winner. 
Otherwise, markets are awaiting this and next week's interest rate announcements from almost all the
major central banks and the US midterm elections. Market pricing for the Fed has crept higher with the
support of slightly calmer markets, but still points to a very high probability of unchanged policy rates
tomorrow. Betting odds and opinion polls still point to a Democratic victory in the entire Congress, but the
margins are small in several important Senate elections. A Democratic sweep would block Trump's fiscal
agenda and new appointments to among others the Fed, but not impede the White House much in other
areas such as foreign, immigration and trade policy. At the same time, the Democrats' ability to push
through their own policies will be limited by Trump's veto, and any initiatives from the Democrats will
probably be more about testing the policies ahead of the 2028 presidential election. From the US and
Canada we are getting signals of progress in the trade talks. President Trump decided the other day to
ease some of the import tariffs to support Maine's Republican Senator Susan Collins ahead of the midterm
elections. 
Household well-being is the focus of today's agenda, with consumer confidence surveys in both France
and the US, and in the US also durable goods orders and housing data. The question is how French
consumers have been affected by the recent sharp rise in bond yields and the violent mass
demonstrations. In the US, consumer sentiment remained near the lows in October, according to the
Michigan index. The Conference Board's index has held up better but has also fallen recently. We are
particularly interested to see households' views on the labour market, where the difference between
those who believe "jobs are plentiful" and those who believe "jobs are hard to get" tends to correlate with
unemployment. 
Deadlock ahead of Israel's general elections today. According to most opinion polls before the election,
the opposition, led by former IDF chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, looks set to win over prime minister
Benjamin Netanyahu but without reaching the necessary majority of 61 in the Knesset. Arab parties,
which several opposition parties have declined to govern with, will likely hold the swing vote. If no
government can be formed, Israel may go to the polls again early next year and Netanyahu will get to lead
an interim government until then. According to Israeli newspapers, the current IDF chief of staff has
opposed a request from Netanyahu to attack Iran shortly before the election, something that could have
