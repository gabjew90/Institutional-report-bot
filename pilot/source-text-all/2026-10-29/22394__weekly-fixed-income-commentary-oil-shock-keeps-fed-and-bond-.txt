Market recap
Global fixed income markets saw another flight-to-quality rally in government 
bonds as AI and technology stocks sold off. Political turmoil kept French bonds 
under pressure, with spreads between French and German bonds widening at 
midweek before retreating from their worst levels. Brent crude climbed to $117 
per barrel, and the advance in oil prices kept energy-driven inflation concerns in 
view. Treasury yields fell, but spreads widened as risk-off sentiment took hold, 
and the decompression extended beyond thematic names into more durable parts 
of the market.
Total returns followed duration. Municipals and Treasuries led as the rate rally 
favored longer-duration assets, while preferreds, senior loans, high yield and 
emerging markets posted losses as spread widening outweighed lower yields. Lower-
rated collateralized loan obligation segments were the weakest performers. Quality 
dispersion persisted within high yield and loans, with higher-rated paper roughly 
flat and CCC credits lagging. 
Outlook
The Federal Open Market Committee meets this week alongside a heavy data slate, 
including PCE inflation and third quarter GDP. We expect the Fed to raise rates once 
more by year-end, then hold through 2027. This path is less hawkish than current 
market pricing and depends heavily on how the energy shock passes through to 
core inflation. With Brent oil prices climbing again this week, that dependency has 
become more acute.
Credit fundamentals remain sound. The recent spread widening is concentrated in 
AI-linked and thematic credits rather than reflecting broad-based deterioration, 
though a second consecutive week of decompression could warrant closer attention. 
We continue to favor high-quality income and, in municipals, longer duration over 
Treasuries. We forecast the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.95% for year-end 2026 and 
4.75% for year-end 2027, with the 2-year at 4.70% and 4.50%, respectively.

U.S. Treasuries 
Yields fell across the curve, with longer-term rates dropping more than short-
term rates for the second straight week — a pattern known as a bull flattener. 
Rate volatility stayed elevated even as the rally progressed. Interest rate swaps, 
which large investors use to manage or hedge exposure to future rates, moved 
down along with Treasury yields. The gap between swap rates and Treasury 
yields barely changed, since the recent tightening in long-term rates stalled. 
The 2-year fell 2 bps to 4.68%, 
the 5-year 6 bps to 4.81%, the 10-
year 8 bps to 5.00%, the 20-year 
10 bps to 5.37% and the 30-year 
10 bps to 5.32%. 
Tax-exempt municipals
Municipals led the complex for a second consecutive week as the rate rally 
favored the sector’s longer duration. With the index yielding 4.31% and 
high yield municipals at 5.90%, absolute yields remain at levels that have 
historically attracted buyers. We view any technical weakness as a potential 
opportunity to add high-quality duration and favor longer-duration municipals 
over Treasuries.
The Bloomberg Municipal Index 
returned +1.14%.
Taxable municipals
Taxable municipals posted a solid gain as falling yields benefited the sector’s 
longer duration profile. Absolute yields near 5.61% provide a natural 
clearing mechanism and typically attract crossover investors. We see the 
current backdrop as a potential opportunity to lock in elevated yields at 
attractive valuations.
The Bloomberg Municipal Taxable 
Index returned +0.71% with 
spreads at 55 bps. 
 
Investment grade corporates
A volatile week brought notable repricing in thematic technology, media and 
telecom credits and in French banks, while broader weakness extended into more 
durable parts of the market. Liquidity narrowed meaningfully, particularly in 
thematic and higher-beta names. The credit curve flattened as selling concentrated 
in shorter maturities amid de-risking. Primary supply decelerated and fell short of 
estimates as higher borrowing costs gave issuers pause, and fund inflows slowed for 
a second week to well below the four-week average.
The Bloomberg U.S. Corporate 
Bond Index returned +0.34% with 
spreads at 89 bps.

U.S. high yield corporates
High yield had a difficult week. It opened roughly unchanged, then weakened 
through midweek as the AI and data center selloff drove sharp dispersion. Higher-
beta names in AI, data center and neocloud saw outsized moves in both directions. 
On Thursday, funding concerns in AI and telecom pushed cash prices lower. Friday 
opened mixed, with up-in-quality paper firm while lower-rated, telecom and data 
center names came under the most pressure. Primary issuance was lighter than 
in recent weeks.
The Bloomberg High Yield 2% 
Issuer Capped Index returned 
-0.11% with spreads at 337 bps.
Preferred securities
Preferreds posted a negative total return, closing lower on heavy early-week 
selling. Hybrids and Additional Tier 1 (AT1) bonds also declined as French 
assets came under renewed pressure, and spreads widened across the capital 
structure. Retail hybrids remained under pressure, with one credit still feeling 
the effects of the prior week’s disruption. The new issue calendar was busy 
as the market reopened. Deals carried elevated concessions but met strong 
demand and traded higher.
The ICE Preferred Index returned 
-0.39% with spreads at 196 bps.
Senior loans
Loans followed a similarly soft path. They opened quietly, then deteriorated 
as the new issue calendar picked up and lower-quality names came under 
pressure. Weakness set in at midweek, with lower-quality levels slipping and 
loan exchange-traded fund outflows amplifying the tone. Higher-quality names 
held up better, staying unchanged to modestly lower. Idiosyncratic moves in the 
airline and cable sectors drove much of the day-to-day activity, and several deals 
were pulled from syndication, signaling caution among issuers.
The S&P Leveraged Loan 
Index returned -0.31% with 
spreads at 518 bps.

Securitized credit
The price gap between agency mortgage-backed securities and the Treasury 
bonds used to hedge them was unchanged over the week. Mortgages performed 
well during Thursday’s rally, with Ginnie Maes delivering particularly strong 
excess returns. The 30-year mortgage rate recovered modestly and remains 
below its early-October high. Mortgage credit new issuance picked up again as 
a major industry conference wrapped. ABS paused with a more cautious tone 
as macro risks and digital infrastructure repricing continue to evolve, though 
technicals and inflows remain strong. High-quality CLO paper was well bid and 
roughly unchanged, while lower-rated mezzanine showed more softness. CMBS 
secondary flows rose as customers bought longer durations, but spreads moved 
wider and the AAA/BBB basis reached a year-to-date high.
The Bloomberg MBS Index returned 
+0.75% with spreads at 36 bps. 
The Bloomberg CMBS Index 
returned +0.30% with spreads 
at 72 bps. The Bloomberg ABS 
Index returned +0.23% with 
spreads at 49 bps.
Global emerging markets
EM hard currency sovereigns decompressed, with investment grade and high 
yield spreads both widening and several frontier credits leading the move 
lower. Corporates followed a similar pattern, with high yield underperforming 
investment grade and an idiosyncratic situation in a Latin American corporate 
acting as a major drag. Local markets gained modestly despite weakness in 
parts of Central Europe and Asia. Retail outflows accelerated for a second 
week, with local currency flows turning negative and non-exchange-traded 
fund redemptions picking up. New issue activity ticked up, though demand was 
below average and books were less oversubscribed than in recent weeks.
The Bloomberg Global EM 
Index returned -0.08% with 
spreads at 191 bps.
