A Moderate Boost to DM Inflation from AI Demand 
 
 
Global central bankers are increasingly citing the AI buildout as an inflation driver. 
Comments have generally focused on the risks from supply bottlenecks and higher 
import prices, as well as the potential timing mismatch between near-term domestic 
demand pressures and more lagged and uncertain productivity gains (Exhibit 1). In this 
Global Economics Comment, we refresh our global model (extending our US team’s recent 
analysis of US inflation pressures stemming from AI) to estimate the inflation impulses 
from AI investment in DM economies. 
 
We continue to consider three main channels through which the AI capex cycle is 
directly boosting inflation: the direct boost to consumer prices from higher memory 
prices, spillovers from higher memory prices to consumer electronics, and the increase 
in electricity prices associated with increased data center power demand.1 
First, increased demand for memory from data centers has led to outsized increases in 
memory prices. This is directly boosting consumer prices for memory cards, but in most 
countries the effect of these increases remains minimal (2-3bp on year-over-year core 
inflation) due to the tiny weight of memory cards in overall inflation baskets. The 
exception to this is US PCE inflation, where the weight of the software and accessories
