CA Economics: On hold this meeting, upside to hike 
The Bank of Canada holds a monetary policy decision on October 28. We expect the BoC 
to leave its policy rate unchanged at 2.25%, although it is a close call, as we think weak 
economic activity and core inflation below target will ultimately favor a hold, while 
headline inflation above 3%, persistently high oil prices, and rising inflation expectations 
leave meaningful upside risks and would likely make any hold a hawkish one. 
Weak activity and contained core pressures call for a hold 
The labor market weakened further in September, with employment falling 68.3k, the 
second consecutive monthly decline, taking cumulative 2026 job creation to -41.2k 
(Exhibit 1). The unemployment rate increased to 6.5%, while wage growth remained 
subdued at 2.3% yoy. At the same time, headline inflation increased to 3.1% yoy, largely 
reflecting higher gasoline prices, while the average of the BoC’s core measures fell to 
1.9% (see report), below target (Exhibit 2). Overall, weaker labor market conditions and 
contained underlying inflation pressures support our view that the BoC will remain on 
hold, although elevated energy prices leave risks skewed to the upside.

Inflation expectations versus trade uncertainty 
The Consumer Expectations Survey adds an important upside risk to the outlook, with 
longer-term inflation expectations rising to match elevated shorter-term expectations. 
This could make the BoC less willing to look through the inflationary impact of the 
current supply shock if expectations continue to rise. Inflation expectations measured 
through markets also show a recent upside trend (Exhibit 3). At the same time, trade 
uncertainty remains elevated and is unlikely to dissipate soon, with no trade deal 
between the US and Canada expected before the mid-term elections. In our view, this 
uncertainty will continue to weigh on the outlook and remains an important 
counterweight to the case for tightening. 
MPR with a new forecasting model 
The BoC will use a new forecasting model to produce the forecasts in the Monetary 
Policy Report (MPR). The new model (PRIMA) has a detailed supply section and can be 
used to produce different scenarios in response to supply shocks. The new model is 
likely to produce higher inflation forecasts in response to a more persistent oil shock, 
which could be used by the BoC to justify a hike.  
A hawkish hold vs a small adjustment 
We expect a hold on October 28, in contrast to the high probability of a hike that the 
market is pricing. But we think that even with a hold the BoC language is likely to remain 
hawkish given that inflation is above the target. If the BoC were to hike (25bp), we

Rates: A hike is largely priced  
As of Friday, Oct 23, markets are pricing in 21bp of a hike at the October BoC meeting. 
A hike accompanied by a hawkish press conference would likely drive modest bear 
flattening. A surprise hold could trigger a sharp decline in rates, although a hawkish 
press conference could quickly reverse the move. We offer our thoughts below.  
Hawkish communication drove the repricing 
Markets substantially repriced the probability of a BoC hike on Oct 19 from ~10bp to 
20-24bp, where pricing has largely remained (Chart of the Day). Despite a below 
expectations CPI print that day, the repricing was largely driven by a hawkish speech 
from Gov Macklem over the weekend and a pickup in inflation expectations in the 
Consumer Expectations Survey. The hawkish speech before the weak print appeared to 
have already boxed the BoC into a hike, based on market pricing. 
BoC hike: modest bear flattener 
A BoC hike is likely to drive a bear flattening in the GoC curve and could pull market 
pricing of additional near-term hikes forward. Macklem will likely point to the rise in 
inflation expectations and upside risk to inflation from higher oil as the primary driver of 
the BoC’s decision. We expect Macklem to keep the door open for another hike in 
December.  
A hike and hawkish press conference is likely to pull forward market pricing of additional 
BoC hikes. We would expect total hikes to stay around 100bp over the next year but Dec 
to move closer to fully priced.  
BoC hold: sharp but short-lived rally; reverse on hawkish press conference 
The BoC has only delivered a dovish hold 3 times since 2008 (Exhibit 4). Most notable 
was Jan 2022 when the market was pricing in 18-20bp of a hike. The announcement 
initially led to a sharp decline in rates but a hawkish press conference that set the 
foundations for a hike in March quickly reversed the move. If the BoC were to surprise 
with a pause, we would expect a similarly hawkish hold and expect markets would likely 
still push up pricing of a BoC hike at the Dec meeting.

Lean long CAD rates post BoC, especially vs the US 
We continue to believe markets are overpricing BoC tightening, given continued 
weakness in the data and limited spillover from higher oil prices into core inflation. We 
recommend clients lean long following the post-BoC price action and fade market 
pricing of additional hikes. 
We particularly favor being long Canadian rates relative to US rates. Over the next year, 
markets are pricing in 105bp of tightening in Canada versus 67bp in the US, despite 
stronger US data and persistently elevated US inflation. We therefore expect the Fed to 
deliver more tightening than markets currently price and by more than the BoC.
