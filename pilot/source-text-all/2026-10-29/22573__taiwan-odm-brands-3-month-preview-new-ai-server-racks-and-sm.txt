Taiwan ODM/Brands: 3-month preview: new AI server racks and 
smartphone models to drive growth in coming months
28 October 2026 | 8:54AM HKT
We preview 3-month revenues for 8 companies under our coverage in the AI 
server/PC supply chain, and update our estimates for Asus, Compal, Inventec and 
Pegatron. Across the supply chain, we are positive on: (1) Apple’s supply chain on the 
back of a ramp-up of new models with form factor changes (i.e. foldable phones in 
2026, and the iPhone 20 in 2027) to support end demand; (2) AI server components 
to ride on specification upgrades; and (3) ASIC AI servers’ rising penetration rate. 
Buy: Hon Hai, Wiwynn, Wistron; Neutral: Quanta, Inventec, Compal, ASUS; Sell: 
Pegatron. 
On a MoM basis, we expect the average revenue growth of the 8 companies to 
come in at -2%/ 0%/ 1% in Oct / Nov/ Dec 2026 (Exhibit 1). We expect largely 
unchanged revenue in the coming months, mainly on the AI server model transition 
and the modest consumer electronic products demand under high memory costs 
and consumption pull-in. 
On a YoY basis, we expect the average revenue growth of the 8 companies to 
come in at +68%/ +60%/ +44% in Oct / Nov / Dec 2026 (Exhibit 2), driven by the 
continuous ramp-up of AI servers, along with a PC product mix upgrade, new 
smartphone models, and high memory costs driving up the selling price.

Hon Hai (2317.TW; Buy, on CL): New AI server racks and Foldable phones to drive 4Q26E 
 
 
We expect Hon Hai’s monthly revenues to continue sequential growth through Dec 
2026, leading 4Q26 revenues to grow at +39% QoQ, driven by: (1) AI data center: The 
next-generation AI server racks and CPO switches ramp-up, with higher ASP and early 
delivery. We expect Hon Hai to be the first to deliver the latest model, around 2-3 
months ahead of peers, driving its Oct and onward revenue growth. Followed by 
GPU-powered AI server racks, the LPU-powered AI server racks should fuel the growth 
in Dec and onward, supporting the YoY increase in 1Q27. As our Global Server TAM 
update shows, we expect the AI server rack global value TAM to grow at 163% CAGR in 
2025-28E to US$1tn, or 63% of the global server value TAM by 2028E. And (2) 
Smartphone: riding on the major brand customer’s latest model launches along with the 
new form factor to attract more end users. As we highlighted in September (report link), 
we reduced our global smartphone volume estimates by 1m/ 8m/ 0.2m units to 1.1bn / 
1.2bn/ 1.2bn in 2026-28E, or -10% / +2%/ +2% YoY on high memory costs; however, we 
remain positive on the potential for global leading brands to outperform the market 
given their strong brand awareness, large procurement power, and customers’ stronger 
consumption power. We remain positive on foldable phones and expect premium 
models to continue to gain market share, which should be positive to Hon Hai. Maintain 
Buy (on CL). 
 
3-month revenues preview: We expect the company’s 4Q26E revenues to be +61% 
YoY/ +39% QoQ to NT$4.2 trillion. 3Q26 delivered strong revenues YoY growth of 47%, 
or 5% / 7% ahead of our / Bloomberg consensus, and we expect to see both YoY and 
MoM growth throughout Oct-Dec, driven by new AI server racks, new smartphone 
models, and CPO switches. Management noted that AI-related products remain on track 
to maintain solid growth momentum through 4Q26, while ICT products continue to 
benefit from typical 2H seasonal tailwinds, which should support overall operational 
performance in line with current consensus expectations.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
