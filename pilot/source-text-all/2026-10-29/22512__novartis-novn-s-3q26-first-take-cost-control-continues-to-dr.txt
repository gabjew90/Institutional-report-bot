Novartis (NOVN.S): 3Q26 First take: Cost control continues to drive 
earnings momentum, guidance upgrade suggests 2% upside risk to Core 
27 October 2026 | 7:25AM GMT
This morning (Oct 27), Novartis reported 3Q26 results, with a broadly in-line net 
sales, and an +8% beat on core operating profit (+$454mn), driven by substantially 
lower costs vs. expectations, with COGS being 2% below consensus ($73mn), SG&A 
being 10% below ($334mn), and R&D being 4% below ($129mn), despite prior 
commentary of increasing cost phasing in the 2H and incremental R&D investments 
and no apparent one-time positives as an offset. Revenue came broadly in-line at 
0%/$70mn, with beats in Cosentyx (4%/$67mn - boosted by price), Ilaris 
(12%/$66mn), and Zolgensma (13%/$45mn - Itvisma launch), offsetting the misses 
in Pluvicto (-8%/-$56mn - timing of ramp up of new indications), Kisqali 
(-1%/-$23mn), and Scemblix (-2%/-$13m). On this basis, Novartis upgraded its 
FY26 guide for Core Operating Profit, with the mid-point suggesting c.2% upside risk 
to consensus estimates on core operating profit, all else equal, and c.-1% downside 
risk to net sales driven by FX (all else equal). Other guidance metrics (i.e., net 
financial result and core tax rate) remain unchanged. On the pipeline, we highlight no 
meaningful changes on the key expected near-term catalysts. Overall, we see this as 
a strong beat on bottom-line, although the quality of the beat could be brought 
into question given communication around cost phasing for the year which has 
not materialised. On this basis, and taking into account the guidance range 
upgrade (which suggests c.2% upside risk to Core Operating Profit at the 
mid-point), recent pipeline updates, and Novartis shares having outperformed 
the SXDP by 4pp since the failures of both pelacarsen and del-desiran, we 
expect the shares to only slightly outperform today.

Guidance Summary 
FY26 guidance upgraded for Core Operating Profit: Novartis upgraded its 2026 group 
guidance for Core Operating Profit, whilst reiterating its guidance for Net Sales. The 
company continues to expect Net Sales to grow low-single digit CER, and Core 
Operating Profit to now be in-line to grow low-single digit CER (prev. low-single digit CER 
decline). If late-October exchange rates prevail for the remainder of the year, Novartis 
forecasts an FX tailwind of +1% on FY26 sales and +1% on core operating income. Based 
on the above, assuming a CER growth range of 1-3% for net sales and 0-2% growth for 
core operating profit for 2026 implies a 2026 financial range of ~$55.6-56.7bn for net 
sales and ~$22.1-22.6bn for core operating income. On this basis, the mid-point of the 
guidance ranges imply c.-1% downside to consensus Net Sales (driven by a slightly lower 
FX benefit from +1-2% to +1%), and c.+2% upside to consensus Core Operating Profit 
(per the guidance upgrade), all else equal. Novartis continues to expect a core tax rate of 
c.16.5% and core net financials of c.$1.7bn. 
 
Results summary. Group net sales came in at $14,522mn, broadly in-line with 
consensus estimates of $14,452mn (GSe $14,365mn). Core operating income came in 
8% above at $6,036mn vs. $5,582mn (GSe $5,490mn), driven by both a gross profit beat 
(37% of the beat) and lower than expected SG&A (c.74% of the beat) and R&D (c.28% of 
the beat). Of note, other operating income/expense came in at $250mn, significantly 
above consensus estimates of $53mn, driven by higher provision charges, including for a 
long-term supply agreement, and higher restructuring expenses. Group core operating 
margin came in at 41.6%, 294bps above consensus estimates of 38.6% (GSe 38.2%). In 
terms of key beats and misses Cosentyx, Ilaris, Zolgensema and Tafinlar+ Mekinist were 
the key drivers of the slight absolute sales beat offsetting misses in Pluvicto, Kisqali, and 
Scemblix.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
