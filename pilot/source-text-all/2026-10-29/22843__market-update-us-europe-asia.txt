U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.60% | Nasdaq +1.18% | Dow +0.03%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher on Tuesday, ending not far from best levels, although S&P breadth was mixed and equal-weight RSP lagged
the cap-weighted index by ~60 bp. The AI trade had another solid session, with semis and memory higher and neoclouds stronger. Big
tech was mostly higher, with TSLA-US again the notable Mag 7 gainer and GOOGL-US and AMZN-US also faring well. Other outperformers
included networking/communications, moneycenter banks, IBs, transports, E&Cs, space, industrial metals, cruise lines (RCL-US), small-
caps, nuclear and quantum computing. Laggards included energy, regional banks, payments, insurers, exchanges, hospitals, managed
care, MedTech, A&D (BA-US), entertainment, food/beverage, hotels (HLT-US), restaurants, casinos, REITs and media. The strength in AI
was attributed to the removal of some high-profile funding overhangs and renewed focus on the strong compute/capex demand narrative
expected to feature in this week's hyperscaler earnings, with some support from AI-related earnings reporters and positive takes on ORCL-
US announcements. Lower oil on hopes for ongoing diplomacy also helped, though there was no real incremental development and con-
cern remains about a near-term return to kinetic activity, and the latest consumer confidence report was supportive. Earnings themes

event tomorrow is the October FOMC meeting, expected to leave rates on hold and offer little guidance for December.
Macro

Broadcom backstops $63B Anthropic financing: Broadcom finalized a $63B debt financing package, the largest ever AI infrastructure
debt deal, to fund TPU chip procurement for Anthropic over a five-year term (Bloomberg). It follows recent reports that Broadcom was
working with a Wall Street syndicate to gather $60B+ of AI chip financing, though the deal ultimately required Broadcom CEO Tan to
secure commitments by negotiating higher interest rates and providing financial guarantees to protect lenders against Anthropic default
risks. The deal seemingly eases some fears around the availability of AI financing, following Monday's Meta $95B equity raise and
SpaceX's $40B debt raise, and frictions to building data centers. However, AI sentiment remains fragile, as some investors say the coming
Anthropic IPO could be the next major litmus test for AI firms' ability to raise capital and hang on to lofty valuations (CNBC).

Iran diplomacy shows little concrete progress: Not much was new on the Mideast front. Discussions between Iran and other regional
powers continue, with nothing concrete so far. Security chief Rezaei voiced some optimism about the previous day's talks, referencing a
"positive atmosphere," and said the region should be responsible for its own security and foreign intervention must be prevented (CBS
News). Some attention fell on nonspecific headlines about Rezaei's meeting with Iran's Khamenei, who stressed the need to secure the
conditions outlined by Iranian officials. It is unclear what impact the talks may have on rising expectations for a renewal of kinetic action,
given headlines about US military preparations and Trump saying he is near a critical decision (CNBC). The NY Times observed that the
timing of concessions remains a significant hurdle and that Tehran has repeatedly ruled out the kind of moves on its nuclear program the
US has been demanding.

Fed broadly expected to hold rates this week: The October FOMC statement is due at 2pm Eastern on Wednesday, followed by a 2:30pm
Warsh press conference. Analysts broadly expect a hold this month, followed by another hike in December and possibly one in March.
Multiple previews pointed to a "wait and see" tone from much recent Fedspeak, notably Waller on 8-Oct stressing that hikes do not need
to be consecutive. Two dissents are seen as likely (Hammack and Logan), and some see Kashkari as a possibility though he said he does
not have a strong view on pacing (Reuters). The big question for the statement is the sentence about a "timelier return" of inflation to
target: some analysts see that September rationale being removed, while Goldman suggests it could be expanded to show Fed focus on
the evolving outlook. Chair Warsh is again seen presenting no explicit guidance, though some think he could hint at a December move
and offer updates on the progress of his task forces.

October consumer confidence well above consensus: Consumer confidence rose to 91.7 in October from 84.2 in September, well above
the 81.0 consensus. Views of the labor market improved, with 26.7% saying jobs were "plentiful," up from 24.3%, while 21.4% said jobs
were "hard to get," unchanged; the labor market differential improved 2.4 ppts to +5.3%. Still, consumers planned less spending on
services and durable goods over the next six months, likely reflecting inflation pressures. Year-ahead inflation expectations declined in
October but remained elevated at 5.8%. Richmond Fed manufacturing remained flat at -2, as weaker new orders and employment offset
improved shipments, while price pressures were mixed. September new home sales came in at 676K versus 650K consensus and Au-
gust's 680K. ADP weekly private payrolls printed at +16.75K jobs/week over the prior four weeks, with the pace of growth decelerating
for a second straight week.

Preliminary September durable goods orders beat: Preliminary September durable goods orders rose +1.8% m/m versus +1.5% consen-
sus, accelerating from August's 0.1% gain and marking the largest increase since Apr-26. Orders ex-transportation were up +0.5% m/m
versus +0.6% consensus and August's +0.3%. Core capital goods orders (non-defense, ex aircraft) were up +0.5% m/m versus +0.7%
consensus and August's +1.6%, while core capital goods shipments, which feed into GDP, were up +1.2% m/m versus +0.8% consensus
and August's +0.6%. The FHFA House Price Index rose 0.3% m/m in August, above the 0.2% consensus and unchanged from July's 0.3%
increase, and the S&P Case-Shiller 20-City Home Price Index rose 0.3% m/m, in line with consensus.

EU Market Recap
Germany +0.7% | France +0.5% | UK -0.8%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished higher on Tuesday, following Monday's firmer close, with the UK FTSE lagging on weak earnings. Basic
Resources, Technology and Construction & Materials were the best performing sectors, while Autos & Parts and Oil & Gas were the main
underperformers. Markets extended gains as the AI trade and a pullback in oil provided support, with a solid day for the AI trade after
recent softness; the ongoing focus on capital intensity remained, but the removal of some near-term funding overhangs and renewed
focus on the positive compute/capex demand narrative were cited as tailwinds. Bonds were mixed, with the Bund down 1 bp at 3.37%
and gilts flat at 5.35%. On the macro data front, French consumer confidence hit a 13-year low due to protests, the BRC UK shop price
measure reached its highest level in two years on higher energy and transport costs, and the ECB bank lending survey showed a drop in
loan demand due to higher rates, while money supply data showed loans to the private sector holding steady in September. In other news,
French President Macron offered students funding and reforms to end protests, and Spain eased rules for data center power usage.
Ahead of Wednesday's UK Budget, reports said Chancellor Healey will raise the national living wage by ~4% but will not close the gap with
young workers, and will also remove import tax on up to 100 products to ease the cost of living for families.
Macro

Construction & Materials and Basic Resources lead; Banks and Autos & Parts lag: Construction & Materials led, with FER-ES detailing the
payment schedule for the I-24 Southeast Choice Lanes concession in Tennessee; the back-end-loaded profile could lift the project's IRR
to at least 15% from 11%. Basic Resources was also stronger, with miners firming up on higher base metal prices. Technology was higher,
tracking global optimism, and BESI-NL rose after Applied Materials said it will raise its strategic stake to ~20%. SMHN-GR cut FY26
guidance and announced ~70 job cuts. Banks were the weakest sector, as HSBA-GB posted a Q3 revenue miss tied to slower wealth
growth, despite a $1B buyback and an unchanged outlook. Autos & Parts was the second weakest sector as MBG-DE cut FY26 guidance,
now seeing an adjusted return on sales of 2-3% versus 3-5% before and industrial FCF slightly below last year, while P911-DE fared better
after 9M EPS of €2.06 and a confirmed FY outlook. Personal & Household Goods underperformed on consumer-staples earnings: RKT-
GB trimmed FY26 LFL net revenue growth guidance despite a slight Q3 revenue beat, and BEI-DE Q3 revenue of €2.36B missed the
€2.38B consensus, with organic growth of -1.3% and a warning on tesa's EBIT margin. Elsewhere, NOVN-CH Q3 core EPS of $2.46
compared with $2.29 expected and the company raised its FY26 core operating income outlook, BSLN-CH raised FY26 guidance, and
ORA-FR's Q3 EBITDAaL beat and it raised FY growth guidance.

Cautious positioning clashes with limited AI exposure, while Q3 earnings are supportive: Bank of America remained negative on the Stoxx
600 in a recent note and projects ~10% further downside by Q2 2027. The broker is underweight semiconductors, capital goods and
mining and overweight so-called "anti-AI" food/beverage and personal/household goods stocks, citing mounting signs that markets are
less willing to fund the AI infrastructure build-out (Investing.com); the recent move was reportedly the sharpest cyclical-to-defensive
rotation since the Covid shock. Citi flagged rising bearish bets, with the prior week's European inflows reversing and shorts added in the
Dax and Euro Stoxx 50, which recorded their sharpest weekly fall since Jul. More constructive views remain: Rothschild suggested
European fundamentals remain resilient, with Stoxx 600 EPS growth expected at ~19.1% for the current year and 8.9% in 2027 and the
index trading near its historical median at ~13.9x earnings, while UBS continues to forecast ~15% earnings growth in both 2026 and
2027 and BCA Research recommended "buying Europe" (Expansion). With ~30% of MSCI Europe market cap reported, Goldman Sachs
noted equal-weighted earnings 1.8% above expectations, with most companies beating, and EPS estimates up 2.1% over the past month
(0.6% ex-commodities). Morgan Stanley recorded a +13% weighted beat skew, down from +31% at the same point last quarter. Price
reactions show guidance and momentum matter more than headlines: reactions were balanced for modest surprises but sharply negative
for meaningful misses.

ECB survey shows higher rates weighing on loan demand: The ECB Q3 bank lending survey showed higher rates weighing on loan demand,
with all segments seeing a decline. Banks reported a net decrease in demand for loans or credit lines for firms, in contrast with
expectations for an increase in the prior survey and following a small net increase in Q2. Some banks cited uncertainty regarding
geopolitical and energy developments suppressing demand, while upward pressure came from demand for inventories and working
capital and several banks indicated a gradual and sustained increase related to AI adoption and financing of digital infrastructure. Higher
rates were the main headwind on housing loan demand, which saw a net percentage of -27%, more pronounced than the decline of -15%
in Q2, and demand for consumer credit and other lending to households also eased slightly amid a further tightening in credit standards.
ECB monetary data showed the annual growth rate of loans to households unchanged in September at 3.1% and loans to firms unchanged
at 4.2%. The ECB is set to leave policy on hold on Thursday, with the focus on whether it will act to lean against the upside in French bond
yields.

French consumer confidence hits a 13-year low as fiscal and political risks persist: French consumer confidence recorded its weakest
level in 13 years in October at 80 versus consensus 85 and prior 85 (revised from 86), the lowest since July 2013, and measures of
consumers' assessment of living standards hit their lowest since the survey began in 1972 (Insee). The economic backdrop has
deteriorated significantly since September amid fiscal worries, which saw 10-year French bond yields hit their highest level since 2002
last week, and the PMI moved back into contraction. President Macron has offered concessions to try to end the protests, but student
leaders are vowing to continue (Reuters). JPMorgan suggested ~5% of GDP in fiscal adjustment is required, that the 2027 budget draft's
€43B in measures faces risk of slippage, and that the OAT-Bund spread is ~55 bp too wide relative to fundamentals until political clarity
arrives; a stable post-election majority is unlikely and a far-right versus far-left runoff remains the base case ahead of the Apr-Jun 2027
elections. The CAC 40 has underperformed EU peers for three years and trades at a record-low relative P/E, with more than 75% of its

Synopsis

Asia equities saw another mixed day on Tuesday, with strong gains for the Kospi and Nikkei 225 benchmarks while the Taiex was flat as
it caught up after being closed for two days. Declines in Greater China were led by Shenzhen and Hong Kong, India and Singapore were
lower, and Australia posted a small gain. Tech stocks rose in a relatively quiet session as traders stayed on the sidelines ahead of the
week's main catalysts, including several key central bank interest rate decisions. The yen was higher, and the NZD was higher ahead of
the central bank decision tomorrow. JGBs were near unchanged and South Korea yields were notably lower. Precious metals were lower
and industrial metals mixed. South Korea's Q3 GDP was stronger than expected, while October consumer sentiment fell following the
stock market correction and higher energy prices. China industrial profits grew 15.5% in the Jan-Sep period, easing from Jan-Aug's 15.7%,
as subdued consumer demand dragged on several sectors. Moonshot AI (0YW033-E) apologized for limiting customers' use of its latest
Kimi model because of computing constraints. Beijing has granted Xpeng (9868-HK) and a mainland Volkswagen joint venture an auton-
omous driving permit (SCMP). The European Commission accepted Temu's (0QT6W6-E-US) plan to remedy the breach of the Digital Ser-
vices Act. TSMC (2330-TW) is planning to increase CoWoS prices by around 20%, according to local press reports. AirTrunk (0G9J9F-E)
has secured a $3B 'green' loan for a western Sydney data center.


South Korea Q3 GDP beats, while Taiwan and Korea central banks warn on an AI slowdown: South Korea GDP expanded 1.0% q/q in Q3,
above consensus 0.8%, following 0.6% in the previous quarter. The main driver was gross fixed capital formation, reflecting a notable
acceleration in facilities investment as well as a moderate rebound in construction. Export growth also picked up to 2.3% from 1.3%,
though the implied external demand contribution was largely mitigated by similar growth in imports. A Bank of Korea statistics official
said similar Q4 sequential momentum would likely leave full-year growth at 3.6%, and a 1.4% q/q print could elevate the 2026 aggregate
to 4% (Yonhap); the BOK in its October economic report flagged upside risks to its growth projections of 3.3% in 2026 and 2.9% in 2027.
Separately, Taiwan's central bank warned that 2027 economic growth would likely moderate following this year's high base, with FY GDP
growth easing to 5.8% y/y from this year's expected 11.5%, although strong technology demand would continue to drive exports (Reuters).
The Bank of Korea said chip exports could decline by $50-100B from earlier forecasts if capital spending by US tech firms slows in 2027,
and in a 'pessimistic' scenario in which chip unit prices fall 10% that could cut around 0.4 percentage points from South Korea's GDP
growth next year, with the current account surplus dipping below its $430B projection (Reuters).

China industrial profits in September jump most in three months: China industrial profits rose 14.4% y/y in September, jumping from
4.2% growth in August and marking the largest monthly expansion in three months. Profits for Jan-Sep rose 15.5%, compared with 15.7%
in Jan-Aug. Bloomberg noted that the rebound in producer prices last month likely contributed to the rise in earnings given elevated oil
prices amid tensions in the Middle East; China posted 5% y/y PPI growth in September, the fastest since 2022. Improvements in profita-
bility are heavily tilted toward a narrow set of sectors, including electronics and mining, with help from the AI boom and higher energy
prices, while consumer-led industries continued to splutter amid weak domestic demand and intense price competition that squeezed
margins.

EU mulls more measures to curb China imports in early 2027: Bloomberg sources said Brussels is planning to use safeguard measures
to further reduce imports from China, ranging from chemicals and furniture to machinery. The new measures, which could be either import
caps or tariffs, may be ready as early as Q1-27, as the EU seeks to replicate its recent move on Chinese hybrid vehicles, where the two
sides reached an agreement that would see the EU cap imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles at 350K per year for four years, with China's
quota rising 6% from the second year of implementation, and Brussels aims to put it into force from December (Bloomberg). The latest
move forms part of a broader EU push to reduce its giant trade deficit with China, now at more than $1B a day, while walking a fine line
so as not to cause a full-blown trade war. It is unclear what Beijing's response will be, but its earlier statement following the hybrid car
announcement warned the EU not to underestimate Beijing's resolve to defend its interests.

BOJ terminal rate projections lean higher, and Japan's life insurers signal more JGB buying: Nikkei QUICK's monthly BOJ Watcher survey
showed the bulk of respondents still see the terminal rate at 1.75% (16 out of 33), but some have revised up since last month's poll,
when a majority (20) were at that level, mostly to 2.00% (10), now a clear second and emerging as a compelling risk scenario. Estimates
of 2% or higher now comprise 45% (15) of the sample, rivaling the count at 1.75%, and the mode response sees the terminal rate reached
in Jun-27. Credit Agricole chief economist Takuji Aida, a reflationist advisor to Prime Minister Takaichi, was at the top of the range at
2.50% (by Jan-29). Median forecasts look for BOJ projections for GDP and CPI to remain unchanged, with no mention of recent reports
indicating the Outlook Report will declare underlying inflation as more or less having reached 2%. Separately, Nikkei reported that Meiji
Yasuda Life plans to buy about JPY2T ($12.7B) of JGBs in FY26, concentrated in superlongs (20y+) and more than double the initial
projection of JPY1T, and Daiichi Life (8750-JP) now expects JGB rollovers to exceed its JPY1T estimate. Of 10 domestic life insurers
interviewed, four indicated they will expand domestic bond holdings and two are increasing FX-hedged foreign bonds; all 10 plan to boost
alternative assets. Observers cited a prolonged Middle East conflict as an immediate risk, as higher petroleum prices stoking inflation
would pressure yields again and slow insurers' purchases, though Nippon Life Insurance hinted at the potential for "significant buying" if
concerns are dispelled.
