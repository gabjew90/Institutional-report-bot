The Week Ahead: 26-30 October
CITI'S TAKE 
We discuss the key macro events for the week ahead, a summary of our 
latest rates views, and upcoming supply for EMU-11, UK, and the US.
Key events — UK Autumn Budget (Wednesday). Central banks: FOMC/BoC/RBNZ 
(Wednesday), ECB (Thursday), BOJ (Friday). Economic data: US conf. board 
consumer confidence/durable goods (Tuesday), Q3 GDP/initial jobless/core PCE 
(Thursday), Chicago PMI (Friday). EA German IFO (Monday); French consumer 
confidence, EA bank lending survey/M3, Spanish unemployment (Tuesday); German 
GfK consumer confidence, EA quarterly balance of payments (Wednesday), Belgian 
HICP flash/Q3 GDP, EA consumer confidence, Spanish retail sales, Italian economic 
sentiment/unemployment (Thursday); German/French/Spanish/Italian HICP flash, 
German/French/Spanish/Italian/EA Q3 GDP, German/EA unemployment, ECB 
survey of professional forecasters (Friday). UK mortgage approvals/M4 (Thursday), 
DMP (Friday). Friday’s ratings: Moody's on EFSF and DBRS on Spain.
Strategy views — The ECB and OATs: The ECB would prefer to not talk about France 
this week, but it’s impossible to avoid. Unanchored OAT valuations likely mean 
careful words, a reminder of (conditional) backstops, but no plans for direct 
intervention (especially as it might not work). OAT-Bund widening began by tracking 
higher ECB hike premium (Figure 2). The causality then switched with OAT-Bund 
widening capping ECB premium and causing it to de-couple from natural gas prices 
(Figure 3). EGB spread volatility may be at a tipping point at which it doesn’t just limit 
further hikes but prevents them. We recommend receiving Dec26 ECB €STR 
(current: 2.63%). Swap spreads: Our analysis shows a simple way to look at the 5y 
forward curves underlying £ spot 5s30s in terms of exposure to swings in policy rate 
expectations while taking into account the latter’s speed of re-pricing. By 
constructing curves with no exposure to the 5y rate, we are able to meaningfully 
mitigate the conditional negative skew of long-end steepeners. 5y5y 10y5y and 
20y5y 25y5y, in particular, have stood out since March in terms of ability to minimize 
exposure to sharp front-end selloff regimes while retaining meaningful exposure to 
gradual rallies. SSAs: French SSAs have underperformed other major SSAs, and even 
OATs, in H2 26. As EUR rates have sold off sharply in H2 26, SSA bid-ask spreads 
have widened for all major issuers, but for French agencies, they have widened the 
most. Whilst their RV versus OATs has optically improved, their current, extremely 
wide bid-ask spreads question at what cost investors are willing, and importantly, 
able to transact in them. For full details, please see the European Rates Weekly. 
Pricing as of 16:30 BST, 23Oct26.

FOMC meeting (Wednesday) – US Economics 
n Citi economists’ base case is for the Fed to remain on hold this week and hike 
25bp in December. However, they note that, given the US economy was not 
overheating to begin with, a sustained sell-off in equities could mean that 
September’s 25bp hike turns out to be both the first and last hike of the cycle.
ECB meeting (Thursday) – European Economics
n Our economists expect the ECB to hold rates at 2.50% this Thursday, as widely 
expected. They expect communication to still focus on a cautious, meeting-by-
meeting approach, with the inflation outlook worsening since September but 
little evidence of second-round effects, while tighter financial conditions do part 
of the job for the ECB in reducing demand. Their base case remains for rate hikes 
in December and March 2027.
BOJ meeting (Friday) – MPM preview
n The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate unchanged this week. Our 
economists expect Board member Takata to again propose a rate hike, while the 
Outlook Report could revise up next fiscal year’s inflation forecast. They also 
expect the characterization of underlying inflation to advance from "approaching 
2%," while the BoJ maintain its rate-hike stance. Their base case remains for 
quarterly rate hikes through mid-next year, with the next hike in December and a 
terminal rate of 2%.
