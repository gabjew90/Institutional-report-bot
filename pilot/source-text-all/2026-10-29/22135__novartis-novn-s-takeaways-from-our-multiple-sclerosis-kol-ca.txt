Views on the data and comparisons between the BTKi class 
KOL views on the REMODEL data - From the data presented at MSToronto2026, 
n
the KOLs noted that it was a positive that there was a benefit vs. Aubagio, but the 
overall efficacy on ARR reduction was seen as moderate. The KOLs were sceptical 
that nominal significance was only achieved on a pooled basis (ideally they would 
have liked to see a benefit on each trial first - but we expect that the trial as 
designed was not powered for an EDSS benefit in each individual trial basis). The 
benefit on 6-month CDP-EDSS was seen as encouraging, however, the KOLs would 
like to see this benefit over an extended time period - of up to 1-2 years. These data 
gave the KOLs confidence that remibrutinib was better than Aubagio but not 
stronger than the CD20 class.  
KOLs see the BTKi class as having moderate efficacy, with fenebrutinib having an 
n
edge on efficacy, but remibrutinib having a better safety profile. The direction of 
the benefits on ARR and CDP-EDSS were seen as consistent across the trials, 
although the KOLs thought that fenebrutinib had an efficacy edge. On safety, the 
liver toxicity for remibrutinib was seen as favourable, but a trade off with efficacy 
was noted. On liver monitoring requirements, both KOLs acknowledged that is 
inconvenient but noted that (1.) it would not be an absolute barrier to use (2.) it is a 
patient led decision on which therapy to use, with efficacy being a key factor when 
deciding therapies for long term use and (3.) given the length of time for the use of 
the drug an inconvenient period at the start of treatment (up to 6 months) may not 
be that inconvenient vs. multiple years of use. 
Covalent vs. non-covalent mechanism - BTK upregulation could impact the long 
n
term efficacy. Dr McEntire noted that for the covalent BTKi mechanism data has 
been noted that while inhibition leads to a decrease in the short term, paradoxically 
longer term inhibition could lead to a paradoxical increase in gene expression of the 
BTK enzyme and activity. Therefore, one theoretical concern was that the covalent 
BTKi could lead to initial positive data over the first 6 months, but then the 
upregulation of BTK leads to a waning of efficacy.

ease of administration. Both KOLs saw all CD20 options (i.e. Ocrevus, Kesimpta and 
Briumvi) as broadly comparable, with uptake decisions being largely driven by 
patient preference on dosing, and insurance access. Initially, KOLs noted a strong

Views on safety profiles and approvability 
Remibrutinib’s liver profile viewed as attractive, although longer-term data are 
n

severe cases of liver injury with the BTKi class have tended to occur shortly after 
treatment initiation (e.g. tolebrutinib with DILI commonly emerging within the first 
90 days of treatment), the KOLs highlighted the need for (i) frequent early liver 
monitoring; and (ii) prompt discontinuation where elevations are observed. While 
remibrutinib has demonstrated a favourable liver profile to date, both experts

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
