Style Exposure
Investors have wondered when Boeing might deliver new cash flow targets and 
mgmt dropped them unexpectedly on yesterday’s earnings call. The 2027-29 
numbers are meaningfully below the standing consensus, triggering BA’s 10% 
underperformance vs the XLI. But Boeing estimates have tended to face pressure 
over time and a reset that diminishes the potential for downward revisions should 
be an opportunity. That said, these new estimates are not simply a mgmt decision 
to push consensus lower; there is real pressure on near-term numbers from delivery 
delays, higher costs, and rising capex. In this context, we think it made sense for 
mgmt to rip off the band-aid but this pressure on estimates near term means that it 
should take some time for the market to assess the viability of the new outlook. The 
key items to track are 777-9 certification, expected by mid next year and the pace 
of 737 and 787 production, deliveries, and unit profitability over time. For now, our 
cash flow estimates track mgmt’s targets and with our cash ramp shifting roughly 
one year to the right, we lower our Dec-27 price target to $240, which reflects a 
4.5% yield on our 2029 FCF estimate of 12/sh, discounted back a year.  
•
2027 FCF faces headwinds from advances, capex. The $3b guide 
incorporates minimal y/y growth ex one-timers and this was surprising to 
investors. It is true that 737 and 787 deliveries will increase, but the unit cash 
flow from these aircraft is low, given that they were priced long ago and that 
Boeing must give customers discounts because they are late. Meanwhile, 
Boeing faces a $1b headwind for capex, in part for F/A-XX, which was more 
than we expected. More significantly, advances should provide a few billion 
less y/y than the near $10b we model for 2026, which will be a record increase 
in the advance balance by far. Advances were also the savior of 2026 cash flow 
and seeing stabilization and then improvement in cash flow ex advances, which 
has faced pressure, should be important for shoring up confidence in the 
targets. The upside that emerged for advances this year came more from 
deposits at order signings, rather than PDPs, and also from international 
defense awards. Going forward, rising production rates and strong pricing 
should mean advances remain a meaningful source of cash, though uncertainty 
around order levels and collection terms mean the base case is that they will 
decline in y/y 2027 and then further in the years beyond. Advances have been 
a wild card in the model but we assume the contribution declines to < $3b by 
2028.  
•
Delivery ramp delayed, potentially baking in some buffer. Boeing 
continues to work toward stabilizing the MAX at 47/mo, delaying the move to 
52/mo into the back half of next year and indicating that incremental rate 
increases would take longer. Wing throughput has prevented Boeing from 
producing steadily at 47/mo, something mgmt mentioned last month. As a 
result, Boeing lowered the 2026 737 delivery guide from 500, and we now 
model 489, implying Q4 deliveries that are flattish sequentially. The move to 
52/mo will incorporate the new line in Everett, WA, where preparations are on 
track. Boeing will also need its Wichita facility (fka Spirit) to ramp as inventory 
winds down and we’ll be watching this. We assume ~550 737 deliveries next 
year, which would be up 12% y/y, just ahead of the ~10% increase mgmt

Summary Investment Thesis and Valuation
Investment Thesis
We rate BA Overweight. With balance sheet concerns addressed 
for the next few years and a backlog of ~$500b, we see potential 
for BA to outperform if the company can improve execution. 
Specifically, this means gradually increasing production of 737s 
and 787s while bringing the 777X closer to entering service. In 
addition, the company needs to stabilize performance in its 
Defense unit. Assuming production can reach the level that 
demand supports, we should see a positive multi-year reversal in 
FCF from 2024’s large outflow, and this should drive de-
levering. 
Valuation
Our Dec-27 price target of $240 is based on 22x our FY29E cash 
flow of ~$12/sh discounted back one year. We are moving the 
driver of our price target out a year as Boeing pushed out cash 
expectations due to additional challenges. While this is above 
where Boeing traded pre-pandemic, given higher multiples 
across the commercial aero group, we think it is reasonable for 
Boeing, given the potential for further cash flow and balance 
sheet improvement.

$10b need not be a ceiling but there is a lot of work to get there. Reaching $10b in 2029 
from $3b in 2027 is a big change, but as we’ve seen, FCF can move significantly in short 
periods at Boeing. The main driver of improvement in this case is rising unit cash flow 
per 737 and 787. It is quite low today but cash margin should be approaching at least 20% 
by 2029 and this is material for a revenue base across the two platforms that should exceed 
$55b. The main driver of improving cash margin is price, most of which is in backlog,

commentary that FCF can exceed $10b into the 2030s and we see this as viable. There

in 2029 on 737 vs a longer term target of 63/mo. In addition, the 787 target of 12/mo in 
2029 should move further into the teens.  777-9 meanwhile, will only be in its first cash-
positive year in 2029, with potential for higher production and better profitability, and we 
should see growth at BDS, in part due to Boeing’s two sixth-gen fighter programs.  
Regarding a new narrowbody, our base case is that additional spending will not be 
especially high at the start of the decade and we've kept our capex and R&D robust in any 
case. Putting all this together, our model shows cash flow growth beyond 2029, reaching 
mid-teens billions in 2032. That said, this involves a number of assumptions. Meanwhile, 
having just cut cash flow for the closer-in periods, it’s early to give much credit for the 
further-out years. The focus instead will be on gradually producing and delivering more 
airplanes with better profitability.  
•
BCA margins facing pressure. BCA margins face near term pressure from the supply 
chain, both on 737 & 787 as well as the 777 and 767. Vaero halted deliveries of the trailing-
edge rib kit for the wing, impacting work on both 777 and 767. The costs will be 
recognized in abnormal production costs over Q4 and early next year, though mgmt did 
not quantify the impact. 737 and 787 face higher projected costs associated with later 
production rate adjustments and higher supply chain costs. Altogether then, the GAAP 
margin at BCA should step down from Q3’s -6.5% and we model -8.5%. BCA margin rate 
should remain negative in 2027 and we assume it turns positive in 2028. Progress here, 
which has slipped multiple times, would be a welcome development and help signal that 
cost growth is under control. 
•
BDS brought a positive update, growth estimates moving higher. Following the F/A-
XX win and other program progress, growth at BDS is expected to accelerate from the 
previous LSD rate longer term toward MSD or higher. We model consistent MSD growth 
through 2030 before accelerating as F-47 and F/A-XX meaningfully ramp production. 
Margin improvement is expected, and we model 150-200bps of expansion annually 
through 2029, reaching the prior HSD target for the end of the decade. We model further 
expansion, at a slower pace, however, given the mix of less mature programs like F-47 
and F/A-XX. Boeing also secured a part of the missile ramp this past quarter with a 7-year 
contract for PAC-3 seekers. We do not know the terms of the agreement, but cash 
neutrality in the near term has been a common principle underlying these missile 
agreements.

Investment Thesis, Valuation and Risks
Boeing Company (Overweight; Price Target: $240.00)
Investment Thesis
We rate BA Overweight. With balance sheet concerns addressed for the next few years and 
a backlog of ~$500b, we see potential for BA to outperform if the company can improve 
execution. Specifically, this means gradually increasing production of 737s and 787s while 
bringing the 777X closer to entering service. In addition, the company needs to stabilize 
performance in its Defense unit. Assuming production can reach the level that demand 
supports, we should see a positive multi-year reversal in FCF from 2024’s large outflow, and 
this should drive de-levering. 
Valuation
Our Dec-27 price target of $240 is based on 22x our FY29E cash flow of ~$12/sh discounted 
back one year. We are moving the driver of our price target out a year as Boeing pushed out 
cash expectations due to additional challenges. While this is above where Boeing traded 
pre-pandemic, given higher multiples across the commercial aero group, we think it is 
reasonable for Boeing, given the potential for further cash flow and balance sheet 
improvement.
Risks to Rating and Price Target
Downside risks include an inability to increase production of 737s and 787s, further delays 
to the 777X program, and further significant Defense charges. Any additional safety issues 
with Boeing’s products are a risk, as is a failure to improve the company’s relationship with 
the FAA.
