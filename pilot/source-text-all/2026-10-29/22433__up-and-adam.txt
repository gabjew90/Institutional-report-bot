Halloween candy sales fell 6.7% through October 17 vs. the same period in 2025, according to NielsenIQ. High prices appear to be the 
main deterrent, with candy and gum prices rising 44% over the past five years, nearly twice as fast as overall food prices. The National 
Confectioners Association expects demand to pick up as Halloween approaches, though affordability frustrations are a key reason we 
are negative on Consumer Staples. On the bright side, consumer surveys point to holiday spending in aggregate increasing YoY.

Roughly 30 million voters have already cast their ballots, which equates to 65% of total early votes in the 2022 midterms. Among states 
that report party registration, Democrats account for an average 47% of early votes versus 35% for Republicans—a net 12% Democratic 
advantage, up from 8.7% in 2022. Looking at states with key Senate contests, Democrats lead in most—though not all. While early 
voting tallies do not guarantee final outcomes, the data points to stronger Democratic enthusiasm in this election cycle.

As Iran Meets With Key Regional Players, Could Diplomacy Prevent The Resumption Of Airstrikes? 
Tomorrow marks the 97th anniversary of Black Monday in 1929. Together with Black Tuesday, the two-day crash erased 25% of the Dow 
Jones Industrial Average’s value. These remain the US equity market’s only consecutive days with losses exceeding 10% each. It took 
more than 24 years to reach a new record high, yet the Dow has since returned ~11.8% annualized. This is a reminder of how volatility 
creates opportunities for long-term gains, while highlighting the importance of aligning investments with time horizons and goals.

The Russell 1000 Value Index is outperforming the Russell 1000 Growth Index by ~22% YTD,  near its strongest level since July, 
highlighting the recent rotation toward value-centric sectors. Energy and Utilities have led gains MTD, while Comm. Services and Tech 
are in negative territory. Upcoming results from Alphabet, Amazon, and Microsoft could help growth stocks regain some relative ground 
versus value. As we noted yesterday, we expect guidance to be more impactful than headline earnings for shaping investor sentiment.
With the US-Iran conflict approaching its nine-month mark tomorrow, officials from three of the Middle East’s top military powers—
Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey—met yesterday with their Iranian counterparts to discuss a plan that could pave the way for a 
diplomatic settlement. This comes amid concerns that the US could resume airstrikes after the midterms, which helps explain why WTI 
closed above $100/barrel the last two days. While dialogue is encouraging, the White House will likely weigh these views before acting.
