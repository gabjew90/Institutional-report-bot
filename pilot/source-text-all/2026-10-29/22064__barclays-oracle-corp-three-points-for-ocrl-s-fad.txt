Oracle Corp.
Three Points For OCRL's FAD
On Wednesday, ORCL will host its analyst day in conjunction
with its annual customer conference. We are looking for
incremental information on three items: (1) funding needs in
FY28; (2) a potentially updated approach to long-term
guidance; and (3) commentary on the overall health of the AI
story.
Oracle is changing as a company on many dimensions. It is moving from a database vendor that
also has a sizable applications business toward becoming a major AI compute provider. We have
also seen a complete change in the executive leadership team since last year, and this will be
the final event for long-standing Head of Investor Relations Ken Bond. The main job for the new
team at this year’s FAD will be to provide some hand-holding given the current uncertainty in
the AI market, especially for players that are relying more heavily on debt financing (CRWV,
ORCL). For us, the main focus will be on the three points mentioned above.
(1) More clarity around FY28 funding needs. Last quarter was almost a “perfect quarter” for
Oracle, with better revenue, solid underlying profitability, and confirmation that financing for
FY27 is well supported, including the $20bn equity raise over the summer. Nonetheless, the
shares did not react well. The main push back we heard, especially from long-only investors,
was that FY28 capex requirements could kick off another heavy round of funding needs,
potentially creating challenges for the team. In this respect, any incremental information at FAD
this week would be very helpful.
As we have discussed before, we completely rebuilt our model to better capture the capex and
financing requirements going forward. We acknowledge, however, that models incorporating all
the moving pieces across capex, depreciation, debt, and leases are quite complex and can result
in a broader range of outcomes. Our current model sees capex of $94bn in FY27 and $103bn in
FY28 (consensus: $84bn and $107bn, respectively). More importantly, we have ORCL turning
unlevered FCF positive in FY29, with lower cash burn than consensus in both FY27 and FY28. Any
additional clarity from the team would go a long way toward increasing confidence in the story.
(2) Does the new team change the long-term guidance approach? The previous management
team decided to provide long-term guidance using point estimates, with specific dollar targets
for revenue and EPS extending all the way through FY30. Initially, this may have been viewed
positively, as it demonstrated the scale of the transformation underway at Oracle. However, it
will be interesting to see how the new CFO approaches the outlook from here.
Our main issue with the old approach is that management is constantly “handcuffed” to a
specific number. This has two implications that are not necessarily working in management’s
