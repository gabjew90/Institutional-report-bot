VALE - Downgrade - 2H26 and 2027 - Fuels/Freight/Production/Pricing - For 3Q26 specifically, we
expect Vale to deliver an EBITDA of $3.3B (-20% q/q, cons 3.57bn) on the back of weaker iron ore
production, lower price realization and higher costs (impacted by oil and freight). Nickel should be
impacted by lower prices and higher costs while copper is expected to be impacted by lower production
and higher costs. forward curves for benchmark price is at $93/t for 2027 vs. $100/t for 2026 average
(GSe). But Vale's product mix has been increasingly concentrated among low margin products, including
ROM sales, pellet feed concentration in China and third party sales (now representing 25% of total
portfolio), which should further weigh on price realization/margins. We are forecasting price realization to
be down by $5/t y/y in 2027.

Coffee - The Good Bad and Ugly....This Agriculture Analyst offers a brief primer on the coffee market and
our outlook. The physical market should loosen as a strong 2026/27 crop is expected to push the global
market into surplus. Because only a small share of global coffee is eligible for delivery against ICE
futures, low ICE certified stocks could keep the futures curve in backwardation and front-month prices
highly volatile even as physical supply improves this year, while early signs point to renewed supply risks
in 2027/28.....Bad: The 2027/28 crop is already at risk. Brazil’s arabica trees typically produce less after a
large crop, making 2027 an off-year following the strong 2026 harvest. Flowering may develop unevenly
after rainfall at the start of the growing season ran ~207% above normal, adding further downside risk. In
Vietnam, this year’s super El Niño has already depleted groundwater used for irrigation during the
December-April dry season. If groundwater does not recover by December 2026, the 2027/28 Vietnam
robusta crop could disappoint, as it did following the strong 2023/24 El Niño.....Ugly: Because only a
small share of global coffee is eligible for delivery against ICE futures, low ICE certified stocks could
keep the futures curve in backwardation and front-month prices highly volatile even as physical supply
improves this year.
