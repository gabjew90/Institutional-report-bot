FOMC : The UBS base case for this evening FOMC is rates are held unchanged likely by a 10–2 vote, with
Warsh maintaining a hawkish message that there is still “more work to do” on inflation. The pause reflects both
the proximity of the mid-term elections and the fact that the problem is sticky inflation around 3%, rather than
accelerating inflation or an overheating economy, reducing the urgency to hike immediately. The expectation is
then for 25bp hikes in both December and January, with Warsh emphasizing the inflation trend and
commitment to price stability while avoiding strong forward guidance. Markets broadly align with this near-term
view, pricing only around a 10% chance of an October hike versus 92% for a 25bp December hike and 33bps
cumulatively priced by Jan. Link here
UK_Budget : UBS Economics - The key message is that the Autumn Budget is likely to be cautious rather
than transformational, with a much tighter fiscal position forcing the government to balance targeted support
and investment against fiscal discipline. Fiscal headroom may have fallen from £23.6bn in March to around
£10bn, with the Chancellor expected to rebuild only a modest buffer of roughly £15bn through smaller tax
increases while deferring tougher welfare and departmental spending decisions to 2027. Near-term priorities
are expected to include cost-of-living measures, housing support and increased public investment. For
markets, maintaining the fiscal rules should be supportive for gilts, GBP is expected to remain relatively
resilient into 2027, and equities offer selective opportunities in housebuilders, property, lower-value consumer
names and public-investment beneficiaries, rather than a broad-based UK equity rerating. A windfall tax
consideration for Banks has been socialised in press in recent weeks - verbal consensus for UK banks
indicates the bank levy moves towards 8% from the current 3%. UBS has modelled 3% -> 6% which translates
to an implied 3-4% cut to NWG/LLOY numbers and 1-2% cut for BARC/HSBC numbers. Link here

• ‘Coca-Cola (UBS Research Rec Buy) – Still Sparkling; Stay Buy Report KO delivered another solid
quarter, with organic sales growth of +7% (+259bps vs. Street) and better margin performance driving an EPS
beat. The company also raised its FY26 OSG outlook above the prior range and increased its adjusted EPS
guidance as well. Despite what was a strong quarter/updated outlook, shares traded lower on the day, which
we think was a function of broader Staples weakness (XLP -1%), elevated valuation, and continued questions
on whether the strong performance in '26 can extend into next year. We acknowledge that '27 may prove to be
more challenging given tougher unit case volume compares, unfavorable impacts from currency/structural
items, and greater inflation, but we think KO has the tools to deliver bottom line growth, which we do not think
will be the case for many across the group. Although valuation screens as expensive, we believe top and
bottom/line visibility as well as continued confidence in the durability of KO's LT organic sales algorithm
warrants a premium vs. history and would expect outperformance to continue.

• ‘Dollar General (UBS Research Rec Buy) - Assessing the Dollar Amount Report The FTC and several
states filed a complaint against Dollar General, alleging violations of federal and state consumer-protection
and weights-and-measures laws. The complaint seeks injunctive relief, monetary damages, civil penalties, and
other remedies. DG disputes the assumptions and methodology underlying the requested redress and intends
to defend the matter. Though, no settlement has been proposed or discussed at this point in time. At this
stage, the ultimate outcome, timing, and scope of any remedy remain uncertain. The proceeding could
nevertheless entail meaningful legal and remediation costs, and an adverse outcome could affect the
company’s consolidated financial statements. DG has navigated through these types of issues in the past and
ultimately we believe that this should be manageable. Trying to put some math around the risks from this
scenario requires some assumptions. Overall, we think this situation has a low probability of changing DG’s
long-run earnings power.
