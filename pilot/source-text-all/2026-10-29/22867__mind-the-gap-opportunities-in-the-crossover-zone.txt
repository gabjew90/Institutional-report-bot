Crossover zone provides select investment opportunities 
We see opportunities to generate appealing returns by investing in rising star 
candidates. Bonds of issuers we expect to move from high yield (HY) to investment 
grade (IG) are offering mid-single-digit yields, and may benefit from credit spread 
compression in case of an upgrade. We view this as compelling for the underlying credit 
risk and highlight several bonds from our Bond Top List in Figure 2. 
 
The number of rising start candidates in our coverage is larger than the number 
of potential fallen angels. This is despite several upgrades to IG in recent months, 
including United Rentals, Carnival, Renault and Teva. Albemarle also avoided a 
downgrade to HY. At the same time, Lanxess and Paramount lost their IG ratings, 
which weighed on their performance this year, highlighting the importance of staying 
selective and avoiding fallen angels.  
However, in the coming 6-12 months, we see a risk of a rising number of fallen 
angel candidates in our coverage. There are currently nine low BBB rated issuers with 
negative outlooks from S&P and four from Moody’s. At the same time, only four high BB 
rated issuers carry positive outlooks at S&P, and none from Moody’s. In our coverage, 
sectors with the most positive rating momentum in the low BB to high BBB space are 
energy and industrials. In contrast, consumer staples and consumer discretionary face 
more rating pressure. 
BB credit spreads look more compelling following recent decompression. Credit 
spreads have widened in recent weeks amid AI concerns, inflationary pressures, and 
rising fiscal concerns in France, with riskier parts of the market underperforming. We 
estimate the yield pick-up currently offered by BB bonds compared with BBB bonds of 
about 100bps in USD and about 140bps in EUR, close to five-year medians. As a base-
case, we expect credit spreads to remain resilient, with spreads in IG and HY declining 
somewhat from current levels by June 2027, supported by low defaults, strong earnings 
growth and comfortable credit metrics.

Organon 
UBS credit view: Improving; Issuer valuation view: n.a. 
Sector: Pharmaceuticals; Country: United States; Moody’s: Ba3, watch+; S&P: BB, watch+ 
The US pharmaceutical company Organon is considered a rising-star candidate in view of the proposed acquisition by investment grade-rated Sun Pharmaceuticals (not 
covered by CIO, see News, views & trades published 30 September 2026). In our view, the 2028 and 2031 bonds are trading at very tight spread levels, reflecting much 
of the expected rating upside and potential benefits from early repayment. The 2034 notes are trading at wider spreads, reflecting greater uncertainty around potential 
early repayment scenarios, in our view. While we continue to see moderate upside potential, depending on the call scenario, we believe current valuations present a good 
opportunity for investors seeking to reduce exposure. Failure to close the transaction, although not our base case, would likely result in significant underperformance in 
Organon bonds. 
Fabrice Schwarzmann, CFA, Analyst 
Levi Strauss 
UBS credit view: Improving; Issuer valuation view: n.a.  
Sector: Consumer Discretionary; Country: United States; Moody’s: Ba1, stable; S&P: BB+, stable 
Global apparel company Levi Strauss (LEVI) is demonstrating that its transformation into a broader lifestyle brand is translating into stronger credit quality. Recent results 
show broad-based revenue growth, margin expansion and robust free cash flow, while shareholder distributions have not increased leverage. The shift toward direct-to-
consumer sales, product diversification and planned Dockers exit has reduced concentration risks and supports medium-term targets of USD 10bn in revenue and a 15% 
operating margin. In our view, credit metrics already appear consistent with higher ratings, making LEVI a credible rising-star candidate. 
Peter Din, Analyst 
Somnigroup International 
UBS credit view: Improving; Issuer valuation view: n.a.  
Sector: Consumer Discretionary; Country: United States; Moody’s: Ba1, stable; S&P: BB+, pos 
Somnigroup International (SGI), has strengthened its leading bedding position following the 2025 Mattress Firm acquisition. Integration is progressing well, with USD 
30mn of synergies realized in 2Q26 and net leverage back within the 2.0x-3.0x target range. The August acquisition of Leggett & Platt adds further vertical integration 
and synergy potential, reinforcing the case for higher ratings. While the consumer backdrop remains challenging, SGI’s premium positioning and strong brand portfolio 
should help mitigate macroeconomic headwinds, as reflected in its continued outperformance relative to the broader market this year. 
Peter Din, Analyst

United Airlines 
UBS credit view: Improving; Issuer valuation view: Core 
Sector: Industrials; Country: United States; Moody’s: Ba1, stable; S&P: BB+, pos 
We maintain our improving issuer credit outlook and continue to view United Airlines as a rising star, supported by its strong pricing power, robust liquidity and a 
management team that remains focused on balance sheet improvement. 3Q26 revenue increased 17.4% y/y to $17.9bn and total revenue per available seat was up 
15.9%. This demonstrates the carrier’s ability to offset higher fuel costs through pricing. Management’s focus on debt reduction rather than share buybacks supports the 
deleveraging story and potential upgrade to investment grade. While elevated jet fuel prices could delay the upgrade, we view this primarily as a timing issue. 
Leticia Zemaitis, Strategist 
Telecom Italia 
UBS credit view: Improving; Issuer valuation view: n.a.  
Sector: Communication Services; Country: Italy; Moody’s: Baa3, pos; S&P: BB+, watch+ 
We maintain an Improving issuer credit outlook on Telecom Italia (TIM) and continue to expect positive rating action following Moody’s recent upgrade to Baa3 with a 
positive outlook. Moody’s upgrade reflects TIM’s improving standalone credit profile, alongside a one-notch uplift stemming from its status as a government-related 
issuer following Poste Italiane’s acquisition of approximately 86% of the company. We also continue to expect S&P to follow with an upgrade to investment grade, 
consistent with the agency’s indication in July that it would likely raise TIM’s rating upon completion of the Poste transaction. While TIM’s bonds have already performed 
strongly and execution risk related to the sale of its subsea cable unit remains a concern, we continue to see value in select instruments.

Bobst Group 
UBS credit view: BBB-, Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: n.a.  
Sector: Industrials; Country: Switzerland; Moody’s: n.a.; S&P: n.a. 
Bobst Group, a leading machine supplier to the packaging industry, reported a 12% y/y revenue decline to CHF 590mn in 1H26, as machine sales were held back by 
geopolitical uncertainties and the strong Swiss franc. Profitability and cash flow were pressured, with both EBIT and operating cash flow negative at CHF -25mn and CHF 
-40mn, respectively. While we expect credit metrics may remain under pressure in the near term, we see Bobst’s long-term prospects as intact, and expect the rating to 
remain investment grade. Nevertheless, we recently affirmed our BBB- credit rating but lowered the issuer credit outlook to Deteriorating. While we note both of Bobst’s 
CHF bonds trade at wider spread than comparable BBB- rated Swiss industrial bonds, we see these as justified and currently view both bonds as fairly priced. 
Ingo Stössel, CFA, Analyst 
Barry Callebaut 
UBS credit view: BBB-, Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: n.a. 
Sector: Consumer Staples; Country: Switzerland; Moody’s: Baa3, neg; S&P: BBB-, neg 
We continue to believe Barry Callebaut (BC), the world’s leading industrial chocolate company, will defend its IG rating, with debt reduction a key priority. Although 
cocoa bean prices have risen due to El Niño-related concerns, management still expects net leverage to be around 3x if prices stay around GBP 4,000 per ton, down from 
4.5x at end-FY24/25. In the medium term, BC aims to regain net leverage of below 2x. For longer-term-oriented investors comfortable with the prospect of near-term 
volatility and investing in the crossover zone, we think select BC bonds offer solid carry and spread pickup over those of similarly rated peers in the consumer non-
discretionary space. But meaningful spread tightening will likely require a more positive stance from the rating agencies. 
Alexandra Bossert, CFA, Analyst

Ford Motor 
UBS credit view: Stable; Issuer valuation view: n.a. 
Sector: Consumer Discretionary; Country: United States; Moody’s: Ba1, stable; S&P: BBB-, neg 
Ford is rated Ba1 by Moody’s and a downgrade at one other agency would be required for the issuer’s bonds to fall out of investment grade indices. We believe there is a 
path toward rating stabilization at S&P in the near term, particularly if Ford’s updated FY26 guidance is achieved. Ford’s steps to control vehicle quality and contain 
warranty costs has been yielding positive results—management noted that vehicle recalls had fallen 40% through 2026, a trend also supported by J.D. Power’s Initial 
Quality Study. We believe Ford is moving in the right direction in achieving cost savings that could offset tariff and raw material costs. S&P responded to Ford’s 2Q26 
results, stating that further progress on the company’s USD 1bn material and warranty cost savings target should support sustainable margin improvement and increase 
the likelihood of a stable outlook revision in the coming quarters.

Campbell’s 
UBS credit view: Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: n.a.  
Sector: Consumer Staples; Country: United States; Moody’s: Baa3, neg; S&P: BBB-, neg 
The US branded food producer Campbell’s Company faces elevated leverage and weak operating performance, reflecting softer consumer sentiment, cost inflation, and 
tariffs. The company’s commitment to deleveraging, including the recent dividend cut and hybrid bond issuance, is positive. However, despite rating agencies allowing 
additional time to strengthen the credit profile, we expect deleveraging to remain slow and rating headroom to be limited. Downgrade risk could therefore rise quickly in 
case of any further deterioration in guidance, particularly if price increases intended to offset inflation result in larger-than-expected volume declines. 
Fabrice Schwarzmann, CFA, Analyst 
Baxter International 
UBS credit view: Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: Avoid 
Sector: Health Care; Country: United States; Moody’s: Baa3, neg; S&P: BBB-, neg 
We maintain our Deteriorating issuer credit outlook on med-tech company Baxter, although recent developments have become more encouraging. Better-than-expected 
2Q26 results, raised guidance, and recent debt reduction efforts have improved Baxter’s credit trajectory. However, leverage remains elevated following earlier 
operational setbacks and preserving investment grade ratings still relies on meaningful 2H26 margin expansion and continued operational stability. While Baxter’s bonds 
offer modest spread pickup to rating peers, we do not believe valuations fully compensate for the remaining execution and downgrade risks and keep the company on 
our Avoid list. Solid 3Q26 results, particularly on margins and deleveraging, could further reduce downgrade risk and potentially prompt a reassessment of our view. 
Helen Habegger, Analyst

Harley-Davidson 
UBS credit view: Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: n.a. 
Sector: Consumer Discretionary; Country: United States; Moody’s: Baa3, stable; S&P: BB+, stable 
Harley-Davidson (HOG) was downgraded one notch by S&P earlier this year, reflecting weaker margins from tariff pressures and restructuring costs associated with its 
“Back to Bricks” turnaround strategy. The multi-year initiative aims to refocus HOG on its core motorcycle heritage while restoring profitability and growth. 2Q26 results 
exceeded expectations and provided early evidence of progress, including lower inventories and improved margins, although cash flow improvement was less apparent. 
While encouraging, execution risk remains elevated and, in our view, further operational missteps could put the company’s remaining investment-grade rating at risk.

Rogers Communications 
UBS credit view: Deteriorating; Issuer valuation view: n.a. 
Sector: Communication Services; Country: Canada; Moody’s: Baa3, stable; S&P: BBB-, neg 
A downgrade to HY is not a foregone conclusion for Rogers, but the risk remains elevated. S&P recently affirmed the company’s BBB- rating with a negative outlook and 
lowered its leverage trigger for a downgrade to 4.0x from 4.25x amid intensifying competitive pressures. Following the planned acquisition of the remaining 25% stake 
in MLSE, S&P expects adjusted leverage of around 4.6x at end-FY26, making the planned sale of a minority stake in Rogers’ sports, media, and entertainment assets 
critical to deleveraging. While management remains committed to maintaining investment grade ratings and has already taken steps to reduce leverage, we estimate that 
around CAD 7bn in proceeds will be required. Execution risk remains and Rogers’ investment grade status with the agency could come under pressure if the stake sale is 
delayed or proceeds fall materially short of expectations. 
Rochus Baumgartner, FRM, Analyst

Additional risk statements 
Issuers mentioned in this report 
Issuer  
Risks 
Barry Callebaut 
Failure to regain the targeted deleveraging may lead to a downgrade to high yield, with both Moody’s and S&P currently having negative outlooks on their Baa3/BBB- ratings. 
Rating upside (beyond a return to a Stable outlook) is limited at the moment, but may result from a lasting improvement in operating performance, positive free cash flows, and 
significantly lower leverage (on a reported basis). 
Baxter International 
Key upside risks to our outlook over the medium term include stronger-than-expected revenue growth, improved operating margins, and a faster pace of deleveraging toward 
agency ranges for the current ratings.  
Bobst Group 
Downside risks to our assessment include large and unanticipated operational setbacks or a prolonged period of negative free cash flow generation. It also does not factor in a 
severe economic downturn, larger debt financed acquisitions, changes in shareholder structure and distribution strategy, and/or other exogenous shocks. Upside risks include 
unexpectedly strong organic revenue growth, sustained improvement in profitability, and a lasting decrease in leverage. 
Campbell’s 
Upside risks to our view include faster-than-expected improvements in profits and cash flows and a more conservative financial policy, including dividend reductions and asset 
disposals. Downside risks to our view include additional guidance reduction, which may come from a further deterioration in consumer sentiment, cost inflation, and tariffs. 
Ford Motor 
Ford Motor could face rating pressure in a prolonged industry downturn, causing a decline in unit sales and profitability. Prolonged or higher than expected tariffs can pressure 
automotive operating margins. Any significant weakening of Ford Motor's liquidity position could also put pressure on its credit ratings. 
Harley-Davidson 
Upside risks include successful execution of the restructuring plan, a meaningful reduction in leverage, an improvement in the macroeconomic environment and customer 
financing conditions, stronger product demand, and better-than-expected profitability and cash flow generation. A failure to deliver on these objectives, however, would likely 
increase downward rating pressure. 
Levi Strauss 
A more aggressive financial policy that increases leverage, or a failure to deliver on margin expansion and free cash flow generation, could weigh on the credit profile. 
Conversely, sustained net leverage below 1.0x, continued margin expansion, and further product diversification could support future rating upgrades. 
Organon 
Downside risks to our view include the planned acquisition by Sun Pharma not being completed, debt-financed acquisitions, shareholder cash returns, significant pressure from 
competition, loss of market exclusivity, and pricing pressure. Upside risks to our view include faster-than-expected deleveraging and higher-than-expected profit growth. 
Rogers Communications 
Downside risks to our views include challenges in integrating Shaw and realizing synergies, intensifying competition eroding market share and margins, slower-than-expected 
deleveraging owing to weaker EBITDA growth or higher capital spending, and further M&A activity. Governance issues related to the family control of the business could also 
present risks. Upside risks include faster-than-anticipated debt reduction owing to further inorganic deleveraging or stronger-than-expected growth in profitability and cash 
generation. 
Somnigroup International 
Over the longer term, and once the Mattress Firm acquisition is fully integrated, we see scope for higher ratings if net leverage is restored and maintained within management’s 
2.0x-3.0x target range. Conversely, ratings could come under pressure in the event of integration missteps or a more aggressive financial policy, including debt-funded M&A 
and/or increased shareholder distributions, resulting in leverage being sustained closer to 4.0x or above. 
Telecom Italia 
Downside risks to our view include weaker operating trends, slower-than-expected synergy realization, renewed competitive pressure in Italy, and any deviation from the group's 
anticipated conservative financial policy. 
United Airlines 
Key risk is an economic downturn that leads to weaker consumer spending and reduced passenger traffic. Higher oil prices that are not temporary would weigh on margins, as 
well.

UBS CIO risk views
Credit risk flags
CIO attaches a credit risk flag to the instruments under its coverage. Credit risk is assessed based on the remaining tenor and / or instrument type. The flag indicates the likelihood that a 
holder of the instrument will not receive a coupon or principal payment when it comes due. For subordinated and hybrid instruments, which are usually callable and have a remote or no 
fixed maturity date, we apply one uniform credit risk flag per issuer and instrument type. The idea is to reflect the possibility of contractual trigger events or regulatory intervention occurring. 
Either can impose losses on bondholders regardless of the remaining term of the instrument or a specific issuer default event. Credit risk flags only indicate our view of the riskiness of a 
particular instrument. Credit risk flags should not be seen as recommendations to buy, hold or sell. In fact, any combination of risk flags and relative value recommendations is possible.
Very low credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is very low (cumulative probability of less than 2%).
Medium credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is low to medium (cumulative probability of non-payment between 
2% and less than 20%).
High credit risk
We believe that the probability of debt payments not being made when they come due is at least one in five cumulatively.
UBS credit rating
The UBS credit rating reflects our view of the creditworthiness of a company (consistent with our risk flags) and represents a long-term (senior) debt rating. The symbols are similar to those 
of rating agencies but UBS credit ratings solely reflect UBS’s opinion, and are distinct from evaluations assigned by rating agencies. The UBS credit rating is not a recommendation to buy, 
hold or sell a particular bond, nor is it reflective of market pricing and/or market sentiment. All combinations of a credit rating and relative valuation recommendations are possible.
Issuer credit outlook
We complement the instrument-specific risk information of the credit risk flags by indicating our outlook for the credit quality of an issuer over the next 12 months. Depending on instrument 
pricing, all combinations of an issuer credit outlook and relative valuation recommendations are possible.
Improving: We expect the credit profile of the issuer to improve, to an extent that may result in upgrades by rating agencies.
Stable: We do not expect the credit profile of the issuer to change meaningfully.
Deteriorating: We expect the credit profile of the issuer to deteriorate, to an extent that may result in downgrades by rating agencies.

UBS CIO valuation views
Relative value bond recommendations
Our relative value bond recommendations are based on an average investment horizon of six to 12 months. They reflect our assessment of a bond's attractiveness relative to comparable 
instruments under CIO coverage. Comparable instruments typically exhibit similar credit quality, are denominated in the same currency, and belong to the same segment of the bond market.
Views on a particular instrument can change within the six- to 12-month time frame, and those that apply to one instrument do not necessarily apply to others of the same issuer. Views 
on a particular instrument may be withdrawn if it does not have a sizeable basket of comparable instruments under CIO coverage.
Attractive
Bonds seen as “attractive” are expected to generate a total return exceeding the average return of comparable instruments. Our recommendation can stem 
from a positive view on the issuer’s credit profile not fully reflected in the price, unduly high risk premiums, our take on an instrument's call probability, the 
risk of coupon deferrals, and external factors including regulatory intervention.
Fair
Bonds seen as "fair" are expected to produce a total return broadly in line with the average return of comparable instruments.
Expensive
Bonds seen as "expensive" are expected to earn a total return that is less than the average return of comparable instruments. Our recommendation can stem 
from a negative view on the issuer’s credit profile not fully reflected in the price, unduly tight risk premiums, our take on an instrument's call probability, the 
risk of coupon deferrals, and external factors including regulatory intervention.
Sell recommendations
Sell
A Sell recommendation is assigned when the risk of an adverse outcome for an instrument exceeds what is reflected in its current valuation. Such situations 
can include those in which the instrument appears likely to post negative total returns until redemption, either due to a highly negative yield to maturity or 
an imminent call at a price below market valuations.
Issuer valuation views
In addition to the relative value bond recommendations, CIO provides issuer valuation views for selected issuers from developed countries. Large, frequent issuers often provide a relatively 
consistent bond curve in their main issuing currencies. A general valuation view on them provides useful guidance when constructing a bond portfolio or assessing new issues. Issuer 
valuation views cannot simply be broken down to the instrument level. Any combination of our credit risk flags and issuer valuation views is generally possible. Issuer views do not restrict 
CIO from having different valuation views on individual bonds.
Preferred list: Bonds of issuers on our Preferred list are generally expected to offer a more attractive relative valuation than those of similarly rated peers.
Core list: Bonds of issuers on our Core list are generally expected to generate total returns in line with those of similarly rated peers. Core issuers offer relatively liquid bond curves and 
comparatively stable credit profiles.
Avoid list: Bonds of issuers on our Avoid list are generally expected to offer a less attractive relative valuation than those of similarly rated peers. The decision to include an issuer on 
the Avoid list reflects, in most cases, relative value considerations, which can but do not have to be based on an expected deterioration in credit quality. As long as we have not issued 
a Sell recommendation existing positions may be held.
