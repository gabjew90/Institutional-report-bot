Market comment 
Wall Street traders walked into the office yesterday morning to see headlines that world peace 
was just beyond the horizon, as Turkish, Saudi, Qatari, and Pakistani officials wrapped up meetings 
in Tehran to try and bring an end to the war. And so, the headlines rocked that “Iran’s Supreme 
Leader Calls for Meeting Conditions to End War.” Markets priced in the likelihood of the war 
actually ending accordingly- given that conversations about nuclear never even made it to the 
table- with brent crude oil one-month futures down about $2 to $114/bbl. 
Only a few blocks away, the Israeli elections were in full swing, with the voter turnout nearing the 
highest level since 1975, as reported by the FT. Turnout was especially strong among voters in 
Arab-Israeli towns who “by the late afternoon had already surpassed their full vote tally for the 
entire 2022 election cycle.” The results, at the time of writing, appear neck and neck, but the odds 
are skewed in Netanyahu’s favor. Even if the Likud party fails to win a majority, and Eisenkot’s 
Yashur party comes into power, the likelihood of Eisenkot to create a coalition to go against the 
right-wing is limited. Therefore, should Eisenkot fail to achieve a coalition majority, Netanyahu 
remains in power until another round of elections are held in a few months. 
Meanwhile, Europe fails to stay unified in its dealings with Russia amidst the Russia-Ukraine war. 
Leaders from Finland and Norway spoke with Putin for the first time in over a year, with Finnish 
President Stubb calling the conversation “frank and substantive.” According to Bloomberg, a 
Kremlin read out of the call notes that Putin “emphasized that achieving a just and long-term 
peace is possible only if the legitimate interests of the Russian Federation are fully and 
unconditionally taken into account.” But other countries were less-than-pleased with this 
unfolding of events, with leaders from Denmark and Italy criticizing the move. Frederiksen said 
that she “sees no signs that Russia desires peace with Europe- neither in the hybrid warfare taking 
place every single day, nor in Ukraine, where they are launching fierce and aggressive attacks on a 
daily basis.” Especially as the Danish Intelligence Agency recently published a report warning 
Europe about the acceleration of time and scope of Russian hybrid attacks. 
So, some headlines suggest world peace may be just beyond the horizon—but the view from here 
remains obstructed. Talks in Tehran have produced conditions rather than an agreement, Israel’s 
election may reinforce the status quo, and Europe still can’t agree, even on a phone call. Are we 
on the verge of world peace, or a world in pieces? 
Day ahead 
Earlier today, the RBNA announced a surprise decision to leave the cash rate unchanged at 2.75%, 
in defiance of market pricing. 
US Treasury yields moved in a bear flattening move yesterday, continuing the trend from earlier 
this month, in anticipation of today’s FOMC meeting. OIS curve implied pricing suggests the 
likelihood of a hike is pretty much off the table, with only around 1.7bp priced in, but all eyes will 
be on Kevin Warsh’s fourth press conference as Fed Chair, and be looking for forward guidance.
