 
After the market closed on Oct 23th, 2026, Macau casino operators 
simultaneously issued announcements stating that all six (Sands China, Galaxy, 
Wynn Macau, MGM China, SJM, MLCO) of them have agreed to participate and 
contribute together a total of MOP9bn (or US$1.1bn) to a new “Government 
Guidance Fund” set up by the government with the aim of promoting the 
ongoing diversification of Macau’s economy. Per the announcement details and 
media, the fund will invest in commercially sustainable projects that (1) introduce 
emerging industries, drive industrial transformation/upgrading, support the 
development of technology innovation and start-ups; and (2) support the 
coordinated development of Macau with the Guangdong-Macau Indepth 
Cooperaiton Zone in Hengqin and with the rest of the Greater Bay area; (3) are 
consistent with the policy direction of Macau government.  
These announcements do not come as a surprise. Earlier this year on Feb 27th, the 
government had held a press conference (link), outlining its intention to create such 
fund with a total size of MOP20bn including MOP11bn initial contribution by the 
government and the rest to be raised via social and private capital. The guiding 
principles were patient capital where the fund would prioritize projects that drive 
long-term industrial guidance and social benefits over short-term financial returns. 
We see the following likely questions by investors and key variables to determining 
potential financial implications on our covered casino operators:  
(1) How much each operator commits and are they separated from the original 
concession capital commitment?  
Sands China, Galaxy, Wynn Macau, MGM China, MLCO and SJM have each committed 
MOP2.3bn, MOP2.27bn, MOP1.4bn, MOP1.24bn, MOP1bn and MOP0.9bn to the 
government fund respectively (Exhibit 1). As stated in their announcements, these 
will replace some other projects committed before and all be counted toward to the 
fulfillment of their concession-related capital commitment (not an addition). They

MOP2.27bn contribution would represent 25% of the MOP9bn government fund size, 
the same % the company had agreed to commit among the six casino operators in order 
to secure the 10-year gaming concession back in 2022 (i.e., MOP27.5bn out of the total 
MOP108bn). Galaxy’s contribution also accounts for 8% of the original non-gaming 
commitment amount (or 7% of higher commitment of MOP33bn if Macau’s GGR 
exceeds MOP180bn).   
(2) What are the terms of the government fund? How quickly will participants need 
to make their contributions? 
The government fund will have an initial term of 15 years, subject to up to two additional 
1-year extensions. This appears to be extended well beyond the existing gaming 
concession period till 2032. Per MGM China’s announcement, the first 5 years will be the 
investment period suggesting that the majority of contribution or payments will likely be 
spread over 5 years. To put the number into context, it would translate to 
MOP166-461mn per annum for the six casino operators, representing 2-6% of their 
reported EBITDA last year (Exhibit 1) which we think look manageable financially.   
(3) Who will manage the fund? Could this arrangement affect casino operators’ 
investment returns?   
As disclosed in the announcements, the Fund will be managed and operated by the 
Macau Investment and Management Company Limited, the designated management 
entity responsible for its day-to-day operations, financial deployment and 
implementation of investment decisions, which are subject to professional review by its 
committee and under the guidance of its steering committee. As investors, the six casino 
operators will only receive periodic reports regarding the fund’s activities and 
performance.  
As the government has full discretion on the fund’s capital deployment, investors we 
have spoken with have raised concerns on the potential that returns (i.e. GGR and 
profitability) from the MOP9bn contribution made by the six casino operators may be 
lower vs. if the operators were to investing directly to beef up their own non-gaming 
offerings to drive foot traffic. 
Judging from MLCO’s -6% share price correction in the US on Friday night, we 
suspect the HK-listed Macau casino stocks could react negatively on Monday, 
although financially, the MOP9bn or US$1.1bn contribution to the government fund 
is relatively small i.e. representing 2-7% of the six casino operators’ market cap 
(except for 12% for SJM). We also believe operators will have no difficulty funding 
the related payments (i.e., only 2-6% of annual EBITDA) as mentioned above.  
In our view, the more important question to ask is whether (1) such contribution is 
one-off in nature with no further requirement in the future or (2) could there be 
more to come. If the former, we would see any stock price correction as a good 
long-term buying opportunity for select Buy-rated names (e.g. Galaxy, Wynn 
Macau, MLCO) - we also note Galaxy and Wynn Macau offer highly attractive (above 
sector avg) dividend yields of 6-9%. The sector is also trading at a trough valuation 
at 7x FY26E EV/EBITDA (vs. 11.6x sector avg), likely pricing in pessimism toward 
4Q26 GGR outlook amid recent softness in VIP and high-end gaming demand. Of 
note, besides GGR growth, sector valuation would also be driven by operators’ ROIC 
which is still tracking at low-to-mid teens % in recent years vs. ~20% pre-pandemic.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
