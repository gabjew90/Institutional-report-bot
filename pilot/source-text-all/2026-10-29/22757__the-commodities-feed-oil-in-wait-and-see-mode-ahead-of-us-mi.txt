The Commodities Feed: Oil in wait-and-
see mode ahead of US midterms
The oil market is stuck in limbo and likely to remain there until after the
US midterm elections, when investors may get a clearer read on
President Trump’s next move toward Iran
Energy - Gasoil crack moves lower
Oil prices edged lower yesterday, settling down for a second consecutive day. With Brent still
trading around $114/bbl, oil prices remain elevated as the market waits for greater clarity on
President Trump's next move toward Iran. The broader move higher in recent weeks suggests
the market is pricing in a greater likelihood of re-escalation. We’ll likely have to wait until after
the 3 November US midterms more clarity on how the situation in the Middle East evolves. An
agreement between the US and Iran may not be needed for prices to pull back. If we get past
the elections without renewed strikes on Iran, that might be enough for the market's risk
premium to start eroding.
The middle distillate market is weakening, with the ICE gasoil crack trading down toward
$70/bbl, its lowest level since early October. Diesel markets have eased after Russia lifted its
diesel export ban earlier in the month, following a deal with Trump to supply more diesel to
global markets. Russia is a major diesel exporter. These flows have come under pressure this
year due to Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries, which ultimately led Russia to impose a

Meanwhile, there are reports that the US administration is proposing the return of US oil
majors to Russia postwar, which forms part of broader negotiations to bring an end to the war
between Russia and Ukraine. In addition, Finland’s president and Norway’s prime minister held
a call with President Putin yesterday. It’s the first time European leaders have spoken to Putin
since July last year, when the French president spoke with the Russian leader. While there was
no breakthrough, it's a positive sign that European leaders are trying to get more involved in
negotiations to end the war.
The latest API data shows US crude oil inventories fell by about 2m barrels last week.
Meanwhile, gasoline and distillate fuel oil inventories rose by 2.3m barrels and 420k barrels,
respectively. The more widely followed EIA weekly report will be released later today.
Metals - Gold hovers around $4,000 ahead of Fed
Gold climbed back above $4,000/oz after briefly falling below the key level in Tuesday’s trading
session, as attention shifts to the Federal Reserve's 28 October meeting. The central bank is
widely expected to leave interest rates unchanged, with markets focused on signals about the
monetary policy outlook.
Gold is finding its feet again after the recent correction attracted bargain hunters and physical
buyers, though ETF outflows have recently been a headwind.
In industrial metals, copper moved higher, supported by signs of tightening supply and firm
physical demand in China. The Yangshan premium, a gauge of Chinese demand for imported
copper, climbed to a record high as lower domestic output and increased flows of metal to the
US tighten availability elsewhere. Tightness is also evident on the LME, where the cash-to-
three-month spread has moved deeper into backwardation, signalling strong demand for
immediately available metal. We expect this tightness to continue to support prices. At current
elevated levels, price-sensitive buyers could start to resist.
Elsewhere, iron ore remained under pressure. Prices fell to their lowest level in more than two
years as a softer demand outlook in China coincided with rising supply. Steel demand is
entering a seasonally weaker period, while elevated port inventories are limiting mills’ appetite
to restock beyond immediate needs. At the same time, rising shipments from major producers
and the continued ramp-up of new supply are adding pressure to the market. We expect iron
ore prices to remain under pressure into year-end, with weak steel demand and ample
availability leaving limited scope for a sustained recovery.
