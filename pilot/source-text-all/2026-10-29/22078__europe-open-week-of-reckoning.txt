Europe Open - Week of reckoning
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Modest moves across the board as we start a catalyst-heavy week. USTs lift higher though volumes are lower, FX
rangebound but mixed in stocks. US equity futures tentatively rose after news that Iran will host top security and defense
officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey for regional security talks, but conviction is stalling as we enter the
London morning session. Ahead, German Ifo is the only major event to watch today other than second tier US regional
fed surveys. Moody's surprise decision to leave France's sovereign credit rating unchanged could drive OATs temporarily
higher. In EM, Brazil election headlines took limelight with President Lula seeing a surprise victory in runoff elections,
gaining 51.8% of the vote. Polling hours were extended by two hours following logistical disruptions and adverse
weather, with Bolsonaro also contesting the result. Both should result in a sharp decline in Brazilian assets. Taiwan
markets were on local holiday overnight.

In Focus‌
Rates: Mixed start to the week with JGBs twist flattening, USTs slightly stronger, US 10s trading at 4.96% at print. Flows
wise, our desk confirms relatively muted activity sliding from 0.9x to 0.6x of recent averages, but did note better bidding in US
10s. Only tier two data such as regional Fed surveys today, but expect low conviction into FOMC on Wed. Our rates
strategists are still receiving Oct OIS (+2.2bp priced), assuming a hike in Dec. Depending on levels out of the meeting, its also
worth considering a Dec/Jan OIS flattener. They also expect the 5y to outperform on further safe-haven led rate rallies, and
recommend a costless 3m fwd 2s5s bull flattener. AU bond yields lifted higher after RBA Hunter says the inflation fight is "not
quite yet done", suggesting that more monetary policy tightening is required. All eyes on AU CPI Wed. 
On Friday, Moody's surprised markets by affirming France's sovereign rating at Aa3 while maintaining a negative outlook.
OAT spreads may tighten modestly at the open, but any relief is likely to prove temporary. With both S&P and Fitch already
having downgraded France, a future Moody's downgrade remains a credible risk should fiscal challenges persist. French
Finance Minister Lescure welcomed the decision, arguing it reflects the underlying strength of the French economy. However,

with another key budget vote due on Oct. 27 and little to suggest a material improvement in the fiscal trajectory, we see
limited scope for a sustained reversal in French risk premia. 10y OAT-Bund yields peaked at 176bp last week. 
Equities: Slight bearish sentiment across Asian stocks with HSI the sole outperformer thanks to short-covering, and Topix
modestly holding gains, but China/Korean/rest of Japan trades in the red. Risk sentiment is being lifted by news of an
Iran/Saudi meeting buoyed US equity futures overnight, though conviction is stalling into the early London trading session.
Last week's moves have all the hallmarks of position de-grossing and/or rotation, CETS write, but they don't like the
risk/reward of fading those drivers just yet. Earnings face a very high bar and there is still large embedded P&L in key
positions like CGUSEPMG. Together that leaves room for disappointment to drive another leg of de-risking, they warn. Indeed
it's a major earnings week with ~41% of the SPX market cap reporting this week.
FX: USD bottomed out and rallying modestly at print, but key levels continue to hold (1.12 in EURUSD, 157 in USDJPY). G10
gamma remains well-supported as we head into a busy week, with FOMC/ECB/BoJ/RBNZ/AU CPI, with the first three
currently priced at 47.5bps, 20bps, 50bps. Flows remain extremely muted, while spot interbank volumes were in line with 30d
averages during the Asia session. The surprise in Brazilian elections (more below) is likely driving demand for USDMXN and
USDZAR, where volumes were 4x and 2x of recent averages for the session so far. 
Commodities: Possible mediation efforts in the Middle East front drove oil prices lower though Brent is finding slight
resistance at $114. Iran will host top security and defense officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey for regional
security talks, the Deputy Secretary of Supreme National Security Council, Ali Bagheri Kani, told state TV. Saudi deputy
minister for public diplomacy is also expected to meet Iran’s president Masoud Pezeshkian and Supreme National Security
Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaee. Ultimately, though, risk of US/Israel escalation remains high. US President Trump told
reporters over the weekend that the Iran conflict will end soon "one way or another" and added that its "getting to be decision
time" on whether he will make a deal with Tehran or resume attacks. Elsewhere Axios reports US intel found Iran has
"substantially increased" its efforts to target American diplomats and lethal plots reaching "advanced stages". This is one of
the reasons why the State Deparment is drawing down personnel at several American embassies across the Gulf.

Weekly Macro Brief by Jim McCormick. With DM real yields near multi-year highs, energy prices surging, French bond
spreads at EGB crisis levels and cracks surfacing around the AI boom, the current level of implied volatility in most markets
looks too low relative to the underlying risks. Jim recommends buying protection into an eventful period. Yen vol is a good way
to position for a potential explosive move lower in USDJPY. In a genuine AI sell-off, expect UST curvee steepening, not bull
flattening. Receiving front-end EURs is the next France stress trade; probably best against USD or GBP given the continued
rise in energy prices. German bunds remain a safe haven, not a credit risk. BRL local bonds and the currency remain long-
term holds, but the risk-reward skew around Sunday's election is far from ideal.
Elsewhere‌
BRL: In a surprise outcome, President Lula won presidential runoff elections, gaining 51.8% of the vote versus Flavio
Bolsonaro's 48.2%. Bolsonaro has called for the annulment of results an accused the Supreme Court of changing the rules to
mandate last minute extension of voting hours (counting began at 5pm local, stopped at 5.06pm with voting extended 2

hours). According to political forecasters, the margin (3.6pp or ~4.4mn votes) is too large to overturn easily, with the voting
extension not being enough to explain the gap between Lula and Flávio. Expect a sharp selloff of Brazilian assets at the open
especially given how long positioning was into the event. This could see a reduction in EM risk overall, so watch out crosses.
Spot FX has no limits, so USDBRL can trade upwards of 5.50, albeit with limited liquidity and wider spreads.
