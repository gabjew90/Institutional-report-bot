WHAT IS CATCHING MY EYES IN GS RESEARCH
Global Views: Downside Growth Risks, Jan Hatzius, Link: here
So far in 2026, global growth has been remarkably unremarkable. Both the world as a whole and the G3
economies are growing in line with our estimates of potential GDP. Our interpretation is that fiscal easing and
the positive impulse from the AI boom have offset the negative impulse from the war in Iran and the lingering
drag from higher US tariffs. Our standing global GDP forecasts project more of the same in 2027, as lower
energy prices offset fading tailwinds from fiscal policy and AI. But the balance of risks is on the downside.
The most immediate concern is that, far from subsiding, energy prices have again climbed sharply on fears
of renewed Middle East escalation. The prices of gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, and European/Asian natural gas
are now near or even above their March/April highs, with likely negative effects on consumers and energy-
importing economies more broadly. The Fed’s recent signals—explicit from Williams/Jefferson and implicit
from Warsh—effectively rule out a hike on October 28. But the FOMC still looks very likely to deliver another
25bp hike to 4-4.25% on December 9 because spot inflation remains too high. And while we continue to
expect significant disinflation next year on the back of reduced impulses from tariffs, energy prices, and AI-
related prices, the risks to our forecast are on the upside, most obviously in energy. Higher inflation could
result in another hike in March, but either way we see renewed cuts in late 2027/early 2028. Like the Fed,
the ECB will likely hold rates steady next week and deliver a final hike (to 2.75%) at the December meeting.
However, the turbulence in the French bond market poses somewhat greater downside risks to that call than
in the US. The turbulence has tightened financial conditions to a degree that is likely to shave ¼-½pp from
annualized growth in coming quarters. And if it intensifies into December, markets might perceive a hike as
signaling insufficient focus by the ECB on the risk of Euro area fragmentation. While the ECB could address
this issue by coupling a hike with renewed balance sheet expansion, the hurdle for such a move is high, in
part because purchases of French bonds would inevitably raise concerns about moral hazard with respect to
the needed fiscal adjustment.
 
GJENSIDIGE, Downgrading to Neutral as pricing normalises, leaving limited room to beat high
expectations, Vash Gosalia, Link: here
Gjensidige has delivered a strong operating performance following the repricing actions implemented
through 2024 and 2025. Despite meaningful price increases, the group has maintained solid retention rates
and stable market shares, while margins have expanded to healthy levels. This underlines the strength of the
franchise, the quality of execution, and the resilience of the customer base, in our view. Despite the

operating performance, the share price is down c.14% YTD on the back of concerns around Autonomous
Vehicles, AI based distribution, and the Danish Workers' Comp ruling.
Looking ahead, we expect the pricing environment to normalise as claims inflation moderates. While the
earn-through of prior pricing actions should continue to support profitability, we expect the pace of further
margin expansion to become more measured from here. Reflecting lower growth and margin expansion, we
reduce our FY27e-30e EPS by c.2.4% on average.
Gjensidige has been a compelling top-line growth and margin expansion story, but we believe exceeding
current expectations has become more challenging as pricing momentum normalises. Consensus
expectations and GSe are comfortably ahead of management’s target of below 81% COR for 2027e and
2028e. Against this backdrop, while Gjensidige remains a high-quality Nordic insurer, we see limited further
upside risk given the current high expectations, strong margins, and a moderating pricing environment.
As such, we reduce our 12-month price target by 9% (to NOK 273) and downgrade to Neutral (from Buy),
reflecting our lower earnings estimates and a growth profile that is now more in line with peers, justifying an
in-line 2027e Operating PE multiple.
 
Below zero – Risk Appetite Indicator negative as Tech dominates cross-asset moves, Link: here
Last week, US PMIs were roughly unchanged, while Euro Area flash PMIs declined below expectations. Our
Risk Appetite Indicator (RAI) fell to -0.5, its lowest level since April 2025 (Exhibit 10). Historically, readings
below -1 and in particular near -2 have been useful contrarian signals. The current level is not yet negative
enough to send that signal but suggests less-constrained upside for risky assets than at the end of the
summer. As in previous weeks, credit components drove most of the decline and weighed on our PC1
“Global growth” factor, while the bond yield rally supported our PC2 “Monetary policy” factor (Exhibit 14).
Credit excess returns saw another multi-standard-deviation sell-off last week, while G4 10y bonds rallied,
providing some protection against the AI sell-off (Exhibit 1). Most Euro Area-exposed assets also
underperformed their betas to global growth and monetary policy pricing as OAT-Bund spreads widened
beyond fundamentals (Exhibit 2). Within equities, defensive/quality styles and Value outperformed their
growth- and policy-implied returns, helping buffer broad equity markets against the Tech-driven momentum
unwind.
Tech has become an increasingly important driver of risk appetite. The correlation between our cross-asset
RAI and Nasdaq 100 performance relative to the S&P 500 equal weight has risen over recent years,
reaching almost 1 in recent weeks (Exhibit 3). Differences in sensitivity to Semiconductor stocks help explain
cross-asset performance over the past 3 weeks (Exhibit 4), more so than sensitivity to oil prices (Exhibit 5).
The Dollar has also behaved in a more pro-cyclical fashion.
Option markets price more uncertainty over the next 2 weeks than in the subsequent month, consistent with
a busy catalyst calendar (Exhibit 6). Nasdaq 100 and other equity markets price larger moves this week,
when Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon report. Long-dated USTs and HY credit instead price larger moves
next week around the US mid-term elections. Investors will also follow the Fed (our economist expect a hold
followed by a hike in December), BoJ and ECB this week.
We remain modestly pro-risk in our asset allocation: OW Equities for 3m/12m, balanced by UW Credit (for
3m/12m), OW Cash for 3m and OW Bonds for 12m. AI concerns, Middle East tensions and mid-term
elections could sustain near-term volatility. That said, we think earnings support, lighter positioning and lower
valuations should help equities into year-end.

INSURANCE
Munich Re – AFC and Munich Re Sign US$1 Billion Managed Debt Mobilization Programme to Unlock
Trade Finance for African Banks (AFC)
• Africa Finance Corporation (AFC), the continent's leading infrastructure solutions provider, today announced a
US$1 billion Unfunded Managed Debt Mobilisation Program (UMDMP) with Munich Re. 
• The programme provides US$1 billion of committed risk-sharing support for AFC's trade finance lending to African
financial institutions.
• For African banks, the programme means more trade finance, delivered faster. Munich Re and AFC have agreed
risk parameters and limits up front, so that Munich Re takes a share of eligible AFC transactions automatically. 
• AFC retains a part of the risk on every transaction, keeping both institutions’ interests aligned. As the transactions
under the programme mature, the programme's capacity is freed up and reused to finance new investments.
 
DIV FINS
FTK announced that Bafin has lowered the group's additional capital requirement (Pillar 2 Requirement,
P2R), determined as part of the Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP), by 100bps from 2.50%
to 1.50%. Bafin has also completely lifted the individual capital surcharge previously imposed on
flatexDEGIRO Bank, with the Bank's SREP capital requirement at 2.25% going forward. The individual
surcharge was mandated several years ago due to identified deficiencies in the Bank's business
organisation, with management noting the progress made since the Bafin special audit in 2022 and its
subsequent efforts to address Bafin’s findings and restore its proper business organisation. We view the
decision as a further positive step in drawing a line under FTK’s historical regulatory issues, while the lower
capital requirements increase capital headroom and, all else equal, provide greater flexibility for shareholder
distributions. This is particularly relevant in the context of FTK’s recently announced €100mn share buyback,
and, as noted in our Friday update, we have also incorporated €100mn of annual share buybacks in each of
2027-29 into our estimates. At c.9.5x 2027 P/E (GSe), we see current valuation as deeply discounted and
view the catalyst pathway as being: (1) a near-term moderation in consensus expectations to reflect softer
commission per trade, (2) an acceleration in customer growth, with management targeting c.100k additions
in 4Q and reiterating annual growth of at least 10% before any potential benefit from German pension
reform, and (3) the appointment of the new Supervisory Board Chair.
