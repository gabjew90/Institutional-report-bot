NXP Semiconductors NV (NXPI): Solid quarter with 2027 outlook intact; 
increased capital returns boost shareholder value
27 October 2026 | 9:23PM EDT
Key stock takeaways: We expect the stock to trade modestly lower following an 
in-line quarter with guidance that fell short of bullish expectations. We believe 
investor expectations were balanced, given datacenter strength across analog and 
constructive commentary on automotive from NXP’s peer group (namely TI) - set 
against guidance for the quarter that had come in below the most bullish 
expectations.  We remain encouraged by management’s comments on sustained 
demand improvements across most industrial and automotive customers, and we 
continue to see management’s 2027 financial targets as achievable.  We also believe 
that the company’s significantly increased buyback authorization - which now 
translates to ~10% of shares outstanding - should meaningfully boost shareholder 
value.  We are Buy rated on NXPI, as we believe that the company’s continued 
operational execution on key financial metrics – automotive revenue growth, gross 
margin expansion, and EPS growth – should bolster investor confidence on the 
sustainability of through-cycle margin expansion and earnings growth. 
Read-through to our coverage: We expect a modestly negative initial reaction for 
the analog group.  We would expect the most direct read-across for ADI (Buy) and 
ON (Neutral) given their relatively high automotive exposure.  
Quarterly results in line with the Street: NXP reported revenue of $3.79 bn, in line 
with GS at $3.80 bn and the Street (Visible Alpha) at $3.77 bn.  Gross margin (excl. 
SBC) of 58.6% was in line with GS at 58.9% and the Street at 58.4%. Non-GAAP EPS 
(excl. SBC) of $4.20 was in line with GS at $4.25 and the Street at $4.14.  Automotive 
revenue of $2.07 bn was in line with GS at $2.09 bn and the Street at $2.06 bn, 
Industrial & IoT revenue of $802 mn was in line with GS at $808 mn and the Street at 
$806 mn, Mobile revenue of $411 mn was in line with GS at $407 mn and the Street 
at $406 mn, and Comms Infra. & Other revenue came in at $503 mn, just above GS 
and the Street at $493 mn. 
Financial outlook for 2026/27 remains intact: Although management did not 
n
provide explicit guidance, NXP expects double-digit revenue growth for 2027 as 
noted its prior call, which is consistent with our estimate of 16.9% in CY27.  NXP 
expects to achieve gross margin of ~60% during 2027, with OpEx of ~23% in 
1H27 and below that level in 2H27.  We see potential upside to these metrics 
based on the industry trends we expect, particularly given the outsized growth 
we expect in the industrial market.

shipments are over 25% below our view of long-term trendline demand. We believe the 
stock can begin to earn a higher multiple as the company drives improved through-cycle 
financial metrics, while delivering greater shareholder returns through buybacks and 
dividends.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
