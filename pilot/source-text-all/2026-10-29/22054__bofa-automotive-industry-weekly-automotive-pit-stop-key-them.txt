Automotive Industry 
Weekly Automotive Pit Stop: Key themes 
from week 1 of auto earnings 
Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Cost inflation in focus as 2027 risk builds 
We think cost inflation is emerging as a key 2027 risk for OEMs and suppliers, with GM 
flagging a $1.0-1.5bn commodity headwind on its 3Q call. That is incremental to the 
~$500mm-$1bn already embedded in its original plan and reflects worse commodity 
assumptions over the last several weeks. Steel remains the largest exposure, with HRC 
up +4.1% since the end of September and nearing four-year highs, while copper is also 
hovering near record levels. Raw material inputs for an average vehicle reached $4,845 
at the end of September, up +1% MoM and +32% YoY, and we think the current figure is 
likely higher given continued increases in inputs. At current levels, we think 1Q27 is the 
largest YoY commodity headwind quarter, though pricing, supplier negotiations, and 
efficiencies remain potential offset levers. We expect Ford to highlight a similar impact 
across its cost base, with 2027 commentary likely tempered as well. We also expect 
suppliers to flag rising cost inflation, as DCH previously pointed to pressure from direct 
inflation and supply chain costs. 
Prolonged diesel spike starting to hit autos 
We see a prolonged diesel spike adding both cost and demand pressure across autos. 
Diesel climbed to $6.28/gal as of 10/19, up 73.4% YoY (October-to-date +69.6% YoY vs. 
+68% in September), and remains near record highs, up ~65% since the Iran conflict 
began. GM noted on its 3Q call that fuel hits logistics with "maybe a two week to four 
week lag," a sizable component of its $400mm of incremental 2026 inflation. Suppliers 
face similar logistics and oil-based input pressure, plus offset requests from Tier 2s. On 
demand, diesel spikes have historically coincided with softer HD truck sales. We expect 
fuel to remain a key theme heading into next week's earnings. 
Soft P&S in 3Q, warranty law a partial offset 
We think LAD's results partly confirmed our concern that affordability is starting to 
weigh on dealers' P&S businesses in 3Q. P&S gross grew +2%, but customer count was 
down slightly, and customer pay was flat, with growth driven by a higher average RO 
value rather than RO count. LAD also pointed to a shrinking VIO, a theme that has gained 
steam recently, as the average vehicle in its service lane is ~4 years old, which lines up 
with the depressed 2022 SAAR that followed the semiconductor chip crisis. We see 
warranty as a partial offset. New Jersey's franchise law change, effective April 2026, 
requires OEMs to pay dealers for warranty labor hours closer to what they bill retail 
customers, rather than the tighter hours in OEM time guides. We think this could help 
LAD’s warranty comps through 1Q27. With more states considering similar laws, it could 
become a broader tailwind to dealer P&S.

Exhibit 11: US inventory in September was at 49DS, above the 5-year 
average of 46DS 
US inventory – total (DS) 
Source: WardsAuto InfoBank, BofA Global Research 
BofA GLOBAL RESEARCH 
Total auto inventory at the end of September 2026 increased +97k 
units from August 2026 to 2.60mm. On a days’ supply (DS) basis, 
total inventory was at 49DS, which is 8% above the 5-year average 
of 46DS, and up 2 days MoM. We note the 5-year average is 
depressed because of Covid and historically the industry had 
tended to run in the range of 60DS-70DS.  
The MoM decrease in absolute inventory was driven primarily by 
Korean OEMs (-22k). The D3 OEMs were +71k. the European OEMs 
were +1k, and the Japanese OEMs were +41k. The MoM increase 
for the D3 OEMs was primarily driven by GM (+37k), followed by 
Ford (+28k) and then Stellantis (+6k). On an inventory days basis, 
Ford (71DS) and Stellantis (84DS) remain elevated relative to GM 
(65DS).

Price objective basis & risk 
Ford Motor (F) 
Our price objective of $19 is based on an EV/EBITDA of roughly 4.75x on our 2027 
estimates. This valuation methodology reflects a multiple within Ford's historical range 
(2-5x). We believe a multiple at this level is warranted considering the more favorable 
regulatory backdrop in the US, enabling the company to mix shift to its most margin 
accretive truck/SUV platforms. In addition, we see near-term benefits from improving 
Model e losses, and stability in its high margin Ford Pro commercial business. 
 
Downside risks: 1) material downturn in US auto sales from weak consumer confidence 
& affordability challenges, 2) a sharp and sustained rise in input costs, 3) disruption in 
the supply base, 4) significant increase in gas prices, 5) new vehicle pricing deteriorates, 
6) market share losses pressure results, 6) stress in capital markets makes borrowing 
more expensive, 7) Incremental execution risk as management ramps up. 
 
Upside risks: 1) better than expected US auto sales, 2) growth in higher margin revenue 
streams remains robust, incl. software & services, 3) mix and pricing remain favorable 
Lithia Motors (LAD) 
Our price objective of $425 is based on a P/E multiple of roughly 10.2x on our 2027 
estimates, which is above the average dealer through-cycle multiple of roughly 8.0x. We 
believe this is appropriate in light of the company's track record of strong earnings and 
recent strong execution of M&A growth actions. 
 
Downside risks: 1) a swift and/or material downturn in US sales, 2) slower improvement 
in operating leverage than forecast, 3) substantial market share loss by domestic brands, 
to which LAD is exposed, 4) higher interest rate environment causes material demand 
deterioration and/or repossessions, 5) consumer dissatisfaction with auto retailing, and 
6) the potential for franchise law and/or consumer finance law changes. 
 
Upside risks: 1) acquisition activity above current forecasts, 2) Upside in the US cycle 
above our forecasts, 2) LAD is able to gain back/maintain some gross profit per unit in 
its new and used vehicles businesses, and 3) interest rates decline quicker than 
expected, stimulating demand.
