News & US Treasuries: 
 
• 
Treasuries: The strong September CPI print initially drove a 
sharp selloff at the front end, with the 2Y yield rising as 
much as 16bp to 4.95% before reversing. The long end ul-
timately rallied, with the 30Y yield tightening 3.4bp, as in-
vestors read the data as reinforcing the case for a Fed 
pause in October, even if the door remains open to further 
hikes. At the close, the 2Y, 5Y, 10Y and 30Y yields stood at 
4.80% (+1.1bp), 4.99% (-1.4bp), 5.19% (-2.3bp) and 
5.52% (-3.4bp), respectively. Markets continue to price a 
hold at the October FOMC, with only a 31.6% probability as-
signed to a move. By contrast, a December hike is nearly 
fully priced, at 82.3%. 
 
• 
The Fed’s latest Beige Book showed that half of the Fed 
districts reported robust or intensifying price pressures, 
despite growth expectations remaining modest. Activity 
was weighted towards manufacturing and continued to be 
weighed down by persistent uncertainty. 
 
• 
Fed Speakers: Several Fed officials commented on 
Wednesday, adopting a broadly cautious, data-dependent 
tone in response to the CPI surprise. Chicago Fed President 
Goolsbee described the September CPI report as “discour-
aging,” warned that tariff-related price effects may prove 
more persistent than expected, and flagged AI infrastruc-
ture investment as a potential source of economic over-
heating, while avoiding any commitment to a specific policy 
response. Richmond Fed President Barkin ( said inflation 
came in “higher than I would have hoped” and noted that 
upcoming PPI data would further inform his outlook; he de-
clined to prejudge the October FOMC decision. Vice Chair 
for Supervision Bowman reiterated that she sees “no ur-
gency” to act further on rates, emphasising that the Fed 
has time to assess how financial conditions evolve. Boston 
Fed President Collins indicated that it may be “appropriate” 
to raise rates “a bit further” before year-end, citing AI and 
energy prices as inflation risks. 
 
• 
Oil Prices: Brent and WTI rose on Wednesday to 
$106.06/bbl and $94.14/bbl, respectively, as Middle East 
supply risks intensified. A Houthi strike hit a building at 
Saudi Aramco’s headquarters in Dhahran, while fresh at-
tacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz kept the ge-
opolitical risk premium elevated. 
 
• 
UK: BOE Chief Economist Huw Pill, among the MPC’s more 
hawkish members, cast doubt on market pricing for four 
rate hikes over the coming year, arguing that more con-
crete evidence would be needed. The 2Y UK yield fell as 
much as 5bp, while the 10Y declined 2.3bp. 
 
• 
Comments on impact of attacks on Saudi Arabia for bond-
holders:  The spread widening has been modest and short-
lived. Across 44 Saudi sovereign USD bonds, the average 
G-spread widened by approximately +7 bps in the two days 
following the first attacks (Oct 7–8 vs. the Oct 6 pre-attack 
trough), but has since fully retraced. The broader trend 
since Oct 1 is actually one of tightening :  spreads are ~13 
bps tighter on G-spread basis over the two-week window. 
The attacks caused a brief ~7 bp blip, not a structural re-
pricing. On Saudi papers, Real Estate carries the widest 
spreads by a wide margin reflecting structural leverage and 
Vision 2030 execution risk, not just geopolitical events. En-
ergy (including Aramco-linked issuers) is the sector most 
directly exposed to the current escalation, given the Oct 13 
strike on Aramco's HQ.     Spreads across GCC are resilient. 
Bahrain is the clear outlier at ~388 bp Z-spread  driven by 
fiscal stress and high debt-to-GDP, and high direct expo-
sure to Iran.  Qatar and Kuwait trade tightest in the region, 
reflecting strong sovereign balance sheets and lower direct 
conflict exposure. Saudi Arabia (~119 bp) sits broadly in 
line with Oman, and is meaningfully wider than Abu Dhabi 
and Qatar  but this spread differential predates the attacks 
and reflects structural credit differentiation, not event-
driven widening. 
 
 
Economic Data: 
 
• 
US: September CPI surprised to the upside, with headline 
inflation rising 0.65% m/m and core CPI increasing 0.33% 
m/m. The stronger core goods reading, together with firm 
transportation services and shelter inflation, points to 
more persistent underlying price pressures. Analysts’ esti-
mates imply a 0.31% m/m core PCE print and a 3.1% y/y 
pace. The release was supportive for inflation markets: 
headline CPI exceeded swap-market pricing by more than 
7bp, the largest upside surprise since March 2024. The 
stronger print improves November TIPS carry and shifts the 
recent inflation-surprise backdrop back towards upside 
risks for inflation pricing. 
 
• 
China: China’s producer-price inflation accelerated to 5.0% 
YoY in September, the fastest since June 2022 and above 
the 4.4%-4.5% consensus, from 3.8% in August, while CPI 
inflation rose to 0.9% YoY from 0.8% and core CPI held at 
1.0% for a second month.   China’s trade surplus reached 
US$938.11bn in the first nine months of 2026, up more 
than 7% YoY and approaching last year’s record US$1.2tn, 
after September exports rose 36.3% YoY and imports in-
creased 31%, producing a US$133.5bn monthly surplus 
versus the US$117.8bn consensus. 
 
• 
Singapore’s GDP grew 5.4% YoY in 3Q26, down from a re-
vised 6.2% in 2Q26 but above the 5.1% consensus, while 
manufacturing output rose 7.5% YoY, moderating from 
12.9% in the prior quarter, supported by electronics, preci-
sion engineering, transport engineering and general man-
ufacturing. 
 
• 
Australia’s September employment fell 39,300 MoM, ver-
sus expectations for a 20,000 decline, while the unemploy-
ment rate rose to 4.7%, above the 4.6% consensus. 
 
• 
Sweden CPI (Sep): MoM 0.9%; poll 0.9%; prior 0.9%. YoY 
1.1%; poll 1.2%; prior 1.2%. CPIF YoY 1.5%; poll 1.5%; prior 
1.5% (LSEG Economic Monitor, 14 Oct).

Spain CPI (Sep, final): YoY 4.9%, in line with the flash; HICP 
YoY 5.0%, in line with the flash.

Alibaba (BABA, A1/A+) – [UP]: On October 14th,  Fitch af-
firmed Alibaba’s senior unsecured ratings at A/Stable, 
constrained by China’s sovereign rating despite Alibaba’s 
stronger standalone credit profile, while expecting nega-
tive free cash flow through FY27-FY28 due to elevated AI-
related capex and a recovery from FY29 as cloud moneti-
zation scales.  For bondholders, the affirmation and stable 
outlook support the A rating, but Fitch’s forecast of 
CNY220bn-230bn annual capex in FY27-FY28—above 
CNY230bn annually from FY29 and following a CNY70bn 
FCF outflow in FY26—means less near-term deleveraging 
and greater reliance on Alibaba’s liquidity and operating 
cash flow.  Overall, the credit outlook is stable rather than 
improving: senior unsecured bondholders are protected by 
Alibaba’s scale, cash generation and market leadership, 
but spread performance will depend on AI/cloud moneti-
zation, capex discipline and the pace at which free cash 
flow returns to neutral or positive from FY29. Alibaba’s lat-
est results highlight strong AI and cloud momentum , resil-
ient e-commerce growth, and a solid balance sheet despite 
profitability impacted by heavy investment spending.  We 
turn from MP to UP on BABA >3yr papers.

Weibo (WB, Baa2 / - / BBB) – [MP]: On October 14th, Fitch 
affirmed Weibo Corporation's issuer default ratings at 
'BBB' and revised the outlook to negative from stable. 
Fitch expects greater near-term pressure on profitability 
from competition with Douyin, Tencent, Kuaishou and 
Xiaohongshu, a weak consumer backdrop and higher in-
vestment in video content and AI, and forecasts EBITDA of 
US$450mn to US$500mn in both 2026 and 2027. Gross 
leverage of 3.3x in 2025 is above that of 'BBB' peers and 
is expected to rise in 2026 before returning to about 3.3x 
in 2027 after the likely early redemption of the US$330mn 
convertible bonds, while net cash was US$997mn at end-
2025 and annual free cash flow should exceed US$200mn. 
The outlook also reflects uncertainty over liabilities from 
parent Sina's appeal of an adverse judgment in its privati-
sation appraisal case; Fitch rates Weibo one notch above 
its assessment of Sina, which holds about 35.7% of Weibo 
and 62.5% of the votes (Fitch release). Next results are due 
on 5 November. Looking at its latest results: In 2Q26, net 
revenue rose 2% YoY to US$453.8mn but fell 4% in con-
stant currency, as advertising and marketing revenue fell 
1% to US$381.0mn and value-added services rose 19% to 
US$72.9mn. Operating income fell 18% to US$118.9mn, 
cutting the margin to 26% from 33%, and adjusted EBITDA 
fell 24% to US$133.2mn. Gross debt was about 
US$1.87bn, about 3.5x annualised adjusted EBITDA, 
against US$2.64bn of cash and short-term investments, 
leaving net cash of about US$769mn. Overall, this is 
credit-negative. Advertising is weak and margins are falling, 
and the Sina litigation adds a risk outside Weibo's control. 
user scale, VAS growth, AI initiatives and net cash offer 
support, but weak underlying advertising demand, higher 
costs and margin compression mean that earnings recov-
ery requires improved monetisation and tighter expense 
control. We remain MP.

Hongkong Land (HKLSP, A) – [MP]:  Hongkong Land is re-
portedly in discussions with Hotel Properties Ltd to acquire 
a majority interest in three Orchard Road assets—voco Or-
chard, Forum The Shopping Mall and HPL House—in a po-
tential redevelopment valued at more than S$2bn. For 
bondholders, a majority-stake acquisition would likely re-
quire meaningful capital deployment and could pressure 
near-term free cash flow or leverage, but a well-executed 
redevelopment could enhance long-term asset value and 
recurring income; the credit impact depends on funding 
mix, with debt-funded expansion more negative for senior 
and hybrid bondholders than an equity- or asset-recycling-
funded transaction. 1H26 result was constructive: operat-
ing trends were improving, the balance sheet was stronger, 
and Hongkong Land is starting to show the earnings bene-
fit of lower leverage and third-party capital initiatives. The 
main limitation is that the Hong Kong office recovery is still 
early, so rental growth will likely lag the improvement in 
spot market conditions. The biggest medium-term posi-
tives are the stabilization of Central rents, the ramp-up of 
Tomorrow’s CENTRAL, and the earnings contribution from 
SCPREF and other capital-light growth platforms. We re-
main MP on HKLSP bonds and we see HK Land as a better 
equity play.

Canara bank (CBKIN, Baa3/BBB-) – [MP]: Released on Oct 
14th, Canara Bank’s 2QFY27 results were solid. 
Standalone net profit rose 8.4% YoY to INR5,175 crore, or 
INR51.75bn, from INR4,774 crore in 2QFY26, and was up 
6.5% QoQ; it was Canara’s highest-ever quarterly 
profit.  Net interest income increased 20.4% YoY to 
INR11,005 crore from INR9,141 crore, while interest in-
come rose 11.5% YoY to INR35,159 crore and interest ex-
pense rose more slowly at 8% to INR24,154 crore.   NII 
growth of 20.4% against 8% funding-cost growth was the 
core driver of earnings quality. Operating profit before pro-
visions and contingencies rose only 1.98% YoY to 
INR8,758 crore from INR8,588 crore.  The gap between 
20.4% NII growth and 2.0% operating-profit growth reflects 
a 68.5% YoY fall in treasury income, which more than offset 
the strong core-margin performance.  For bondholders, 
this is a moderate quality concern: the result demonstrates 
strong recurring lending profitability, but the bottom-line 
beat relies partly on non-recurring or volatile treasury gains 
and lower provisioning rather than equally strong fee and 
trading income. Net interest margin improved to 2.55% in 
2QFY27 from 2.52% in 1QFY27 and 2.50% a year earlier; 
the cumulative NIM was 2.53%.  Management expects to 
sustain the 2.55% quarterly NIM, aided by repricing of 53% 
of the loan book and MCLR revisions, although further de-
posit-cost pressure or competitive lending spreads could 
limit upside. Asset quality improved materially. Gross NPAs 
declined to 1.33% of gross advances from 1.57% in 
1QFY27 and 2.35% a year earlier, while net NPAs fell to 
0.30% from 0.36% sequentially and 0.54% YoY.  Both met-
rics were better than the bank’s full-year guidance of 
1.50% GNPA and 0.40% NNPA, indicating that credit per-
formance is running ahead of management’s baseline as-
sumptions. Provision coverage rose to 95.34%, up 175 bp 
YoY, while slippages were 0.58% and annualized credit 
cost was 0.47%, both better than the bank’s 0.80% slip-
page and 0.75% credit-cost guidance.  Total provisions de-
clined to INR3,583 crore from INR3,814 crore a year ear-
lier, while NPA-specific provisions fell to INR1,339 crore 
from INR1,504 crore; quarterly provisions were also below 
the INR2,080 crore recorded in 1QFY27.  This is clearly 
supportive for creditors: a 95.34% coverage ratio and 
0.30% net NPA ratio provide a substantial buffer against 
future credit deterioration.  CAR improved to 17.19% at 
end-September 2026 from 16.20% a year earlier, while 
CET1 stood at 12.80%.   Overall, the 2QFY27 print was 
strong: record quarterly profit, 20.4% net interest income 
growth, improving asset quality and a higher capital ade-
quacy ratio all support the bank’s ability to absorb losses 
and service debt. The main offset is weak operating-profit 
growth of only 1.98% YoY, caused by a 68.5% decline in 
treasury income, which means the earnings beat was 
driven more by lower provisions and core lending momen-
tum than by broad-based revenue strength.  We remain MP 
on CBKIN 29s trading at fair valuations.

SEB (SEB, AT1 Baa2/BBB+) – [MP]:  Sweden’s regulator, 
Finansinspektionen (FI), has imposed a temporary capital 
add-on on SEB after finding that deficiencies in the bank’s 
internal credit-risk (IRB) models were more extensive than 
previously known. The Swedish FSA’s decision will in-
crease risk exposure amounts by around SEK 150 billion 
from 31 December 2026. This adds approximately SEK 10 
billion to SEB’s CET1 requirement, on top of the roughly 
SEK 10 billion add-on in place since 2023, bringing the 
combined surcharge to about SEK 20 billion.  The effect is 
split between a 20 bp methodological change and a 100 
bp Pillar 1 calibration, producing the total 120 bp reduction 
in the pro forma 1H26 capital buffer. The immediate con-
sequence is that SEB’s MDA buffer falls from 250 bp at 30 
June 2026 to about 180 bp, placing it near the bottom of 
the analyst’s bank coverage universe.  That does not mean 
SEB is close to breaching its MDA threshold; rather, it 
means the bank has less room to absorb earnings volatility, 
risk-weighted-asset growth, regulatory changes, or further 
model-related capital impacts before restrictions on distri-
butions could apply. The add-on remains until the FSA ap-
proves remediated models, and SEB expects to begin sub-
mitting IRB model applications by 3Q27 at the latest, cre-
ating a potentially multi-year capital constraint. For SEB 
bondholders, the reduction of the MDA buffer by about 120 
bp to 180 bp is a negative development for capital flexibility 
and, in particular, for AT1 coupon continuity, but it is not yet 
a direct threat to senior debt service or the bank’s solvency. 
The impact is most relevant to subordinated and AT1 in-
vestors because a thinner buffer raises the probability that 
management restricts distributions to preserve CET1, 
while senior preferred and non-preferred creditors remain 
protected by SEB’s strong standalone capital and liquidity 
position. For senior preferred and non-preferred bondhold-
ers, the direct credit impact is limited. SEB reported a 
17.2% CET1 ratio and SEK 10.8 billion of operating profit 
in 2Q26, while Fitch has characterized its capitalization as 
strong and expects CET1 to remain comfortably above 16% 
over the long term.  The key risk is indirect: a lower MDA 
buffer could constrain buybacks, dividends, or internal 
capital generation, reducing the pace at which SEB builds 
additional loss-absorbing capacity. However, senior credi-
tors’ claims remain ahead of AT1 and Tier 2 instruments, 
and the bank’s ability to meet senior obligations is not con-
tingent on maintaining a particular MDA buffer. For Tier 2 
investors, the news is modestly negative but manageable. 
Tier 2 is loss-absorbing and notched below the bank’s via-
bility rating, but coupon payments are not automatically 
cancelled by an MDA restriction in the way AT1 distribu-
tions can be.  The main concern is that a prolonged capital 
constraint could limit SEB’s willingness or ability to re-
finance, call, or support subordinated liabilities on favour-
able terms, and could increase spread volatility if investors 
demand more compensation for regulatory uncertainty. 
For AT1 holders, the development is the most credit-rele-
vant. AT1 coupons are typically discretionary and subject 
to MDA restrictions, so a buffer of 180 bp materially re-
duces the cushion before SEB might need to conserve cap-
ital.   Overall, the announcement is a negative for capital 
flexibility and AT1 risk, mildly negative for Tier 2, and 
broadly neutral for senior preferred and non-preferred 
bonds. The central question for bondholders is whether 
SEB will accept a thinner MDA buffer or rebuild it through 
capital conservation; until management clarifies this, AT1 
and Tier 2 spreads are likely to remain sensitive to regula-
tory developments, model-approval progress, and SEB’s 
3Q26 capital plans. However, the news is already priced in 
and SEB AT1 are trading too wide in our view compared to 
other Nordic AT1s. we remain selectively OP on them.
