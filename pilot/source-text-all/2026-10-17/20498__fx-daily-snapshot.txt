USD: AI sell-off triggers quick reversal of US CPI report driven gains   
The US dollar has weakened overnight quickly giving back yesterday’s initial gains 
after the release of the stronger than expected US CPI report for September. After 
hitting a fresh year-to-date high at 102.95 yesterday, the dollar index has since fallen 
back towards the 102.00-level. It has been a similar story for US yields. the 2-year US 
Treasury initially jumped higher by around 18bps after the release of the US CPI report 
hitting a high of 4.95% before dropping back towards 4.70% overnight. The reversal 
lower for Us yields and US dollar appears to have been driven mainly by an unwind of 
AI-related trades overnight resulting in more risk-off trading conditions. The biggest 
sell-off has been in South Korea’s equity market where the Kospi index has fallen 
sharply by almost 9% extending its decline since the peak in June to just over 40%. 
The share prices of South Korea’s two main chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung 
Electronics have both fallen by around 12% after breaking below support from their 
200-day moving averages this week.

The release yesterday of the stronger US CPI report for September will keep pressure 
of the Fed to keep hiking rates and has raised the probability of a back-to-back hike 
later this month. The report revealed that both headline and core inflation picked up by 
more than expected to 3.7% and 2.6% respectively in September. The pick-up in the 
headline rate of inflation since the US-Iran conflict began is still mainly driven but higher 
energy prices but there is building concern over broadening inflation pressures. Core 
goods prices increased by 0.25%m/m and core services inflation by 0.37%M/M. The 
report has raised market expectations for core PCE deflator to increase by around 0.3-

are not in a rush to hike again as soon as this month. Fed Cahir Kevin Warsh’s 
comments at the Annual IMF meetings in Thailand on Friday will be scrutinized even 
more closely now after the stronger US CPI report.    
 
JPY: BoJ comments signal further hikes on the way   
The yen has benefitted from more risk-off trading conditions overnight resulting in 
USD/JPY dropping back below the 158.00-level although its performance has been 
more mixed against non-USD currencies. The stronger yen has also been encouraged 
by encouraged by hawkish comments from BoJ board member Junko Koeda. In a 
speech to local leaders in Kumamoto overnight, she stated “given that there are 
considerable upside risks to prices in addition to continued accommodative financial 
conditions, the Bank should not become overly cautious about raising the policy 
interest rate”. She believes that the “Bank should adjust the policy interest rate in a 
timely manner with an eye on the economic and price situation, while keeping in mind 
the estimated range of the neutral interest rate”. The BoJ has indicated that the 
estimated range of the neutral policy rate is between 1.1%and 2.5%. Junko Koeda 
stated that she believes the level of the neutral interest rate is considerably above 1%” 
which indicates that she still sees room for further rate hikes with the current policy rate 
at 1.25%. The next BoJ policy meeting is scheduled for 30th October. We still expect 
the BoJ to leave rates on hold this month but hike again before the end of this year in 
December providing more support for the yen.
