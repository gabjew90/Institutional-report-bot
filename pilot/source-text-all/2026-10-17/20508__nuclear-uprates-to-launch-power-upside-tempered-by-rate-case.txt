Nuclear Uprates To Launch? Power Upside
Tempered by Rate Case Uncertainty
Maintain Hold ahead of NJ rate case. Salem nuclear plant co-owner CEG's
nuclear uprate with Google has caused renewed excitement: a similar ratio deal
could be worth +4-5% EPS uplift. PEG offers nuclear optionality, but the base
business profile lags higher growth/low risk peers today. Net lower LT EPS for
approx -$0.10 PSE&G lower ROE/equity ratio (AWK comp), +$0.15 merchant
power, & -$0.10 unregulated interest expense. 2028 cut more with less power
uplift.
How far until the bottom in NJ? We reiterate our Hold as we cut our utility EPS again, offset by
net higher merchant power prices and parent/power financing costs for utility growth. We recently
met with management to discuss the regulatory outlook, the upcoming general rate case filing,
unregulated nuclear asset optionality, and more. Management remains very comfortable with the
outlook for NJ regulation and expects a fair rate case outcome. In our view, upcoming regulated
utility catalysts skew negative with ratemaking reform and lower ROE/equity ratios awarded to
peers recently. Merchant power fundamentals and uprate potential continues to improve and
provide upside currently. Most investors we speak to are missing PEG's disproportionate interest
rate sensitivity (granted a product of less equity dilution). We reduce 2031 EPS by -7¢ as we see +15¢
merchant price upside being more than offset by -10¢ at PSE&G (for AWK -100bp equity ratio & -10bp
ROE comp) and -11¢ interest rate mark-to-market. Management has managed through hurdles well. We
forecast that consolidated EPS growth is still matching the long-term EPS guidance
Fear of the unknown: YE26 rate case filing looms large. It is not eminently clear how the Sherrill
Administration will view a large electric rate case on the heels of its 'rate freeze'. We have seen
multiple rate cases but PSE&G has a spotlight for its size. American Water's recent rate case
settlement with -10bps ROE and -100bps equity ratio are unfavorable, albeit manageable. Electric
utilities appear to be the primary focus of Sherrill's reforms, and state scrutiny could be highest for PSE&G,
but time will tell. The uncertainty on the potential rate case is likely worse than the reality. Investor interest
has been very low on PEG into the rate case, with a notable uptick after the recent uprate deals.
Nuclear uprate deals have emerged, PSEG Power could also deliver. We have seen high profile
nuclear uprate announcements between Constellation-Amazon/Google recent nuclear uprates and
Southern Company/Google. CEG-GOOGL likely included its share of NJ Salem. PEG has ~120MW
share of the ~200MW Salem uprate. Applying a 3-4x ratio would imply ability contract 360-480MW
worth up to +18¢ at ~+$25/MWh blended premium. NJ Governor Sherril was very supportive of
Google's uprate with CEG. We do not see an announcement as imminent but difficult to predict.
This is the clearest opportunity for PEG to go on the offense. We see contracting the balance of the nukes
as unlikely.

 The Long View: PSEG
Investment Thesis
PEG is 80%+ regulated New Jersey-oriented utility with the balance related to
valuable merchant nuclear plants in NJ/PA. PEG's strong story was derailed
by high electric rates in 2025 and becoming the center of the election cycle
with the New Jersey Governor Mikie Sherrill focusing her attention on a utility
rate freeze. A solid utility story now needs to work within the confines of a less
accommodating regime, leading to a slower pace of investments, lower rates
of returns, and increasing reliance on unregulated earnings growth. Additional
considerations have reduced the probability of the nuclear plants securing
premium data center contracts. More upside than downside purely from a
nuclear deal, but low conviction.

a priority.

Where NJ stands currently. The Sherrill Administration launched the effort through Executive Order
No. 1 in January 2026, directing the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities (BPU) to evaluate utility
affordability and potential reforms to the electric utility business model. The order aimed to address
electric rate pressure, limit or review actions that could raise customer bills, and use available tools
to offset near-term electricity cost increases. The BPU has since moved into a formal utility business
model reform study, conducted with Energy and Environmental Economics (E3). The Phase 1 report
was published in July 2026 and was qualitative in nature, focused on how electric bills are determined,
how the existing utility business model affects customer costs, and what reform options could help
improve affordability. The report did not prescribe a specific final policy package, but rather provided
a menu of options that could be adopted.
Through these efforts, the Sherrill Administration has arguably provided clear (if indirect) guidance
on its regulatory priorities, namely that the current affordability environment encourages a more
protective stance toward the ratepayer, to the degree that earning authorized ROEs could be pressured.
This could imply further degradation to the regulatory environment, especially since PSE&G will be
opening itself up to scrutiny with a full rate case. The situation harkens back to Exelon-subsidiary
PECO filing a rate case earlier this year in Pennsylvania only to face higher-than expected stakeholder
pushback and withdraw the case. We are strongly not saying this is the fate of PSE&G's upcoming filing
but investors we speak to have made this parallel. The signals are mixed in New Jersey with multiple utility
rate case conclusions (Veolia water, Consolidated Edison electric, and American Water) and applications
(New Jersey Resources gas, Jersey Central Power & Light electric, etc.) are incompatible with a rate freeze.
Electric utilities appear to be the main target of reforms. The administration's focus is specifically
on the electric utility sector, where it deems near-term cost pressures to be most acute. This explains
why water and gas utility proceedings have advanced this year; they are not facing the same level of
scrutiny as electric utilities with the highest rates.
Consider the recently resolved American Water general rate case (Docket WR24010056), in which
the company initially requested a $161.7mm annual revenue increase, later updated to $157.7mm,
based on a proposed 9.70% ROE and 56% equity layer. In September, the BPU authorized a $79.5mm
annual revenue increase (roughly 50% of the ask), with a 9.60% ROE and 55% equity layer, implying
a relatively modest 10 bps ROE cut and 100 bps equity-ratio reduction versus the company’s ask.
