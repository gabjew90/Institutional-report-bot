First Horizon National Corp. (FHN): First take: Slight PPNR miss on 
higher costs; Sells portion of mortgage book to create balance sheet 
15 October 2026 | 7:49AM EDT
Bottom-line on the stock: FHN’s 3Q26 results were mixed and there were a handful 
of moving pieces given the announced balance sheet restructuring.  That will likely 
get a lot of airtime on the call as the sell side tries to pick through the underlying 
pieces; however, the overall quarter was mixed as revenues were relatively in-line (a 
touch soft NII; fees in-line) while expenses came in higher.  While the margin was 
in-line (3.48% vs. street at 3.47%), deposit costs rose 12bps (VA consensus +6bps) 
while loan yields advanced more than expected. Balance sheet metrics were mixed 
as loans (ex. sale) were a little softer driven by a big decline in LMCs while deposits 
were a little stronger. Credit was impacted by the release due to the mortgage sale 
but even ex. that provisions were lower as NPLs improved.  
In terms of its guidance, FHN is pointing to the low-end of its 3-7% revenue guide 
(street at +3.6%) which we think points to in-line revenues and lower costs in 4Q.  
Overall we like the restructuring – it lowers the LDR  ratio to 88% (from ~96%) and 
provides a nice earnings uptick.  While investors may show concern about the long 
earnback (implies ~10 years) and its basically the same accretion as using the 
$473mn to buy back stock, it strengthens certain financial metrics (LDR) and boosts 
liquidity which has been an issue over time. So when we put it all together, we believe 
positioning (based on our investor conversations) was mixed as some expected soft 
fundamentals; however, given the re-rating the stock has undergone, option value for 
M&A is high.  So while we think this should be well received, it comes down to how 
confident the market is in the path of deposit costs and its ability to effectively 
deploy the liquidity. 
Four key questions for the call: 1) Can you provide additional color on some of the 
key metrics for the balance sheet restructuring, including loan yield of the portfolio 
you sold and the moving pieces of the $45mn of after-tax earnings accretion in 2027 
and how do you expect to deploy proceeds? 2) Can you talk about your expectations 
for deposit costs and incremental betas in 4Q and into 2027 post the Fed rate hike? 
Do you expect residual pressure above what is associated with the rate hike? 3) What 
are your expectations for NII and net interest margin expansion embedded in your 
4Q guide and how does the mortgage restructuring change your expectations for a 
go forward NIM? 4) Given the transaction you only bought back $4mn of stock in 3Q.  
With CET1 at 10.3% and at the low-end of your updated 10.25-10.5% expectation, 
what does that mean for buybacks going forward?

higher occupancy, outside service, and other expense. Credit continues to be strong as 
NCOs of ~0.22% approximated expectations and the core provision came in below 
expectations. Lastly, CET1 was 10.3% as FHN bought back $4mn of shares (vs. street at 
~$126mn).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
