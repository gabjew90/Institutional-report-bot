Downgrading Some Enterprise
Names after a Significant Run and
on Enterprise AI Timing
We are taking a more cautious approach on the stocks in our
coverage that are more aligned with Enterprise spending.
Valuations have run, commodity costs have helped revenues,
and we don't see a meaningful AI inflection yet.
We entered the year positive on several stocks with Enterprise exposure (DELL, HPE, NTAP) with
a more cautious view on some others (CSCO, FFIV, P, HPQ) in the sector. At the time we thought
investor concerns around increased memory costs were overdone, and ultimately the Hardware
companies proved that they could pass through commodity costs. We are lowering our ratings
to Equal Weight from Overweight on DELL, HPE and NTAP, remain Equal Weight on CSCO, FFIV
and P, and Underweight on HPQ. The group of stocks has performed very well:
• This group of seven stocks is up an average of ~140% YTD compared to the SP500 at up ~14%
and the COMP up ~17%.
• The average multiple of these stocks stands at a CY27E P/E of 21x. Typically this group
averages (5-year average) a multiple of ~15x.

1. Many investors were negative on this group entering the year given the high percentage of bill
of material from DRAM and NAND. Memory content makes up 20-50% of the BoM for
networking, PCs, storage systems and servers. 
Our view was that investors underestimated the hardware companies' ability to pass through
pricing to enterprise customers.  Historically, memory is a pass-through for servers and storage
and to a lesser degree networking. In January, we printed a note detailing how companies make
up for higher commodity costs (Feedback from Year Ahead Pieces - Our View on Memory Price
Impacts, 1/20/2026).  Here are the key tables.
What we have seen play out is exactly this dynamic. Companies in general have been beating
revenue numbers while also maintaining gross margins.  A look at the last few years of
enterprise server units and ASPs illustrated this trend. We believe the trend in enterprise
storage would look similar. The higher ASPs have propped up revenues for the enterprise
focused names.

2. There is growing sentiment about enterprise AI adoption creating a hardware refresh.  We
believe the strong enterprise revenues are more of a function of memory costs than a move to
enterprise AI at this time. 
Enterprises will certainly take on a more proactive approach to deploying and using AI over
time. Historically we have seen transitions in enterprise happen during a standard refresh cycle,
typically driven by depreciation schedules. In other words, enterprises replace hardware on a
regular cadence while keeping the overall budget flat to slightly up.  During these cycles, the
new hardware purchased supports the new technology (in this case AI).
When looking at our most recent survey of 100 CIOs in the US and Europe (2H26 CIO Survey: 2026
Expectations Ticked Up and Initial 2027 Outlook Solid), we note two interesting trends.  
1. We have been asking "What percentage of your IT spending goes to AI."  While the number has
been increasing, we are not seeing much of an acceleration and are currently at slightly over 5%
of the budget. The overall budget has improved a little with more AI, so it is possible there is a
slight benefit overall, but it does not seem meaningful.

2. For the first time we have asked for more detail on the complexion of the AI spending
between on-prem hardware, spending with model developers, and buying tokens from cloud
players. For hardware, the first bucket is all that matters.
The survey showed that only 26% of the AI spend would be for using their own hardware. To
deploy hardware on-line, capex needs to spent and the IT department may need to add
expertise in AI. In the early days of AI, it looks like enterprises prefer to outsource the
infrastructure spend and pay others for the technology on a consumption based model.
We want to discuss our views on the three names we are downgrading to Equal
Weight from Overweight: 
DELL. DELL is different than most on this list as it is fairly aligned with the AI trade. Regardless,
we believe the way that DELL has executed on offsetting memory inflation is responsible for a
big part of the stock performance this year (up ~355%+ YTD vs. SP500 up ~14%). A few key
points:
• AI servers have been a successful business for DELL. We believe operating margins have
stabilized at ~5%. Although this is dilutive to the corporate average, our model shows AI
servers tripling this fiscal year to $75b.  We wouldn't be surprised to see upside to our FY28 AI
server revenue  estimate of $100b given the strong AI capex backdrop, but growth should be
decelerating going forward.  
• DELL has executed flawlessly across enterprise categories. Guidance calls for just over 100%
revenue growth in enterprise servers, mid teens in storage, and mid teens in CSG for FY27. We
believe the vast majority of this growth comes from higher ASPs to offset memory inflation
(see Figure 4 above). As a result, we expect a meaningful deceleration in growth over the next
two years and memory price normalize and potentially decline.  
• DELL has also been executing well on opex. The company has reduced its opex as a % of revs
from 15.6% in FY24 to 8.0% in FY27e.

• When the company first discussed the integration of JNPR last year, revenue guidance was
viewed as a disappointment. Management has steadily increased the outlook and now
expects a networking FY26-FY29E CAGR of high teens %.
• The strength in networking has been broad based. The AI outlook has improved and HPE/
JNPR is well positioned in campus as well as in newer AI markets like scale up and scale
across. 
• In server and storage, HPE has followed along with industry participants and used higher
ASPs to bolster revenues while maintaining GM.  We see less upside as memory prices
normalize. 
• While we like the networking exposure, we see the improved mix as reflected in the current
valuation. We make no model changes but lower our price target to $77 based on a P/E
multiple of 13x our FY28 EPS estimate (from our prior PT of $83 on 14x our FY28E EPS of
$5.92). 
NTAP.  We have liked NTAP based on valuation and an under appreciated cloud business (10%
of sales, SaaS model, growing ~20% with 85%+ GM). With the stock up ~123% YTD, a few points: 
• We still view the Cloud business at NTAP as one of the best segments in our coverage. Recall
NTAP sells storage as a service on all three major hyperscaler store fronts. Revenues have
been growing about 20%, GM has reach 85%, and the business is now 10% of total sales. 
• NTAP has a high percentage of recurring revenues as the service line continues to grow off a
large installed base.  
• While there is always a focus on product GM, product only represents ~49% of revenues in
FY27 and a lower percentage of operating income. NTAP does have the benefit of selling AFA,
but also hybrid arrays using some disc as a means of providing a more cost effective solution. 
• While we are seeing some upside to the storage industry in 2H26 based on NAND pricing, we
also expect a normalization next year. 
• We are not making any changes to our model or our price target of $219.
And for the other names we are Equal Weight: 
CSCO. CSCO (up ~49% YTD vs. SP500 at up ~14%) has improved its position in AI by catching up
in networking and leveraging a very strong optics market with Acacia.  We see cloud/AI as only
mid teens % of revenues in CY26. In enterprise, the company has ended support of some old
switches and has a new product set out at the same time. While this is positioned to drive
growth, we see CSCO growing in-line with the campus market in FY26 and FY27e. 
FFIV. Investors are still trying to assess the AI impact on FFIV (up ~87% YTD vs. SP500 at
up ~14%).  The company recently gave an estimate for its direct AI business in the LSD% of total,

Valuation Methodology and Risks
IT Hardware and Communications Equipment
Dell Technologies Inc. (DELL / DELL)
Valuation Methodology: Our price target of $574 reflects 20x our FY28 EPS estimate of $28.71.
Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target: Downside Risks: Slowdown in customer AI
investments could lead to AI server revenue deceleration, increased competition in AI space, PC slowdown, slowdown in legacy businesses ex-AI,
rising commodity costs. Upside Risks: Stronger than expected AI demand, higher ASPs boosts revenue for longer than expected, stronger than
expected server and storage end market strength
Hewlett Packard Enterprise Company (HPE / HPE)
Valuation Methodology: Our price target of $77 assumes the stock trades at a P/E multiple of 13x our FY28 EPS estimate of $5.92. We see balanced
risk/reward in the current valuation relative to expectations.
Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target: Risks to the downside: 1) Cloud Competition. In
addition to white box supplying cloud vendors, each segment has become more competitive, with Dell rebounding in storage and competing in server.
2) Macro. HPE and other traditional hardware vendors are susceptible to macro impacts. 3) Longer enterprise recovery could delay product refresh
and AI story. Risks to the upside: 1) Stronger networking business 2) higher memory prices bolster ASPs and revenue for longer 3) Greater AI systems
growth
NetApp, Inc. (NTAP / NTAP)
Valuation Methodology: We use a P/E multiple of 20x on our FY28e EPS estimate of $10.93 to arrive at our $219 price target.
Risks which May Impede the Achievement of the Barclays Research Valuation and Price Target: Downside Risk: The overall storage demand
environment remains challenged, and the move to public cloud may hurt NetApp, though some workloads have been moving back, and NetApp is
ramping its Public Cloud Data Services business. Competition also remains a risk from DELL as well as Pure Storage, while HP Enterprise is still a
relevant player. Upside Risk: 1) acceleration in AFA storage demand 2) faster growth in cloud and data services revenue driven by expanded
hyperscaler partnerships 3) AI data management and AI data infrastructure emerging as a larger growth opportunity than expected
