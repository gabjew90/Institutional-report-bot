Our view
TSMC delivered another strong set of results in 3Q26, 
highlighting the sustained momentum and widening reach 
of AI-driven compute demand. Revenue rose 41% y/y to 
TWD 1.49tr (USD 46.7bn), above the company’s guidance 
range of USD 44.6-45.8bn and 2.5% ahead of consensus. 
Gross margin came in at 66.4%, in line with guidance 
and up 7 percentage points from a year earlier, while the 
58.7% operating margin exceeded management’s outlook. 
Management noted that the 2nm ramp weighed on its 
gross margin in 3Q26 because of initially lower yields, but 
demand-led pricing gains more than offset the impact, 
leaving the overall margin trajectory firmly upward. Net 
income reached TWD 763bn, up 68.7% y/y and 3% 
above consensus, reflecting continued operating leverage 
and robust profitability as AI infrastructure investment 
accelerates.
Beyond the strong 3Q26 results, the guidance reinforced 
confidence in both the strength and durability of the current 
AI-driven upcycle. TSMC guided to 4Q26 revenue of USD 
52.3-54.3bn, implying growth of 14% q/q and 58% y/
y at the midpoint and coming in 5% above consensus. 
Management expects profitability to remain resilient, with 
a gross margin of 65-67% and operating margin of 
56.5-58.5%, despite continued dilution from overseas fabs.

The company also raised its FY26 revenue growth forecast 
to 44% y/y in USD terms from its previous expectation 
of “slightly above 40%,” pointing to better-than-expected 
demand visibility. Taken together, the faster revenue growth 
and sustained margin resilience suggest a stronger AI cycle 
than previously anticipated.
Rising ASP to be key earnings driver
Given management’s constructive outlook for AI accelerator 
demand, we expect pricing to become an increasingly 
important earnings driver for TSMC heading into 2027, 
with scope for further increases across both mature and 
leading-edge nodes. Channel checks suggest TSMC has 
begun discussing 2027 pricing with customers, with mature-
node wafer prices potentially rising by 3-10%. Supply is 
even tighter at advanced nodes as AI demand accelerates. 
TSMC’s technology leadership and constrained industry 
capacity should support greater pricing power, particularly 
as customers prioritize securing leading-edge capacity over 
near-term cost savings.

Given that N2 is only in its third quarter of volume 
production, yield ramp remains a near-term drag on 
profitability relative to mature nodes, weighing modestly 
on overall gross margin as reflected in both 3Q26 results 
and 4Q26 guidance. That said, we believe N2 offers the 
clearest pricing upside. Strong adoption and limited global 
supply could support a further 6-8% increase in N2 wafer 
prices beyond current expectations. We also expect more AI 
workloads to migrate from N5 to N3 from 2H26, further 
tightening leading-edge capacity. A successful N2 ramp with 
improving yields, faster N3 adoption, and sustained hyper-
scaler investment should support further price increases and 
gross-margin expansion, providing a credible path toward 
70% in 2027.

Planning global capacity expansion to match clients’ 
demand
Meeting customer demand appears to be a key strategic 
priority for TSMC, with management emphasizing its 
focus on supporting customers’ product launch schedules. 
Current conditions also appear less consistent with a 
typical semiconductor cycle and more reflective of a 
multi-year expansion in AI-related demand. Demand for 
AI accelerators, CPUs, networking chips, and advanced 
packaging continues to exceed supply, supporting further 
global capacity additions.
Visibility into customers’ longer-term product roadmaps has 
supported TSMC’s decision to expand capacity, with nearly 
25 fabs under development worldwide. The continued 
addition of N3 capacity through 2027-2028 suggests that 
management expects AI-related demand to remain elevated 
over a multi-year period.
At the same time, we think TSMC’s expansion strategy 
remains focused on retaining operational control and 
technology leadership. The company said it is evaluating 
further capacity expansion in Texas and discussing potential 
cooperation with Terafab, which is seeking dedicated 
manufacturing capacity, while reiterating that any fab 
would need to be operated by TSMC. This approach could 
allow TSMC to address customer demand and geographic 
diversification while retaining control over its manufacturing 
processes and execution standards. The planned expansion 
should support its role in the AI infrastructure supply chain 
across leading-edge logic and advanced packaging.
Expect strong capex across 2026 to 2028
Investors remain focused on TSMC’s FY26 capex plan after 
management raised the target in each of the previous two 
earnings releases. Although the company maintained its 
guidance at USD 60-64bn this time, it emphasized that 
demand remains very robust and expects aggregate capital 
expenditure in 2026-2028 to be meaningfully higher than 
in 2024-2026.

Conclusion
We maintain a constructive view on TSMC following its 
strong results and higher growth outlook, which support 
its leading position in the AI supply chain. The stock’s 
premium valuation of roughly 17-18x forward P/E appears 
supported by its profitability, pricing power, and exposure 
to the multi-year AI capex cycle. With demand visibility 
extending into 2027-2028, TSMC remains a key option for 
investors seeking exposure to structural AI growth. Risks 
to our positive view on TSMC's outlook include lower-
than-expected outsourcing from key clients as they enhance 
in-house capabilities, a weak macroeconomic environment 
that could dampen end-market demand, and intensifying 
competition.
Also, uncertainty around tariffs, geopolitics, and the 
sustainability of AI spending warrants a selective approach 
overall and continued attention to risk management.

many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific 
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.
