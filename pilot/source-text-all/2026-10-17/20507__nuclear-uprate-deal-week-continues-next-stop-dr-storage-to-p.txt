Nuclear Uprate Deal Week Continues: Next Stop
DR & Storage to Pair?
CEG/GOOGL contract for 890MW nuke uprates & 2,700MW existing nuke: 3x
ratio, similar to AMZN last week. Positive for nuke sector, but the readthroughs
are more nuanced. In contrast, investor feedback has been mostly positive
with a view that it is "game on" for contracting existing assets. We are not
convinced. Nuclear assets remain unique, especially those that facilitate
uprate additionality. TLN, VST, PEG, & D have uprates available (coming next).
A now exhausted nuclear uprate fleet leaves others to step in still. Constellation Energy
announced its latest nuclear uprate/existing asset hyperscaler transaction, now with Google at
a 3x ratio (~$4,800/kW). CEG has been clear in articulating this strategy. This follows Southern
Company's similar action and highlights that nuclear uprate transactions have broad political
support, despite the affordability impacts. We expect Department of Energy loans on uprates like
Vistra recently.
Positive development for the nuclear power sector. We continue to view the nuclear uprate assets as
unique with political support and not broader readthroughs to natural gas assets. Google framed the
ratio deal as aligning with the Ratepayer Protection Pledge.
The next step is taking this concept and pivoting to storage & demand response. The big debate
was never about whether IPPs could contract nuclear uprates as this is truly unique asset class
with only 3-5GW available. As the outlook for new AP1000 and small modular reactors in the US
has become more opaque, the value of uprates has seemingly grown. The core question remains
as to whether IPPs can pair up to use the term capacity resources that provide a nominal 'BYOG'
qualifying asset with energy sold off of IPP existing assets. We think this best meshes with capacity-
only resources like battery and demand response, both of which have been explicitly called out in the
CEG announcement Tuesday. While, technically, new gas built could also be paired up with existing
in a similar fashion that the nuclear uprates happen, we are less inclined to see this permutation
materialize in the current environment.
We think the next step in the sector will be to leverage a much higher price point for demand
response to meaningfully, expand the scope of what is possible in this front as well as offering up
bilateral contracts on rapid deployment of storage to mesh with energy offtake tales the extent of
this opportunity could well soak up remaining energy exposure into long-term contract. It would
help really continue the narrative of pivoting the IPP sector into a more long-term contracted space.
We still perceive limited interest in hyperscalers in existing assets: the existing nuclear assets are the
cost to get the new nuclear assets in this environment. Bottom line, the real reset case is yet to come,
but this would be seen as a net positive if proven out. The question is whether a ratio beyond simply
1:1 is possible. Management teams indicate they can scale energy offtake from existing assets beyond
notional capacity from incremental resources.

Constellation and Google announced a 20-year PPA supporting 890MW of incremental nuclear
capacity across Constellation’s PJM fleet along with a 15-year energy supply agreement with their
existing fleet. The agreement will fund uprates across 11 nuclear units in Illinois, Pennsylvania and
New Jersey, with the first incremental capacity expected online in 2028. Constellation expects to invest
more than $4.3Bn across six nuclear sites to deliver the uprates. Separately, Google entered into a 15-
year energy supply agreement for an additional 2,700MW from Constellation’s existing PJM fleet. The
broader collaboration also incorporates load-shaping/demand response and a framework to evaluate
additional clean generation, storage and demand-response opportunities, initially focused on PJM.
October 6th 20-year Google agreement is effectively using CEG’s previously identified ~1.1 GW
uprate opportunity. For more details on the recent Amazon announcement please see our note:

The Governor's support for contracting nuclear assets in her state is notable given the sharp focus on electric

We see a higher probability now that PEG announced some kind of similar transaction, albeit perhaps not
at the same ratio as CEG, but nonetheless leveraging their megawatts for some kind of wider deal. We have
been skeptical for months that PEG could or would contract its nuclear plants but there appears to be a
window forward. This could be upside to estimates. The uprate could be offered into the reliability backstop
procurement in early 2027 or more potentially via a bilateral with a hyper scaler. Management has made
pains to flag this on its latest earnings call.
The totality of uprates contracted between CEG, VST & SO of late - and others shows clear evidence
that the nuclear 'renaissance' will happen principally through the expanding the existing capacity.
These additional megawatts come with premium prices and are expensive on a dollar per kilowatt;
the cost of these are being amortized across existing assets. The question was not if nuclear uprates
would happen, the question to us was the ratio of new assets that could be contracted with it. The
latest deals are in the 2.5-3.0x range. Some think we have been deeply skeptical of all contracting, but the
nuclear asset class remains unique, particularly the scarce uprate megawatts.
We see an upward bias to PPA prices vs the guidance various companies have disclosed on these
deals, as market power prices and costs trend higher. We anticipate PPA prices for these products as
likely modestly above $100/MWh for the latest deals, with an upward bias on implied costs we have
seen of late: $7k/kW 2025 disclosure for Limerick, ~$5k/kW for overall uprate portfoio disclosed today,
and ~$10k/kW implied for Meta deal).
We stress the actual allocated capex to the uprates specifically (rather than asset life extension) is less than
headline capex would suggest and puts a downward bias to the offtake prices otherwise implied. Look for
clearer disclosures in coming weeks. Still, PPA prices for latest vintages of uprate deals would appear to be
firmly below $100/MWh on a blended basis.
We would not say that existing gas plants are going to get contracted. This is simply not the bullish
takeaway that the market and many investors have been making. This will happen on the margin still
too, but we do not see a 'breakthrough'. Rather, we would continue to focus on remaining uncontracted
units such as Vistra's available Comanche Peak unit and/or remaining existing capacity at Beaver
Valley. Comanche appears the easier path than PJM and Pennsylvania Beaver Valley which has a
curtailment requirement being implemented by the state.
 
Investor feedback has been mixed: generalists have clearly liked the nuclear updates whereas
dedicated investors have described this as having limited repeatability. We have received significant

transactions. The new generalist investor interest is perhaps the most bullish element of the
announcements. The consensus trade for most has been to own behind-the-meter and powered land
companies, funded by short/underweight existing power generation IPPs.

Google's demand response efforts are key to watch: a natural partnership with IPPs? We have seen
the sector advocate clearly for expanding curtailment in recent years, and we see 2027 as a critical
turning point to see RTOs & hyperscalers take this more seriously. We see the Google announcement
(amidst a series of other related actions) as accentuating this attempt to satisfy BYOG and curtailment
requirements by agreeing to procure demand response (effectively paying others to curtail) as a clear
alternative.
Curtailment is also an enabling factor to ensure accelerated access to transmission; many investors
fail to appreciate this avenue as a key angle to mitigating protracted transmission ramp issues as
physical grid-upgrades are executed. The bullish angle for IPPs would be to enable more overall
demand on the grid, faster, albeit limiting the scarcity pricing upside that has been to price formation
in markets like ERCOT.
How is a nuclear contract structured? As retail. Google stated that the contract "aligns" with
the Ratepayer Protection Pledge and Constellation will continue to deliver power into the grid via
the "financial structure". With the agreement involving contracts on more existing generation than new
generation, the alignment comment is an interesting point. If the load is subject to IRAS and is fully curtailing
at peak, that would be an interpretation. In practicality, if there is more new demand than new supply for
most peak hours, that will be a factor for pricing and customers.
 
VST: Nuclear uprates set for Federal support, but highlight the elevated price point. On October 5th,
US Energy Secretary Chris Wright is visited the Perry Nuclear plant and confirmed a $4.2Bn Department
of Energy loan package to Vistra for its nuclear uprates, consistent with an earlier ~$4Bn Bloomberg
report. Vistra has guided to 433MW of uprates for the 2,176MW base capacity across 1,268MW Perry
and 908MW Davis-Besse. The uprates are primarily 213MW Perry, 140MW Beaver Valley, and 80MW
Davis-Bessee. At $4.2Bn cost, this represents ~$9,700/kW, potentially a new high mark for nuclear
uprate costs. In general, we have been assuming ~$7,000/kW for nuclear uprates, besides the lower price
de-bottleneckings. We understand that there is an element of risk sharing on higher uprate costs so a
need to build in significant contingency and pay for some expensive life extension investments. The legacy
FirstEnergy Solutions/Energy Harbor units are top-of-mind as requiring long-term investments and Beaver
Valley appears to be the most expensive of the category.

What is the Street not focusing on in Power?
1) Texas - and power likely getting heavily scrutinized. Expect more stringent bring your own
generation (BYOG) legislation beyond Senate Bill 6 in coming months into the new session. How far
this goes is unclear but the added debate today around whether the latest nuclear deals herald a new
era for existing gas generation deal making appear to miss this looming overhang.
2) Curtailment as an avenue to expedited transmission access: this is the Achilles heel of BTM.
While we are quite bullish on behind-the-meter (BTM) generation given the acute shortage of grid
access, enabling grid access through expanded curtailment tariffs appears a clear priority. This would
be a preferred avenue likely for many developers to avoid the over reliance on BTM. We are surprised
how few investors are focused on this. Novel structures and tariffs are under development in ERCOT
& elsewhere. Curtailment and interuptible frameworks do not exist today to support the service, even
if desired.
3) FERC & PJM are stymied: investors should expect a further extension in the price cap of ~
$330/MW-day capacity price cap for the May 2027 considering no viable alternatives have been
put forward. Moreover, the relative degree of inaction bodes favorably for protracted & elevated
