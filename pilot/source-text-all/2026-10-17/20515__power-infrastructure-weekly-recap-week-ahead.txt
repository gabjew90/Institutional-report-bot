STDN (Buy, PT $18) | Initiation: the only merchant TRISO fabricator shipping commercial fuel; replication is the next leg (10/6) 
•
We initiated on Standard Nuclear (Oak Ridge, Tennessee; IPO July 2026) at Buy with an $18 PT. STDN delivered the first complete, independently manufactured commercial TRISO core (to 
Radiant Industries) in 2026 while competing X-energy and BWXT capacity is still under construction or expansion, and its toll-conversion model (customers generally procure and retain 
ownership of the enriched uranium; STDN earns a fabrication fee) avoids direct uranium-price exposure and limits inventory requirements while creating operating leverage as throughput and 
Process Yield improve. Funded Backlog rose to $119.3MM from $8.2MM in roughly five months (Total Contract Backlog $576.9MM; Qualified Pipeline ~$696MM), with Radiant and Antares 
Nuclear behind the step-up. SN-TN and SN-ID are substantially constructed and target DOE Hazard Category 2 authorization in 4Q26 at ~1.0 MTU/year each, and the 66.7%-owned Framatome 
JV adds an NRC-regulated route at Richland with production targeted for 2027. STDN does not bear uranium commodity risk, but customer HALEU availability will set how quickly installed 
capacity turns into revenue. We model revenue of $14.6MM / $61.9MM / $328.9MM in FY26E / FY27E / FY28E and $1,088MM in FY30E; the $18 PT is 10x 2030E EV/EBITDA discounted at a 
20% WACC.

Fusion Energy (Industry Update) | Reuters Fusion Energy 2026 takeaways: the debate moves from physics to capital, cost and scale (10/8) 
•
We attended the Reuters Fusion Energy conference in Boston. Capital availability did not feel like the immediate constraint; matching the form of capital to each stage is, as demonstration plants 
and commercial deployment move the stack from venture capital toward strategic equity, public-private funding, customer commitments, government risk-sharing and eventually project finance. 
Scientific gain is necessary but no longer sufficient: first-of-a-kind (FOAK) plants may find behind-the-meter customers (data centers and other power-constrained loads) willing to pay for 
reliability and speed-to-power, but fusion ultimately competes in a commodity electricity market. Manufacturing architecture is moving earlier in design (Pacific Fusion's Albuquerque 
demonstration system is expected to use ~156 identical pulser modules, with startup targeted for late 2028 or early 2029 and facility gain by 2030; Thea Energy builds repeatable planar HTS 
coils with software-based field control), and the supply chain is deepening ahead of deployment (Commonwealth Fusion Systems has a long-term agreement to design and manufacture HTS 
magnet systems for Realta Fusion and licensed its HTS cable technology to Type One Energy; ATI in specialty materials; Boston Metal upstream in critical metals). We remain constructive on 
the enabling layer of materials, magnets, diagnostics, engineering and power systems, which can see commercial activity well before the first fusion plants reach the grid.

•
TOYO used its 2026 Analyst Day, held alongside a ribbon-cutting at its Humble, Texas facility, to highlight progress on its U.S. manufacturing strategy, with a second module production line 
increasing nameplate capacity to 2 GW and supporting approximately 600 employees. The expanded footprint underpins ~$240MM of binding U.S. supply agreements announced on September 
28, 2026. CFO Yasunari Harada reported 1H2026 revenue of $261.0MM (+87.6% y/y), gross margin expansion to 32.5% from 16.6%, net income of $45.8MM and adjusted EBITDA of $82.3MM, 
supported by shipments of 2.61 GW of cells and ~192 MW of modules. Cash and restricted cash totaled $123.4MM as of June 30, 2026. Management outlined plans for a $357MM, 1.5 GW 
heterojunction technology (HJT) cell facility at Humble, with design work complete, permitting underway and pilot production targeted for 1Q2028, subject to financing and regulatory approvals.

internally generated cash flow, alongside equipment payment terms weighted toward the post-commissioning period. Longer-term ambitions include 3 GW of HJT cell capacity and 6 GW of 
domestic ingot and wafer production, although these expansions remain uncommitted. On trade policy, TOYO is pursuing a company-specific Section 232 onshoring agreement with the 
Department of Commerce ahead of potential December 4, 2026 import pricing and tariff measures, while U.S. Customs and Border Protection continues reviewing detained Ethiopian cell 
shipments. Separately, the Ethiopia circumvention inquiry initiated July 17, 2026 is expected to reach a preliminary determination in mid-December. Management reiterated expectations for 
positive 2H2026 EBITDA but did not provide revenue or net income guidance. Overall, the Analyst Day reinforces TOYO's U.S. vertical integration strategy, although execution of its next 
manufacturing phase remains contingent on securing financing, completing permitting and navigating ongoing trade-related uncertainty.

continues to advance its proposed 460 MW Turtle Creek natural gas project adjacent to Merom, having filed an air permit application on September 25. The development would increase the 
company's total generating capacity by more than 40%, with management citing recent contracting momentum as supportive of future offtake opportunities. Overall, the latest agreements 
reinforce Hallador's ability to capture stronger forward capacity and energy pricing while extending contracted cash flow visibility, with Turtle Creek representing incremental development upside

BWXT (NR) | BWXT’s BANR microreactor selected for up to 50 MW transportable nuclear power project in New Brunswick, Canada (10/5) 
•
BWX Technologies (BWXT) announced that its BWXT Advanced Nuclear Reactor (BANR) technology was selected by Prodigy Clean Energy and the Pabineau and Eel River Bar First Nations 
for a proposed transportable nuclear power plant (TNPP) in Belledune, New Brunswick, representing the technology's first planned commercial and international deployment. The Indigenous-
owned project would incorporate two BANR microreactors, each designed to produce 22 MW of electric output (75 MW thermal), supporting a facility targeting up to 50 MW of total generation 
capacity and commercial operations in the early 2030s. BANR utilizes high-temperature gas-cooled reactor technology and high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) in TRISO fuel form, with 
passive cooling and factory-fabricated components intended to reduce on-site construction complexity. Prodigy's transportable platform targets an installation period of approximately 18 months 
following fabrication, with the reactors and associated equipment expected to be manufactured primarily at BWXT's Ontario facilities. The Government of New Brunswick has provided a letter of 
intent to purchase electricity from the project, while BWXT and Prodigy are working toward definitive agreements covering engineering, licensing, financing, manufacturing and deployment. The 
selection follows BWXT's August 2026 award under the U.S. Army's Janus Program at Fort Campbell, Kentucky, providing another potential commercialization pathway for BANR across 
defense, industrial and remote power applications. While financial terms were not disclosed, the project expands BWXT's prospective advanced reactor deployment pipeline and supports its 
longer-term commercial nuclear growth opportunity, although definitive contracting, regulatory licensing and project financing remain key milestones.
