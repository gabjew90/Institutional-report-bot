Solid Gain in Retail Sales in September, Although 
Much of It Was Due to Higher Gas Prices 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 
Retail sales rose 0.8% in September, well above the consensus 
expectation. 
 
 
Gasoline sales rose 6.4% over the month. Retail sales excluding autos and 
gas were up 0.2%. 
 
 
Retail sales were up a strong 6.3% in September from a year earlier. Some 
of that is due to higher gas prices, but sales excluding autos and gasoline 
were up a solid 5.0%. 
 
 
Drivers for consumer spending growth are mixed. The labor market is solid 
and the stock market is elevated, but high inflation and rising interest 
rates are drags. 
 
 
PNC expects steady growth in consumer spending going forward. 
 
Details
Retail 
sales 
increased 
0.8% from 
August 
to 
September, according to the advance estimate 
from the Census Bureau. This was well above the 
consensus 
expectation 
of 
a 
0.3% 
increase.  However, much of the growth was due to 
a 6.4% increase in gasoline sales over the month as 
gas prices jumped. Sales excluding autos rose 0.9%, 
but sales excluding autos and gasoline increased 
just 0.2%. Retail sales growth in August was revised 
up to a 1.3% increase, from the initially reported 1.1% 
gain. These numbers are not adjusted for inflation. 
 
Control sales—retail sales excluding autos, gasoline, 
food service, and building materials, which go into 
nominal consumer spending in GDP—rose just 0.1% 
over the month. But that followed a big 1.1% increase 
in August. 
 
On a year-ago basis, overall retail sales were up 6.3% 
from a year earlier. Sales excluding autos were up 
7.2%, while sales excluding autos and gasoline were 
up 5.0%. Higher gas prices have been a driver of 
retail sales growth since the U.S. conflict with Iran 
started; gasoline spending was up 28% in August 
from a year earlier. But sales growth has been solid 
outside of gasoline. Some of the increase has come 
from higher prices, but inflation-adjusted consumer 
spending is also rising. Control retail sales were up 
4.6% year-over-year in September, close to the 4.5% 
pace in August.

There was a large 1.1% increase in sales of food 
services and drinking places (restaurants and bars) 
in September, following a 1.4% increase in August. 
On a year-ago basis restaurant and bar sales were 
up 7.0% in September. Since restaurant sales are 
largely discretionary, strong growth in this segment 
is further evidence that consumers are in decent 
shape. Surveys of consumers clearly indicate that 
they are unhappy. The University of Michigan 
consumer sentiment index was at its second-lowest 
level ever in October, with the data series going 
back to the 1950s. Consumer confidence from the 
Conference Board has held up better but dropped 
sharply in September to its lowest level since 2014. 
But even though consumer survey measures are 
historically soft, households continue to boost their 
spending. 
 
The drivers for consumer spending going forward are 
mixed. The labor market is solid. Job growth has 
slowed from 2023 but is up from last year, and the 
unemployment rate is up from a few years ago but 
remains historically low. Wage growth is slowing. 
Inflation remains high, and higher gasoline prices 
since the spring are a particular drag. Higher 
interest rates are weighing on purchases of big-
ticket items. But household wealth continues to rise 
thanks to the very strong stock market of the past 
year and, to a lesser extent, house price gains. This 
rising wealth is particularly important for spending 
by high-income households. Another positive for 
consumer spending is the recent upward revision to 
household income growth over the past few years 
from the Bureau of Economic Analysis; this shows 
households in significantly better financial shape 
than previously thought. PNC data also shows 
continued growth in household spending.  
 
Household spending should continue to increase in 
the near term at a steady pace as the labor market 
remains solid. Risks are to the downside, however. 
Higher interest rates could become more of a drag. 
There is also the potential for a stock market 
correction, tied to tech stocks, that would weigh on 
purchases by higher-income households. 
 
Sales growth was mixed across segments in 
September. Building materials sales fell 0.6% 
because of the soft housing market, although sales 
of furniture and home furnishings were up 1.3%, and 
sales of electronics and appliances rose 0.6%. 
Clothing sales were flat over the month, while sales 
at general merchandise stores fell 0.1%. Sales at 
nonstore retailers, primarily online, rose 0.3% in 
September. Sales of motor vehicles and parts rose 
0.2% in September.
