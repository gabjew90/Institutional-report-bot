Uranium Market Update
Customer-backed US uprates (c.1.1GW) strengthen future nuclear demand,
while Namibia’s production rebound restores near-term supply. Term
awards remain 34% below historical levels despite firm US$96/lb pricing.
Higher discount rates and slow contracting continue to weigh on long-
duration equities, with developers leading the decline. New utility awards
and stabilising yields would strengthen the recovery case. We prefer NXG
and PDN.
Demand and policy | Contracted uprates move ahead of fuel awards. Google and Amazon
signed 20-year agreements backing 890MW across 11 US units and 190MW at Calvert Cliffs
(c.1.1GW combined), with delivery expected in 2028-32. Google's programme includes more
than US$4.3bn of investment, while its separate 2.7GW supply agreement adds no capacity.
DOE conditionally committed US$4.2bn to Vistra's previously announced 433MW Meta-
backed uprates, with full output targeted by 2034. Oracle proposed taking Point Beach output,
subject to regulatory approval. Rooppur Unit 2 (c.1.2GW) received its first fuel consignment and
Unit 1 cleared a commissioning valve test. A US$120bn US-South Korea framework outlined
six AP1000 and two APR1400 reactors, with sites and project schedules unresolved. Holtec
advanced applications for two Palisades SMRs (0.6GW). Poland moved first-plant start-up to
2036. No new uranium purchase was disclosed.
Supply | Production recovery remains concentrated in Namibia. Namibian July output
rebounded 81% MoM to 1.94Mlb, up 18% YoY, recovering c.86% of June's loss following Husab
maintenance. Output remained 7% below May. Sustaining July's rate would imply c.5.8Mlb in
3Q26, against 5.0Mlb in 2Q26 and 5.4Mlb a year earlier. Russia's coverage-adjusted production
proxy rose 5% MoM to c.0.58Mlb U3O8-equivalent, still 6% below the prior-year estimate.
Namibia accounted for almost all the combined c.0.9Mlb monthly increase. The rebound
supports a 3Q recovery without establishing a sustained annual run-rate.
Contracting and fuel cycle | Awards lag demand visibility. Reported uranium volume reached
81.9Mlb through the past week, comprising 39.7Mlb spot and 42.2Mlb term. Combined and
term volumes were 28% and 34% below their respective 10-year same-week averages. No
new utility term award was recorded in weeks 37-40. Utilities are evaluating 2027-35 offers,
including up to 1.3Mlb for 2028-32 and c.0.5Mlbpa for 2027-31, with three RFIs active.
Historical remaining-year activity would imply c.125Mlb combined and c.70Mlb term, below
replacement proxies of 150Mlb and 145Mlb. September spot volume was just 1.4Mlb across
19 deals, its lowest since December 2009. Long-term uranium held at US$96/lb versus US
$89.50/lb spot and US$111/lb five-year forward. Conversion term prices held at US$53/kgU in
North America and US$52/kgU in Europe. Spot enrichment remained at US$215/SWU, up 13%
YoY, with a 7.2m SWU tender for 2028-39 awaiting offers. Firm prices have yet to translate
into sustained awards.
Equities | Long-duration under pressure. North American and ASX developers fell 16.9% and
10.6% over the past fortnight, against producers down 4.3% and broadly unchanged spot
uranium. Higher discount rates and slow contracting remain headwinds despite improving
demand visibility. SPUT's holdings remained at 81.7Mlb through 5 October, with an 11.5%
NAV discount constraining its physical bid. New utility awards and stabilising yields would
strengthen the recovery case. We continue to prefer NXG and PDN.

Uranium prices hold firm as fuel-cycle costs diverge
 
Elevated pricing within a narrow trading range. Uranium spot ended September at US$89.50/lb,
almost unchanged MoM and 9% higher YoY. The 2026 YTD average of US$86.57/lb is 19% above
the 2025 average of US$72.63/lb, while the inflation-adjusted September price exceeds 85% of
month-end observations since 1969. The YTD trading range of US$82.00-93.75/lb represents c.14%
of the average price, against a 30% long-run average. Prices have therefore remained elevated
without the volatility associated with earlier uranium cycles.
September’s 1.4Mlb of spot transactions, the lowest monthly volume since December 2009,
shows that this stability has occurred alongside subdued buying. Firm pricing on low turnover
is consistent with limited seller concessions, although it provides less evidence of depth than
sustained transactions at the same level. The physical market has retained its price gains while
awaiting a stronger procurement cycle.

Recovery holds as annual momentum moderates. Spot remains 34% below its June 2007 nominal
peak of US$136/lb, compared with a 40% drawdown a year earlier. 10 of the past 12 month-
end observations were higher YoY, although September’s 9% annual increase was below August’s
21%. The cumulative recovery remains intact, with the latest phase characterised by consolidation
around US$90/lb.
The smaller drawdown and slower annual growth describe different aspects of the cycle. Prices
have recovered relative to their earlier lows, while the rate of appreciation has moderated. A further
advance would require stronger buying or reduced available supply, with the 2007 peak providing
historical context rather than a project incentive-price benchmark.

Future coverage retains a premium. Long-term uranium carried a US$6.50/lb premium to
September spot, compared with US$6.40/lb in August and parity a year earlier. The dollar premium
is close to its US$6.60/lb historical average, while the 1.07x long-term-to-spot ratio remains below
its 1.20x period average. Higher absolute uranium prices make a similar dollar spread smaller in
proportional terms.
Three- and five-year indicators of US$104/lb and US$111/lb were unchanged MoM, respectively
16% and 24% above spot. The curve supports a higher cost of longer-dated coverage without
establishing an immediate prompt shortage. Its significance for producers increases as utilities
convert offers into delivery commitments at those levels.

Conversion retreats from its prompt-market peak. North American spot conversion fell US$2/kgU
in September to US$60/kgU, down 5% YoY and 38% below its December 2024 peak of US$97/
kgU. European spot also stood at US$60/kgU. Long-term prices remained at US$53/kgU in North
America and US$52/kgU in Europe, preserving prompt premiums of US$7-8/kgU.
Long-term-to-spot ratios of 0.88x and 0.87x show the earlier inversion narrowing. Softer conversion
reduces current fuel-input costs, while stable term prices leave future coverage broadly unchanged.
Conversion is consequently providing a partial offset to firm uranium and enrichment prices.

Enrichment retains record pricing. Spot enrichment held at US$215/SWU in September, up 13%
YoY and 6.3x its July 2018 trough. Long-term enrichment remained at US$181/SWU, up 6% YoY.
The 19% prompt premium and 0.84x long-term-to-spot ratio, against a 1.05x period average, make
enrichment the strongest prompt-service pricing signal in the fuel cycle.
The tender for up to 7.2m SWU across 2028-39 provides a substantial longer-dated procurement
requirement, with offers due on 13 October. An eventual award would add evidence on the price
and availability of future service coverage. The current tender establishes demand interest, with
supplier selection and contracted volumes still outstanding.

UF6 trades close to component value. North American UF6 ended September at US$293.75/kgU,
almost unchanged MoM and 6% higher YoY. Its calculated uranium-plus-conversion value was US
$293.85/kgU, leaving a discount of just US$0.10/kgU against US$2.11/kgU in August. The market
price is now closely aligned with its underlying inputs.
The narrowing discount removes most of the earlier component-value advantage from buying
UF6. Uranium and conversion account for almost all the quoted value, with little independent UF6
premium. Changes in the component spread provide a cleaner measure of UF6-specific pricing
than the absolute price alone.

Higher feed and SWU costs lift derived enriched-product costs. Derived spot-basis enriched
uranium product cost reached US$4,186/kgU in September, up 8% YoY, while the term-
basis calculation reached US$4,045/kgU. Uranium and enrichment strength outweighed softer
conversion, extending the increase in aggregate fuel-input costs.
The calculation uses consistent feed and SWU requirements across the price series, allowing
the contribution of each stage to be compared through time. It excludes fabrication and other
procurement costs and should be read as a fuel-input build-up. Differences from separately quoted
EUP indicators also reflect assay and calculation assumptions.

Uranium remains the largest component as enrichment’s share rises. Uranium feed contributed
US$2,156/kgU to September’s US$4,186/kgU spot-basis EUP cost, followed by enrichment at
US$1,477/kgU and conversion at US$553/kgU. Their rounded shares were 51%, 35% and 13%,
respectively. Enrichment’s share increased from 34% a year earlier as SWU prices outpaced
uranium.
Feed remains the largest cost exposure, while enrichment accounts for more than one-third of
the total. The composition leaves utilities exposed to both natural-uranium and SWU pricing, with
conversion’s decline providing only a partial offset. Uranium-price performance alone therefore
understates the breadth of fuel-cost pressure.

Conversion’s relative contribution narrows. The UF6-to-uranium-content ratio declined to 1.26x
from 1.29x a year earlier, remaining above its 1.16x historical average. The EUP-to-UF6-feed ratio
increased to 1.55x from 1.51x, although it remained below its 2.07x period average.
Conversion’s contribution relative to feed has narrowed, while enrichment’s contribution has

larger share of the current cost base. Record absolute SWU pricing can therefore coexist with a
below-average enrichment-to-feed ratio.

Fuel costs remain near highs despite lower volatility. Uranium’s rolling 12-month annualised
volatility was 16%, below its 20% long-run average, compared with 10% for conversion and 8% for
SWU. The calmer price environment has not returned fuel costs to earlier levels. September uranium

Downstream fuel costs are consequently closer to their peaks than uranium alone. Lower
volatility improves the stability of quoted inputs, while the elevated cost base remains relevant to
procurement. Stable prices and inexpensive fuel are separate conditions.

Reactor demand advances through delivery and fleet retention
 
 
Outages temper capacity-led generation growth. Tracked nuclear generation reached 186.9TWh
in May, up 1% YoY, while LTM output of 2,326TWh was 1% below the preceding period. US
generation rose 5% to 65.0TWh, France increased 13% to 28.8TWh and China grew 5% to 38.2TWh,
offset by weaker South Korea (-10%), Sweden (-26%) and Canada (-17%). Subsequent data
show continued availability constraints. China’s H1 generation fell 2.8% despite capacity growth,
reflecting longer Taishan refuelling outages and heavier Fuqing overhauls. France’s August output
declined 8.4% amid heat, low river flows and outages, although YTD generation remained 2.5%
higher. UK outages and Belgian life-extension works added to European weakness, leaving stronger
output from the largest fleets yet to establish a sustained aggregate recovery.
Generation-derived reactor requirements were 44.2Mlb in 1Q26, down 4% YoY. The 27.1Mlb
recorded for 2Q26 excludes June and cannot be compared directly with complete quarterly
production or interpreted as a quarterly demand decline. Generation provides an indirect
measure of fuel consumption, with procurement and reloads following different schedules. New
connections and life extensions strengthen longer-term requirements, while outage recovery
and commissioning ramps determine how quickly installed capacity translates into additional
generation.

Primary supply recovery remains dependent on delivery
 
 
Primary supply approaches balance but remains concentrated. Tracked production, covering
more than 90% of primary supply, fell from 42.9Mlb in 1Q26 to 41.4Mlb in 2Q26 and was broadly
flat YoY. The apparent surplus against 27.1Mlb of implied demand reflects incomplete June
generation data. On a comparable LTM basis to 1Q26, supply of 171.8Mlb covered c.99% of implied
requirements of 173.7Mlb. The longer-run production recovery has brought primary supply close to
balance, although recent momentum has slowed. Disruptions must consequently be absorbed by
secondary supply, inventories or changes in enrichment feed requirements. Contractual allocation
can also leave discretionary availability tighter than the aggregate balance implies, supporting term-
price resilience.
Kazakhstan supplied 18.7Mlb, or 45% of tracked output, while Canada declined to 6.2Mlb from
9.9Mlb and Namibia to 5.0Mlb from 6.8Mlb. Together, the three countries accounted for 72% of
production. Budenovskoe’s increase to 1.95Mlb from 0.82Mlb a year earlier represented c.99% of
Kazakhstan’s YoY growth, lifting the KAP-Uranium One JV group to a series-high 7.3Mlb. Growth
remains concentrated in a small number of assets, with Russian- and Chinese-linked offtake limiting
the translation into discretionary Western availability. Cameco’s Canadian operations produced
6.2Mlb on a 100% basis, with lower Cigar Lake and McArthur River output increasing reliance on
second-half delivery. Execution at these large assets remains more consequential to the near-term
balance than progress across smaller restarts.

Canadian additions drive the early-2030s buffer. Mine supply rises from 172.7Mlb in 2026 to

SPUT’s discount limits the marginal physical bid
 
 
The NAV discount interrupts capital formation. SPUT traded at a discount on 236 of 261
observations in the latest 12-month window. Its average discount was 4.3%, compared with 6.2%
in the preceding year, but the recent three-month average widened to 8.9%. The last premium
observation was on 30 April.
The trust raised US$883m across 66 days in the daily window, compared with US$612m previously,
with issuance concentrated around stronger NAV-relative pricing. No raise was recorded after 12
May. The cumulative funding total therefore overstates current momentum, with the persistent
discount constraining the replenishment of purchasing capacity.
