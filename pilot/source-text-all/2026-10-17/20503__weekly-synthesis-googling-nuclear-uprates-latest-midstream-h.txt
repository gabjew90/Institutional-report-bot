Bottom Line:
Here is our latest “The Weekly Synthesis” report where we highlight the news/events
from the past week that we think are important for our coverage and the broader Energy
Transition and Infrastructure sector. In addition, we highlight what pieces of data, issues,
and events investors have been most focused on. This week we highlight GOOGL 
Committing to nuclear uprates from CEG; BE Project Tembo and Jupiter Updates, 
Wärtsilä latest DC order takes it to over 3 GW in supply to data centers. ET acquisition
of Permian (Delaware Basin) acquisition and CVX and HESM restructure their Bakken
commercial agreements. We update our short interest data with ENPH and FLNC 
short interest climbing from already high levels.
Key Points
Thakkar-ed Out? All You Need to Know From Last Week
1.) Googling Nuclear Uprates. Google’s agreement with CEG supports 890 MW of nuclear
uprates across 11 existing units—incremental supply, not simply a reshuffling of existing 
generation. We see broader read-through to nuclear modernization, life extensions, and 
equipment/services demand, including potential opportunities for GEV, as hyperscalers 
increasingly fund dependable carbon-free capacity rather than wait solely for new builds.
2.) Project Tembo Moves From Concept to Contract. BKH’s definitive Google agreement
formalizes a hybrid power model combining 564 MW of grid-tied generation with a 2.1 
GW microgrid, including nearly 900 MW of BE fuel cells and 1,150 MW of Mitsubishi gas 
turbines. For Bloom, the agreement marks another step forward on a closely watched 
deployment that was already in most estimates. 3.) Project Jupiter Air Permit Process 
and Trucking Gas? A newly appointed judge should help restart the air-permitting process
and, we believe, would be helpful in ensuring project timing. Separately reports emerged
that ORCL is considering trucked natural gas underscore the urgency: supplying up to 400
MMcf/d by truck looks extraordinary and unlikely to be sustainable, but exploring interim
options suggests some Bloom fuel-cell infrastructure may be approaching energization.
4.) Engines Keep Winning Data-Center Orders. Wärtsilä’s latest 282 MW order brings 
cumulative U.S. data-center capacity sold above 3 GW across seven projects. Delivery in 
2028 and operations in 2029 reinforce demand for alternatives to large-frame turbines; 
importantly, the order was booked in 3Q26, not incremental to future bookings.
5.) ET Adds Captive Permian Volumes. The $2.63B Vaquero acquisition adds 675 MMcf/d
of Delaware Basin processing capacity, expandable to 1.2 Bcf/d. Consideration comprises 
$1.95B in cash and 33.3MM ET units. Existing connections to ET’s downstream gas and 
NGL infrastructure strengthen the strategic fit and opportunity to capture more volumes 
across its integrated system; disclosed financial details remain limited. Recent Permian 
G&P deals have generally traded at 7–8x forward EBITDA before synergies; we suspect 
this transaction sits toward the upper end, although no acquisition multiple was disclosed.
6.) Bakken Tariffs Get Marked to Market. CVX/HESM’s restructuring trades lower tariffs
for longer contracts, cutting Chevron’s midstream costs by approximately 50% through 
2033. Producers remain focused on costs and cash flow—not volume growth—and greater
scrutiny of mature-basin G&P tariff durability.

Here is our latest “The Weekly Synthesis” report where we highlight the news/events from the past 
week that we think are important for our coverage and the broader Energy Transition and Infrastructure 
sector. In addition, we highlight what pieces of data, issues, and events investors have been most 
focused on. This week we highlight GOOGL Committing to nuclear uprates from CEG; BE Project 
Tembo and Jupiter Updates, Wärtsilä latest DC order takes it to over 3 GW in supply to data 
centers. ET acquisition of Permian (Delaware Basin) and CVX and HESM restructure their Bakken 
commercial agreements. We update our short interest data with ENPH and FLNC short interest 
climbing from already high levels.
Thakkar-ed Out? All You Need to Know From Last Week
1.) Google/CEG Deal Highlights Growing Value of Nuclear Uprates for AI Power Demand. Alphabet 
(GOOGL, Outperform, $351.66, covered by BMO analyst Brian Pitz) signed agreements with Constellation 
Energy (CEG-NYSE; $298.07; Outperform; covered by BMO analyst James Thalacker) supporting 
approximately 890 MW of nuclear uprates across 11 existing nuclear units, alongside a separate long-
term power supply agreement in PJM. We view the announcement as further evidence that hyperscalers 
are increasingly willing to support investments that increase output from the existing nuclear fleet 
rather than waiting solely for new-build generation. Beyond the direct implications for Constellation 
Energy, the transaction may strengthen the investment case for nuclear life-extension, uprate, 
and modernization projects across the broader U.S. fleet, creating potential opportunities for 
incumbent nuclear equipment and services providers such as GEV, as well as other suppliers 
exposed to reactor maintenance, fuel, engineering, and balance-of-plant upgrades. More 
broadly, the deal reinforces our view that accelerating AI-driven load growth is increasing the value of 
dependable carbon-free baseload generation and may support additional contracting activity tied to 
existing nuclear assets.
2.) BKH/GOOGL Execute Data Center Agreement at Project Tembo. Black Hills' (BKH-NYSE; $76.08; 
Outperform; covered by BMO analyst James Thalacker) definitive agreement announced this week 
with GOOGL provides another sign of progress on one of the more closely watched data center projects 
utilizing fuel cells (see our Utilities Team's note here). The announcement from Tuesday formalizes the 
project's contractual structure, which will combine 564 MWs of grid tied power and 2.1 GWs of islanded 
generation capacity. Recall, the 2.1 GW microgrid comprises of nearly 900 MWs of BE fuel cells and 
1,150 MWs of Mitsubishi's J-class gas turbines. The project pairs utility-owned generation with privately-
managed dedicated generation resources which BKH will collect microgrid management fees on.
3.) Project Jupiter in New Mexico Updates; Trucking Gas a Possibility? Earlier this month, a new 
judge was appointed, retired Federal Judge James Browning, to oversee the air permitting process 
for the fuel cell infrastructure at Project Jupiter which should help the process get back on track.
As a reminder, in August the New Mexico Supreme Court granted requests for a stay related to the air 
quality permit application for the fuel cells but lifted the stay on September 18. The initial public hearing 
regarding the air permit was postponed and has not yet been rescheduled. As we have previously 
highlighted, Project Jupiter has faced fuel procurement challenges due to delays to the Green Chile 
pipeline. That pipeline has since been rerouted to avoid state lands with the most recent target ISD for 
Green Chile now during 1Q 2027. That said, reports this week suggested ORCL ($141.40; Outperform; 
covered by BMO analyst Keith Bachman) is evaluating the use of trucked natural gas at the New 
Mexico site as an interim solution. ORCL has reportedly used trucked compressed natural gas during 
the initial start up phases for its data centers in UT and TX. Trucking up to 400 MMCf/d of natural gas is a 
fairly extraordinary solution in our view and is unlikely to represent a viable long-term answer; however, 
the willingness to explore such measures highlights the urgency around securing fuel supply and the

the fuel cell infrastructure may be near ready to be energized.
4.) Wärtsilä Discloses Another Large U.S. Data-Center Power Order. Wärtsilä (WRTBY US-ADR; 
Not Rated) announced a 282 MW onsite power order for a U.S. data-center project utilizing 15 50SG 
gas engines. The order marks the company's seventh U.S. data-center project and brings 
cumulative U.S. data-center capacity sold to more than 3 GW. Delivery is scheduled for 2028 with 
operations expected in 2029. While neither the customer nor project location was disclosed, we view 
the announcement as further evidence that engine-based generation is emerging as an increasingly 
important solution for hyperscale and AI-related power demand, complementing large-frame gas 
turbines and fuel-cell deployments. The order was booked in 3Q26 and should not be viewed as 
incremental to future backlog disclosures.
5.) ET Acquires Permian Delaware G&P Platform. On Tuesday, Energy Transfer (ET-NYSE, Not Rated) 
announced the acquisition of Vaquero Midstream for $2.63 billion, including $1.95 billion of cash and 
33.3MM ET units. The Vaquero platform adds a Delaware Basin G&P platform that consists of 
100,000 dedicated acres, 300 miles of pipeline infrastructure and the 675 MMCf/d Caymus 
Processing Complex with expansion potential up to 1.2 bcf/d. Strategically, the acquisition appears 
highly complementary to ET's broader NGL franchise. The Vaquero assets are already connected to ET's 
downstream natural gas and NGL infrastructure, providing another source of captive volumes onto a 
system that has historically relied on a meaningful amount of third-party supply. Financial details were 
limited, but recent Permian G&P platforms have generally transacted in the 7-8x FY1 EBITDA, excluding 
synergies, and we suspect this most recent deal was struck towards the upper end of that range on 
acquired EBITDA.
6.) CVX/HESM Restructure Bakken Commercial Framework. Chevron (CVX-NYSE; $211.98; Outperform; 
covered by BMO analyst Phillip Jungwirth) and Hess Midstream (HESM-NYSE, Not Rated) announced 
a series of agreements this week that will see CVX transfer its ownership stake in HESM along with 
certain DJ Basin midstream assets in exchange for revised commercial terms that are expected to reduce 
its Bakken midstream costs by ~50% through 2033. See our Energy Team's note here. For midstream 
operators in the Bakken, the transaction we think increases focus on the sustainability of G&P tariffs as 
producers are focused on optimizing costs and improving free cash flow in a mature basin where volume 
growth is not the primary objective. Notably, HESM's updated framework contemplates lower tariffs 
alongside longer-duration contracts suggesting producers may be willing to trade length and volume 
visibility for improved economics.
What We Heard Last Week
We have done quite a bit of marketing the past few weeks on both the West Coast and Canada. Most 
common areas of discussion and what stood out is as follows:
1.) BTM generation demand durability. Most asked about topic. Yes, some investors expressed some 
concern about rate of growth in backlogs for FTM power infrastructure (i.e., peak CCGT orders) but more 
interest in BYOG related equipment providers. Our view has evolved over the course of 2026. The political 
blowback towards data center development has intensified beyond our initial expectations. That said, 
while it's a headwind towards all new large load centers we think in this environment focusing on 
BTM and local approvals, while avoiding larger state PUC related approvals, is a preferred approach and 
strengthens durability for BTM solutions.
2.) Canadian investors remain interested in new nuclear development. Recent postponement of 
nuclear IPO but recent award of a construction permit to TVA to commence SMR development at its Clinch 
River, TN site using GEV BWRX-300 SMR reactor design and GOOGL/CEG transaction likely spurred some of 
the interest.
3.) Generalists interest in gas levered midstream companies growing. WMB remains our top pick 
midstream pick, but we sense some more bullish sentiment on KMI than we expected, as expectations

Updated Short Interest Data
Short-interest data through the end of September have been updated in the chart below for each of our 
primary subsectors. Our summary is as follows:
•
Inverters: Short interest expanded for ENPH and contracted for SEDG in the time between the 
last short interest data update and the most recent data. ENPH short interest was up 332 bps and 
SEDG short interest fell 87 bps. Short interest for ENPH is now at 22.0% of float, while SEDG SI 
now sits at 18.8%.
•
Residential Solar: Short interest for RUN is down 104 bps compared to the last update from mid-
September. RUN short interest now sits at 30.4% of float.
•
Fuel Cells: Short interest in BE is up 71 bps since the last update and sits around 7.6% of float. 
Short interest in PLUG fell 20 bps from the last update and sits at 21.3% of float. BLDP short 
interest is down 169 bps, now at 6.6% of float.
•
Storage: Short interest in FLNC as a percentage of float is up 668 bps since the last update. 
STEM SI is down 36 bps. Short interest for FLNC and STEM now stand at 38.0% and 12.5% of float, 
respectively.
•
Utility-Scale Solar: Short interest in FSLR expanded 57 bps compared to data from mid-
September. NXT short interest expanded 63 bps while ARRY short interest expanded by 189 bps; 
short interest now sits at 11.0%, 6.5%, and 25.1%, respectively.
•
GEV: Short interest in GEV is down 52 bps and sits at 2.8% of float.
•
PWR: Short interest in PWR is mostly flat from the last update and now sits at 2.2% of float.
•
U.S. Midstream: Short interest increased for both WMB and LNG, up 26 bps and 41 bps 
respectively. SI contracted modestly for TRGP and KMI, down 16 bps and 2 bps, respectively, from 
the last update in mid-September. Short interest for WMB, KMI, LNG, and TRGP sits at 2.2%, 2.4%, 
2.3%, and 2.5%, respectively.

Despite continued growth in U.S. power demand (see our latest Taking the Temperature on Power 
Demand report here), gas burn from the power sector remains largely flat to modestly down on an 
absolute basis. See our recent gas demand report here. The step-change in U.S. solar generation 
year over year provides a clear offset to incremental power burn, effectively displacing gas-fired 
generation at the margin. However, a meaningful gas generation buildout is now under way to support 
reindustrialization, broader electrification trends, and data center load growth. As utilities begin to file 
IRPs for the 2027+ planning horizon, we expect to see this dynamic shift the generation mix back toward 
gas and expect new gas-fired generation impacts to begin overtaking headwinds from solar and drive 
the need for more gas infrastructure.
