Modest Growth in September According to Beige Book 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Headlines 
 The economy expanded modestly entering the fall, according to the 
Beige Book. Consumer spending growth was slight at best. 
 
 Employment was ﬂat or up slightly across districts. 
 
 Price pressures remained elevated. 
 
Details
According to the latest Beige Book from the Federal 
Reserve, the U.S. economy expanded in September 
and early October, although conditions varied 
across districts. Consumer spending was flat to up 
slightly over the period, with gains coming from 
high-income 
households. 
Tourism 
spending 
continued to expand. Manufacturing growth was 
modestly positive on net. The outlook was for 
modest 
near-term 
growth, 
weighted 
toward 
manufacturing, but uncertainty was expected to be 
a drag. 
 
Employment was flat or increased slightly in most 
districts. Labor supply remained adequate, although 
there continued to be shortages in some skilled 
positions. Five districts reported that artificial 
intelligence was weighing on demand for labor. On 
average, wages were up slightly entering the fall.  
 
According to the Beige Book, price growth was 
moderate to robust, with a few districts reporting 
inflationary pressures from higher input costs. 
Higher oil prices were a factor across all districts, 
with price pressures from other commodities as well. 
Higher diesel prices were pushing up costs 
throughout the supply chain. Consumer firms noted 
customer resistance to price hikes and pressure on 
margins. Respondents expected further inflationary 
pressures in the near term. 
 
The 
Richmond 
and 
Dallas 
districts 
reported 
moderate growth. There was modest growth in the 
Cleveland, Atlanta, and St. Louis districts. Growth 
was slight in the Boston, New York, Philadelphia, and 
Minneapolis districts. The economy was flat in the 
remaining districts: Chicago, Kansas City, and San 
Francisco. 
 
The latest Beige Book is consistent with continued 
economic expansion entering the fall, but with 
mixed conditions across the country. Consumer 
spending growth was modest on average, with 
high-income households leading. Consumers were 
pushing 
back 
against 
price 
increases.
