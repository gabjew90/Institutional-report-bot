earnings growth and improving opportunities

in these sectors. Financials are supported by a more fa-

and improving capital markets activity. Health Care com-

growth drivers and attractive valuations. Communication 
Services remains an attractive way to participate in AI-re-

– 
Information Technology (maintain neutral): Technology re-
mains the strongest sector on our scorecard, supported by 
positive economic, fundamental and behavioural signals.

Consumer Staples continue to face pressure from weak

the weaker areas of our sector framework. 
– 
Market leadership remains concentrated: Equity indices 
remain near record highs despite relatively narrow partici-
pation. While only a limited number of companies have 
driven a large portion of recent market gains, historical ev-
idence suggests that market breadth alone has not been a 
consistent indicator of future market performance. A 
broadening of participation could provide additional sup-

Global sector strategy update 
Our bi-monthly global sector strategy integrates macroeco-
nomic insights with structural and valuation analyses across 
the 11 GICS sectors. We begin with top-down macro convic-
tions—covering growth trends, inflation dynamics, monetary 
policy, currency direction, and commodity markets—and 
translate them into sector-level implications. By combining cy-
clical sensitivity, structural growth drivers, and earnings mo-
mentum, we aim to highlight where risk-reward is most com-
pelling, where expectations are stretched, and where defen-
sive or selective positioning is prudent. 
 
Earnings growth remains concentrated 
 
Consensus expectations point to approximately 27% year-on-
year earnings growth for the S&P 500 during the third quarter. 
While this represents a moderation from the previous quarter, 
it remains one of the strongest starting points for an earnings 
season in recent years. 
Earnings revisions have surprised positively, with forecasts in-
creasing during the quarter despite the historical tendency for 
analysts to lower estimates ahead of reporting periods. Never-
theless, earnings growth remains concentrated. 
Technology and Energy are expected to contribute most of 
the aggregate earnings growth, while AI-related businesses 
continue to represent an important driver of overall profit ex-
pansion. 
Consumer-oriented sectors remain comparatively weaker, 
with limited earnings growth expected. 
 
Higher yields create winners and losers 
 
The rise in government bond yields remains an important 
driver of sector performance. 
Historically, sectors often viewed as bond proxies, such as 
Utilities, Real Estate and Consumer Staples, have tended to 
be more challenged when bond yields rise, as their dividend 
income becomes less attractive relative to risk-free alterna-
tives. Real Estate may also face pressure from higher financ-
ing costs. 
 
Financials are generally among the sectors that can benefit 
from higher yields, as banks may earn wider lending margins 
and insurers can reinvest assets at higher rates. However, the 
shape of the yield curve remains equally important. The sec-
tor underperformed during September as short-term rates 
rose faster than long-term rates following the Fed's rate hike,

resulting in a temporary yield-curve flattening and increased 
pressure on bank profitability. Since then, the yield curve has 
steepened again as markets reduced expectations for further 
policy tightening, easing one of the key headwinds. Upcoming 
earnings results should provide further insight into whether 
credit quality and loan growth remain as resilient as recent 
trends suggest. 
 
Information Technology has also demonstrated notable resili-
ence despite higher yields. Traditionally, growth stocks can be 
sensitive to rising discount rates given that a larger share of 
their value is derived from future earnings. However, the larg-
est technology companies have continued to benefit from ex-
ceptional earnings growth, strong cash generation and robust 
balance sheets. As a result, sector performance has remained 
strong even amid rising yields, highlighting the importance of 
company fundamentals and earnings momentum in today's 
market environment. 
 
Valuations have become more attractive 
 
An encouraging development in recent months has been the 
broad valuation adjustment across equity markets. 
Despite continued earnings growth, higher bond yields have 
driven multiple compression across most sectors since mid-
summer. Technology has experienced some of the largest 
valuation adjustments and now trades closer to historical av-
erages despite remaining among the strongest sectors from 
an earnings perspective. 
As a result, the broader MSCI World Index now trades near its 
long-term average valuation, creating a more balanced back-
drop than earlier in the year. 
 
Positioning 
 
Within our sector framework, Financials, Health Care and 
Communication Services remain the most attractive sectors 
on a relative basis. 
Financials are supported by improving fundamentals and at-
tractive valuations. Communication Services continues to pro-
vide exposure to companies that may benefit from ongoing AI 
adoption through multiple revenue streams, including cloud 
computing, digital advertising and applications. Health Care 
offers a combination of defensive earnings characteristics, in-
novation-led growth opportunities and comparatively attractive 
valuations. 
 
We retain a neutral stance on Information Technology, pri-
marily reflecting diversification and risk-management consid-
erations rather than concerns about sector fundamentals. 
Technology continues to rank as the strongest sector within 
our framework and has proven resilient despite the recent in-
crease in bond yields. Nevertheless, given its significant index 
weighting and investors' existing exposure to AI-related 
themes, we believe a balanced allocation remains appropri-
ate. We continue to see attractive opportunities within se-
lected areas of AI infrastructure and related technologies. 
 
Our least preferred sectors remain Consumer Staples and 
Real Estate, where structural challenges and interest-rate 
sensitivity continue to weigh on the outlook.
