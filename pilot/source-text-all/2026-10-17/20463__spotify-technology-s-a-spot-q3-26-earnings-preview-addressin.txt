Evolution of Spotify’s Monetization Strategy 
In the sections below, we examine how the industry’s monetization framework has 
evolved, the role of recent pricing actions, and why we believe Spotify’s long-term ARPU 
opportunity increasingly extends beyond the core Premium subscription. We believe the 
evolution of Spotify’s pricing strategy can be viewed through three distinct chapters: 1) 
a prolonged period where music streaming remained relatively under-monetized 
compared to other forms of digital entertainment; 2) Spotify’s transition toward more 
frequent subscription price increases; and 3) Spotify’s emerging strategy of 
supplementing traditional pricing actions with a growing ecosystem of premium 
add-ons. 
How has the music industry’s monetization compared to other forms of 
entertainment? 
Over the past decade, subscription video streaming services have regularly raised prices 
alongside expanding content offerings, broader functionality, and deeper engagement. 
By comparison, music streaming subscription pricing remained largely unchanged for 
over a decade despite platforms evolving well beyond on-demand music consumption. 
In Spotify’s case, its product has expanded from a music library into a broader 
engagement platform spanning music, podcasts, video, audiobooks, and AI-powered 
product offerings. 
 
DSPs become more proactive on pricing, led by Spotify 
After more than a decade of largely stable pricing across premium music subscriptions, 
DSPs have become increasingly proactive in capturing the growing value delivered 
through their platforms. Spotify has been at the forefront of this transition, 
implementing multiple rounds of price increases across key markets since 2023, 
including its most recent increase in the U.S. and select European markets in January 
2026, after raising prices in both June 2024 and June 2023 (see Exhibit 3). We note that 
this cadence is on the faster end of our previously expected cadence of price increases 
every ~1-2 years (link). Notably, SPOT’s management has consistently highlighted limited 
churn despite multiple pricing actions, which we believe reinforces the view that music 
streaming remains an attractive and relatively under-monetized form of entertainment.

continue to be the US, India and Brazil. During Q3’26, we observed roughly stable share

We include HundredX data to track Net Purchase Intent, Net Promoter Score, and Net 
Positive Percent as an indicator of future demand and consumer sentiment around 
music products/services. In Q3’26, Spotify Net Purchase Intent ticked up slightly, and 
remains positive above music streaming service peers. Spotify consistently remains 
above most peer music services on consumer sentiment on sound quality and features, 
though consistently underperforms peers on brand values/trust and price. 
HundredX Terms Glossary 
We note that Net Purchase Intent (NPI) is calculated by taking the percent of 
respondents who expect to use more of a product minus the percent of respondents 
who expect to use less over the next 12 months. A positive number indicates industry or 
company/brand growth; a negative number indicates industry or company/brand 
decline. Net Purchase Intent (NPI) can be used as an indication of future demand. 
Net Promoter Score (NPS) represents a widely used measurement that asks 
respondents, “would you recommend this product or service to a friend” on a scale of 
0-10. The responses allow individual customers to be sorted into promoters (answered 9 
or 10), passives (7 or 8), and detractors (0 to 6). An NPS score is calculated by 
subtracting the percentage of detractor responses from the percentage or promoter 
responses, where a higher score generally reflects higher customer loyalty. 
Net Positive Percent (NPP) calculation is taken by subtracting the percentage of 
customers who view a factor (e.g., price, sound quality) as negative from the percentage 
of customers who view the same factor as positive. HundredX has 18 non-exclusive 
drivers that respondents can select as good, not so good, or skip. Drivers are consistent 
across companies within a single industry to allow for direct peer comparison.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
