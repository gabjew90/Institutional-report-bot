EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Tokenization expands into market infrastructure
Market share of tokenized assets, Sep 14 2026
Sources: Deutsche Bank Research, RWA.xyz. Data as of Sep 14, 2026. “Distributed” denotes tokens 
transferable outside the issuing platform and between wallets; it does not measure trading activity. Note: 
Credit combines asset-backed credit, corporate credit, diversified credit and specialty finance. Other 
assets comprise non-US government debt, private equity, venture capital and real estate. 
–
The global tokenized assets market, excluding stablecoins, grew from 
~$10bn in Jan 2025 to ~$39bn in Sep 2026. US Treasury debt remains the 
largest segment outside stablecoins, with money market funds leading 
near-term adoption. Despite the US Digital Asset Market Clarity Act failing 
to advance through to the Senate yesterday, we remain positive about the  
momentum to tokenize both assets and capital markets in the coming 
years. 
–
Institutions are increasingly applying tokenization to settlement, funding 
and collateral management. Notable examples include DTCC’s tokenization 
service, DLT-based intraday repo and collateral initiatives, and the 
Eurosystem’s Pontes solution, which connects DLT platforms to TARGET 
Services and is expected to launch later this month.
–
A broad tokenized capital market requires progress across three areas: 
assets, settlement money and market infrastructure. The tokenized RWA 
market remains small relative to traditional markets, and token issuance 
does not automatically create secondary-market liquidity.
–
In this chartbook, we broaden our focus from tokenized assets, such as 
bonds and stocks, to the infrastructure through which they are issued; and 
review (i) market size; (ii) adoption trends; (iii) the regulatory drivers of 
tokenization; and (iv) barriers that will determine the pace of growth.

–
Tokenization records assets or claims as digital tokens on a 
distributed ledger, using public or permissioned networks. 
–
Potential benefits include faster transfers, automated servicing and 
smaller investment units.
–
Shared records can reduce reconciliation, but trading hours, 
redemption terms and investor access remain product-specific.
Two issuance models:
–
On-chain (or native): The security or fund share is issued directly on a 
distributed ledger.
–
Off-chain (or tokenized representation): The underlying asset 
remains outside the blockchain, but ownership claims and economic 
rights are represented digitally on-chain.

STABLECOIN SPOTLIGHT: The settlement side of tokenization
Sources: Deutsche Bank Research, taken from DB’s FX Special Report: Tokenised money and the future of FX - 
Deutsche Bank Research
–
Stablecoins account for ~89% of the combined value of stablecoin and 
distributed tokenized assets. We assess them separately because their 
principal role is payment and settlement.
–
They support near-instant, round-the-clock transfers for remittances, 
business payments and treasury operations. Cross-border savings depend on 
transaction fees, FX costs, compliance and conversion into bank money.
–
Chainalysis projects adjusted annual transfer volume could rise from $28tn in 
2025 to $719tn by 2035; higher scenario approaching $1.5 quadrillion. 
Regulation and competition
–
The US Treasury has proposed implementing rules for payment stablecoins. 
The GENIUS Act takes effect on the earlier of 18 January, 2027, or 120 days 
after final implementing regulations are issued.
–
Stablecoins could increase competition for bank deposits and payments. 
Exchange rewards are distinct from issuer-paid interest, which GENIUS 
prohibits for permitted payment stablecoins.
Risks
–
Trust depends on reserve quality, legal redemption rights and operational 
resilience. Stablecoins remain dependent on their issuers and supporting 
institutions.
Tokenized deposits and central bank money provide alternative settlement options. Connecting the cash and securities legs of a transaction can improve settlement 
efficiency and reduce fragmentation.

–
Early efforts focused on tokenized claims on traditional securities (i.e. BlackRock’s 
BUIDL, Franklin Templeton FOBXX), while the underlying assets remained off-chain. 
–
Financial institutions are now extending tokenization to the infrastructure through 
which assets are distributed, traded, settled and financed.
DLT adoption: small, but intraday repo offers a practical use case
–
Global DLT fixed-income issuance reached €4.8bn in 2025, up 48% from 2024. 
–
DLT intraday repo (i.e. JPM Kinexys and Broadridge’s DLR) can accelerate collateral 
transfers and settlement, helping institutions manage short-term funding needs. 
–
A Finadium study estimated that using intraday DLR for 15% of activity could reduce 
intraday liquidity buffer needs by 8–17%. 
Tokenized securities on blockchain rails 
–
DTCC to launch its tokenization service in Oct 2026, covering Russell 1000 constituents, 
ETFs tracking major indices, and US Treasury bills, notes and bonds.
–
Initial production trades took place in July with >30 participating firms. Tokens are 
recorded on DTCC's Hyperledger Besu network or on Canton, settling on either. The 
SEC granted a three-year authorization.
Official-sector initiatives and interoperability 
–
Project Agorá, led by the BIS and IIF with central banks and financial institutions, 
demonstrated a prototype for cross-border settlement using tokenized deposits and 
central bank reserves. Real-value testing is the next phase.
–
Clearstream, DTCC and Euroclear published a joint interoperability framework with 
BCG in March 2026 to address fragmentation across digital asset markets.
–
Eurosystem’s Pontes solution connects DLT platforms to TARGET Services for 
settlement in central bank money.

SHORT-TERM ADOPTION: Tokenized Treasuries and money market funds lead
Tokenized US Treasury market value reaches $15bn
– Tokenized cash-management funds, including BUIDL and USYC, 
combine short-term yields with more flexible transfers and collateral 
use.
– Potential benefits include faster settlement and lower transaction costs, 
making Treasury exposure more accessible across eligible digital 
platforms. 
– Direct Treasury bond tokenization accounted for 27% of overall US 
Treasury tokenization in 2025, indicating adoption beyond fund 
structures. 
– These assets can also serve as collateral in repo and margin 
arrangement, supporting demand beyond investment returns. 
Secondary-market constraints
– Tokenized bonds are often held to maturity. Limited investor 
participation and weak interoperability between platforms constrain 
secondary-market liquidity.
– Some exchanges are attempting to improve liquidity by connecting 
digital and conventional markets through dual listings, including SIX 
digital exchange and Swiss Stock exchange). 
Risks
– Redemption terms can differ from the settlement hours and liquidity of 
underlying assets, creating potential liquidity mismatches. 
– Blockchain transparency can improve visibility but may also accelerate 
withdrawals during stress. 
– Using tokenized fund shares across multiple products and as collateral 
can create additional channels of contagion.

MEDIUM-TERM ADOPTION: Tokenized equities remain small; DTCC’s launch could 
broaden adoption
Market value of tokenized equities 
Sources: Deutsche Bank Research, RWA.xyz. Data up to Sep 10, 2026.
– Tokenized equities remain small (<1%) relative to traditional stock markets. 
– 68% of equity tokenization in 2025 consisted of direct tokenization of 
individual stocks or baskets of stocks, vs. tokenized equity funds. 
– Suggests that blockchain adoption is increasingly focused on the securities 
themselves (i.e. DTCC method) rather than through third-party platforms (i.e. 
Ondo and xStocks). 
Developments
– DTCC’s tokenization service, scheduled to launch in Oct, will allow eligible 
existing securities to move across blockchain infrastructure within its 
regulated framework. 
– These tokenized securities will retain the entitlements, investor protections 
and ownership rights associated with their traditional form.
– NYSE has announced plans for a tokenized securities platform designed to  
support 24/7 trading of US-listed equities and ETFs, with near-instant 
settlement and stablecoin funding.
Benefits: Reduce barriers for foreign investors; automated post-trade processes 
and longer trading hours; reduce information asymmetry (e.g., embedding listing 
or disclosure rules directly into the tokens). 
Risks & limitations
– Tokenized equities require clear legal ownership, enforceable investor rights 
and alignment with existing securities rules.
– Under some third-party wrapper structures, the token provides exposure to a 
share without making its holder a shareholder in the underlying company.
– Investors may therefore face issuer or custodian risk and may not receive the 
same voting, dividend or other rights as direct shareholders.

LONG-TERM ADOPTION: Private credit expands through record-keeping; real 
estate is growing fast but from a negligeable base
Real estate tokenization process 
Sources: Deutsche Bank Research, RWA.xyz. Updated September 10. Excludes platforms that issues tokens using blockchain as a 
recordkeeping layer (i.e. Represented Layer) 
Private credit
–
Private credit stands ~$8bn in total, including asset-backed credit ($2.6bn), 
specialty finance ($2.5bn), corporate credit ($1.9bn), and diversified credit 
($798mn).
–
Activity includes lending linked to digital assets and selected corporate credit 
products.
–
A larger use case is blockchain-based record-keeping: represented credit stands 
at ~$35bn, over 4x the distributed market.
–
Companies like Figure Technologies and Tradable use blockchains to support 
loan administration, ownership records, payments tracking, and servicing. 
Challenges
–
Tokenization remains concentrated among a small number of issuers and lacks a 
deep, and liquid secondary market. 
–
Legal fragmentation, credit quality and servicing arrangements remain 
important risks. Blockchain records do not remove the underlying borrower’s 
default risk.
Tokenized credit market stands at ~$8bn
Real estate
–
The market expanded from ~$35mn in 2025 to $226mn in Sep 2026, although it 
remains small. 
Benefits
–
Fractional ownership can lower minimum investment sizes (typically for $100-
$1,000 per unit) and widen access, particularly where traditional capital markets 
and mortgage markets are limited. 
Challenges
–
A BIS study found that liquidity improvements can depend on platforms offering 
to buy tokens back. These arrangements can help investors exit but expose 
platforms to liquidity and solvency risks.

OUTLOOK: Tokenized capital markets could become a core part of financial by the 2030s
Conservative forecasts for tokenized real-world 
assets (ex stablecoins)
Source: Deutsche Bank Research.
– Tokenization will modernize financial infrastructure rather than eliminate intermediaries. We expect a gradual 
transition onto digital rails, with banks retaining key roles across payments, FX, treasury, financing, collateral 
and trading settlement.
Assumption, on the positive side reflects…
– So far, tokenization has achieved its greatest scale in stablecoins, with activity concentrated in crypto trading 
and payments gaining ground.
– Short-term: Adoption will be driven by tokenized US Treasuries and money market funds, expanding as yield-
bearing collateral in repo and margin arrangements.
– Long-term:  The opportunity extends beyond assets to the infrastructure supporting issuance, settlement, 
custody, collateral management and repo markets.
– We are seeing 3 growth catalysts: (i) infrastructure: major financial market infrastructures (DTCC, NYSE, 
Nasdaq) are integrating tokenization into core workflows; (ii) liquidity: Growth of regulated on-chain money 
(stablecoins and tokenized deposits) providing a trusted settlement foundation; (iii) and regulation: US 2025 
GENIUS ACT continues to encourage global digital asset adoption, although the failure of the CLARITY act to 
advance yesterday likely rules out progress in this congressional session until Jan 2027. Looking ahead, focus 
will turn back to the SEC and CFTC for crypto rules. 
…Risks to adoption 
– Accelerated liquidity runs: Tokenization may create a new form of systemic liquidity risk that spreads faster than 
traditional finance.
– Money market funds yield-chasing: Declining interest rates or rising competition may push MMF issuers into 
risker assts, less-liquid assets to boost yield, undermining their safety. 
– Crypto contagion: Tight integration with crypto markets via stablecoin reserves and collateral risks transmitting 
digital asset volatility to conventional financial markets.
– Operational risks: Smart-contract vulnerabilities, cyberattacks, network outages could halt trading and prevent 
access to the underlying assets.
– Financial Crime: Traditional institutions must scale up compliance and AML controls to manage illicit activities 
on decentralized networks.
