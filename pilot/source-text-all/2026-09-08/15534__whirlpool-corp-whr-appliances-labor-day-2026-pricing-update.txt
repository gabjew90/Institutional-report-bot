Modest Deceleration in Net Price as Industry Navigates 
Challenging Backdrop: To better assess the competitive 
environment around Labor Day, we scanned appliance pricing 
across three retailers and five major manufacturers. Overall, we 
found net industry price grew 501bps YOY, vs +702bps over Fourth 
of July and +508bps over Memorial Day, as manufacturers look to 
offset rising costs including commodity and freight inflation along 
with Section 232 tariffs. The gain is led by price while promotions 
rose 105bps vs Fourth of July to 21.6%, though they are down 
267bps YOY. Whirlpool was in line with the industry as discounts 
were up 226bps vs Fourth of July to 20.5% but 221bps lower YOY. 
LG was the most promotional at 27.6% while Electrolux trailed at 
17.5%. The spread between categories narrowed vs the recent past 
with washers at 24.2% vs more discretionary products including 
ranges and dishwashers at 23.7% and 24.2%, respectively. We 
believe the sequential growth in promotions partially reflects 
retailers’ efforts to stay in front of consumers and drive traffic to 
their stores. We highlight the complexity of supplier agreements 
provides retailers with some flexibility to adjust pricing. Our channel 
checks also suggest a move towards a slightly shorter promotional 
period for some manufacturers, including Whirlpool. In our view, the 
findings are consistent with operating conditions as manufacturers 
and retailers look to balance rising costs against tepid demand as we 
forecast 2026 North America industry shipments down 3%. 
Nevertheless, we believe Whirlpool will stay price/cost positive in 
the back half while cost reduction initiatives offset the loss of 
operating leverage. This supports our estimate for the 3Q26 North 
America major domestic appliance segment operating margin up 
78bps YOY to 5.7% with the full-year off 105bps YOY to 3.9%.

Price Leads Net Gains as Industry Looks to Offset Inflation Amidst Weak Demand 
 
 
Discounts Rise Sequentially Given Competitive Pressures But are Down Meaningfully 
YOY 
In order to better assess the promotional environment around the Labor Day holiday, we 
scanned appliance prices across three major retailers. The weighted-average discount of 
21.6% was 267bps lower vs 2025 but 105bps above Fourth of July. This compares to a 
156bps decline over the same period in 2024. There was a 1,003bps variation among 
brands compared to 496bps last year. LG was the most promotional at 27.6%, up 
242bps sequentially, while Electrolux trailed at 17.5%, off 83bps. Whirlpool discounts 
averaged 20.5%, up 226bps sequentially but off 221bps YOY. Our scan does not 
consider a slightly shorter promotional period versus a year ago, though this could be 
extended as producers and retailers look to balance rising costs and volumes.

Cost Update: Monitoring Input Costs and Tariffs 
 
 
The rate of change in the price for key commodity inputs to appliances is expected to 
moderate in the second half while staying elevated given trade actions and the lingering 
conflict in the Middle East. As such, we estimate $374mn in raw material inflation and 
tariff exposure for Whirlpool in 2026, roughly in line with management’s expectations. 
Against this, we model $209mn of price/mix tailwinds coupled with $125mn in cost 
reductions contributing to our 2026 consolidated adj. EBIT margin forecast of 4.0%, 
down 72bps YOY. Steel and resins each account for 8-9% of COGS, while logistics are 
10%.  
We provide more details around the key cost inputs below. 
Key Input Costs Remain Elevated 
US hot rolled steel prices further grew this summer and currently trade at $1,223/ton, 
up 31% YTD and 53% YOY. Our steel team expects realizations to moderate in the 
coming months averaging $1,090/ton in 4Q as they normalize towards mid-cycle levels 
of ~$1,000/ton next year. This is led by: 1) a widening discount for Asian imports that 
should result in more volumes coming to the US, 2) increases in domestic production as 
mills under maintenance come back online in late 3Q26, along with broader capacity 
expansion starting in 2027, and 3) normalizing lead times and inventories. However, 
mid-cycle prices are expected to remain elevated reflecting: 1) strong demand across 
several verticals with order books high and backlogs extending into 2027, 2) higher 
shipping costs increasing the landed cost of imports and pushing import parity prices 
upward, and 3) disruptions in global supply chains and shipping routes creating more 
uncertainty around availability of imports.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
