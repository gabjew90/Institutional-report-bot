Americas Retail: Takeaways from IR/management; 2Q26 earnings 
summary; August traffic trends; Updating estimates
8 September 2026 | 6:01AM EDT
In this note, we discuss key takeaways from meetings with the IR/management teams 
of Costco Wholesale (COST), Five Below (FIVE), Ollie’s Bargain Outlet (OLLI), and 
Petco Health and Wellness (WOOF). We also provide an earnings summary for 2Q26, 
summarize management commentary on the consumer, discuss QTD trends, and 
analyze Placer traffic data for various hardlines sub-sectors, which showed mixed 
trends for average visits per venue (y/y) in Aug’26. Additionally, we outline the tariff 
refund status across our retail coverage universe, detailing whether each company 
received a refund and, if so, whether we included the benefit in our Non-GAAP 
calculations. Finally, we update our estimates for BBWI, ULTA, and WMT. 
Takeaways from IR/management meetings 
 
 
COST 
Costco’s August comp was negatively impacted by the Labor Day shift. Costco 
reported August SSS (ex-gas/FX) of +5.4%, decelerating from +6.6% in July. That 
said, the shift of Labor Day timing in the US and Canada negatively impacted total 
and comparable sales by a little less than 75 bps during the month. Excluding this 
calendar shift, we estimate Costco’s comp in August would be roughly in line with 
July, though management noted a slowdown in Canada likely due in part to recent 
tariff news. September sales will likely benefit from the timing shift of Labor Day, 
with an estimated tailwind of less than 75 bps given that it will be a 5-week retail 
month (vs. 4 weeks in August). 
Consumer trends and price gaps remain relatively consistent sequentially. 
Management did not call out any changes in consumer trends in August. In terms of 
the grocery pricing environment, management is not seeing anything different from 
competitors when doing comp shopping; the value Costco is providing to its 
members is in-line with what the company would expect on like-for-like goods. 
Non-foods was up +MSD in August. Strength in the category was driven by home 
furnishings, sporting goods, and jewelry. While Costco continued to see strength in 
jewelry, management noted that the related tailwind started to decrease amid 
tougher laps from prior years. Looking more broadly, the non-foods category also 
faced a tougher lap y/y given that some programs from last year (e.g., Uber Eats and 
DoorDash gift cards) were not in place this year.

Looking ahead, we continue to have confidence in Costco’s value proposition resonating 
with consumers, and we expect the company to continue gaining share. Membership 
trends remain healthy, with total members up 4.1% y/y in F3Q, and with US and Canada 
renewal rates at 92.2%. While Costco has seen significant membership growth over the 
past few years, the company is now focusing on growing sales through a better member 
experience, which is largely in the form of infill locations and relieving pressure from its 
higher volume buildings in the US. We currently estimate F4Q SSS (ex gas/FX) of +6.4%, 
above consensus (LSEG Data & Analytics) at +6.1%, and F4Q EPS of $6.62, above 
consensus at $6.54. 
FIVE 
Merchandising strategy and product exclusivity. Management emphasized that the 
company is becoming increasingly disciplined in how it buys inventory, with a greater 
focus on purchasing with conviction rather than simply expanding SKU counts. Over the 
past two years, Five Below has reduced its SKU count while consolidating merchandising, 
product development, and planning functions into more agile teams that can identify 
trends and react quickly. Management also highlighted stronger partnerships with 
vendors, as they are requiring vendors to participate in risk and take a more active role 
in investing in new product opportunities. As the company continues to scale, Five Below 
is seeing more opportunities for exclusive product, becoming increasingly selective with 
vendor partnerships, and attracting brands it had not previously worked with, such as 
LEGO. 
Product category outperformance. Management highlighted room décor as one of the 
more surprising areas of strength, noting they have invested in improving and refining 
the assortment. The company has also seen fashion trends increasingly translate into 
room décor, driving strong sell-through and encouraging customers to return for a 
broader assortment within the category. Beyond room décor, management pointed to 
continued success in Lounge Nation, where items such as pajama bottoms, sweats, and 
maxi dresses have resonated with customers. 
We reiterate our Buy on Five Below and believe the strong 3Q guide (reflecting a +23.3% 
two-year stack at the midpoint) demonstrates the strong momentum in the business and 
the ability of the company to lap harder compares through transaction growth. While we 
acknowledge that FY27 may see moderating comps, we believe management gave 
several reasons to support continued growth, including improved new store 
productivity, increased partnerships with vendors, and an amplified marketing strategy 
that leverages customer data. For 3Q, we model a +10% comp (vs. consensus at +9.9%) 
and EPS at $1.12 (vs. consensus at $1.12).  
OLLI 
Pricing investments and the promotional environment. While Ollie’s typically has 
larger price gaps compared to competitors, management noted that the increased 
promotional environment, especially flash promotional marketing, impacted consumers’ 
shopping decisions. Unlike retailers that can periodically use price rollbacks and 
promotional events to drive traffic, Ollie’s operates an everyday value model and is less 
reliant on promotions. While management invests in price every quarter, they 
acknowledged that they were slow to deploy their tariff refunds and did not anticipate 
the level of pricing activity that emerged across the industry. However, management

QTD trends. August is trending better than expectations, which management attributed 
to three factors: 1) an easier comp comparison in 3Q vs. 2Q, 2) the timing of a flyer shift 
into the final week of 2Q, which is generating carryover benefits into 3Q given the 
Back-to-School and Back-to-College focus of the promotions, and 3) price investments.  
Gross margin is benefiting from supply chain efficiencies. Ollie’s reported a 43.5% 
gross margin in 2Q, which included a +380 bp benefit from tariff refunds and a -70 bp 
impact from price investments. Excluding the tariff refund benefit, gross margin would 
have been ~40.3% (vs. 39.9% last year), with management attributing the underlying 
expansion to shrink and supply chain efficiencies. While transportation costs 
represented a 20-30 bp headwind in the quarter, lower tariff rates helped offset the 
impact. Management also noted that its continuous inventory counting process allows it 
to identify shrink improvements more quickly than other retailers, creating 
opportunities for gross margin upside. 
While the 2Q comp was weighed on by weather, a more promotional environment, and 
softer consumer trends, we still view the business as structurally intact following the 
print. We acknowledge that Ollie’s will likely continue to face consumer pressure, as its 
~700-store footprint is smaller than competitors and creates less convenience during 
periods of elevated fuel prices. That said, we were encouraged by management’s 
acknowledgment that the company was slow to deploy pricing investments and their 
willingness to respond more aggressively going forward. As we move into the 2H, we see 
potential for acceleration as pricing investments begin to benefit results, holiday drives 
traffic and momentum, and weather sensitivity plays a smaller role compared to 1H. For 
3Q, we model a +0.5% comp (vs. consensus at +0.0%) and EPS at $0.78 (vs. consensus at 
$0.77).  
WOOF 
Capital allocation and fleet optimization. Management emphasized a conservative 
approach to the balance sheet, targeting ~$150mn in cash to avoid reliance on its 
revolver. The company remains committed to voluntary debt paydown, balancing 
deleveraging with disciplined business investments. Regarding capital expenditures, 
management acknowledged past overspending on veterinary hospitals but reaffirmed 
hospitals as a core long-term strategy, moving forward with stricter ROI discipline. On 
the store fleet, the vast majority of the ~1,400 locations remain healthy. While 
management is addressing a watch-list of roughly 60 underperforming stores for 
potential turnaround or closure, they do not anticipate any large-scale, episodic fleet 
rationalization. 
Comp dynamics in the 2H. Management noted that transaction growth has seen a 
strong recovery, and the focus for the second half of the year will shift toward driving 
basket size, supported by the recently relaunched loyalty program. Moving forward, the 
company plans to use the data generated by the membership program to deploy more 
surgical, data-driven marketing strategies. 
Looking ahead, we expect the company to continue executing its turnaround strategy.

2Q26 earnings summary  
 
 
With Hardlines/Broadlines 2Q26 results complete, we summarize company results vs our 
estimates and consensus, and include all companies’ commentary on the health of the 
consumer. We note that most companies beat earnings and raised FY26 guidance, 
though we note some of these beats and raises were partially driven by tariff refunds. 
Companies called out a cautious consumer, who continues to be pressured by persistent 
inflation and higher energy prices. As a result, the consumer remains focused on value, 
newness, and convenience.

August traffic trends  
 
 
We looked at monthly average visits per venue (y/y) for selected hardlines categories, 
including Broadlines, Dollar Stores/Discounters, Specialty, Home Improvement, and Auto 
Parts. Within Broadlines, average visits per venue (y/y) were mixed in August 2026. TGT 
led the group at +5%, followed by COST (+1%), while BJ (-3%) and WMT (-1%) saw a 
negative y/y trend. Dollar Stores/Discounters led the coverage in August, with all 
discounters experiencing positive traffic (y/y) except for OLLI at -6%. FIVE led the group 
at +8%, followed by DLTR (+4%) and DG (+1%). Specialty retailers experienced largely 
negative traffic (y/y) in August 2026, with DKS (-6%), WOOF (-7%), Eyeglass World (-9%), 
and ASO (-12%), but ULTA (+2%), BBY (+1%), and America’s Best (~flat y/y).

Updating our estimates 
 
 
BBWI 
We update our BBWI model to include the $80mn benefit from tariff refunds received in 
2Q26. BBWI expects to reinvest ~$30mn to offset incremental tariff and input cost 
inflation (largely related to the additional Canadian tariff) and ~$35mn into Consumer 
First Formula initiatives, primarily marketing. Looking at 3Q26 and 4Q26, we also 
increase our expectations for SG&A spend. As a result of including the 2Q tariff refund 
benefit, our FY26 EPS estimate increases to $2.73 from $2.48 prior. Our FY27/FY28 EPS 
estimates decrease ~2% to $2.60/$2.85, respectively. The company is guiding to FY26 
adj. EPS of $2.60-2.80, which benefits from the $15mn in net tariff refunds. 
 
Valuation & Risks 
We are Sell rated on BBWI. Our 12-month price target of $19 (unchanged) is based on 
downside/base/upside relative P/E multiples of 25%/30%/35% (unchanged) and a 
higher market multiple of 22.8x (vs. 22.6x prior).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
