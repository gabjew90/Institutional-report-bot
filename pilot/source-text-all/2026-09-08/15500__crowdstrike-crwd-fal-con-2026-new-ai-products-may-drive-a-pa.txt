We attended Fal.Con in Las Vegas, Sept 1-3. For at least the last 
decade in Security, practitioners have been on their back foot, and 
there has been increasing concern that industry dynamics get worse 
in a world of AI-enabled attacks before they get better. There were 
two tangible products announced at Fal.Con that may directly 
address the AI security threat and potentially create a paradigm 
shift in security back toward empowering the defenders: 
SafeMind (for continuous agentic penetration testing) and 
Guardian (for agentic discovery and control). SafeMind is built on 
CrowdStrike’s newly-introduced cyber frontier model. We have 
argued this year that domain-specific SLMs may offer a more 
compelling value proposition to enterprises, as taking a solid 
foundation of LLM technology, pairing it with business context, and 
training it on proprietary domain-specific data can yield better 
results than a frontier model. CrowdStrike’s Cyber Superintelligence 
Lab takes Nvidia’s Nemotron open-source and open-weight models 
and then trains it on its 15+ year history of kill chain datapoints and 
human reinforcement labelled data. In doing so, remediations with 
SafeMind now cost as little as $0.03 per query, vs. frontier models at 
$10/query (a 99% reduction in cost). The combination of a strong 
vision, tangible product innovations, and a conference that 
meaningfully raised the bar on what a flagship conference looks 
like in Security reinforces our view that CrowdStrike is well 
positioned to capture outsized share as security budgets inflect, 
with the best part of the AI security product cycle still to come.

SafeMind was the clearest example of CrowdStrike moving from AI 
o
security vision to product architecture. CrowdStrike launched the Cyber 
Superintelligence Lab and SafeMind, a set of cybersecurity-specific models 
and harnesses developed with NVIDIA. The core idea is familiar to security 
practitioners - red team vs. blue team - but applied to AI models: Red Tempest 
acts as an offensive model that searches for attack paths, while Blue Solano 
acts as the defensive model that learns from those attacks, identifies 
detection gaps, and helps deploy new protections. The company framed this 
as a continuous learning loop that can improve the Falcon platform over time, 
rather than a standalone chatbot or generic copilot. Management also 
highlighted early benchmark results showing stronger detection, faster 
response, and lower cost per task than general frontier models, and plans to 
monetize SafeMind through token packs supported by Falcon Flex. 
Falcon Guardian gives CrowdStrike a more concrete answer for how 
o
enterprises will secure AI agents in practice. The product is designed to 
discover and secure AI agents where they run, across endpoint, cloud 
workload, and SaaS environments. CrowdStrike framed the problem as a lack 
of visibility and control over agentic activity, including which agents are active, 
who deployed them, what they can access, and what actions they are taking. 
Guardian is intended to address that by mapping known and shadow agents, 
connecting prompt-layer activity with operating-system telemetry, and 
turning governance policies into runtime controls. In our view, this is important 
because it frames AI security less as a posture-management problem and 
more as a runtime detection-and-response problem, which is more aligned 
with CrowdStrike’s core competencies. 
The Agentic SOC announcements extend the same AI strategy into analyst 
o
workflows. CrowdStrike announced that Charlotte AI can now dispatch 
domain-specific agents in parallel across endpoint, identity, SaaS, cloud, and 
network, all operating on a shared context layer that provides persistent 
memory across agents and investigations. The company also announced 
Charlotte Agentic SOAR, which brings AgentWorks and Foundry into one 
workspace with per-workflow autonomy settings; and Falcon Onum certified 
data pipelines, which can lower storage costs by up to 50%. In our view, the 
broader point is that CrowdStrike is trying to turn its unified data architecture 
into an advantage as SOC workflows become more autonomous, with the 
company explicitly contrasting this against competitors that are adding agents 
on top of more fragmented data stacks. 
Agentic Identity Provider aims to address the emerging identity 
o
opportunity.  CrowdStrike is extending its identity strategy from humans to 
agents, with auto-registration through Falcon Guardian, cryptographically 
verifiable agent identities, short-lived scoped tokens instead of standing 
credentials, and attribution back to the human behind each agent action. The 
broader strategic bet is that agentic workflows will require a new identity layer

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
