Company Description 
Centrus Energy Corp. is a supplier of nuclear fuel 
components for the nuclear power industry and provides 
advanced uranium enrichment and technical services for 
public and private customers. Centrus currently focuses on 
enriching High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium (HALEU) and 
other capabilities necessary for production of advanced 
nuclear fuel to power existing and next generation nuclear 
reactors.

Centrus Energy benefits from strong barriers to entry, solid 
industry fundamentals, and financial and regulatory support 
from the U.S. government. However, we rate the stock at

Centrus Q2’26 results 
Centrus reported Q2’26 adjusted diluted EPS of $1.77 above BofAe at $1.12 and 
Bloomberg (BBG) / Visible Alpha (VA) consensus at $0.75 / $1.32. The adjusted EPS 
excludes ‘growth’ costs ($8m or $0.37/sh) and share-based comp ($13.7m or $0.63/sh). 
Reported (GAAP) Q2’26 diluted EPS came in at $0.77. The LEU segment reported a 
gross margin of $52m (vs. $28m in Q1), above BofAe at $41m and BBG/VA at 
$32m/$39m, mainly on higher Uranium sales. The TS segment posted a loss of $1.7m 
(vs profit of $3.6m in Q1), below BofAe +$3.9m, and BBG/VA at +$3.4m/+$3.0m, mainly 
on lower revenue realized under the HALEU operation contract.  
The backlog increased to $4.5 billion (bn) as of 30-Jun-26 vs. $3.9bn as of 31-Mar-26 
(both through 2040). The increase was mainly due to higher LEU segment backlog of 
$3.7bn, up from $3.1bn prior, with contingent sales commitments at $3.0bn (vs $2.4bn 
prior). The TS segment backlog was unchanged at $0.8bn. Q2’26 free cash outflow was -
$53m, largely in-line to BofAe (-$57m). Centrus held net cash at quarter-end of $691m 
vs. $692m at the prior quarter-end.

Enrichment prices strong and rising 
Recently updated monthly enrichment pricing from UxC, LLC showed a strong increase 
in spot enrichment pricing during August, rising 7.5% to $215 per separative work unit 
($/SWU). Meanwhile, term pricing for enrichment increased by 1% during August to 
$181/SWU. Both prices are at all-time highs, reflecting a substantial supply imbalance in 
the global markets that has emerged in the wake of both official and unofficial bans on 
the import of Russian nuclear fuel products, across the Western world.  
Record prices also reflect strongly growing demand as the number of operating civil 
nuclear reactors global has increased from 443 at the end of 2020 to 451 expected by 
year-end 2026. UxC forecasts this growing to 493 reactors by the end of 2030.  
It’s this positive enrichment market backdrop that Centrus finds justification in its goal 
to build up its domestic US enrichment production capacity.

Company Overview 
Centrus Energy Corp (NYSE: LEU) is a nuclear energy company that supplies nuclear fuel 
and nuclear engineering services to companies in the energy, aerospace and defense, 
and chemical industries. Centrus is one of only two companies with a license to produce 
enriched uranium (LEU) in the U.S and the only one licensed to supply LEU to the U.S. 
government for national security applications. Furthermore, Centrus is the only company 
with a Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) license to enrich uranium up to 20% U 
assay HALEU, in the U.S. As such, Centrus stands to benefit from lucrative US 
government contracts that prioritize domestic sourcing. 
Centrus traces its roots to the 1940s when the U.S. government began enriching 
uranium for defense purposes. In the 1960s, it began selling enriched uranium to the 
commercial nuclear power industry worldwide. The company was incorporated in 1998 
as the United States Enrichment Corporation Inc. (USEC) as part of the privatization of 
the U.S. government’s uranium enrichment business. Centrus emerged in 2014, after 
USEC filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy and a financial restructuring. The company now 
operates in two broad segments: Low-Enriched Uranium and Technical Solutions.

Low-Enriched Uranium – The Low-Enriched Uranium segment is the largest 
segment by revenue (79% in F24). The segment supplies nuclear fuel primarily to 
utilities, which use the fuel to operate commercial nuclear power plants (Its main 
function is to essentially act as a broker) The Low-Enriched Uranium segment is 
further broken into two components: Separative Work Units (SWU) and uranium. The 
SWU unit sells low-enriched uranium (measured in SWU) while the uranium unit 
sells natural uranium hexafluoride (UF6). Centrus also occasionally sells other 
uranium concentrates and conversion services under this segment. Utilities typically 
provide the natural UF6 to Centrus in exchange for Centrus’ low-enriched uranium 
(customers charged using SWU). However, in some cases, customers will purchase 
the uranium and low-enriched uranium from Centrus. The segment’s contracts are 
typically medium-to-long-term, with fixed-commitment (meaning that customers 
are obligated to buy a specific amount of fuel from Centrus) and extend through 
2040. As of 31-Mar-25, the segment has a backlog of $2.8bn, including revenue 
from future SWU and uranium deliveries under its medium-to-long-term contracts 
with fixed commitments and $2.1bn in contingent LEU sales commitments. 
• 
Technical Solutions – This segment provides uranium enrichment services to the 
U.S. government and advanced engineering/technical services to 
government/private sector customers. It is comprised of the Fuel Components 
Manufacturing (FCM) unit and the Engineering Services (ES) unit. The FCM unit 
provides uranium enrichment services for the U.S. government (i.e., producing low-
enriched uranium and high-assay-low-enriched uranium; HALEU). The ES unit 
provides technical services (i.e., engineering or design work) for 
government/commercial entities. Centrus currently has a contract with the 
Department of Energy (DOE) through which its Technical Solutions segment is 
deploying uranium enrichment capacity needed to produce HALEU/other nuclear 
fuels. As of 31-Mar-25, the Technical Solutions segment has a backlog of $0.9bn, 
which includes both funded amounts (services for which funding has been both 
authorized and appropriated), unfunded amounts (services for which funding has not 
been appropriated), and unexercised options.

Price objective basis & risk 
Centrus Energy Corp. (LEU) 
Our price objective (PO) of $205 per share is derived based on 2.5x our estimated net 
asset value (NAV). The 2.5x 2026E NAV target multiple is a premium to the nuclear fuel 
supply peers to account for barriers to entry, its emerging next generation nuclear fuel 
growth business, strong enrichment market fundamentals to which its peers have 
minimal exposure, and U.S. government support, that is now being realized via the early 
2026, $900m DoE funding award. 
 
Downside risks are:1) lower than forecast enrichment pricing, 2) loss of access to 
enriched uranium product, 3) unexpected restrictive regulatory changes, possibly 
including sanctions, 4) shipping disruptions, and 5) technical disruptions. 
 
Upside risks are: 1) higher than forecast enrichment pricing, 2) unexpected government 
financial and/or regulatory support, 3) a potential acquisition, and 4) faster than 
expected deployments of small and advanced modular reactors.
