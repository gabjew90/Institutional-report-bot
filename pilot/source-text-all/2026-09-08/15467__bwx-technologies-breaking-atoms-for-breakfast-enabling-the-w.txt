Breaking Atoms for Breakfast: Enabling the
West's Nuclear Capex; Initiate Buy
BWXT combines operating leverage to the maintenance and expansion of the
commercial nuclear fleet and a unique position as a sole-source provider of
nuclear reactors and fuels to the U.S. navy, while avoiding direct P&L volatility
for swings in uranium or enrichment prices. We estimate 2x PEG ratio supports
a price target of $181/sh. Initiate with Buy.
Thesis: BWX Technologies' core business is focused on the design, engineering, and manufacture
of precision naval nuclear components, nuclear reactors, and nuclear fuel. BWXT is the sole-source
supplier of naval nuclear reactor components and fuel for the U.S. Navy's submarine and carrier
fleets, with 420+ reactor cores delivered and zero safety incidents. Given the strong nuclear cycle,
we expect the company to deliver 6%-7% sales growth and 13%-17% EPS growth through 2030 and,
barring a policy shock, most likely through 2040.
Key Debates: (1) Pros and cons of a significant part of revenues tied to the U.S. government; (2)
HALEU & enrichment project: attractive niche or step into a riskier foray; (3) Medical sale: given the
attractive multiple, focus shifts to realizing proceeds and capital allocation.
Our Take: Two-thirds of sales and three-quarters of EBITDA are directly tied to the government, and
the balance is highly regulated. With naval propulsion ~70% of the government business, BWXT
has an advantage making its case on commercial projects. Most revenue is generated in long-
cycle businesses, with long lags from decisions to revenue. We believe visibility is strong on a
12-18 month horizon, and growth capex adds 2%-3% to EPS each year with a 3-5 year lag. With a
significant exposure to open-book contracts, the path to earnings surprises is a mix of the timing of
milestone payments (we estimate $75-150m swings QoQ are plausible) and better-than-expected
productivity (so focus on contract mix and ROIC, not incremental margins). Maintaining a reliable
relationship with the government, however, entails being transparent about ongoing investment to
address future needs (we estimate 3.5-4.0% of sales), while avoiding potential flag activities such
as large buybacks. Our price target implies a 50% premium to the S&P 500, compared to a 10-year
average of 30%.
Risks To Our Thesis: (1) Sustained federal budget disruption or reprioritisation which delay
Government Operations revenue; (2) Slippage of commercial SMR deployments into the mid-2030s
could leave expanded capacity underutilized; (3) Labor shortage could cause schedule overruns;
(4) State-actor cyber threats could jeopardize classified contracts; (5) The medical business sale
could be delayed, repriced, or blocked by regulators, complicating the capital allocation narrative.

Investment Positives
Government Services Set The Tone
BWX Technologies' core business is focused on the design, engineering, and manufacture of precision
naval nuclear components, nuclear reactors, and nuclear fuel. This has several implications. First,
~66% of sales and ~75% of EBITDA are directly tied to government contracts, with the balance being
highly regulated. Second, most revenue is generated in long-cycle businesses, with long lags from
decisions to revenue, and while there is lumpiness QoQ, visibility is solid on a 12-18 month horizon.
We estimate growth capex contributes to EBITDA with a 3-5 year lag. Third, as in other industrial
sectors, BWX's ability to serve a demanding customer (in this case, naval propulsion represents ~70%
of the government business) makes it a preferred provider for other projects. Fourth, with a significant
exposure to open-book contracts, the path to earnings surprises is a mix of the timing of milestone
payments (we estimate $75-150m swings QoQ are plausible) and better-than-expected productivity
(so focus on contract mix and ROIC, not incremental margins). Fifth, maintaining a strong relationship
with the government requires continued transparency regarding ongoing investment to address future
needs (we estimate 3.5%-4.0% of sales), while avoiding potential flag activities such as large buybacks.

Naval Nuclear Defense Monopoly and Regulatory Moat
BWX Technologies occupies a structurally advantaged position in U.S. naval nuclear defense, serving
as the sole-source supplier of certain naval nuclear reactor components and fuel for the U.S. Navy's
submarine and aircraft carrier programs, effectively powering the entire nuclear fleet. The company's
technical scale is proven by the successful fabrication and delivery of >420 reactor cores to the U.S.
Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program with zero safety incidents, establishing a premier reputation for
quality in a zero-tolerance industry. This position is reinforced by ownership and operation of the only
two NRC Category I-licensed facilities in the US authorized to handle, store, and process highly enriched
uranium, creating barriers to entry that could require decades and billions of dollars to replicate. The
durability of this franchise is evidenced by a $2.6bn multi-year pricing agreement signed in mid-2025
for Virginia-class and Columbia-class submarine components and Ford-class carrier work, along with
an additional $1.4bn in Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program contracts booked in 1Q26. The trilateral
AUKUS security agreement is expected to significantly ramp up component demand and capacity
requirements over the medium to long term.

U.S. Initiative To Increase Domestic Enrichment Capacity
The company has secured a $1.5bn IDIQ contract with the NNSA to build out a domestic
uranium enrichment capability under the DUECE program, which began with the rapid seven-
month construction of the Centrifuge Manufacturing Development Facility in Oak Ridge, Tennessee.
Additionally, the $101.1m acquisition of Aerojet Ordnance Tennessee (A.O.T.) in 2025 positioned
BWXT as the sole domestic processor of depleted uranium for defense applications. Following this
transaction, BWXT secured a $1.63bn, 10Y contract to produce up to 300 tpy of High Purity Depleted
Uranium (HPDU), with the company now serving as the sole domestic supplier for this program.
This initiative is focused on defense applications due to the government's willingness to pay for the
capacity.
 
Leverage To New Wave Of Commercial Nuclear And SMR Projects
BWXT sells equipment that is engineered and tailored for a specific opportunity. We estimate the
potential revenue opportunity on a new reactor build at $350-700m on a CANDU reactor, $300-500m
for an AP1000, and $50-100m at an SMR. The company is currently manufacturing the reactor
pressure vessel for North America's first SMR, the GE Hitachi BWRX-300 at the Darlington site, and
has signed a detailed design contract to supply nuclear steam generators for the Rolls-Royce SMR.
To support this positioning, the company invested $60m to expand its heavy component capacity in
Cambridge, Ontario by ~50%.
 
M&A Adds Adjacencies
The July 2026 acquisition of Precision Components Group (PCG) for ~$200m represents a strategic
milestone, establishing BWXT's first commercial nuclear component manufacturing footprint inside
the United States. This acquisition added 500k square feet of heavy-walled manufacturing capacity
and >450 nuclear-qualified employees across sites in York, Pennsylvania and Florence, New Jersey,
allowing the company to support domestic SMR builds and commercial life extensions without utilizing
its defense-restricted naval propulsion facilities. The May 2025 acquisition of Kinectrics for C$782.7m
(representing a net purchase consideration of C$614.5m or C$440.6m, net of assumed liabilities)
added >1.3k nuclear-qualified employees across 20 global sites: we estimate ~$325m (80%-85%)
of Kinectric sales involve testing and gear for maintaining existing power plants. The company has
also captured stable, high-margin revenue from commercial utility nuclear life-extension programs,
including a major contract to manufacture 48 steam generators for the Pickering Nuclear Generating
Station life-extension program in Canada. Also, in January 2025, BWXT acquired Aerojet Ordnance
Tennessee, Inc. for ~$101.1m (net of cash acquired), establishing BWXT as the sole domestic
processor of depleted uranium for defense applications. Following the transaction, BWXT secured a
$1.63bn, 10-year contract to produce up to 300 tpy of HPDU.
 
Advanced Nuclear Fuels and Microreactor Programs
BWXT is at the forefront of commercializing Tristructural Isotropic (TRISO) fuel, often described as
the most robust nuclear fuel on Earth. The company completed production and delivered the first full
core of TRISO fuel for the DOD Strategic Capabilities Office's Project Pele, a 1.5 MW transportable
microreactor. Furthermore, BWXT launched BWXT Advanced Fuels to scale up TRISO and HALEU
production commercially, supporting advanced reactor developers, like Antares Nuclear, which utilized
BWXT-manufactured TRISO to achieve its first reactor criticality under a DOE testing program. The
company's TRISO fuel enabled a new reactor criticality milestone under a DOE program in 2026.

Investment Concerns
Extreme Government Revenue Concentration
The most material concern is customer concentration. The U.S. Government accounted for ~68% of
consolidated 2025 revenue, with sales to the U.S. government representing 89%-91% of Government
Operations segment revenues. This extreme concentration subjects the company to political budget
gridlock, continuing resolutions, debt ceiling limitations, and potential program delays or halts during
government shutdowns. The company is also exposed to severe federal budget uncertainties, delays
from continuing resolutions, and potential program schedule disruptions. Our constructive view could
be undermined by an extended government shutdown, a materially adverse continuing resolution,
shifting federal funding priorities, budget cuts under continuing resolutions, or reprioritization of naval
shipbuilding budgets. On the other hand, the critical importance of the nuclear industry to the DoD,
coupled with the severity of failures in terms of both practical and political impacts, suggests that
BWXT’s operations and customers should be insulated from secular trends towards scaling back the
administrative state and unwinding the related concentrations of expertise: this could give BWXT a
scarcity premium in the 2030s.
 
Commercial Revenue Timing and SMR Deployment Uncertainty
The commercial growth thesis carries meaningful timing risk, representing an active and unresolved
investor debate. The central question is whether North American utilities will place firm, multi-unit
SMR orders before 2030, or whether execution and financing delays will defer meaningful commercial
revenue to the mid-2030s. Expert perspectives indicate that while obtaining construction permits has
become more streamlined, securing a final operating license from the NRC represents a more complex
hurdle. BWXT's aggressive capacity expansions in Canada and the U.S. could suffer under-utilization
and margin dilution if utility SMR projects, such as Darlington or Clinch River, slip. Firm multi-unit
SMR order announcements from tier-one utilities would be the key catalyst validating the bull case;
conversely, deferrals or cancellations of announced SMR projects would falsify the commercial growth
thesis.
 
Near-Term Margin Compression and Capital Intensity
Near-term margins face identifiable drags from the company's capital-intensive business model.
Pioneering micro-reactor programs are initially structured as cost-plus-fixed-fee contracts, which act
as a near-term drag on consolidated operating margins until programs transition to higher-margin
production phases. New national security special materials programs, which represent a significant
portion of near-term growth, initially deliver below-average margins due to front-loaded, customer-
funded capital expenditures required for specialized infrastructure before high-margin execution
milestones can be recognized. Capacity investments—including the $60m Cambridge expansion
and the greenfield Mount Vernon, Indiana plant—compound this near-term pressure until programs
transition to higher-margin production phases.
 
Execution, Supply Chain, and Workforce Constraints
Execution and supply chain risks are non-trivial given the company's highly technical operations. BWXT
relies on several single-source suppliers for critical raw materials and components, which can exert
significant pricing or logistical power. The company faces a nationwide shortage of qualified nuclear
engineers and specialized technicians, and delays in recruiting and retaining this highly credentialed
workforce could lead to schedule overruns. The company is also exposed to input cost volatility
in specialized materials, such as zirconium, as well as potential logistical bottlenecks in procuring
long-lead raw materials. BWXT operates in capital-intensive industries where its business segments
are heavily focused on major, high-consequence projects, and delays in procurement or failures by

Low Probability, High Severity Cybersecurity Threats
As a key defense contractor handling classified and sensitive nuclear propulsion designs, BWXT is a
high-value target for persistent cyber-based attacks from highly-organized state adversaries. Failure to
comply with strict, evolving military standards like the Department of Defense's Cybersecurity Maturity
Model Certification (CMMC) program could lead to contract terminations.
 
Nuclear Build Out Hinges on the Health of the Ecosystem
The domestic HALEU supply build-out remains a critical constraint for Gen IV reactor deployment
and one of the primary bottlenecks facing the advanced nuclear industry. BWXT is positioning
itself to address this challenge through its DUECE and HALEU deconversion contracts with the
NNSA. However, a key open question is whether the company can successfully scale its centrifuge
manufacturing capabilities and secure the necessary enrichment approvals to establish a leading
domestic fuel position, or whether regulatory hurdles and technical complexities will delay the
development of commercial HALEU fuel banks. The Tc-99m product line has already experienced
repeated launch delays as the company perfects product attributes and scales up complex
manufacturing. The commercialization timeline depends on navigating complex, multi-year licensing
approvals from the FDA, and any unexpected regulatory delays or denials could significantly delay
commercialization, impairing capitalized R&D investments. The unresolved question is whether
BWXT can successfully clear the FDA's rigorous approval processes and secure market share from
established incumbents like Curium Pharma, SHINE, and Jubilant, or whether high commercialization
costs will drag down segment returns, prompting a potential divestiture or partnership.
 
Macro Cycle and Demand Drivers
End-market demand for BWXT is driven by a powerful mix of multi-year structural mega-trends and
policy-driven cycles rather than short-term GDP fluctuations. Core demand is driven by the "great power
competition," which accelerates national security spending, and the global push for decarbonization,
and energy security, which has revitalized the nuclear power sector. Short-term cyclical noise typically
relates to the timing of utility maintenance and refueling outages, or the federal budget appropriation
schedules under continuing resolutions.
The transition to a "low trust" geopolitical environment has made national security a top priority. Many
governments are increasing defense spending to modernize nuclear deterrents, build naval fleets,
and secure domestic supply chains. BWXT's U.S. Navy reactor core programs have >30 years of
visibility under the 30-year shipbuilding plan. Surging electricity demand from AI data centers and
semiconductor chip fabs requires massive, continuous baseload power. Hyperscalers are increasingly
committing to nuclear co-location, supporting management-cited expectations for substantial new
nuclear capacity globally by 2050. The life extensions of existing fleets, such as CANDU reactors
in Canada (Darlington, Bruce, and Pickering), represent highly recurring services and component
replacement demands.

At any given time, a relatively small number of major projects can represent a significant portion of

BWXT primarily makes money through the execution of these high-value contracts, which are
structured under both government and commercial frameworks. The company recognizes revenue
over time using a cost-to-cost method to measure progress as control is continually transferred to the
customer. For commercial manufacturing of standard parts, revenue is recognized at the point in time
control transfers to the customer, consistent with the transfer of ownership.
 
The company operates and reports through two primary business segments:
•
Government Operations: This segment engineers, designs, and manufactures precision naval
nuclear components, reactors, and nuclear fuel for the U.S. Naval Nuclear Propulsion Program.
The U.S. Government is the primary customer for this segment, representing ~91% of segment
revenues in 2025. This segment also downblends Cold War-era stockpiles of high-enriched
uranium (HEU), develops capabilities related to high-purity depleted uranium (HPDU), designs
advanced reactors, and fabricates specialized materials for commercial, military, and space
applications. Additionally, the segment provides site management, environmental remediation,
and nuclear materials management services to the DOE, NNSA, and NASA, primarily through
unconsolidated joint ventures.
•
Commercial Operations: This segment fabricates commercial nuclear steam generators, nuclear
fuel, fuel handling systems, pressure vessels, reactor components, heat exchangers, and other
auxiliary equipment for global utility customers. Large utility customers accounted for ~63%
of segment revenues in 2025, principally within the Canadian market. The segment also offers
specialized engineering, in-plant inspection, and maintenance services for nuclear power plants
undergoing major life extension and refurbishment projects. With the Kinectrics acquisition, we
estimate this segment is 60%-65% aftermarket.
 
BWXT possesses unique capabilities that allow it to manufacture and deliver highly complex nuclear
products and services:
•
Naval Nuclear Propulsion: BWXT is the sole-source manufacturer of naval nuclear reactors and
related fuel for U.S. Navy submarines and aircraft carriers. Its components power 100% of the
U.S. Navy's nuclear-powered fleet.
•
Advanced Nuclear Fuels and Microreactors: The company is a pioneer in developing
Tristructural Isotropic (TRISO) fuel, which is designed for passively safe, high-temperature
advanced reactors. In 2026, BWXT said its TRISO fuel enabled the first new reactor criticality
milestone under a DOE program. Additionally, the company is manufacturing the reactor core for
Project Pele, a transportable 1.5 MW microreactor for the Department of Defense.
•
Heavy Commercial Components: BWXT supplies heavy components, including commercial
nuclear steam generators, reactor pressure vessels, and heat exchangers, serving as a merchant
supplier to both large-scale baseload plants and the small modular reactor (SMR) market. It is the
manufacturer of the reactor pressure vessel for the GE Hitachi BWRX-300 SMR in Canada.
•
Nuclear Medicine and Radioisotopes: BWXT Medical manufactures diagnostic isotopes
(including Indium111, Iodine-123, Strontium-82, and Germanium-68) and acts as the exclusive
CDMO for therapeutic products like TheraSphere. The company is currently scaling up its
proprietary Molybdenum-99 (Tc-99) product line and expanding therapeutic isotope capabilities,
such as Actinium-225 and Lutetium-177.
