THE TD COWEN INSIGHT
Spot uranium prices have remained firm over the last few weeks, while term prices hit a record
$96/lb, supported by constructive policy developments and buyer interest despite lower
summer trading volumes (see Pg. 2). Investor attention is turning to next week's World Nuclear
Symposium, where utility procurement activity, fuel-cycle developments, and broader trends
will be closely monitored.
Spot uranium prices edge modestly higher this week to ~$89.60/lb, and are now up ~10%
YTD. As of August 31, spot volumes in 2026 reached 36.9Mlbs across 368 transactions, up
~7% y/y, largely driven by sizable purchases from SPUT earlier this year. Despite modest
transactions volumes through August, prices continued to trend higher as buyers aggressively
sought timely delivery while short-term spot supply remained tight.
Term uranium prices reach a record $96.00/lb, up ~12% YTD. As of August 31, term volumes
exceeded 38Mlbs, down ~15% y/y. Term prices were up $2/lb w/w despite thin volume. Prices
are now at the highest levels we have on record, exceeding the previous high of $95/lb set
in mid-2007 according to data complied by UxC. Considering the significant inflationary
pressures since 2007, there is considerable room for the term and spot price to run if we see
any material shifts in the supply/demand dynamics toward deficits. As a reminder, we are
forecasting LT uranium prices of $95/lb, suggesting upside to our forecasts based on current
term price trends.
The 51st World Nuclear Symposium, hosted by the World Nuclear Association, will be held
in London from September 9-11. In our view, the spot and term volume weakening in the
summer is consistent with historical patterns observed in recent years (see Figures 11/12) as
buyers and sellers became less active in the market. Next week's WNA Symposium, could be
a catalyst to spur increased trading volumes as the event brings together major utilities, fuel
cycle participants, policymakers, and industry leaders. We will be focused on key takeaways
from the conference with utility procurement trends, fuel cycle developments, and nuclear
policy updates likely to be key areas of discussion.

Other Important Market and Policy Developments 
On August 25, the U.S. Department of Energy's National Reactor Innovation Center 
(NRIC) selected 13 additional projects for its Nuclear Energy Launch Pad program, 
materially expanding the pipeline of advanced reactor and fuel-cycle technologies 
receiving federal support. The program provides early-stage projects, including not 
only reactor development but also fuel cycle, enrichment, conversion, or fuel 
fabrication advancement, with a DOE authorization pathway, access to federal sites, 
and national laboratory expertise. The program is designed to accelerate 
commercialization and strengthen domestic supply chain resilience, although project 
owners are responsible for project financing. By reducing technical, licensing, and 
deployment barriers at an early stage, the initiative is expected to improve the 
probability of successful project execution and improve the competitiveness and 
supply chain resilience of the U.S. nuclear industry. 
On August 21, Kazatomprom reported its H1/26 financial results and maintained its 
preliminary 2026 production and sales volume guidance. While the company 
lowered its revenue guidance, it attributed the revision primarily to the appreciation 
of the Kazakh tenge, which increased C1 cash costs and AISC in USD terms as key 
local-currency inputs, including sulfuric acid, become relatively more expensive. 
Separately, Kazatomprom raised its capital expenditure guidance to reflect higher 
sulfuric acid costs and broader cost inflation across its operations.  
More importantly, in our view, Kazatomprom announced a 6-12-month delay to the 
commissioning of its planned TQZ Sulfuric Acid Plant from Q1/27 to a range of 
Q3/27 to Q1/28, following a regulatory suspension of construction. The delay will be 
factored into Kazatomprom's 2027 production guidance. In our view, given the 
importance of sulfuric acid in ISR recovery and uncertainty of the length of delay, the 
ultimate impact of the delay on global uranium supply remains uncertain, but a 
downward revision of uranium output in 2027 is possible. 
On August 14, India's Department of Atomic Energy released draft regulations 
implementing the SHANTI Act, opening the country's nuclear sector to private 
investment for the first time. The regulations will allow private companies to build, 
own, and operate nuclear power plants, while also establishing the associated 
licensing and regulatory requirements. We believe the reforms represent one of the 
most important nuclear policy developments outside the U.S. in recent years, as they 
could potentially unlock additional sources of financing and accelerate deployment 
beyond India's traditional state-led nuclear program. The reforms are also 
supportive of long-term reactor growth and increasingly constructive for uranium 
demand beyond 2030.

VALUATION METHODOLOGY AND RISKS
Valuation Methodology
Metals & Minerals
Our valuation methodology may include two approaches: a relative value approach or an
intrinsic value approach. Our relative value approach relies upon utilizing a blend of P/NAV
and EV/EBITDA multiples, which we consider as the most relevant metrics. Our intrinsic value
approach utilizes discounted cash flow (DCF) methodology as well as our sum-of-the-parts NAV
analysis, which values a company based on its mineral resources and selected balance sheet
items. Mine plan, capital costs, and operating cost assumptions applied in our DCF are based
on published technical reports, our best estimates using our experience and observations from
other projects and information publicly disclosed by management.
We make investment recommendations on certain early stage, pre-revenue companies based
upon an assessment of their asset quality and upside potential, technology, probability
of market success, and the potential market opportunity, balanced by an assessment of
applicable risks. Such companies may not be assigned a price target.
Investment Risks
Metals & Minerals
Technical Risk – Mining projects are all subject to a certain level of technical risks depending on
the nature of the project.
Permitting and Political Risk – Mining projects are subject to the geopolitical risks in their
respective jurisdiction. Project permitting can also be adversely affected by the jurisdiction's
political climate and social issues.
Financial & Forecast Risk – The company's future financial performance is dependent on a
number of assumptions, including metal prices, foreign exchange rates, operating costs, and
capital expenditure levels. The company may require additional funding beyond our forecasts
and there can be no assurance that these funds will be available, or at an acceptable cost.
Environmental Risk – Risks assessed include water and waste recycle rates, electricity and
energy from renewables, emissions and energy intensity, scope 1 & 2 & 3 emissions, greenhouse
emission targets, reclamation and tailings targets, environmental incident counts and
environmental certifications, and preventative practices and monitoring.
Social Risk – Both national and local community support are critical to successfully building
and operating a mine. Some of the key elements to assess include workplace safety metrics,
community investment, economic value generated and distributed, procurement from local
vendors, percentage of costs spent with local suppliers and good stewardship of the local
environment.
