How will deposit cost trend amidst heightened competition and incremental rate hikes? 
 
 
While we expect deposit yield to remain elevated, driven by mixshift and rising 
competition, we believe this will be offset by stronger asset growth and repricing, as 
the company expects NIM to stabilize in 3Q/4Q26E. Specifically, we see three drivers 
of deposit cost pressure, which has driven WFC’s deposit cost closer to peer levels 
(Exhibit 14): 1) WFC deposit mix has shifted toward higher yielding commercial and 
corporate deposits following the lifting of the asset cap, as the bank regains balances it 
previously had to limit (Exhibit 11). Further, as loan growth continued to outpace core 
deposit growth, the company has supplemented funding with brokered corporate 
deposits, which are more expensive and priced at near Fed Funds (Exhibit 12, Exhibit 
13); 2) industry deposit competition has remained elevated due to rapid expansion of 
large bank balance sheets (Exhibit 10); 3) the higher for longer rate environment also 
presents upward pricing pressure, with elevated inflation data pointing to higher likely 
hood of hike(s) in the near term. However, we expect the rising deposit costs to be offset 
by stronger asset growth and repricing, as the company expect its NIM to stabilize 
through the balance of 2026E, after a ~15bp decline in 1H26 (Exhibit 15). 
In addition, even after incorporating higher deposit costs, we believe an 
incremental hike has net neutral effect on 2026E/27E NII. We set out an illustrative 
downside rate sensitivity analysis for an additional Sept. hike using historical cycle betas, 
while also accounting for a more intense deposit pricing backdrop. Overall, we believe 
the impact of a hike would be largely net neutral to both 2026E and 2027E NII, with 
higher deposit costs offset by higher asset yields, as the bank remain slightly asset 
sensitive all else equals (Exhibit 16, Exhibit 17). For 2026E in particular, our analysis 
implies the impact is deminimis given the late year timing of any potential hikes.

What is the overall incremental return from lower yielding capital markets asset growth? 
 
 
In 2026TD, WFC has meaningfully expanded its capital markets balance sheet, with 
repo balance/trading asset up ~40%/25% YoY. While these assets carry lower 
spreads and are therefore NIM dilutive, in our view, the associated fee income uplift 
supports an attractive marginal return profile, and translate to continued capital 
markets share gains. We set out an illustrative analysis comparing the overall 
revenue yield from deploying incremental balance sheet into capital markets assets 
vs. loan growth, where we find that capital market deployment offers a slightly 
stronger (10bps) incremental revenue yield (Exhibit 28). After applying a steeper 
funding costs closer to benchmark rates, we calculate incremental spread on capital

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
