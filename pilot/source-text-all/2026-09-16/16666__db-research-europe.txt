AENA  (AENA.MC) - Dora III tariffs better than we feared; u/g to Hold; Sell to Hold, Tgt EUR20 to EUR24. 
Last Close EUR25. 
Harishankar Ramamoorthy: We upgrade Aena from Sell to Hold, with our price target revised to €24 (€20 
earlier) as the regulatory risk we have been cautious on has failed to materialise. News reports from Spain 
highlight that the Council of Ministers has approved Aena's Dora III investment programme (of c. €13bn in 
total), while allowing for a tariff increase of +0.33% each year for the Spanish network. The reports suggest 
that traffic projections under Dora III go from 321m in 2025 to 346m in 2031 (a CAGR of +1.3%). We had 
estimated tariffs c.-2% each year based on a stronger pax growth assumption at a CAGR 2025-31 of c. +3.5%; 
Aena's YTD pax growth in Spain is +4.1%. Overall, we think the implied return allowed on RAB under Dora III is 
likely ahead of 8% (Aena's ask 9%, CNMC views at 7.4%); this appears generous. 
  
AMADEUS  (AMA.MC) - Owning the Journey, Not the Search - Upgrade to Buy; Hold to Buy, Tgt EUR58 
to EUR71.5. Last Close EUR54.04. Buy. 
Nooshin Nejati: We upgrade Amadeus as we believe the market is pricing the GDS business as a low-value 
infrastructure layer, assuming AI agents and direct connectivity will structurally pressure both volumes and 
  


[Page 3]
pricing. We think that is too harsh following discussions with key Amadeus executives as well as our own agentic 
AI experiments around travel booking. AI can change where travel demand starts and increase search intensity, 
but it does not remove the need for supplier connectivity, fulfillment, servicing, disruption management, 
corporate workflow or Airline IT. In our view, investors are extrapolating too quickly from front-end disruption 
to full-stack earnings pressure. The stock works if Amadeus remains the execution layer behind a changing 
interface. We believe that outcome is significantly more likely than current market expectations imply. Buy. 
  
EUROPEAN BEVERAGES  - A future with a few cheers; Recommendation and Target Price Changes: 
Anheuser-Busch InBev - Hold to Buy, tgt EUR72 to EUR79, Carlsberg - Buy, tgt DKK1220 to DKK1160, Coca-
Cola Europacific Partners - Buy, tgt USD112 to USD117, Coca-Cola Europacific Partners - Buy, tgt 8480p to 
8725p, Coca-Cola Hellenic - Buy, tgt 5700p to 5725p, Campari Group - Hold to Buy, tgt EUR6.2 to EUR7.1, 
Diageo - Hold, tgt 1770p to 1734p, Heineken - Hold, tgt EUR80 to EUR76, Pernod Ricard - Hold, tgt EUR60 to 
EUR61, Royal Unibrew - Hold, tgt DKK425 to DKK420. 
Mitch Collett: European Beverages continue to experience bifurcation due to a combination of structural 
challenges, cyclical headwinds and idiosyncratic factors. With our chartbook, published today, we refresh our 
sector analysis for recent data and key trends. We remain cautious on Spirits, selective on Beer and constructive 
on Soft Drinks. We upgrade ABI and Campari to BUY as a result. We continue to believe Beverage Alcohol 
growth in developed markets faces headwinds from 1) Health; 2) Younger consumers; 3) GLP-1; 4) Cannabis. 
In addition, Spirits face headwinds from RTD growth. At the same time soft drinks appear to go from strength. 
We update our medium and long term DCF assumptions for our coverage as a result. 
 
 
  
  
KIER GROUP PLC  (KIE.L) - High teens TSR opportunity; Tgt 295p to 330p. Last Close 257.6p. Buy. 
Jonathan Coubrough: Kier has reported 10% EPS growth in FY26 and upgraded its medium-term targets to 
include a double-digit EPS cagr. New Strategic Priorities are to focus on the core Infrastructure and Construction 
businesses, target >£200m average net cash by FY29 while winding down Property and repaying the bond, and 
enhance quality of earnings. We view the 10x PE multiple as highly attractive for a double-digit EPS cagr 
opportunity, in addition to a 6% distribution yield. Buy. We estimate circa half the double-digit medium-term 
EPS cagr can be delivered by holding the margin on the upgraded mid-single-digit revenue growth target, 
underpinned by growth across water, defence, energy and healthcare markets. We estimate the other half can 
be delivered by interest cost savings, with the 9% coupon £250m bond due Feb-29. 
  
NORDIC BANKS  - Mind the (narrowing) gap; Target Price Changes: Danske Bank - Buy, tgt DKK388 to 
DKK409, DNB - Hold, tgt NOK298 to NOK307, Nordea - Buy, tgt EUR19 to EUR20, SEB - Hold, tgt SEK190 to 
SEK197, Handelsbanken - Hold, tgt SEK135 to SEK140, Swedbank - Buy, tgt SEK388 to SEK419. 
Marlene Eibensteiner: Nordic banks continue to trade at a premium to the broader European banks sector, 
underpinned by superior cost efficiency and asset quality, as well as solid capital return profiles. However, the 
gap has narrowed notably as improving profitability and earnings momentum elsewhere have supported a 
broader sector re-rating. Currently trading at a more moderate premium (11.2x PE '27E), while maintaining 
attractive profitability levels (17.5% ROTE) and solid capital returns (7.5% yield), qualify Nordea as our Nordics 


[Page 4]
top pick. Nordic banks have historically commanded a certain valuation premium to the broader European 
banks sector, reflecting a combination of resilience, superior asset quality and cost efficiency, as well as strong 
profitability and capital returns, in our view. While many of these attributes remain intact, improving 
profitability and earnings growth elsewhere have supported a broader European bank sector re-rating, 
narrowing the relative Nordic appeal to some extent. Our valuation analysis suggests, that the aggregate Nordic 
PE premium has narrowed to 13% vs. the 18% five year average, even more pronounced on a P/BV level with 
currently 15% vs. a five year average of 39%.

DIVFIN  - Review of "AI in Wealth Management - Disrupter or Enabler" expert speaker call 


[Page 6]
David McCann: We recently hosted an expert speaker call on the topic: AI in Wealth Management - Disrupter 
or Enabler? We highlight our key takeaways and thoughts from the event itself and potential implications for 
specific companies. We also provide an edited transcript of the key speaker comments and a link to the event 
full video replay. Our key takeaways are: 1) the adviser productivity opportunity may be larger than expected, 
2) winners and losers are not yet determined (and the future industry could include incumbents, adjacent 
businesses expanding and new entrants), 3) regulatory change may matter as much as AI itself and 4) the 
biggest long-term threats could come from outside the industry. Our key conclusion is that AI is not yet 
changing competitive outcomes, but the foundations of future competitive advantage are being laid.

EUROPEAN REAL ESTATE  - German Residential: Berlin election – housing socialization scenarios 
Thomas Rothaeusler: he upcoming Berlin state elections on September 20 will be a crucial event for the 
German Residential sector and specifically for Berlin-focused players, as two major parties claim for a 
socialization of private rental housing stock. While capital markets are aware of the risk and therefore have 
already largely priced it in, and despite actual physical socializations being unlikely, prolonged regulatory 
uncertainty remains structurally damaging. As long as the current political rhetoric remains active, it’s likely to 
continue to drag on the sector’s performance. We’ve drafted various scenarios with a potential CDU-SPD-The 
Greens government in Berlin and a ban of socializations as the two best-case outcomes for the sector. A Left-
wing government in Berlin passing a socialization law and a referendum on a legally binding draft law with a 
positive outcome would be the two worst-case scenarios. Net-net, we see an outcome slightly skewed to the 
positive currently. Among the players with the highest Berlin exposure are Vonovia, Heimstaden, ADLER Group 
and Covivio. 
  
EUROPEAN STAPLES  - Brand and deliver 
Mitch Collett: We believe A&P / sales is a key aspect of stock selection for Consumer Staples. Since 1998, 
Staples companies that increase A&P/sales YoY have outperformed those that decrease A&P sales by 122% (an 
annual CAGR of 300bps). In addition, companies that increase A&P / sales have consistently outperformed 
those that decrease for operational delivery with organic sales growth 80bps higher, organic EBIT growth 250bp 
higher and EPS growth 500bps higher. During 2023-25, larger players have lost share and smaller players have 
gained share in 6/6 staples categories in the US and 4/6 staples categories in Western Europe. The shift towards 


[Page 7]
smaller players appears to have become a more meaningful headwind from 2022 which implies a broad based 
deterioration in competitive dynamics. The continuing threat from smaller players makes brand investment 
crucial to success. 
  
STEEL SPREAD TRACKER  - US spreads continue to move higher 
Bastian Synagowitz: European demand remains soft and the conflict in the Middle East remains a lingering 
headwind for the broader economy. Nevertheless, Western prices and margins remain strong and EU prices 
continued to rebound after the start of the new EU safeguard tool. This marks a significant step up in trade 
protection and should support a sustained rebound in European spreads (with CBAM adding to that). Hence, 
we keep our constructive view on the sector. Our top picks in carbon steel are: thyssenkrupp (TKAG.DE, Buy; 
EUR15.01; TP: EUR18.0) on possible catalysts and policy tailwinds in steel, and Voestalpine. 
  
UK FOOD RETAIL  - Back-to-school reset slows grocery growth 
Benjamin Yokyong-Zoega: Worldpanel data showed total take home sales slowing to +2.0% in the four weeks 
to 6th September +2.5% in the prior period to 9th August, while inflation stepped up +20bps to 2.3%. Sales 
growth was supported by inflation, but fewer shopping trips reflected a post-summer return to school and 
work routines. On a 12 week basis, Tesco continued to cede market share (-30bps yoy; -30bps previously), 
while Sainsbury's market share was flat yoy (+10bps previously). M&S growth was robust at +14.8% vs +15.9% 
in July as Cyber impacts wash through. Overall the data is mixed for TSCO (interims on 8th Oct) and SBRY (22nd 
Oct) but positive for MKS (4th Nov). 
  
 
 
 
 
  
ENPHASE ENERGY  (ENPH.OQ) - Fireside Chat Conclusions: An 'inevitable' transition to 800V DC; Tgt 
USD37. Last Close USD36.72. Hold. 
Corinne Blanchard: We hosted a Fireside Chat with ENPH Co-founder and Chief Products Officer, Raghu Belur. 
We discussed ENPH’s IQ solid-state transformer (SST) for AI data centers, the transition to 800V DC, technical 
differentiation and potential to extend the platform into adjacent applications. Key Takeaways: Overall, the 
tone was more optimistic than last week’s expert call we hosted with Hervé Tardy. Adoption of 800V DC 
architectures is ‘inevitable’ and should be driven by power density, efficiency and data-center build rather than 
any specific GPU transition, according to Raghu. Adoption timing is as fast as SST is available. The core potential 
differentiator and competitive advantage for ENPH is in its single-stage, distributed architecture, with sub-
millisecond response time.
