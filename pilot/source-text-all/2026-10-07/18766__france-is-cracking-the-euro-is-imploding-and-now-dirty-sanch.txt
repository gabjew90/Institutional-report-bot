Dirty Sanchez
OAT/Bund spread acceleration and fiscal anxiety are clearly bleeding into the currency.
And the political backdrop just got another complication. Pedro Sánchez has called a snap Spanish
election for November 29 after his minority government suffered another major parliamentary defeat.
Spain itself isn’t a huge tail risk, but it adds to the broader sense of political instability across Europe.
France remains the real focus. OAT/Bund has blown out as markets increasingly question the fiscal
trajectory, with French debt now around 119% of GDP and the 10-year spread trading at levels not
seen since the euro crisis.
The key this week is whether any of the alternative French candidates can produce something
resembling a credible path back toward fiscal sustainability. Spain adds noise, but France remains the
market’s fiscal stress test.

The gas connection
European gas has eased somewhat lately, but it hasn't been enough to slow the euro sell-off.
Europe remains the global energy price taker, so any renewed energy stress hits the region
disproportionately hard. For now, even the modest relief in gas isn't giving the currency much help.

25-year extreme
GS's Narain calls the current move the most extreme tightening in European financial conditions in 25
years, with echoes of both the 2011 sovereign crisis and the Taper Tantrum.

The irony is that the market may now be doing the ECB's job for it. GS estimates the tightening in
financial conditions since July is equivalent to roughly half an additional 25bp hike and could shave
around 0.4pp from growth.
That materially reduces the urgency for another ECB hike, even as French political/fiscal stress, higher
US yields and the energy shock keep pressure on European assets.
The next question is TPI, basically the ECB's circuit breaker for sovereign spreads. If the sell-off
becomes sufficiently disorderly, the ECB can step in and buy bonds. The hurdle should be lower for an
"innocent bystander" such as Spain than for France, where the widening reflects genuine fiscal
concerns.
The market is doing the ECB's tightening for it. The question is whether it eventually does
too much.
