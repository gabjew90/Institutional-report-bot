DB CoTD: Keep Calm and Muddle On 
 
Our latest global survey results are out, free to all at the DBRI here. One question, highlighted in today's 
CoTD, asked respondents to rank six ways the US might ultimately deal with its debt burden. On the 
headline measure, muddling through indefinitely wins comfortably: 40% put it first, almost double the 23% 
who chose inflation. But re-cut the same answers by looking at both ends of the ranking and the picture 
gets more interesting. 
 
As we can see from the second chart, inflation is actually the more consensual answer. 57% rank it first or 
second versus 55% for muddling through, only 7% put it in the bottom two (13% for muddling through), 
and just 14% rank it fourth or lower against 28% for muddling through. To be fair it could be less either/or, 
and more a question of sequencing: muddle along until inflation quietly does the heavy lifting. 

 
Stronger growth is a respectable third (45% top two), which, given the arithmetic required, is either a vote 
of confidence in AI-driven productivity or an act of faith. Possibly both. 
At the other end, fiscal consolidation is almost friendless. Just 4% see it as the most likely route and 49% 
put it in the bottom two, a worse showing than financial repression, which at least splits opinion evenly 
(29% top two, 29% bottom two). Only default or restructuring ranks lower, with 92% placing it fifth or sixth. 
Indeed, this answer is way behind the other five. 
For me I would have put inflation, financial repression and fiscal consolidation in the top 3. Ultimately, I 
think bond vigilantes will put a cap on deficits, even in the US, and the Fed will probably own more bonds 
again. None of this is imminent and therefore there is time for AI-driven productivity growth. However, it 
would need to be significant to move the needle.
