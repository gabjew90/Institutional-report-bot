Arms folded, waiting for more
Investment Recommendation
The R2 has arrived, and with it, Rivian’s finest delivery quarter on record.
Sometimes fortune smiles on an automaker. In this case, it was the serendipity of
high fuel prices meeting an automotive market where the cost of ordinary family
transport has drifted into the stratosphere. Not to mention a paucity of true EV
competion in the US. Against that backdrop, the R2’s pricing doesn't merely look
competitive--it looks like common sense, in our opinion. The reviews have been fairly
glowing, the buzz genuine. It feels like a magnificent setup.
The appetite is there, and it is gathering speed, we think. Rivian launched with
a single, top-flight Launch Edition at $57k, and buyers have stepped up to that
higher sticker faster than the company anticipated. Over the next year, the ladder
extends downward - a mid-tier variant in the mid-$50ks, followed by standard trims
from $45k, settling into a blended average selling price around $50k, according to
management.
And this appears to be not just a duel with Tesla’s Model Y. The R2 is poaching
traditionalists from the combustion heartland, according to management, as drivers
stepping out of RAV4s, 4Runners, Broncos, CR-Vs, and Audi Q5. Test-drive volumes
have more than doubled, serving as Rivian’s primary metric for converting first-time
EV buyers.
As Tesla leans heavily into full self-driving (FSD), Rivian is preparing its own
response: “point-to-point” autonomy, rolling out to early adopters late this year on
R1 Gen 2 and newer R2 hardware before broadening via Autonomy+ next year. We
watch.
The friction, for now, lies within. Assembly lines can hum only as fast as their
humblest link, and CEO RJ Scaringe has made it clear: speed is dictated by the
slowest-ramping Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers. A second labor shift is slated to clock in
later this month, choreographed with care to match incoming parts and avoid swollen
inventory.
Of note, management held yearly deliveries guidance firm.
With the R2, Rivian has caught lightning in a bottle, in our opinion: a chef’s kiss with
styling, feature density, democratic pricing, and fortunate timing.
Now comes the hard, unglamorous part. Go build (more of) the thing - and build it
at a profit. Ramp it. The market, arms folded, is waiting before it is willing to re-write
the stock’s valuation.

• Initial R2 reviews largely positive. Following strong early feedback on
prototypes, reviews of the production-intent R2 have continued to reinforce
enthusiasm around the vehicle. Last month, InsideEVs published a positive review,
stating that "the company struck me right through the heart. With an unparalleled
blend of incredible performance, cutting-edge software, and off-road chops, the
R2 is the do-everything machine I've always dreamed of. I want one, badly." The
publication went on to argue that "legacy automakers have been promising to match
the Tesla Model Y for almost a decade. Rivian actually did it," highlighting the R2's
combination of performance, technology, utility, and value. More broadly, the review
suggests Rivian may have successfully translated the strengths of the premium
R1 platform into a more affordable mass-market vehicle - comparable to Tesla's
Model Y - potentially expanding the company's addressable market and supporting a
meaningful increase in unit volumes over time.
• Advanced technological features. In addition to favorable driving
impressions, the review highlighted Rivian's software ecosystem and technology
stack, including a new onboard charger capable of bidirectional charging,
allowing owners to send up to 11kW of AC power from the vehicle directly to a
home or the grid without requiring an external inverter. The feature makes the
R2 one of the first vehicles in the U.S. to offer true vehicle-to-X capabilities and
could become an increasingly valuable differentiator as distributed energy and
backup power applications gain traction.
• Early consumer demand off to a strong start - with supply chains posing the
biggest threat. Last night, Axios reported comments from Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe
indicating that demand for the upcoming R2 remains strong, with the company
seeing "tons of demand" heading into launch. However, management highlighted
growing concerns around the availability of critical memory chips, a supply constraint
that echoes semiconductor shortages that complicated the R1 production ramp
several years ago. While Rivian remains confident in customer interest, management
emphasized that the primary uncertainty is no longer demand, but rather the
company's ability to secure enough components to meet production targets.
• According to CEO RJ Scaringe, "I think the biggest risk for R2 is actually on
the supply side, and there's a lot of unknowns here... the whole semiconductor
space is gonna be quite constrained. So, by far my biggest worry about ramping
R2 isn't demand. It's, you know, can we get enough parts to build cars? We're
quite nervous about global supply."
• >50% total cost reduction relative to R1. According to management, "for R2,
our bill of materials is expected to be approximately half of our R1 platform. For
non-BOM cost of sales, we expect to see a reduction of more than 50% resulting
from a focus on design for manufacturing, and leverage fixed-cost efficiencies
through higher production volumes. This is how we expect to profitably deliver R2 at
an accessible price point at scale without compromising performance and utility."

Rivian's autonomous overhaul
A robotaxi power couple. On March 19th, Uber and Rivian announced a partnership
expected to "accelerate both companies’ autonomous vehicle plans." According to
the agreement, Uber will invest up to $1.25B in Rivian through 2031, subject to the
achievement of specified autonomous milestones. An initial $300M investment has
been committed following signing, subject to regulatory approvals. In addition, Uber,
or Uber's fleet partners, will take a phased approach to fully autonomous R2 robotaxi
deployments made exclusively available on Uber's network. Uber is expected to pay
usage-based software licensing fees in connection with Rivian's Level 4 autonomous
driving system.
• Robotaxi rollout: phase 1. 10k R2 robotaxis will be deployed on Uber's network
as part of Phase 1, with initial deployments beginning in San Francisco and Miami in
2028.
• Robotaxi rollout: phase 2. Uber has the option to add an incremental 40k Rivian
robotaxis to its network in 2030. The company's plan to scale the partnership to 25
cities by 2031. "Should all milestones be achieved, the companies will have deployed
thousands of unsupervised Rivian R2 robotaxis across 25 cities in the US, Canada,
and Europe by the end of 2031."

Step-by-step model improvements. In December, at the company's inaugural AI
& Autonomy Day (see our note), management anticipated model improvements and
associated launch timing – “a series of steps.”
• Universal hands-free: launched in 4Q25. Last quarter, Rivian released "universal
hands-free," expanding the North American R1 Gen2 “hands-free” operating domain
to >3.5M (from <150k miles of road).
• Autonomy+. Universal hands-free autonomous capabilities will be bundled into
the Rivian Autonomy+ platform. Customers can pay a one-time $2,500 fee, or ~
$50/ month to access such features. Prior to March 2026, Universal hands-free

• Point-to-point: rolling out in 2026. This year, Rivian plans to begin rolling out
“point-to-point” capabilities, meaning vehicles can drive directly from one address to
another. Point-to-point will be embedded in R2 vehicles.
• “Eyes-off": reliant on Gen3 hardware. Rivian’s next autonomous capability
milestone is “eyes-off” autonomous driving. “Eyes-off” means “you can navigate
point-to-point with your hands off the wheel, but, importantly, your eyes off the
road. This gives you your time back. You can be on your phone or reading a book,
no longer needing to be actively involved in the operation of vehicle.” Management
refrained from providing specific timing guidance but noted “eyes-off” functionality is
reliant on Gen3 hardware architecture - slated to launch on R2 vehicles in late-2026.
• Personal L4. The final autonomy milestone is referred to as personal level 4 (L4);
“with this, the vehicle will operate entirely on its own. This means it can drop the
kids off at school, it can pick you up from the airport. It allows it [autonomy] to
really be integrated into life in ways that we can’t imagine, [in ways we] haven't
seen yet today.”
• While management has refrained from providing personal Level 4 launch timing,
level 4 capabilities underpin robotaxi operations. Therefore, in partnership
with Uber, the company is targeting personal Level 4 rollouts in 2028, if not
sooner. We view this - potential - rollout schedule as accelerated relative to prior
expectations.

Level 3+: increasingly sticky. According to CEO, RJ Scaringe, on our webcast, Rivian
is "extremely bullish on just how sticky Level 3 and Level 4 [will be]... getting hands-off
and eyes-off... really becomes a sticky experience, different than Level 2, where you
truly start to get your time back. We think that the shift is going to drive disruption...
to market share, such that companies that have Level 3, and soon Level 4, capabilities
relative to companies that don't, will create a significant migration of customers."
3 ways to monetize L3+ autonomous systems. Mr. Scaringe continued, "there's
really 3 ways we think about monetizing this technology area. One is we grab more
market share, we sell more vehicles. The second is new business models, [like]
robotaxis... that's just one of what [business models] will become. We think many
new ways of consumability [will emerge] once the vehicles are capable of driving
themselves. And then the third, of course, is by selling the technology to other
manufacturers which don't have it."
• Rivian to license the autonomy platform to other OEMs. While timing on
autonomous system licensing remains elusive, management is "extremely bullish on
it. We do think there's going to be opportunities [to license autonomous systems]
for other manufacturers that do not have the technology... we're demonstrating, vis-
à-vis our relationship with Volkswagen, that [we are capable of] this truly singular
ability to deploy software across a complex portfolio of vehicles within existing,
incumbent OEMs. That's that's a skill set in and of itself, being able to deploy
electronics and software at scale across a complex, large, existing OEM. That's the
existence proof that it not only can happen for base software and associated ECUs,
but it can also happen for self-driving and the associated perception and compute
platforms."
Introducing the Gen-3 autonomy platform. Also during the December AI &
Autonomy Day, Rivian unveiled the Gen-3 autonomy platform, expected to launch on
North American R2 vehicles in late-2026. The Gen-3 autonomy platform features 11
cameras (65 megapixels), 5 radar and 1 LiDAR. At the core of the platform are 2 RAP1
chips - Rivian's first, in-house autonomy processor.
• A non-trivial development effort. Similar to ECU vertical integration rationale,
Rivian is pursuing in-house silicon chip development to enhance velocity and
performance while minimizing cost.
The largest autonomous system cost driver: compute. Management discussed
sensor cost structure, noting the cost of lidar, specifically, is "now significantly lower
than... a lot of folks believe it to be." Mr. Scaringe continued, "this whole idea that
sensors are competing... it's no longer relevant in an AI-centric way of developing self-
driving. The biggest cost drivers is not the sensors. This is, I think, the biggest missed
point. It's the compute, it's the inference, and it's because of that, that we made the
decision to bring inference in-house."

Research Analysts may, from time to time, discuss “short-term trade ideas” in research reports. A short-term trade idea offers a
near-term view on how a security may trade, based on market and trading events or catalysts, and the resulting trading opportunity
that may be available. Any such trading strategies are distinct from and do not affect the analysts' fundamental equity rating for
such stocks. A short-term trade idea may differ from the price targets and recommendations in our published research reports that
reflect the research analyst's views of the longer-term (i.e. one-year or greater) prospects of the subject company, as a result of the
differing time horizons, methodologies and/or other factors. It is possible, for example, that a subject company's common equity that
is considered a long-term ‘Hold' or 'Sell' might present a short-term buying opportunity as a result of temporary selling pressure in
the market or for other reasons described in the research report; conversely, a subject company's stock rated a long-term 'Buy' or
“Speculative Buy’ could be considered susceptible to a downward price correction, or other factors may exist that lead the research
analyst to suggest a sale over the short-term. Short-term trade ideas are not ratings, nor are they part of any ratings system, and
the firm does not intend, and does not undertake any obligation, to maintain or update short-term trade ideas. Short-term trade
ideas are not suitable for all investors and are not tailored to individual investor circumstances and objectives, and investors should
make their own independent decisions regarding any securities or strategies discussed herein. Please contact your salesperson for
more information regarding Canaccord Genuity’s research.
