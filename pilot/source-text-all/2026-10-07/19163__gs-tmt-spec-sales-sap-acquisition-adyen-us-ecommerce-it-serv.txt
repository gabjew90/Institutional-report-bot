GS TMT SPEC SALES – Wednesday 7th October
US markets edged higher despite softer Asian trading; semiconductor investors rotated from equipment
into Marvell and optical names; memory pricing firmed; and software updates remained supportive.
Semiconductors and memory: Investors rotated from crowded equipment names into Marvell and optical
stocks, including Nokia. Marvell (see Jim’s take) rose after lifting its FY28 revenue outlook to $20bn and
outlining strong long-term AI growth, with Ciena up 13%, while AMD plans to increase chip supply
significantly in 2027 amid sustained AI demand and tight memory capacity. Nanya’s proposed DRAM
price increase of up to 20% points to a firmer memory market, while HDD stocks fell on reports that
Toshiba may bid several billion dollars for TDK’s magnetic-head business, raising competition concerns.
Software: HubSpot announced a restructuring affecting about 7% of staff but reaffirmed 3Q and FY
guidance, while Atlassian expanded its OpenAI partnership to access the latest frontier models.
Company Updates
SAP to Acquire TechWolf. TechWolf focuses on workforce data and skills intelligence, using its
proprietary data model called context graph for work, working from existing HR and business systems.
SAP’s current plans are for TechWolf to remain as an independent entity under CEO Andreas De Neve in
its Ghent headquarters, with offices in London, New York and San Francisco, and for its platform to
remain available to both SAP and non-SAP customers. The transaction is expected to close in Q4 2026.

AI Infrastructure
AI credit: SpaceX tapping Apollo for $40bn SPV debt collateralised by chips, 16 weeks after the IPO. Not
directly a semiconductor fundamental, but it shows how much financing the AI buildout requires in a
rising-rate market. SpaceX is seeking about $10bn in bank loans and $30bn in investment-grade debt to
fund the chips.

More data-centre delays are making press headlines: after Jupiter, Oracle's 1.3GW Wisconsin data centre
is the latest project to face major delays.
Anthropic reportedly began a custom AI chip programme with Broadcom using Samsung’s 2nm process,
targeting an initial 200k-unit ramp to reduce costs and avoid TSMC capacity constraints, while it is also
considering a large Google TPU allocation for 2027. The update is positive for Samsung Foundry and
reinforces Broadcom’s custom-chip momentum, but volumes are unlikely to materially affect TSMC in
2026–27 and Google TPUs remain Anthropic’s main near-term silicon option.
Mistral AI: France’s Mistral has launched Mistral Large 4, a 1tn-parameter multimodal model designed to
compete with leading US and Chinese systems while offering a European alternative. The model was
trained on just 4,000 Nvidia GPUs, highlighting strong compute efficiency, and its weights are expected to
be released after safety testing. Mistral is targeting enterprise applications including cybersecurity, finance
and chip design, with backing from ASML and Samsung.
Policy & Regulation
Taxing US tech: The European Commission is considering a broad corporate levy that would capture large
US tech groups such as Apple, Google and Meta without introducing a targeted digital tax. Under a
revised CORE framework, companies operating in the EU with more than €100mn of global annual
revenue could pay a fixed yearly contribution of €100,000–€750,000, depending on size. Applying the
charge across sectors is intended to raise EU budget revenue while reducing the risk of US retaliation, but
the proposal faces a difficult path because it requires unanimous approval from member states.
AI Applications
New AI assistants: Hark and Underdog are the latest challengers to Muse, Instinct and Dots, with both
emphasising privacy. Hark Pro acts as an AI interface for a user’s computer, handling tasks across email,
calendars, files and payments while showing how it navigates the web to build trust. Underdog takes a
more privacy-first approach by running entirely on-device, keeping personal data local and avoiding cloud
inference costs; it will initially be free and ad-free, with revenue coming from a small fee on transactions
completed through Stripe.
Website blocking: The bigger challenge is whether AI agents can access the websites needed to complete
tasks such as shopping, booking flights or making reservations. Amazon has deliberately blocked Meta’s
Muse, while other agents are often stopped by conventional anti-bot and human-verification systems,
creating frustration for users and retailers. Meta, Walmart, Stripe and other industry partners are therefore
developing an open commerce standard to help businesses distinguish legitimate agents acting for
customers from malicious bots, which could be essential for wider adoption.
Worth a listen - Invest Like the Best speaks with neuroscientist Andrew Huberman about the convergence
of AI, biotechnology and neuroscience. His central view is that leading AI companies such as Meta,
OpenAI and Anthropic could increasingly move into biotech as the next frontier shifts beyond LLMs
towards technologies that can read and influence the nervous system. Early applications may include
smarter sleep devices and wearables that move from simply tracking physiology to actively improving

focus, relaxation and sleep, while Neuralink and less-invasive technologies such as vagus-nerve
stimulators represent longer-term routes to directly interfacing with the brain and nervous system.

TECH
• Adyen 3Q preview: Mo expects 3Q revenue growth of 21.5% YoY ex-FX and keeps FY26 growth at
22.2% yoy ex-fx (21.2% yoy ex-fx organic), which is modestly above Visible Alpha Consensus, with
guidance viewed as conservative. M&A dilution trims FY27 EBITDA margin to 52.0%, but medium-term
growth should be supported by customer wallet-share gains, new wins, product launches, embedded
finance and agentic commerce, consistent with feedback from the Communacopia + Technology
Conference 2026. Following recent share-price weakness, valuation looks attractive at about 18x 2027E
P/E; Mo remains Buy with a new €1,750 price target.
• US eCommerce Q3 Preview - Ahead of Q3’26 eCommerce earnings reporting, we address key industry
themes and debates and refresh our operating estimates based on industry data and channel checks. After a
year characterized by macro uncertainty, shifting consumer spending patterns, and debates around both the
health of the consumer and the future impact of AI, we believe investors enter earnings season focused on
three core questions: 1) Where does the digital consumer stand today? 2) How will the emergence of
agentic commerce and action-based agents ultimately reshape the competitive landscape? 3) How do we
expect these dynamics to play out during the upcoming holiday season?
• IT Services 3Q preview: Results are expected to be broadly in line, with 2026 guidance largely
unchanged. However, weak bookings and delayed client decisions since April–May are likely to lead
companies to lower 2027 growth expectations and modest cuts to consensus estimates. Investor sentiment
remains cautious but has improved slightly following the sector’s rebound from recent lows and
Accenture’s solid results.

• Orange: Orange is in advanced talks to acquire Telecel Group’s 70% stake in Telecel Ghana, marking its
first entry into the Ghanaian market and expanding its presence in Anglophone Africa. The deal supports
Orange’s 2026–30 strategy to add around 40mn mobile and fixed customers by 2028, with Africa and the
Middle East as key growth drivers. Discussions could conclude by end-2026, subject to regulatory and
local approvals; the Ghanaian government would retain its 30% stake. (Universfreebox)

• AT&T: AT&T is forming a US wholesale, open-access fibre joint venture with BlackRock’s GIP and CPP
Investments, combining Gigapower with the fibre assets acquired from Lumen. AT&T will own 50% of
the business, which will initially cover nearly 5mn locations and more than 1mn subscribers across 16

close in 1H27, subject to approval, with proceeds helping fund investment and reduce leverage towards
2.5x net debt to adjusted EBITDA. (Telecom Paper)
