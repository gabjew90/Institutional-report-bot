What happened last week?
U.S. Treasuries weakened as fiscal financing concerns outweighed lower oil prices and reduced 
expectations for near-term Fed tightening. Better-than-expected PCE inflation and cautious Fed 
commentary lowered October hike odds, while Friday’s weaker-than-expected jobs report 
reinforced expectations for a pause. October hike probabilities fell to roughly 22% from 64% a 
week earlier, although markets continued to price another increase by year-end. The shift in 
policy expectations supported the front end, while longer maturities remained under pressure as 
investors demanded greater compensation for inflation, fiscal, and supply risks. The 10-year 
yield rose roughly 10 bp on the week, while the 30-year yield reached 5.63%, producing a sharp 
steepening of the curve.
Explaining the yield curve steepening
•
Softer PCE inflation, dovish comments from Williams and Jefferson, and a 29k payroll gain 
versus 90k expected sharply reduced October hike odds. (Front end)
•
September ISM showed rising input costs, while AI investment, government spending, and 
freight pressures kept longer-term inflation risks elevated. (Long end)

The Week Ahead: Data/Events/Observations
•
Key Economic Releases/Events: Attention will remain 
centered on any progress toward de-escalating the conflict in 
the Middle East. In the U.S., investors will closely watch the 
FOMC minutes, ISM Services PMI, and preliminary 
University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment for additional 
insight into the growth outlook, inflation pressures, and the 
Fed’s policy path. Several Federal Reserve officials are also 
scheduled to speak, while the Treasury’s 3-, 10-, and 30-year 
auctions will provide an important test of demand for 
duration at elevated yields.
•
One and Done Has Historical Precedent: Markets have 
moved toward an October hold but have not embraced this 
view for the full year. Following last week’s payroll data, 
odds of an October hike fell to 22% from 64% a week earlier, 
while the odds of a December hike remained near 100%. 
History nevertheless supports the view that September’s 
hike could be the last in this episode. In March 1997, the Fed 
raised its target rate by 25 bp to 5.50% following the 1995-
96 easing cycle, then kept policy unchanged until cuts 
resumed in 1998 (Chart 1). The episode demonstrates that 
one hike need not initiate a sustained hiking cycle.

Recent news & views for Treasuries to selloff
•
Global bond selling broadened across Europe and Japan, 
raising the risk U.S. duration remains caught in global 
repricing
•
The 10-year repeatedly failed to sustain rallies below 5.20%, 
suggesting buyers remain reluctant to chase duration
•
Friday’s post-payroll rally fully reversed, a bearish price-
action signal after objectively bond-friendly data
•
U.S. tech giants issued roughly $220 billion of debt in 2026, 
more than double last year, adding duration supply
•
Heavy upcoming Treasury issuance this week: $58bn 3yr,
$39bn 10yr, and $22bn 30yr
Recent news & views for Treasuries to rally
•
The G7 announced a 100-million-barrel emergency oil and 
diesel release, potentially easing near-term energy inflation
•
Treasury and government bond funds attracted $4.3 bn in
U.S. inflows, suggesting 5%-plus yields are attracting buyers
•
Consumer confidence fell to 81.9, a 12-year low
•
Fed speak is turning dovish – Williams said “no urgency” to 
hike again, while Jefferson said “may take more time”
