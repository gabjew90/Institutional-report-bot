We refresh our global lithium S/D balance, incorporating an updated supply outlook, 
while keeping demand assumptions mostly unchanged. With easing S/D balance, we 
maintain our cautious view on the benchmark lithium price outlook. 
We expect the Chinese and global lithium market to move from 16% deficit in most 
of 2026, to nearly balanced in 4Q26, and 8-18% surplus in 2027-2028E. We estimate 
apparent demand growth to become stagnant in 2027-2028E, as the underlying 
growth rate in EV and ESS softens, while the pace of supply chain inventory 
restocking tapers off. On the supply side, the project based estimates by our team 
point to 32% growth in 2027E, and 19% in 2028E, driven by projects in China, Africa 
and Australia. As a result, we expect a moderate supply surplus in 2027E, as the 
market recovers from deep deficit, and considering the range of the uncertainties 
around the supply and inventory changes on the demand side. However, the 
fundamentals in 2028E will be significantly deteriorated. 
Our revised benchmark price forecast for China spot lithium carbonate is 
US$13.7k/t-LCE (or ~Rmb100k) for 2027E, including US$13.0k/t-LCE in 2H27E 
where it meets marginal cost of the global supply. We expect price to further correct 
to US$11.0k/t-LCE (or Rmb84k) for 2028E, representing 80%-ile cost position on the 
global cost curve. These revised prices are +33% and -31% versus our prior 
estimates for 2027E and 2028E, respectively. 
Cost inflation in both operation and effective capex has been meaningful, although 
the change of marginal projects has led to us lower our mid-cycle price to 
US$14.0k/t-LCE, versus our prior forecast of US$16.0k/t-LCE set in 2023. 
We refresh earnings and 12m TPs for the China stocks under coverage (see our 
earnings revision note for details).

Demand deceleration  
 
 
We expect global lithium apparent demand growth to decelerate significantly to +1% in 
2027E and -1% in 2028E, from +41% in 2026E, on the deceleration of underlying 
demand growth (from 26% in 2026E to 16% and 5% in 2027-2028E), combined with less 
aggressive inventory restocking.  
Specifically, we expect growth in EV/ESS demand to add 295kt-LCE or 12% of global 
lithium demand yoy in 2027E. However, this will be more than offset by a 307kt-LCE 
demand reduction (or 12%) resulting from slower inventory restocking.  
GS global auto team expects global NEV demand to grow by 10-11% in 2027-28E, 
n
mainly driven by growth in EU, China, and other emerging markets. Specifically, GS 
China auto team expects China NEV output growth to moderate to 7-9% in 
2027-28E, with domestic demand weakness offset by continued export growth. 
GS China battery team expects global ESS shipments to change by +174GWh and 
n
-194GWh yoy, or +18% and -17%, in 2027E and 2028E. The cyclicality is expected to 
be mostly driven by a potential negative swing in China ESS demand, which is 
expected to peak in 2027E. 
Overall, global EV and ESS battery shipment is expected to increase by 110-410GWh 
n
yoy, or 4-18%, in 2027-28E, with EV batteries to provide a steadier growth anchor, 
while ESS to drive a sharper 2027E acceleration before turning into a drag in 2028E. 
Inventory rise, mostly in battery form in our estimate, contributed to 462kt-LCE in 
n
lithium demand in 2026E, equivalent to 18% of the market. The change is partly 
driven by both strong growth in ESS, accompanied by other factors in a strong 
demand environment. We expect the pace of the restocking to decelerate in 2027E, 
as demand growth decelerates, leading to 12% haircuts in apparent demand of 
lithium carbonate.

Revised cost support and mid-cycle price  
 
 
Cost inflation has been aggressive in the past year, in particular for ex-China operations - 
over the past year, we see broadly flat to +35% cost inflation in Australia spodumene, 
9-40% for Zimbabwe hard rock projects, while South America brine are seeing more 
moderate changes. We estimate the marginal cost level of refreshed global cost curve is 
now at US$13.1-14.6k/t-LCE for the last 5% of the global supply, versus US$9.0k/t-LCE 
a year ago. At 80%-ile, we estimate the unit cash cost would now be at US$11.0k/t-LCE. 
On capex intensity, the major moving factors are the increasing requirements of 
downstream investment or free-carried interest in selected Africa projects, leading to 
not only disruptions in supply but also an average increase of US$1,500-5,000/t-LCE, or 
8-34% in the total integrated equivalent capex intensity. The change would require 
US$2,000-4,000/t-LCE higher lithium carbonate price to compensate for the same 
return, in our estimates, and remain attractive.  
We use returns of marginal projects (high capex or high cost) to estimate sustainable 
mid-cycle price for the lithium market. Subject to required cost of capital that may range 
between 10% to 20% depending on the companies. Our mid-cycle price of 
US$14,000/t-LCE, at which the EBITDA/Capex of 20-30% would barely make cost of 
capital. Our new mid-cycle price is US$2,000/t-LCE lower than our prior estimates, 
mostly due to the change of marginal projects to Zimbabwe hard rock, selected SA 
brine, and Australia spodumene projects, from SA brine mostly in our prior analysis.

As Australian projects ramp up their base operations, or look to expand/de-bottleneck, we see the top end 
of the FOB cash cost curve declining toward ~US$700/t SC6, while the all-in cost curve (AISC) continues to

expansion and Covalent’s Mt. Holland expansions recently FID, PLS’ P2000 at Pilgangoora expected to be 
sanctioned 4Q CY26) sees the all-in costs/FCF breakevens of Australian spodumene producers lift well 
above the AISC curve, with this spend persisting through over the near-to-medium-term to support this 
future widening and further flattening of the cost curve.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
