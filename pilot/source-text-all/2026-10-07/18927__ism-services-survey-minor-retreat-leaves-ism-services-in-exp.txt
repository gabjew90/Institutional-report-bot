Minor Retreat Leaves ISM Services in 
Expansion with More Sustainable Strengths 
Headlines 
 
The Institute for Supply Management (ISM) Services Purchasing Managers 
Index (PMI) fell in September 2026 to 54.9. 
 
 
Business Activity and New Orders both moderated in September, backing 
down from August’s three-year highs. 
 
 
The ISM Services Employment Index crossed back into expansionary 
territory in September 2026 at 50.1. 
 
 
The ISM Services Prices index moved higher again, posting a result of 74.0 
for September 2026. 
Details
The ISM Services PMI came in slightly below 
expectations at 54.9 for September 2026 (see Figure 
1). This result, however, can hardly be considered a 
disappointment as the topline index remains solidly 
in expansionary territory and is built on strong 
current Business Activity, New Orders, and a newly 
expansionary Employment sub-index result. Only 
the New Export Orders component of the ISM 
Services report indicated contraction in September. 
But internally driven weakness for the Euro is more 
likely to be dampening U.S. services exports recently 
as opposed to the past two years’ adversarial trade 
environment’s 
impacts 
and 
forward-looking 
expectations. 
 
The ISM Services PMI is a diffusion index that 
measures the net share of service-sector businesses 
reporting expanding versus contracting activity 
across a range of categories. A reading above 50 
indicates 
expansion 
in 
the 
services 
sector. 
September’s reading of 54.9 marked the 27th 
consecutive month of expansion. According to ISM, 
readings above 48.1 generally indicate growth in the 
overall U.S. economy. 
 
Consumer spending remains a key source of support 
for economic activity, accelerating in the second 
quarter of 2026 and receiving a further upward 
revision in the Bureau of Economic Analysis’ most 
recent report on economic activity. Personal 
consumption expenditures on the services front 
shared in that outcome. Despite a decline for the 
month to a reading of 56.5, the Business Activity 
component of the ISM Services report aligns with 
other healthy consumer spending signals. Business 
Activity in September still allowed the index to post 
its highest six-month average (April through 
September) since February 2023.

competitive environment as households contend

Hiring activity across the services sector breached 
the expansionary threshold of 50 in September 
2026. The Employment sub-index came in at 50.1 for 
the month, which represents its first expansionary 
reading since June. The ISM Services Employment

Cost pressures for U.S. service providers are showing 
no signs of retreat in the ISM Services report. The 
Prices index rose to a 51-month high of 74.0 in 
September 2026. High energy prices, persistent cost 
pressures 
on 
upstream 
manufacturers, 
and 
unyielding U.S. consumer demand all factor into the 
prices services businesses face. Select survey 
responses quoted in the ISM report point to a waning 
ability or willingness among services businesses to 
absorb 
higher 
costs 
without 
passthrough 
to 
consumers. 
 
The ISM Services PMI and ISM Manufacturing PMI 
reports 
for 
September 
2026 
both 
showed 
accelerating cost pressures for those sectors, and oil 
prices are again near $90 per barrel (West Texas 
Intermediate). The Fed has moved on from patient 
observation of price shocks to raising interest rates 
through monetary policy out of the September 2026 
Federal Open Market Committee meeting. PNC 
expects two further 25 basis point rate hikes from 
the Fed through early 2027, which appears justified 
by acceleration in the ISM survey components 
related to prices across the U.S. economy.
