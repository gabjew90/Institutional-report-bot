Beyond AI: The Power of Humanoids
and Automation
Equity preferences
While industrial robots and advanced industrial automation have been with us for
several decades, significant breakthroughs in processing power and artificial
intelligence are triggering “AI in motion” across several industries, presenting
dynamic growth opportunities.
We used to say that with Moore’s Law processing power was doubling every
18-24 months, but AI advancements are doubling every seven months, setting
off “AI in motion” across many industries; that is to say that AI is driving a
quantum leap in the “smartness” of autonomous systems such as humanoid
robotics, Advanced Driving Assistance Systems (ADAS), and robotaxis, as well as
transforming traditional industries including aerospace, agriculture, smart-
glasses, and medicine. The emergence of vision-language–action (VLA) models
in particular represents a transformative shift in robotics and ADAS, by enabling
systems to convert sensory input and natural-language instructions directly into
motor actions.
Ageing populations and shrinking work forces are creating unprecedented labor
gaps globally, offering scalable opportunities for the application of embodied AI
such as humanoid robots and other forms of autonomous automation. Current
reshoring trends are reshaping global manufacturing, presenting dual
challenges of high labor costs and labor scarcity, against a backdrop of anti-
immigration sentiment. This accelerates demand for rapid adoption of
embodied AI solutions requiring minimum human intervention, in our view.
We advise gaining broad exposure to “embodied AI” verticals “set in motion”
by accelerating AI advances. Here we identify the highest growth opportunities
in humanoid robots, advanced driving assistance systems (ADAS), and robotaxis,
as well as niche segments within industrial automation, agritech, smart glasses,
robotic surgery, and drones. Humanoids and automation operate largely
independently and can serve as a fresh, complementary source of alpha not yet
captured by existing TRIO strategies.

have removed KION and Xiaomi. We have also increased the portfolio weights of
BlackRock, Inc. and MediaTek Inc. by 1.0 percentage point (pp) each and Autodesk
Inc and Oracle Corporation by 0.5pp each.

Positioning and key trends
We used to say that with Moore’s Law processing power was doubling every 18-
24 months, but AI advancements are doubling every seven months triggering “AI
in motion” across several industries. We advise gaining broad exposure to
“embodied AI” verticals “set in motion” by accelerating AI advances. We identify
the highest growth opportunities in humanoid robots, advanced driving assistance
systems (ADAS), and robotaxis, as well niche segments within drones, agritech,
smart glasses, and robotic surgery. We conservatively estimate the total
addressable global market for humanoids and automation at USD 402bn within a
decade, growing at a CAGR of 11%. UBS forecasts a USD 40bn global humanoid
market by 2035 and a global automation hardware TAM of USD 100. For ADAS,
including full self drive (FSD), we forecast a global TAM of USD 88bn by 2035 and
a global robotaxi TAM of USD 40bn. Other AI driven verticals, including agritech,
robotic surgery, EVTOL, and smart glasses, are forecast to grow at a 23% CAGR,
reaching an aggregate TAM of USD 134bn by 2035.

Why it is Most Preferred
ABB offers exposure to automation and robotics through its leading
position in industrial robots; and its focus on AI integration for
smarter solutions, diverse applications (manufacturing, healthcare, e-
commerce), user-friendly software (OmniCore), and commitment to
reducing costs, boosting efficiency, and enhancing safety for various
industries. With the global automation industry in a new cycle
upturn, valuations on a P/E basis look attractive, in our view.
Company profile
ABB Ltd, headquartered in Zurich, Switzerland, is a global leader in
power, automation products and solutions. ABB has four business
units: Electrification (offers variety of products & solutions in low- and
medium-voltage), Motion (offers range of electrical motors,
generators, drives, services as well as mechanical power transmission
products and digital powertrain solutions), Industrial Automation
(offers a range of solutions for process and hybrid industries) and
Robotics & Discrete Automation (provides value-added solutions in
robotics, machine and factory automation).
What drives our opinion
ABB has become leaner, improving its profitability; the new focus is
on sustaining high profitability and accelerating growth. By
decentralizing operating responsibilities and cutting costs, it lifted its
operating efficiency and margins, and is now benefitting for
structurally higher growth. The group is a beneficiary of electrification
from investments in data centers, renewable energy as well as power
generation and distribution (medium and low voltage). We expect
this to result in double-digit organic sales growth this year, and close
to double-digit growth in 2027/28. Efficiency measures, economies of
scale and portfolio changes should continue to boost margins. As a
result, we expect double-digit annual profit growth in 2026-28. A
high and rising order backlog provides good earnings visibility.
Meanwhile, its balance sheet remains strong, allowing both share
buybacks and further bolt-on acquisitions. Key risks include a
significant global economic downturn, quickly rising input costs,
unavailability of raw materials or components, and possible currency
losses. Other important corporate risks include cybersecurity, politics,
regulation, reputation, operational execution, product liabilities, and
disruptive competition.

Why it is Most Preferred
We favor Adyen as it stands out from other payment providers with
its unified, single-platform architecture. The company is likely to be a
key beneficiary of the expanding opportunities in embedded finance
and software-led payments.
Company profile
Adyen is a modern payment processor based in the Netherlands,
primarily operating in the e-commerce channel, but also supplying
POS and omnichannel solutions. The company has become well
known for its single technology stack and purely organic growth
strategy (zero M&A in its history). It is a full-stack processor with
direct connections into the card networks Visa and Mastercard, as
well as a licensed bank in the EU, the UK and the US.
What drives our opinion
Adyen is benefiting from industry disruption and merchant migration,
and we think it is well positioned to gain further market share from
traditional payment providers. Its innovative agentic commerce
strategy acts as a “universal translator” for merchants, enabling them
to retain control over transactions and shopper data. The company’s
suite of optimization modules especially the widely adopted and
monetized Protect module further enhances merchant outcomes and
drives higher payment volumes. Adyen’s robust payment volume
growth and strong traction with platform customers highlight its
ability to capture a growing addressable market in embedded finance
and software-led payments, supporting our conviction in its long-
term growth potential. Key risks include a macroeconomic downturn
hurting transaction volumes and idiosyncratic risks from high
customer concentration.

Why it is Most Preferred
We favor AGCO for its strong R&D commitment to precision
agriculture and its differentiated dual-channel approach to market
penetration.
Company profile
AGCO designs and distributes farm equipment, replacement parts,
and precision agriculture technology in approximately 140 countries.
The company’s products include tractors, combines, hay tools and
forage equipment, planters, implements, and other equipment.
AGCO’s 2024 revenue was ~$11.7bn, with 58% from EME (Europe
and the Middle East), 24% from North America, 11% from South
America, and 7% from APA (Asia-Pacific and Africa). The company
has ~24,000 global employees.
What drives our opinion
We believe AGCO Corp is well-positioned to benefit from the long-
term transformation of agriculture, driven by its growing portfolio in
precision agriculture and autonomous technologies. The company’s
joint venture with Trimble represents a pivotal advancement in its
agritech capabilities, enabling scalable, mixed-fleet solutions. AGCO’s
dual-channel strategy, leveraging both OEM and retrofit dealer
networks, gives it a unique edge in delivering cost-effective upgrades
for existing equipment. Its capital allocation is heavily focused on
smart agriculture and clean energy, with approximately two-thirds of
R&D investment directed toward these areas. This strategic focus
supports the acceleration of its precision and autonomous technology
initiatives. Key risks to our thesis include a weaker-than-expected
demand environment due to low crop prices, intensifying
competition, and potential execution challenges within the precision
agriculture segment.

cloud, digital entertainment & media and international commerce

commerce industry and the growing consumption of China shoppers,
in our view. With investments within the user experience of its
domestic platform, as well as in international commerce and cloud

its core e-commerce business and further step-up in shareholder
return, in our view. Key risks to our view include macroeconomic
headwinds in China and intensified competition.

customer sets: consumers, sellers, and enterprises. It operates a range
of businesses including e-commerce/retail, AWS/cloud services, TV

Amazon fosters a culture of innovation, enabling long-term
investments with a willingness to try new things and enter new
markets. AMZN is the leader in cloud, with most of its profits coming

AMZN as a structural leader in e-commerce, as it continues to take
share from various brick and mortar retailers. Risks include slowing
cloud spend, wage pressure, regulatory/anti-trust actions, and tariffs.

Why it is Most Preferred
We like Autodesk for its ability to leverage AI, cloud, and digital twin

Company profile
Autodesk is a digital design software company servicing the
architecture, engineering, construction, product design,
manufacturing, media, and entertainment verticals. The company’s
platform offers a unified solution for designers, builders, and
engineers to leverage a myriad of tools across respective industry-
based clouds and is most known for its 2D/3D design software used
to make buildings, roads and infrastructure, auto design, and movies.
The company is increasingly moving into construction and lifecycle
management solutions. Autodesk was founded in 1982 and is
headquartered in San Francisco, CA.
What drives our opinion
We believe Autodesk is well-positioned to benefit from the digital
transformation in design, construction, and manufacturing. Its
leadership in AEC through tools like Revit and Autodesk Construction
Cloud enables the creation of digital twins that extend beyond
design, integrating IoT, energy management, and predictive
maintenance to turn static structures into smart buildings. In
manufacturing, Fusion 360 supports automation, simulation, and AI-
driven generative design, helping manufacturers innovate faster.
Autodesk also intersects with robotics, from automated construction
workflows to collaborative robots on-site, reinforcing its role in
driving automation.
A subscription-based cloud model underpins recurring revenue and
ties growth to trends in digitization, sustainability, and efficiency. By
integrating AI, cloud, and digital twins across its ecosystem, Autodesk
strengthens its competitive moat and expands into operations and
lifecycle management. As industries prioritize smarter, more
sustainable infrastructure, Autodesk is evolving from a design tool to
a strategic platform for managing complex systems. Its leadership in
AEC, manufacturing, and media positions it to capture growth from
smart buildings, digital factories, and AI-enabled design. Key risks
include execution in AI and robotics, weaker demand in construction
and manufacturing, and competitive pressure from tech disruptors.

offered to institutional and retail clients worldwide through mutual

management clients.
What drives our opinion
BLK has unmatched size and scale, and the increasing diversity of its
businesses and revenue streams improves the company’s outlook.

products is a long-term trend, BLK’s increasing diversity is shifting is
mix of management fees towards higher yielding products.

positive flows even in unfavorable market environments.

include arbitrary tariffs on its exports, price competition in the

landscape.

Equity selection systems
Equity selections in Equity Preferences lists (EPLs) are assessments made relative to a sector/industry, country/regional or
thematic benchmark. The chosen benchmark is disclosed on the front page of each EPL. It is also used to measure the
performance of the EPL owner(s). Including a stock in the EPL constitutes neither a view on its expected, standalone absolute
performance nor a price target. Rather, EPLs are meant to support the UBS House View, with the stocks included in them
selected for their superior risk/return profiles.
Our selection is based on an assessment of the company’s fundamental outlook and valuation, the risks owning the stock
entails, including material sustainability risks and the diversification benefits it provides in an investment portfolio, among
many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.
The lead strategist is responsible for the CIO view on a stock, while the EPL owner(s) is/are responsible for the stock selection
within an EPL. Stocks can be selected for multiple EPLs. For consistency’s sake, a stock can only be selected as either Most
Preferred or Least Preferred, not both simultaneously. As EPL benchmarks differ, stocks do not need to be included on every
list to which they could theoretically be added.

particular structured investment, you must read the relevant offering materials for that investment. Structured investments are
unsecured obligations of a particular issuer with returns linked to the performance of an underlying asset. Depending on the
terms of the investment, investors could lose all or a substantial portion of their investment based on the performance of the
underlying asset. Investors could also lose their entire investment if the issuer becomes insolvent. UBS does not guarantee in
any way the obligations or the financial condition of any issuer or the accuracy of any financial information provided by any
issuer. Structured investments are not traditional investments and investing in a structured investment is not equivalent to
investing directly in the underlying asset. Structured investments may have limited or no liquidity, and investors should be
prepared to hold their investment to maturity. The return of structured investments may be limited by a maximum gain,
participation rate or other feature. Structured investments may include call features and, if a structured investment is called
early, investors would not earn any further return and may not be able to reinvest in similar investments with similar terms.
Structured investments include costs and fees which are generally embedded in the price of the investment. The tax treatment
of a structured investment may be complex and may differ from a direct investment in the underlying asset. UBS and its
employees do not provide tax advice. Investors should consult their own tax advisor about their own tax situation before
investing in any securities.
