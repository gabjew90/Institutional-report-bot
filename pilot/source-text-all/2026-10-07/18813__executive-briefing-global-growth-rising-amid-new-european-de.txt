Macro Research 
 
    
Global growth indicators strengthened over the past month, not least 
underpinned by the ongoing AI investment boom. Global PMI data continued 
to increase in September with a synchronized lift across regions. Even though 
the data centre build-out is strongest in the US, trickle down effects lift growth 
across the world as much of the supply chain for data centres are outside of 
the US. Advanced microchips are mainly produced in Taiwan and South Korea, 
where export data continues to boom, but Europe is also a supplier, not least 
when it comes to machines for chip production and inputs for energy 
systems. European manufacturing PMI remained robust in September and 
service PMI improved again. Private consumption continues to rise at a 
decent clip, especially in the US, providing tailwind for global growth as well.  
We look for the global economy to continue to cruise ahead with GDP growth 
around trend into 2027. There is a definite risk the AI investment boom will 
create overcapacity at some point and trigger a sharp downturn. However, the 
current challenge in the AI value chain is rather capacity constraints amid a 
sharp rise in the demand for compute to power AI. US hyperscalers plan to 
increase investments from around USD800 bn in 2026 to USD1.3 trn in 2027 
(4% of US GDP). Hence, for the coming year AI investments look set to be a 
continuing global growth driver. 
Over the past week, stress in the European bond markets reached the 
highest levels since 2011 amid new uncertainties around the budget and 
debt outlook in France. Contagion lifted bond spreads in Italy and Spain as 
well. Our baseline scenario is that France will ultimately take needed budget 
measures to cool down market stress, but the turmoil adds a new risk to the 
growth outlook. Rising global bond yields have also provided a headwind to 
global growth but it has happened in response to stronger growth, so it will 
act more as a balancing factor rather than derail growth, in our view.  
Oil prices have fluctuated again over the past month rising to USD110 per 
barrel in mid-September followed by a decline back to around USD100 per 
barrel lately. Key drivers have been continued stalemate in US-Iran talks, 
uncertainty over a possible US diesel export ban ahead of mid-term elections, 
global inventory depletion but also more oil flowing through the Strait of 
Hormuz. Our baseline scenario is more muddling through in the oil market 
amid continued volatility. 
Inflation developments have been mixed. Euro inflation surprised to the 
upside in September with headline inflation moving up to 3.8% from 3.2% 
while core rose to 2.5% from 2.4%. In the US core PCE inflation was revised 
lower but was still 3.0% y/y in September, clearly above the 2% target. Robust 
growth amid upward inflation surprises keep central banks in hiking mode. 
The Fed raised policy rates by 25p for the first time in three years on 16 
September taking the Fed funds rate to 4.0% while the ECB delivered a second 
hike in three months on 10 September lifting rates to 2.5%. We look for 
another two hikes by both central banks over the next 3-6 months.

Denmark 
Domestic demand growth in Q2 was revised up from 0.2% to 0.9% q/q, 
painting a more positive picture with little overall impact from higher energy 
prices. However, private consumption still stagnated during the quarter. 
Relatively strong domestic growth seems to have continued in Q3, judging 
from increasing business confidence. Employment growth was only 400 
persons in July, but summer data is often misleading and other data such as 
hiring plans suggest that job growth is still running at about 2,000 per month. 
Apartment prices in Copenhagen have stagnated or even declined slightly, 
but prices are still rising in the overall housing market. The government has 
presented a technical budget for 2027 which includes a fiscal easing of 0.4% 
of GDP, the actual budget will likely not be less expansionary. 
Sweden 
Data continues to show strength for the Swedish economy. Consumer 
confidence rose to the highest level since inflation started to take off in 2022. 
Retail sales increased by 6.9% y/y in August, underscoring solid domestic 
demand. Manufacturing PMI rose to 58.1, driven by a significant rise in new 
orders and production. The Riksbank noted and signalled a high probability of 
a rate hike at its next meeting in November, while holding rates steady at 
1.75% in September. Inflation risks have increased, and we anticipate higher 
inflation readings going forward. The opposition parties won the Swedish 
election, but no new government has formed. Given the division between the 
left (Vänsterpartiet) and the centre (Centerpartiet), government formation 
may take time. Any majority will be slim and Centerpartiet holds a strong 
bargaining position; significant shifts in fiscal policy are unlikely. 
Norway 
The respondents in Norges Bank's regional survey expected growth in the 
current quarter (Q3) of 0.3 % and next quarter (Q4) of 0.3 %. Companies 
reported that capacity utilization dropped to 28 % and the proportion 
reporting a labour shortage fell from 18 % to 17 %. All in all, growth is holding 
up but remains sub-trend so capacity utilization drops further. Wage 
expectations came in at 4.5 % for and 4.0 % for 2027, so wage expectations 
remain elevated. Despite lower capacity utilisation and lower inflation, Norges 
Bank decided to raise the key policy rate by 25bp. to 4.5%. In the short run, the 
decision was slightly dovish as the communication suggesting the peak most 
likely has been reached. As long as unemployment remains low and there are 
no signs of severe financial or credit stress, the central bank can focus on 
bringing inflation and inflation expectations down. 
Finland 
The Finnish economy is in a moderate growth phase driven mainly by private 
consumption and the export sector. The trend indicator for output (GDP 
Monthly) grew 0.3% m/m in August signalling that higher energy prices are 
not yet disturbing growth momentum. Business confidence was above the 
long-term average in all major sectors in September and consumer 
confidence was slightly below the long-term average. Orders for the export 
industry have remained strong and the global investment cycle continues to 
support the Finnish manufacturing sector. Labor market conditions remain 
historically weak with unemployment at 10.1%, but conditions are finally 
improving. Data revisions show that unemployment has been decreasing and 
employment has been increasing steadily since May. The housing market 
remains in a slump with prices moving sideways during the summer, but sales 
continue to decrease.

Currency markets 
In the FX markets, broad USD has rebounded strongly. The three main drivers 
behind the move have been 1) strong US macro data lifting expectations of 
the Fed’s policy tightening, 2) elevated energy prices boosting US terms-of-
trade and 3) rising debt concerns in Europe fuelling safe-haven demand. The 
latter has also supported the likes of CHF and JPY, while risk-sensitive SEK has 
been under pressure. We continue to expect further USD strength and weaker 
SEK and NOK over the coming year. EUR/USD reached our 12M target of 1.12 
faster than expected, but if the recent pressure on European government 
bond markets persists, the cross could well continue trading even lower in the 
near-term. EUR has also weakened against DKK, and the risk of an 
independent Danish rate hike has hence declined further. 
Bond Markets 
European bond markets have come under pressure amid growing concerns 
surrounding the public finance outlook in France. The French-German 10Y 
government yield spread has widened to 150bp, the highest level since the 
European debt crisis. While the move remains largely contained to France, the 
peripheral countries such as Greece and Italy have also widened against 
Germany. In the EUR swap market, yields have dropped significantly with the 
2Y EUR swap rate dropping 25bp and 10Y 15bp as markets scale back on ECB 
hikes. We continue to expect both short and long-end EUR rates to move lower 
in the next 12 months. In the US, we expect the rate curve to steepen 
modestly. In the Danish market, the price of 30Y callable bonds has increased, 
with the 4.00% coupon remaining the benchmark. We expect this will 
continue to be the case. 
Credit markets 
Primary market supply remained at a high level despite ongoing market 
tremors relating to AI debt sustainability, rising interest rates and French 
budget concerns, just to name a few. Secondary spreads started widening 
towards the last week of the month in the HY segment, and in recent days we 
have seen signs that it is also spreading to IG. Widening in HY has been rather 
forceful, with HY spreads around 60bp wider than where they ended in August 
in EUR. This is consistent with the moves observed in equities and thus 
indicative of a broad-based risk-off tone rather than a specific credit sell-off. 
Meanwhile, in the US, credit has widened more than the equity index move 
would have suggested, highlighting the difference in composition, with the 
AI/hyperscaler exposure in equities and more diversified exposure in credit, 
leading to different rates dynamics. 
Equities 
September saw a striking divergence between the two largest asset classes. 
Bond volatility surged while equity volatility remained remarkably contained. 
This is not necessarily inconsistent. Bond markets are being challenged 
mainly by supply-side forces, whereas equities continue to benefit from a 
positive demand and earnings story. Macro momentum remains supportive 
and earnings expectations continue to rise, providing a strong fundamental 
anchor despite considerable headline noise. Higher yields may temporarily 
constrain equity returns, but history suggests that when yields rise alongside 
resilient growth and contained risk spreads, equities typically regain 
momentum once rates stabilise. Sector leadership remains exceptionally 
concentrated: technology has significantly outperformed other sectors, 
supported by powerful earnings revisions and the unprecedented AI 
infrastructure buildout.
