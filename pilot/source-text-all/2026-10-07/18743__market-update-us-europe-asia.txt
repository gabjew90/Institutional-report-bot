U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.73% | Nasdaq +1.19% | Dow +0.49%
Synopsis

US equities were higher in Friday trading, though ended off best levels. Stocks extended Thursday's mostly higher finish, though the S&P
500 and Nasdaq capped off narrowly mixed weekly performances. Breadth was positive, though the equal-weight S&P still trailed the cap-
weighted index and posted a seventh-straight weekly decline. Semis and semicaps were broadly higher, while memory saw some weak-
ness, with HDD makers under pressure from competition headlines. Big tech was broadly higher, with TSLA-US (deliveries) and SPCX-US
the notable gainers. Other outperformers included road and rails, machinery, E&Cs, networking and communications equipment, casual
diners, cruise lines, IBs and PE. Laggards included athletic apparel (NKE-US), homebuilders, pharma, biotech, A&D and moneycenter
banks. Treasuries were weaker in very choppy trading with some curve flattening, with yields up 6-7 bp at the front end and back above
pre-payrolls levels. The dollar index was off 0.2% after finishing at its highest level since Apr-25 on Thursday. Gold finished down 1%,
silver was down 1.2%, bitcoin futures were down 0.6%, and WTI crude ended down 1.9%, though well off session lows. The stock rally to
end the week was sparked by a softer-than-expected September payrolls report. Economist takeaways said the print strengthens the case

track for the middle of November. AMZN-US is reportedly looking to offload $8B of NVDA-US chips into an SPV to help improve balance
sheet health. TSLA-US Q3 deliveries topped consensus. AVGO-US is reportedly assembling a $60B financing package to fund chip pur-
chases for Anthropic and other tech firms. STX-US and WDC-US were lower after a Nikkei report that Toshiba plans to nearly double HDD

despite depressed expectations into earnings, with focus on weaker FY27 guidance that called for revenue down HSD and EPS 25%+
below consensus. DIS-US is reportedly planning a restructuring of its television operation that will involve hundreds of layoffs and consol-
idation of divisions. SYNA-US was boosted by revised takeover terms from ON-US. FICO-US and other credit bureaus were hit by the latest
FHFA regulatory concerns.
Macro

September payrolls, analyst reading: Payrolls increased just 29K m/m against a +90K consensus, while July and August were revised
down a combined 60K, lowering the 3M average to +51K. Analysts said seasonal payback likely exaggerated the weakness. Goldman
noted September tends to underperform when Labor Day falls later, while UBS said seasonal effects boosted August at September's
expense; both see underlying job growth around 50-60K. Gains were concentrated in health care, construction and manufacturing, with
Goldman linking the latter two partly to AI/data-center capex. Unemployment edged up to 4.18%, partially linked to a 20 bp rise in partic-
ipation. Wage growth was notably soft at +0.1% m/m. Analysts largely see the report strengthening the case for an October Fed pause,
though inflation remains the bigger policy driver. UBS still expects a December hike, while Citi still sees no further hikes this year. Focus
now shifts to the 14-Oct CPI.

G7 reserve release and the US diesel export threat: G7 nations confirmed they would release as much as 100M barrels of emergency oil
and diesel stocks after pressure from the Trump administration (Bloomberg, CNBC). Earlier reports indicated the release would be split
equally between oil and diesel. It is to be coordinated by the IEA and take place over the next four months, according to French President
Macron. It comes a day after the administration warned European leaders to release emergency diesel inventories or face a potential US
diesel export ban (Reuters), and after China suspended oil product exports for October, prioritizing domestic inventory levels over inter-
national market demand (Reuters). The US supplies over 50% of EU diesel imports and ~31% of the UK's. Trump has not ruled out a ban,
and Treasury Secretary Bessent said US farmers and truckers should not carry the burden into midterms. Goldman Sachs said a ban
could raise EU diesel prices by ~2%, while Citi said even a partial ban could mean shortages in Europe. Polymarket puts a broad US ban
by 31-Oct at 11%, while Kalshi puts a ban before election day at 17%. Energy Secretary Wright has opposed a blanket ban (Bloomberg).
EU countries and the EU Commission agreed to answer with one voice, take any release to the IEA, and de-escalate with Washington
(Politico), which represents delay and multilateralisation rather than a concession. The UK is seen as the weak point with just ~42 days
of cover, against ~400 in Spain and Germany.

Nike guidance: Nike fiscal Q1 revenue missed by ~1% but EPS beat with help from better gross margin. Despite depressed expectations
into the print, the stock was pressured by disappointing FY27 guidance for revenue down high-single digits and EPS 25%+ below the
Street at the midpoint. Takeaways focused on continued softness in Sportswear (down LDD), Jordan and Greater China (down 25%+).
Performance was a widely discussed bright spot, with HSD growth driven by strength across Running, Global Football, Basketball, Tennis
and Golf, and traction behind the Sport Offense strategy. There were also positive takeaways around Pace, a new multi-year operational
transformation strategy expected to generate ~$2.5B in cost savings through FY31.

Renewed Middle East risk: Headlines shifted toward the potential for renewed US-Iran kinetic activity as last month's diplomatic hopes
wane. Multiple reports said a third carrier battlegroup, involving nearly 20K troops, is headed to the region (Washington Post), and the US
is sending additional anti-missile batteries to Saudi Arabia and Qatar to help protect energy infrastructure (Axios). Rhetoric about a pos-
sible attack has ramped up, with Trump reiterating that a strike could come after the midterms and saying Iran is "ready to fold up" (CBS
News). Iran continues to talk up diplomacy while preparing an expanded target list should hostilities resume (Reuters), and Axios flags
tensions between these camps. Iran has continued to strike at SoH shipping while failing to hit its targets (NY Times).

following the softer NFP quickly reversed; weak breadth remained a key area of scrutiny, with the equal-weight S&P 500 on track for a
seventh straight weekly decline; price pressure was a highlight in the September ISM manufacturing report; consumer confidence fell to
its lowest level since 2014; there was no diplomatic traction in the Middle East; credit spread widening received some attention, including

EU Market Recap
Germany +1.2% | France +0.8% | UK +0.3%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished higher, stabilizing after booking sharp losses on Thursday that saw the Stoxx 600 post three-month
lows, with the index down ~1% on the week. Cyclicals and growth sectors were the biggest gainers, while defensives, autos and retail
lagged. Technology was the strongest-performing sector, with European semiconductor and equipment names benefiting from positive
US read-across and continued optimism around AI and data-center demand. Industrials benefited from a mixture of company-specific
catalysts and positive defense and infrastructure developments amid rising geopolitical worries, with Kone (KNEBV-FI) higher after reports
it is preparing to sell most of TK Elevator's European business to address antitrust concerns around its proposed €29.4B acquisition, and
BAE Systems (BA-GB) was in focus on reports it is among potential bidders for Dutch counter-drone specialist Robin Radar. Basic re-
sources outperformed amid some stabilization in precious metal prices, though base metals were weaker, with Glencore (GLEN-GB) higher
after raising its FY26 Marketing adjusted EBIT expectations to above $5B and introducing a revised medium-term marketing framework
ahead of its planned secondary listing in Australia. Travel and leisure benefited from strong company-specific trading updates alongside
a retreat in crude prices: JD Wetherspoon (JDW-GB) surged after reporting 8.6% like-for-like sales growth over the nine weeks to 27-Sep,
helped by hot weather, and Wizz Air (WIZZ-GB) traded higher after September passenger traffic increased 24.2% y/y to 7.78M, although
load factor slipped 0.8pp to 92.0%. Banks were under pressure amid this week's sharp rise in European bond yields, stronger-than-
expected Eurozone inflation and a higher rate outlook, with Commerzbank (CBK-DE) weaker after RBC downgraded the stock to Sector
Perform, while Julius Baer (BAER-CH) was higher after announcing a CHF600M share buyback and a revised capital-distribution policy.
Autos and parts were higher on the whole but a relative underperformer against the broader market: Stellantis (STLAM-IT) was a notable
outperformer after reporting flat Q3 US sales of 324,277 vehicles, with a 29% increase in Ram sales offsetting a 20% decline at Jeep,
reaffirming 2026 guidance and outlining 11 new US models, while Volvo Car (VOLCAR.B-SE) fell after Q3 sales were down 10.7% y/y and
it said it will not meet its FY26 volume and cash-flow outlook and withdrew short-term guidance. KER-FR adopted a more cautious Q3
tone, with Gucci now expected to fall 6% in Q3 and be flat in Q4. There was a reprieve for European bonds following the latest yield backup
this week. The dollar index was off 0.3%, gold was down 0.6%, silver was down 0.6%, bitcoin futures were up 1.1%, and WTI crude was
down 2.8%, off session lows. On the macro data front, Eurozone inflation was the main highlight, with headline CPI accelerating to 3.8%
versus 3.2%, a little ahead of forecast, and core also edging up, as expected. The BoE monthly DMP survey showed stable inflation ex-
pectations and an unchanged wage growth outlook. Press discussed the recent performance of French debt, which is among the notable
underperformers on worries over the fiscal outlook with a presidential election next year. UK Chancellor Healey is to meet bank chiefs
next week, with worries within the sector that it could be targeted with a tax hike to fund other spending commitments, and there was
more speculation of an early UK general election. STLAM-IT also saw its US share tracking to 8.2% versus mid/high-7s expectations, and
BAER-CH approved its CHF600M share buyback.
Macro

Eurozone inflation and the ECB: Eurozone flash inflation for September was 3.8% versus 3.2% in August, the quickest acceleration since
Mar-2026 and the highest since Sep-2023 (FT, CNBC), above the 3.6-3.7% consensus range. Energy did the work, jumping to 18.8% from
14.3% as the Iran war and wider refining margins lifted fuel prices, and food also firmed. Core inflation was 2.5%, in line with forecasts,
services rose to 3.2% from 3.0%, and non-energy industrial goods eased to 1.1%. Nordea and ING noted the energy overshoot reflected
refinery margins, not just crude and gas, so pass-through strengthened, while brokers saw little sign that second-round effects were
broadening quickly; transport services were the exception, with mixed views on wages. Gas storage still well below normal levels added
to concerns over winter energy costs, with tighter global LNG supply, competition for LNG from Asia and the loss of Russian pipeline gas
as further risks. On policy, Bloomberg Economics said quarterly averages were broadly in line with ECB forecasts and the core rise looks
largely like an airfare base effect; with bond yields already tightening conditions, it expects no October move and one last hike in
December, while Nordea keeps December and Mar-2027 on the table. Lagarde said there are still no signs of energy prices feeding into
wages and argued for a measured response, while Schnabel said the coming months will show how far rates need to be tightened; both
said there is no rush.

French and Italian bonds: France was at the center of the Eurozone selloff this week, with the 10-year yield rising above 4.8% and the
France-Germany spread widening to around 140-150bp over the credibility of the government's deficit reduction plans amid political
gridlock heading into the 2027 election. The OAT-Bund 10-year spread reached ~149 bps on Friday, the widest since the euro crisis
(Bloomberg), with French yields near 4.94% and Bunds falling toward 3.45%, indicating flight to safety rather than a common rates move.
Italy widened faster than France, with the BTP-Bund spread jumping 16 bps Thursday and opening Friday near 123 bps, and the two-year
gap almost doubling to 55 bps, the biggest rise since 2020. MUFG said the French budget plan failed to restore confidence, and UBS
suggested shorting Italian bonds against Bunds, targeting 150 bps from 119. The 2027 French draft budget targets a deficit of 5% of
GDP, down from an expected 5.4%, on 1% growth, though interest costs are set to rise by ~12B to €91B and debt is still projected near
121-122% of GDP. Household measures are the political flashpoint: Socialists called the text a provocation, Greens and LFI said they will
censure, and RN looks less ready to topple the government, with a revised bill via Article 49.3 the likely path. Failure, or a special law that
rolls over 2026, would leave the deficit ~6.5% and push issuance above the €340B already planned. Italy is borrowing less, with PM
Meloni cutting planned spending so the deficit can fall below the EU's 3% limit, while Germany's political instability and Chancellor Merz's
weakening position, and renewed election risk in Spain after PM Sanchez's minority government suffered a parliamentary defeat, added
further uncertainty. The same fear reduced bets on ECB rate hikes to ~70 bps from ~100 bps prior.


UK gilts and the BoE: The UK was caught in the global bond sell-off, with 30-year gilt yields reaching 6% for the first time since 1998 and
10-year yields moving above 5.4% (to the 5.45% area on Thursday) before easing by the end of the week. A Bloomberg economics gauge
implies the benchmark bond carries an extra credibility premium of ~30 bps due to past policy missteps and shocks. The BoE monthly
DMP survey of CFOs showed little evidence of second-round effects, with realized own-price growth unchanged at 3.7% in the three
months to September, year-ahead own-price inflation expected at 3.7% versus 3.8% prior, year-ahead CPI expectations unchanged at
3.1% and three-years-out at 2.8%. Annual wage growth was unchanged at 4.0% with year-ahead expectations at 3.4%. BoE MPC member
Mann said the initial response to the Iran conflict was a mistake that pushed up borrowing costs, arguing that the rise in market rates
reflected expectations of higher inflation and possibly a "monetary policy uncertainty premium". The BoE has stayed on the sidelines,
saying the tightening in financial conditions was equivalent to at least 75 bps of rate hikes, but some sell-side economists think this
position is beginning to look unsustainable, and Governor Bailey and the deputy governors have all highlighted the possibility of shifting
their position given upside inflation risk.

UK budget and politics: The London Times reported that Chancellor Healey has summoned UK bank chiefs for talks next Tuesday, fueling
worries he is preparing to raise levies on the sector in the 28-Oct budget; the news was a contributory factor in Thursday's UK bank selloff.
UK Finance said UK banks face a total effective tax rate of 46.4%, and Sky News reported that a dozen mid-tier lenders have asked Healey
to lift the threshold for the corporation tax surcharge from £100M to £500M. The Guardian cited a senior Labour insider who said Healey
risks a fresh market shock on 28-Oct if he is not clearer on taxation, fiscal headroom and spending; he is said to be open to running
narrower headroom after the Iran war cut at least half of the £24B buffer. Separately, ministers are working on sweeping welfare reform,
including scrapping a key disability benefit for under-25s in favor of a multibillion-pound support package, pending reviews by Alan Milburn
and the Timms panel due later this year. Inews cited sources close to Downing Street saying PM Burnham has left the door open to a
general election in May 2027 despite publicly insisting there will be no early poll, and Burnham suggested rejoining the EU could feature
in the next Labour manifesto.

Equity market internals, week ending 2-October: The Stoxx 600 was down ~1% and posted a 3-month low, and the cumulative
advance/decline line mirrored the move. New 52-week lows outpaced highs, the first time since the week ending 11-Sep and before that
May. 41.17% of Stoxx 600 constituents closed above the 50EMA (versus 48.50% prior) and 59.17% were above the 200 EMA (versus
63.83% prior). The equal-weight index outperformed the cap-weighted index this week, though cap-weighted outperformed over the past
month. Dispersion remained near 2026 lows and the 3-month realised average stock correlation stayed near YTD lows, near zero. VSTOXX
implied volatility increased, and realised volatility also rose but remains below implied levels. Value outperformed growth, taking the
growth/value ratio to a new YTD low. Oil & Gas took the top structural outperformer spot, Banks declined to second for the first time since
May, and Technology rose to third. Personal & Household Goods and Autos & Parts remained the top laggards, with Health Care closely
following.

Asia equities ended with a negative tilt Friday, with the Nikkei reversing some of Thursday's gains, the Topix lower again, and a steep

gain while the yen was stronger, Treasury yields were little changed after the pullback overnight, crude futures were sharply lower in the

in Treasury yields, disappointment over China's mini stimulus and a lack of southbound buying due to the holiday in the mainland. In
regional developments, Tokyo core inflation accelerated by more than expected to its highest in 11 months, exceeding the BOJ's 2% target
for the first time since January. The main drivers came largely from special factors (nursery school fees, water utilities), and services
inflation was the highest since Nov-2023. The data is seen as backing the case for another rate hike by year-end. South Korea inflation
eased in line with estimates, and the BOK said inflation is expected to remain around 3% in October amid core pricing pressures, rein-

for each other. In company news, Bain Capital said it would explore an investment in Hong Kong's embattled developer New World Devel-


US-China tariffs and overcapacity: Bloomberg cited comments by USTR Greer at a G20 Trade Ministerial in Milwaukee that there is no
timeline for the '30-for-30' tariff cuts under the US-China Board of Trade, citing the pending outcome of the investigation into excess
capacity. He also noted that follow-through depends on a public comment process, reducing the chance of implementation by the holiday
season. A cross-reference of the carved-out item list with customs trade data shows China-made toys as the largest category, comprising
$13.4B of the $30B worth of US imports. In a Nikkei interview, Greer signaled overcapacity countermeasures will be unveiled in coming
weeks and was adamant the US will not tolerate inflows of excess production. Washington has been considering additional tariffs on 16
countries including China and Japan. Greer also cited Japan's legacy of 'zombie firms' as a source of overcapacity concerns, while saying
Vietnam is forced to export goods its domestic consumers can't afford and pushing for measures to grow domestic demand.

Tokyo CPI, Japan labor data and the BOJ: Tokyo core CPI rose 2.7% y/y in September, above the 2.3% consensus and following 1.8% in
the previous month, marking the highest since November 2025. Inflation ex-fresh food and energy rose to 3.0% (highest since Aug-25)
versus a 2.5% consensus and 2.0% prior. Nursery school fees (+0.29 ppt) as the city's free childcare program expired, water utilities
(+0.24) and accommodation (+0.12) made the notable contributions, and broader public utilities turned positive for the first time since
Jun-25. Non-fresh food rose a steady 0.1%, rice declines deepened amid the current supply glut, and processed food edged up to 0.3%
from 0.2% ahead of price hikes on thousands of items over October. The unemployment rate rose to 2.5% in August versus a consensus
and prior month of 2.4%, and the job offers-to-applicants ratio was steady at 1.18 as expected. Economist takeaways from the September
BOJ Tankan were broadly upbeat, though with some thoughts the results weren't strong enough to support an October rate hike. FY capex
projections were softer than expected, but growth remains solid and backed by upgrades to sales and profits, and softer price metrics
tipped the scales against an October hike. Most appear to favor a December hike, maintaining the recent quarterly interval; Deutsche
noted the December Tankan is due 14-Dec with the MPM on 17-18 and suggested the January meeting would allow more time for as-
sessment and preparatory messaging.

the discussions to the public and later conveyed to US Treasury Secretary Bessent that the prime minister is not a reflationist. Economic
and Fiscal Policy Minister Kiuchi, a standard-bearer for Takaichi's "responsible and proactive fiscal policy," has adjusted his tone since
the summit. Yet after the talks Takaichi expressed frustration and questioned why Japan must listen to US concerns. The article noted
the historical pressure the US has applied to Japan, with past calls to expand domestic demand now changed to rate hikes and fiscal
discipline. There are no clear implications as yet, as it remains unclear how the US views current progress.
