Stabilization, green shoots, and robo-anticipation
Investment Recommendation
2Q26 deliveries. Off ~2% from last year, yes, but ahead of consensus and CG. More
quietly, and perhaps more tellingly, we estimate Tesla crossed its 10-millionth car sold
last quarter - a milestone easy to miss amid the noise, yet real all the same.
Tesla’s sales, battered by the doldrums of 2024/2025, has found its footing. With
pump prices and sticker costs moving higher worldwide, you can spot the green
shoots if you look closely. High interest rates will keep the party quiet for now, of
course. But down the road, as full self-driving (FSD) gains genuine traction, Tesla
may find itself with enough runway to nudge prices upward again for the first time in
years. We shall see.
The deliveries were good news, the kind that steadies nerves. But, in our opinion,
the stock is a bet on other things: robots; some arriving on 4 wheels, and others on
hands and feet.
As for those with wheels, doubt has long shadowed Tesla’s promises of a rapid
robotaxi rollout, and so far, reality has vindicated that hesitation. From regulatory
mazes to technological hurdles, the ledger of concern remains long. Whether the
recent debut of the Cybercab will spark a true ascent - not merely across a scattering
of new cities, but the market is looking for geographic density - remains to be seen.
This could be the threshold. We – and the world - are watching.
Then there are the humanoids.
Production on Optimus Gen 3 is underway, but Tesla’s leadership is taking pains
to preach patience. The company’s first dedicated robot line is carving out a home
in Fremont- fittingly enough, inside the old Model S and X footprint - with an eye
toward an eventual capacity of 1M units/year. On the last call, Elon Musk was sober
about the mountain ahead: this, he warned, will be the hardest product to scale in
Tesla’s history. With no off-the-shelf supply chain to lean on, nearly every tendon and
actuator must be forged from scratch. The classic manufacturing S-curve will apply,
he noted, but expect that opening stretch to be long and stubborn.
Speaking at the Abundance Summit in March, Musk sketched the timeline with a
familiar mix of caution and ambition. Optimus 3 ramp would be slow at first, before
finding its stride toward volume by mid-2027. Beyond that lies the broader march: an
Optimus 4 blueprint ready next year, and an ambition to debut a new generation of
mechanical labor each year after.
The EVs have found their footing with green shoots of spring showing through the
frost. And, the robots await.

3Q26 deliveries. This morning, Tesla reported 3Q26 production and delivery results.
3Q26 deliveries of ~486.5k—representing a ~1.3% sequential growth from ~480.1k
in 2Q26 and a ~2.1% annual decrease from ~497.1k in 3Q25. 3Q26 deliveries came
in higher than our estimate of 458.5k. Tesla produced ~464.4k units during 3Q26—
representing a ~2.8% sequential increase from ~451.8k vehicles produced in 2Q26.
Source: Tesla, Factset, Canaccord Genuity
Details on deliveries/production by segment
• Total deliveries grew to 486,532 units during the quarter, a ~1.3% increase
compared to 480,126 units delivered in 2Q26 and a ~2.1% decrease compared to
497,099 units delivered during 3Q25.
• Total production grew to 464,391 units during the quarter, ~2.8% higher/lower than
the 451,758 units produced during 2Q26 and ~3.8% higher than the 447,450 units
produced during 3Q25.
• Deliveries of Model 3/Y were 478,237 - representing a ~2.2% sequential increase
from 467,762 in 2Q26 and a ~.61% annual decrease from 481,166 in 3Q25. Model
3/Y production accounted for ~98.5% of total production compared to ~97.4% in
2Q26 and ~96.7% in 3Q25.
• Deliveries of “Other Models” were 8,295 — falling ~32.9% sequentially from 12,364
in 2Q26 and falling ~47.9% annually from 15,933 in 3Q25.
• 3Q26 energy storage deployments. In 3Q26, Tesla deployed ~13.7 GWh of
energy storage products—representing a ~1.5% sequential growth from 2Q26
energy storage deployments of 13.5 GWh and a ~9.6% annual increase from ~12.5
GWh deployed in 3Q25.

Cybercab event recap
We were there... on X. We watched from afar (sigh), tracing the digital breadcrumbs
on X and catching the presentation playback thanks to the work of Sawyer Merritt—a
courtesy we note with gratitude.
And let us say it plainly: the Cybercab looks like tomorrow. Mr. Musk appears to have
achieved his goal that “the future should look like the future” It is slick, it is luminous,
it commands the eye. The butterfly doors are a theatrical stroke - though how they will
manage the tight, crowded corridors of a great city remains to be seen, one assumes
the engineers at Tesla have accounted for the friction of the real world.
Beneath the sleek gold shell lie several features of engineering, including:
• Brake-by-wire: As Mr. Musk noted on X, "Having electric brakes avoids the
complexity of a hydraulic system: no need to rout plumbing all around the car."
• The Supermanifold V3 thermal system: A work of thermal engineering that strips
away unnecessary refrigerant valves and lines, consolidating the high- and low-
voltage controllers. Its modular design makes its production 80% automated and its
operation 38% more efficient than other automotive thermal systems.
• Rare-earth-free motors: As Mr. Musk revealed on X, "the Cybercab motor uses
no rare earth metals, but maintains the same range! This was extremely hard to
achieve."
• The 4680 structural pack and unboxed manufacturing: A reimagining of
how a car is born from the floor up, paired with paintless rim injection molding to
dramatically reduce costs and environmental impact.
• Fleet ownership models: A new option for buyers to operate their own Cybercab
network.
• Starlink integration: As Mr. Musk revealed on X, "Starlink will allow you to watch
4k live video while riding in Cybercab."
Also, a brief summary of the presentation:
• Cameras and end-to-end neural network. Rejecting traditional reliance
on expensive lidars and radars, Tesla’s AI-first, end-to-end vision stack treats
autonomous driving as a challenge of pure intelligence. Just as human drivers
navigate seamlessly using only eyes and a brain, then camera-driven neural network
processes the world continuously to anticipate what comes next. Having achieved
1 million miles of unsupervised robotaxi operation, the company believes that this
technology proves that scalable autonomy is here.
• Safety and design. Powered by billions of miles of real-world fleet data and refined
through years of engineering, the Cybercab helps to ensure protection, in our

end neural network approach is safer than a human. And, that humans, after all, rely

neural network approach may eventually outdrive humans. And yes, human drivers
possess no built-in lasers (as far as we know).
But, to us, the true measure is not merely beating the human benchmark; it is the
margin of safety. Why refuse the full tech suite—lidar, radar—when lives hang in the
balance? These sensors have come meaningfully down in price. And, AI has helped
conquer sensor fusion complexities. Is technology not meant to conquer risk, not
merely equal it?
• Tesla’s counter-argument likely rests on the altar of scale: that a simpler, easily
replicated system deploys faster, saving lives on a mass scale today rather than
tomorrow. But speed is not the sole virtue—to us. Can a camera-only fleet navigate
the grueling "march of the nines" faster than Waymo? Can it master the infinite
cruelty of edge cases? Time will tell—and Elon Musk has confounded his doubters
before.
We—and the world—watch.
Tesla Semi event recap
Tesla Semi is gearing up for mass-production. Last week, Tesla held an event
highlighting the Tesla Semi and its Nevada production facility. Tesla plans to release
two trims of the vehicle, a Standard Range and Long Range variant. The vehicles will
be manufactured at the company's dedication production facility in Nevada, which was
completed late-last year. At full capacity, the factory can build 50,000 units annually.
• Standard Range. The Standard Range model is set to start at $250,000 per unit
and offers 325 miles of range per charge.
• Long Range. The Long Range trim is set to start at $290,000 per unit and
incorporates a third battery module, enabling 500 miles of range per charge.
• Additional specs. In addition to the varying ranges, the Tesla Semi offers similar
hauling capacity relative to diesel trucks, fast charging up to 1.2 MW—enabling
60% battery recovery in ~30 minutes—with plans to develop wireless charging, an
electric steering assist system similar to the Cybertruck, a powersharing mechanism
to allow the batteries to power refrigerated trailers and a million-mile battery life.
• Initial reviews trending positively. Initial reviews—including an in-depth video
with Jay Leno—have been trending incredibly positively, with users highlighting
how quiet the vehicle is, its acceleration and ease of physical demands given the
automatic gear shifting.

Rising gas prices
Rising gas prices could be stoking EV demand. Elevated gas prices likely played a
role in Tesla's strong 3Q deliveries. According to a recent note from our Home, Outdoor
& Auto Analyst Brian McNamara (you can find it here), the national average regular
gas price has increased +56% YTD and +49% since February 27th, the day before the
war started. Gas prices have averaged $4.09 since the Iran war began, compared to
$2.88 YTD prior, implying consumers have paid an incremental ~$130bn at the pump
in seven months.

production ramps.
