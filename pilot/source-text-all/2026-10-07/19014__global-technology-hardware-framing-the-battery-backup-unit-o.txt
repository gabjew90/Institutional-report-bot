We are focusing on power supply-related products as a key theme of technological 
innovation in generative AI data centers (AI DCs). Among these, we see Battery Backup 
Units (BBUs), a new form of distributed power backup, as particularly critical. In 
addition to standard power backup functions, BBUs are experiencing increasing 
adoption rates as devices that handle the sharp power consumption fluctuations (peak 
shaving) unique to generative AI data centers. In the long term, they are also expected 
to be deployed in tandem with Capacitor Backup Units (CBUs). 
At present, investor sentiment toward AI investment is not entirely favorable due to 
concerns over massive capital expenditure risks in generative AI, the impact of rising 
interest rates, power shortages, and the profitability and ROIC of LLMs. While no AI-
related products can be completely immune to these risks, the BBU remains one of 
the most promising areas, as it is poised to benefit from both secular AI growth 
and rising attach rates (increased unit volume per system). For BBUs, design-ins 
appear to be locked-in several years in advance. Indeed, Panasonic HD has stated that 
it has already secured “Awards” (formal agreements on product development and 
future orders) for its combined BBU and CBU sales targets through FY3/29. 
Consequently, we believe there is minimal downside risk to BBU attach rates across 
successive GPU generations. 
In this report, in collaboration with our Global Tech Team, we project the global BBU 
market size to expand 23x from $2.2 billion in 2025 to $50.2 billion in 2030. We also 
map out the competitive positioning of global players in this space. Based on this 
analysis, we raise our earnings estimates and target price for Panasonic Holdings 
(HD), one of the primary beneficiaries.

The key takeaways we highlight for investors are as follows: 
The Technological Pivot: Generative AI servers are characterized by sharp 
n
fluctuations in power consumption between active GPU execution and CPU 
processing. Direct connection of these servers to the power grid would impose 
severe grid instability. BBUs are deployed to mitigate these extreme power 
fluctuations, and their significance is growing rapidly as GPU power consumption 
rises. As GPU power demands increase with each successive generation, BBU 
adoption (attach rates) is climbing in tandem. 
Surging BBU Attach Rates: The BBU attach rate is set to climb alongside the 
n
increasing power requirements of newer GPUs. We estimate attach rates at 40–50% 
for the Blackwell generation, rising to 55–65% for the Rubin generation (which 
entered mass production in 2026), and exceeding 90% for the Feynman generation 
slated for shipment in 2028. Consequently, the growth rate of the BBU market over 
the next few years is poised to substantially outpace that of typical AI-related 
hardware. 
Robust BBU Market Outlook (2025–2030): Driven by the dual tailwinds of surging 
n
global GPU power consumption (CAGR of +62% from 2025–2030) and soaring 
average BBU attach rates (climbing from 38% in 2025 to 95% in 2030), we look for 
the BBU market to grow at a CAGR of +87%, expanding 23x from $2.2 billion in 2025 
to $50.2 billion in 2030. 
Scenario Analysis: We also perform scenario analyses for cases where BBU 
o
adoption accelerates faster than expected (Bull Case) or progresses more 
gradually (Bear Case). We estimate the 2025–2030 CAGR at +103% in our Bull 
Case and +73% in our Bear Case.

Panasonic HD: Key Monitoring Points and Potential Risks for the BBU Business : 
n
While we currently rate Panasonic Holdings (HD) highly for its strong 
competitiveness and dominant market share, holding a high share in the early stages 
of a market does not guarantee that a company will remain the winner as the market 
scales. To track Panasonic HD’s long-term competitive advantages, we believe the 
following key metrics and risks are crucial: 
Revenue Growth vs. Market Growth: It is critical that the revenue growth 
o
rate of Panasonic HD’s BBU business matches or exceeds the expansion rate of 
the overall BBU market. 
OP margin sustainability: Operating margins must remain at high levels. A 
o
decline in profit margins could indicate downward pressure on ASPs driven by 
intensifying competition. 
Technological Dynamics (NCA/NMC vs. LFP): There is a trend in the industry 
o
toward adopting Lithium Iron Phosphate (LFP) chemistry alongside the 
NCA/NMC chemistry currently favored by Panasonic HD (NCA). At present, 
NCA/NMC are preferred due to high energy density, which delivers superior 
space efficiency in constrained server rack environments. However, if power 
sidecars are introduced, these space constraints will ease. This could 
accelerate the adoption of LFP, which offers distinct advantages in unit 
economics and cycle life, potentially boosting the market presence of 
LFP-centric competitors. 
Capacity Expansion Execution: 
o
Battery Cells: Panasonic HD currently utilizes its domestic battery 
§
factories in Japan for BBU production, but plans to leverage its Kansas 
plant in the future. Mass production of data center battery cells at the 
Kansas facility is scheduled to begin in FY3/2029. Any delay in this 
transition could lead to capacity constraints. 
Modules: Panasonic HD is expanding its module assembly footprint in 
§
Mexico. Mass production at the second factory is scheduled to begin in 
Q2 of FY3/27, followed by the third factory in FY3/28. The successful 
ramp-up of these facilities is vital for securing adequate supply 
capacity. 
Decision-Making Agility: Panasonic HD is a large conglomerate, which 
o
typically exhibit slower decision-making due to multi-layered corporate 
hierarchies. In a tech sector characterized by exceptionally rapid technological 
advancement and market scaling, the company’s organizational agility and 
ability to keep pace with industry shifts will be a primary focus for investors.

Overview of BBU TAM and related companies 
 
 
We expect the BBU TAM (Bull/Base/Bear) to grow rapidly at a CAGR of 
+103%/+87%/+73% from 2025 to 2030. We forecast the market size 
(Bull/Base/Bear) will expand from USD 2.2 bn in 2025 to USD 75.1 bn/USD 50.2 
bn/USD 34.2 bn in 2030. This market expansion is set to be driven by the following 
three factors: 
Rising GPU power consumption: GPU power consumption is raised with each new 
1.
generation, requiring greater backup and peak shaving capacity. 
Increasing GPU shipment volume: Shipments of GPUs for data centers are 
2.
expanding in tandem with the acceleration of AI investments. 
Rising BBU adoption rates: BBU adoption rates are increasing alongside the 
3.
adoption of next-generation GPU architectures. 
We assume BBU adoption rates are currently around 40% for Blackwell, 50% for 
Blackwell Ultra, and 55% for Rubin, and will be 60-65% for Rubin Ultra, and 90% or more 
for Feynman. As these new products gradually become mainstream, the overall average 
adoption rate should improve, and as a result, we expect the BBU market size to grow 
rapidly over the next few years. 
BBU-related players 
The BBU-related supply chain is divided into battery manufacturers, module 
manufacturers, and energy storage system manufacturers. The main companies are as 
follows. Panasonic HD can be described as a company with advanced vertical integration 
among these. 
Battery manufacturers: Panasonic HD, Murata Manufacturing, TDK, Samsung SDI, LG 
n
Energy Solution, Ampace, Tenpower, etc. 
Module manufacturers: Panasonic HD, AES-KY, Sysgration, Dynapack, STL 
n
Technology, Celxpert Energy, etc. 
Energy storage system manufacturers: Panasonic HD, Delta Electronics, LITEON 
n
Technology, Flex, XING Mobility, Lead Wealth, Megmeet, etc. 
In addition, MinebeaMitsumi, whose BBU protection module business is growing rapidly, 
can also be said to be a beneficiary stock.

Revision to our Panasonic HD earnings estimates 
 
 
Based on the aforementioned BBU market forecast (base case), we revise our earnings 
estimates, centering on Energy, and revise up our operating profits estimates from this 
fiscal year onward by +3%/+11%/+11%. For valuation, we switch from a format that 
references the company’s historical EV/EBITDA and EBITDA margins to a valuation based 
on the correlation between EV/EBITDA and EBITDA margins for global competitors, 
including BBU. As a result, we raise the applied EV/EBITDA from 9X to 10.5X. With this, 
we raise our target price for Panasonic HD from ¥5,100 to ¥6,300.

The BBU Industry Ecosystem 
 
 
In this chapter, we provide an overview of major players in the BBU industry supply 
chain. 
Major players in the BBU industry 
In the BBU industry, there are mainly three categories of players: (1) battery 
manufacturers, (2) module manufacturers, and (3) energy storage system 
manufacturers. 
(1) Battery manufacturers: Manufacture battery cells and supply them to 
module/energy storage system manufacturers. Major players include Panasonic HD, 
Murata Manufacturing, TDK, Samsung SDI, LG Energy Solution, Ampace, Tenpower, etc. 
(2) Module manufacturers: Procure battery cells externally, integrate them with DC-DC 
converters, BMS, MCUs, etc., assemble them into BBU modules, and supply them to 
energy storage system manufacturers. Major players include Panasonic HD, AES-KY, 
Sysgration, Dynapack, STL Technology, Celxpert Energy, etc. 
(3) Energy storage system manufacturers: Procure BBU modules externally, or procure 
battery cells and components to assemble BBU modules themselves, integrate them 
with enclosures, busbars, cooling fans, capacitors, etc., and ultimately complete them as 
BBU systems. In some cases, they manufacture battery cells in-house and operate a 
vertically integrated business. Major players include Panasonic HD, Delta Electronics, 
LITEON Technology, Flex, XING Mobility, Lead Wealth, Megmeet, etc.

BBU Technology: Its Power Peak Shaving Role  
 
 
Role of BBUs in data centers 
A Battery Backup Unit (BBU) is a distributed energy storage system installed within 
server racks in a data center. It supports efficient power operation of the data center by 
supplying backup power to GPUs during momentary outages or voltage drops, and 
providing a peak shaving function to compensate for shortfalls during peak power 
consumption. Recent AI servers consume massive amounts of power in short periods to 
maintain parallel processing by multiple GPUs. As a result, power peaks rise sharply and 
voltage tends to become unstable, making highly reliable and stable power supply 
indispensable for AI data centers. Against the backdrop of this demand environment, 
the importance of BBUs as a rack-level distributed power supply solution is growing. 
BBUs consist of energy storage modules containing lithium-ion battery cells and shelves 
to house them, connected to the DC busbar of the server rack.

specifications for data center hardware equipment. OCP was established by Meta

servers, storage, and network switches.

BBU battery chemicals and formats 
The chemistry (chemical composition) and format of battery cells are currently 
dominated by cylindrical ternary (NCA/NMC) battery cells, but they could diversify in the 
future to include prismatic LFP and others. The strength of ternary batteries lies in their 
high energy density, enabling them to achieve high space efficiency even in limited 
installation spaces. LFP has strengths in terms of cost and lifespan, and in addition, the 
prismatic format offers the advantage of allowing cells to be stacked together without 
any gaps. 
Power supply systems in 800VDC architecture 
Current data center backup power systems feature a coexistence of centralized UPS 
units installed in dedicated power rooms (grey space) and distributed BBUs built into 
server racks or power sidecars. On the other hand, for future 800VDC architectures, 
discussions are underway to transition to DC-direct systems combining SST (solid-state 
transformers) + ESS + BBU, eliminating traditional UPS units which suffer from power loss 
due to AC -> DC -> AC conversion. In this next-generation system, high-voltage AC 
transmitted from the grid is converted directly to high-voltage DC (800VDC) using SST 
and fed directly to ESS and BBUs, which is said to enable reductions in power conversion 
loss and the shrinking of grey space used for UPS installation. 
That said, as SST has not yet achieved technological maturity in terms of cost and 
reliability, hurdles remain for immediate adoption on a GW scale. As such, in the current 
transition period, inquiries for MV UPS (medium-voltage UPS), which improve power 
efficiency by eliminating step-up/step-down transformers, are surging, particularly 
among major Western electrical equipment manufacturers (LINK). As a result, we expect

BBU Players & Key Business Opportunities  
 
 
Panasonic HD 
Panasonic HD is the largest player, and according to the company it is holding an 
approximate 70-80% share in distributed power supply systems (BBU) for data centers.  
It is the only company that comprehensively handles everything from battery cell 
development to module design and system integration in-house, and its strengths lie in 
the high reliability of its battery cells and modules and its ability to propose total 
solutions to customers. In the data center energy storage system business, the company 
aims for sales on the scale of ¥1 tn in FY3/29 and ¥1.5 tn in FY3/31, and management 
has commented that its award acquisition ratio (projects for which product 
development has been promoted or orders agreed upon) has reached almost 100% 
regarding its sales guidance for FY3/29. 
Furthermore, in addition to Panasonic Energy, which handles BBUs, Panasonic Industry 
said that it is developing CBUs (Capacitor Backup Units) and supercapacitors, with plans 
to begin mass production of first-generation products during FY3/27. We believe that if 
these devices, which absorb power load fluctuations, are launched, combining them 
with BBUs will lead to further value-add enhancements. 
Murata Manufacturing 
We expect BBU-bound sales to account for more than 30% of Murata Manufacturing’s 
battery business sales in FY3/27. With structural reforms completed and profitability 
having improved to a break-even level for the battery business as a whole, the increase 
in BBU-bound sales is expected to make a positive contribution in terms of enhancing 
profitability depth. The company is heading toward expanding its business for BBUs in 
the form of LFP cell supply, and its stated strategy is to advance business expansion by 
leveraging LFP’s high safety and its status as the only domestic Japanese cell 
manufacturer (making it easy to differentiate itself geopolitically for Western 
customers). While we believe its number of customers is currently likely to be limited and 
its industry position is low, we believe it will grow into a more material business scale 
amid robust demand and changing technologies. 
TDK 
TDK’s medium-sized batteries have up to now been used mainly for household 
ESS/e-bikes/drones, but per the company, it is expected to make a full-scale push for

major growth driver for the next medium-term plan (FY3/28-FY3/30), drawing on 
strengths in LFP materials and design structure. Aside from supply via its JV with CATL, 
Linergy (Malaysia), which it acquired in May 2026, could play an important role. Linergy 
is currently mainly focused on UPS and power tools, but is moving to handle BBU 
applications as well. We believe medium-sized batteries for AI/data centers, which 
contributed almost nothing to sales through FY3/26, have the potential to expand to 
nearly ¥300 bn by FY3/31, and management said that it is aiming for major expansion in 
line with GPU technological evolution, especially in the latter half of the next 
medium-term plan.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
