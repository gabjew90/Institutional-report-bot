We remain more positive on the fundamental backdrop for our Industrial Tech 
coverage than for Autos. We expect a good fundamental environment in Industrial 
Tech driven by both growing datacenter capex and strength in the broader industrial 
market (both a cyclical recovery in core industrials, as well as pockets of secular 
growth including in energy and robotics). However, we expect there to be a high bar 
for the stocks to trade higher, similar to 2Q reports. For Autos, we continue to expect 
a muted cyclical backdrop, and we remain more selective with the stocks.   
Heading into 3Q26 earnings the median stock in our Autos & Industrial Tech 
coverage is down 5% since reporting 2Q26 results and down 4% YTD (with Auto 
exposed stocks down 5% since earnings and down 6% YTD, but Industrial Tech 
stocks up 9% at the median since reporting 2Q results and up 30% YTD at the 
median).

We downgrade Gentex (GNTX) shares to Sell from Neutral and lower our 12-month price 
target to $20 from $23, implying 9% downside vs. 19% average upside for our coverage. 
We believe that memory/electronics costs could pressure its margins over the near to 
medium term, either from the direct exposure (with electronics ~60% of its BOM) or 
from knock-on effects of higher costs (either from OEMs looking to reduce content to 
offset costs and/or because of negative elasticity of demand). In addition, Gentex has 
the lowest exposure to Chinese domestic OEMs in our tier 1 coverage, which could lead 
to a more difficult customer-weighted LVP environment for Gentex than other suppliers 
over the medium to long-term if Chinese auto OEMs continue to take share. Finally, 
Gentex trades at a premium to other tier 1s in our coverage. 
We are modestly lowering our EPS estimates to better reflect these challenges, and now 
model 2026/2027/2028 EPS at $2.04/$2.05/$2.30 from $2.07/$2.15/$2.35 prior. Our 
EPS estimates are below the Street at $2.09/$2.21/$2.47.   
What would make us more positive: There are three key things we’ll be monitoring: 1) 
We could be more positive if Gentex saw improved top-line momentum. This could come 
from the fact that Gentex is pursuing certain areas for growth (e.g. dimmable 
sunroofs/moonroofs, EMS). We think those efforts will take time to be a bigger factor for 
fundamentals. However, if these new product areas have a larger/quicker impact than 
we anticipate, then we could more positive. Gentex could also see better demand than 
we expect from its current products (e.g. FDM and DMS). 2) Gentex could see improved 
sales to Chinese OEMs on international business. For example, Gentex is setting up a 
factory in Morocco to better address European OEM localization requirements. Gentex 
expects this factory to be operational in 2028, and this could allow for better sales to all 
OEMs selling into Europe longer-term.  3) Gentex could more effectively offset cost 
pressures than we expect, with pricing and/or mix.  
1) Gentex’s exposure to memory and electronics could pressure margins 
Gentex has commented that about 60% of its BOM is from electronics (which we 
estimate is ~$800 mn), and we believe costs are rising in particular for memory. While 
more specialty types of DRAM that have been more typically used in autos such as DDR3 
have not seen as much cost pressure as DDR4, more recently DDR3 pricing has also been 
rising materially per Trendforce data (Exhibit 36).  
Recall that in 2H22 during the semiconductor driven challenges, Gentex reported 
significantly lower gross margins than consensus (FactSet) due to both cost and mix 
challenges (Exhibit 37). We see the potential for margin downside again over the 
near to medium term.  
Besides direct margin headwinds from higher costs, OEMs could seek to de-content to 
offset higher costs overall. In addition, higher costs may be difficult to pass on in its 
consumer VOXX business (e.g. premium audio) due to elasticity of demand dynamics.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
