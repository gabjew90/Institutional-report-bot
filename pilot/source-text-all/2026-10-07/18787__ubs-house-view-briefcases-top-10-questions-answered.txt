How will Fed rate hikes impact markets?
Key message
Strong US economic data and higher energy costs pushed Treasury yields 
to multi-decade highs and raised concerns about an extended tightening 
cycle from the Federal Reserve. But we expect only one further rate increase 
in December and believe resilient economic growth and robust corporate 
earnings should help equities withstand moderate policy tightening. Higher 
starting yields are also creating opportunities to add portfolio income.
House view
01
Strong growth and inflation concerns pushed Treasury 
yields higher.
•
The FOMC raised the federal funds target rate by 25 basis points in September.
•
High energy prices and strong economic data have added to inflation concerns.
•
The 10-year US Treasury yield rose to 5.34% in early October, its highest intraday 
level since 2002.
02
But markets may be pricing a longer tightening cycle 
than the Fed ultimately delivers.
•
We expect one 25-basis-point increase in December, while markets continue to 
price roughly three hikes over the next 12 months.
•
We expect underlying inflation data to continue to moderate. The three-month 
annualized rate of core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation fell to 
2.05% in August, its lowest level since July 2024.
•
The September labor report showed signs of cooling, reducing the pressure 
for an imminent rate hike. Nonfarm payrolls rose by 29,000, below consensus 
expectations for a 90,000 increase.
03
So, resilient earnings and higher starting yields 
continue to offer investment opportunities.
•
We forecast S&P 500 earnings growth of 25% in 2026 and 14% in 2027, which 
should help equities absorb moderately higher yields while economic growth 
remains firm.
•
We recommend positioning for further equity gains while diversifying across 
regions, sectors, and return drivers and managing concentrated single-stock 
exposure.
•
We rate fixed income as Attractive and see opportunities to add portfolio income 
through medium- to long-duration high-quality bonds and medium-maturity 
credit from stronger investment grade issuers.
New this week
Recent Fed commentary has signaled a more patient 
approach toward further tightening. New York Fed President 
John Williams said “there is no need for urgency, and we 
have time to gather more information.” Governor Michelle 
Bowman said she did not “currently see an urgent need for 
further action.”
One liner
We expect only one further Fed rate increase, with resilient 
growth and robust earnings helping equities withstand 
a shallow tightening cycle while higher yields create 
opportunities in quality bonds.
Did you know?
•
US factory activity has remained in expansion 
territory for eight consecutive months. Historically, US 
manufacturing expansions have lasted nearly three 
years on average.
•
US nominal GDP was growing 6.6% year over year at 
the end of the second quarter, comprising around 2.1% 
real growth and a 4.4% increase in the broad price level. 
This can help companies grow revenues and profits in 
current-dollar terms.
•
Past US economic downturns generally did not begin 
until at least two years after the Fed started raising 
rates, although the timing varied with the scale of the 
tightening cycle.
Investment view
We expect one further 25-basis-point Fed increase in 
December and do not believe a measured tightening cycle 
will derail the US economy or equity rally. Investors should 
maintain diversified equity exposure and use the rise in yields 
to add selectively to quality bonds, while remaining cautious 
on the longest maturities and lower-quality credit.

What role can gold play in portfolios?
Key message
Gold remains under pressure after a difficult September, as elevated real yields 
and a firm US dollar increase the opportunity cost of holding the metal. But 
softer US employment data and more patient signals from Federal Reserve 
officials have reduced the immediate risk of another rate increase in October. 
We remain constructive over the next 12 months and view price weakness as 
an opportunity to add exposure.
House view
01
Higher real yields and a firm US dollar have weighed 
on gold.
•
Gold lost around 7% in September as rising government bond yields increased 
the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset.
•
Elevated US yields and broad US dollar strength could continue to create near-
term volatility for precious metals.
•
While expectations for an October Fed rate hike have receded, investors are still 
poised for a 25-basis-point increase in December.
02
But the strategic drivers of gold demand remain intact.
•
Central bank demand remains robust, supported by reserve diversification and 
efforts to reduce concentrated exposure to US dollar assets.
•
Elevated government debt, fiscal sustainability concerns, and questions about 
currency purchasing power continue to support demand for real assets.
•
Gold is not another institution’s liability, reinforcing its appeal as a store of value 
and an alternative reserve asset during periods of financial or geopolitical stress.
03
So, we continue to view gold as a strategic portfolio 
hedge and diversifier.
•
We forecast gold to rise toward USD 5,400 an ounce by September 2027, 
supported by central bank buying, reserve diversification, elevated government 
debt, and geopolitical uncertainty.
•
Pullbacks toward USD 4,000 an ounce can be seen as opportunities for investors 
to add exposure.
•
Investors with an affinity for gold can consider maintaining a mid-single-
digit allocation, while those with substantial holdings can diversify into other 
commodity sectors.
New this week
Softer US employment data and more patient signals from 
Federal Reserve officials have reduced expectations for an 
October rate increase, easing one immediate headwind for 
gold even as Treasury yields remain elevated.
One liner
Near-term yield and currency headwinds may keep gold 
volatile, but its structural demand drivers support using price 
weakness to build or maintain a strategic allocation.
Did you know?
•
Gold fell 7.2% in September but gained 3.2% over the 
third quarter, illustrating how rapidly its performance 
can shift as expectations for interest rates and the US 
dollar change.
•
Central banks have accumulated an average of around 
1,000 metric tons of gold a year over the past four years, 
around twice the average of the preceding decade.
•
Gold does not generate income, so higher real yields 
increase its opportunity cost relative to interest-bearing 
assets. But its resilience during some periods of 
rising yields suggests that reserve diversification, fiscal 
concerns, and geopolitical risks also influence demand.
Investment view
We remain constructive on gold over the next 12 months 
despite the near-term headwinds from elevated real 
yields and a firm US dollar. Central bank demand, reserve 
diversification, elevated government debt, and geopolitical 
uncertainty continue to support its strategic portfolio role. 
Investors with an affinity for gold can consider using price 
weakness to build or maintain a mid-single-digit allocation.

Where are the opportunities in fixed income?
Key message
As major central banks turn more hawkish on still-persistent inflation and 
lingering supply-side risks, we believe investors should revisit fixed income 
exposure to find the right balance between yield, potential capital gains, and 
diversification to match their goals and constraints. We like quality bonds—
including potential opportunities in longer-dated bonds—and diversified fixed 
income exposure.
House view
01
Major central banks have taken a synchronized, 
hawkish turn.
•
The European Central Bank raised rates by 25 basis points in September, while the 
Bank of England indicated greater willingness to tighten before inflation pressures 
become entrenched.
•
The Federal Reserve raised its target range by 25 basis points to 3.75-4%, and 
the Bank of Japan increased its policy rate by the same amount to 1.25%—the 
highest level since 1995.
02
In quality bonds, there may be opportunities to add 
interest rate risk selectively.
•
The recent rise in yields has created tactical opportunities to add some duration 
in high-quality bonds. Medium- to long-duration high-quality bonds have scope 
for price gains and can provide valuable diversification.
•
We are more cautious on the longest-dated bonds, given fiscal sustainability 
concerns, growing AI-related debt issuance, and the potential for term 
premiums to remain elevated.
03
And we see opportunities to diversify, especially in 
emerging markets.
•
We like select exposure to more growth-sensitive and higher-yielding bond 
market segments such as emerging market credit and high yield debt.
•
Emerging market credit offers attractive income, diversification benefits, and 
increasingly resilient fundamentals.
•
In low-rate economies like Switzerland, we also see merit in equity income 
approaches and select structured strategies.
New this week
Concerns around fiscal sustainability in quarters of Europe 
led to sharp moves in government bond yields last week. 
The spread between French and German 10-year bonds saw 
its largest weekly widening since 1990, while the difference 
between Italian and German debt of the same maturity saw 
the biggest weekly move since the pandemic.
One liner
Combining high-quality bonds with selective emerging 
market exposure can broaden sources of portfolio return.
Did you know?
EM sovereign and corporate yields stand at around 
7.2% and 7%, respectively (JPMorgan EMBI and CEMBI 
indices). These yield levels should underpin high-single-
digit returns over the next 12 months, well above cash, in 
our view.
Investment view
We view fixed income as Attractive, with opportunities across 
regions and market segments. Emerging market bonds offer 
appealing yields and diversified return potential. In high-
quality bonds, investors can consider adding exposure in the 
medium- to long-term maturity range. Stronger investment 
grade issuers offer attractive carry across medium maturities, 
in our view, while higher-risk credit should remain relatively 
short-dated. We are more cautious on the longest maturities 
given fiscal concerns and rising AI-related issuance. Investors 
should calibrate credit and duration risk to their objectives
—for example, more income-focused investors may prefer 
shorter maturities to reduce duration risk.

Why should investors broaden beyond US stocks?
Key message
Stock investors may be reluctant to look beyond the US. After all, the nation's 
companies make up more than 60% of global equity indexes and S&P 
500 companies' second-quarter earnings grew 35%. But we expect strong 
earnings to broaden beyond AI, while the concentration of the US market 
may expose investors to company-specific risks. We like broadly diversified 
stock allocations across preferred regions, sectors, and return drivers to 
help investors participate in further potential market gains while managing 
concentration risk.
House view
01
Investors may be focusing chiefly on US stocks given 
their importance and the pace of earnings growth.
•
The US is the largest stock market in the world, accounting for over 60% of 
global stocks (MSCI AC World data).
•
S&P 500 companies' underlying second-quarter profits grew by nearly 35% year 
over year, the strongest quarter in more than four years.
02
But we believe the earnings story extends beyond the 
US and AI.
•
We expect European corporate earnings to grow by 15% in both 2026 and 2027, 
supported by a cyclical pickup in manufacturing, investment in structural trends, 
and operating leverage as revenues recover.
•
We forecast earnings growth of 72% this year for the MSCI Asia ex-Japan, with 
the region’s AI hardware supply chain and a recovery in cyclical segments both 
contributing to a broadening of equity returns.
•
Japanese equities, meanwhile, should also be supported by robust earnings, driven 
by AI-related capex, cyclical recovery, and ongoing structural reforms.
03
So, investors should diversify stock allocations across 
preferred regions, sectors, and return drivers.
•
Beyond the US, we like European equities—especially those in the Eurozone—as 
well as Asian and emerging-market equities.
•
At a sector level, our preferences include industrials, health care, and consumer 
discretionary. We also like European banks and European IT.
•
Investors can also consider structured strategies to manage risk, generate yield, or 
acquire stocks at a lower effective price.
New this week
The Chinese government recently announced a modest 
package of targeted stimulus and easing measures aimed at 
channeling cheaper credit into infrastructure, technology, and 
housing. Meanwhile, data showed the official manufacturing 
purchasing managers’ index rose to 50.1 in September from 
49.8 in August, ending two months of contraction thanks to 
a bounce in production and new orders.
One liner
Now is the time to look beyond US markets for equity 
opportunities for return potential and risk management.
Did you know?
•
Japanese equities should continue to benefit from 
ongoing reforms and shareholder-friendly policies in 
addition to the market’s solid positioning in the global 
AI value chain. We expect earnings growth of 12% in 
the fiscal year ending in March 2027.
•
We favor balanced exposure to Japanese stocks across 
AI, cyclical, and structural beneficiaries.
•
Earnings growth for Stoxx Europe 600 companies 
accelerated to more than 20% year over year in the 
second quarter.
Investment view
We expect equity markets to move higher as strong earnings 
broaden beyond AI, but wide gaps in performance between 
stocks mean portfolio outcomes depend more than ever 
on what investors own. Excluding strategic holdings, our 
analysis shows nearly 40% of self-managed equity investors 
on our platform hold more than half their portfolio in just 
10 stocks or fewer—amplifying risk. To balance opportunity 
and concentration, investors should ensure their core equity 
allocation is broadly diversified across our preferred regions, 
sectors, and return drivers.

What does the US-Iran conflict mean for markets?
Key message
Continued attacks and the lack of a durable political settlement leave Middle 
Eastern energy supplies vulnerable to renewed disruption. But exporters have 
adapted routes and logistics, helping Gulf oil flows recover and Brent crude 
retreat from its conflict peak. We remain constructive on global equities, rate 
fixed income as Attractive, and believe diversified commodity exposure can 
provide portfolio protection and access to a broader commodity upcycle.
House view
01
The conflict remains unresolved, leaving oil 
infrastructure and shipping routes exposed to further 
disruption.
•
At least seven tanker incidents were reported in the past week, while attacks have 
continued around the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Aden.
•
Almost 3 billion barrels of oil supply have been lost since the conflict began, 
according to Saudi Aramco’s chief executive, versus 1 billion barrels of releases 
from inventories.
•
Replenishing depleted inventories while continuing to meet consumption could 
take up to two years, he added.
02
But the global energy system has adapted, enabling 
Middle Eastern exports to recover despite persistent 
security risks.
•
Gulf crude exports reached 18.3 million barrels a day on a seven-day average at 
the end of September, slightly above their pre-conflict average.
•
Exports exceeded pre-conflict levels on 14 days in September, supported by 
Saudi loadings from both the Gulf and the Red Sea.
•
The price of Brent crude has fallen from a recent peak close to USD 110 a barrel 
in mid-September to around USD 102 at the time of writing.
03
So, resilient growth, robust earnings, and patience 
from central banks should help markets cope.
•
Broad commodity exposure can help diversify portfolios when energy disruption 
or inflation weighs on equity and bond returns.
•
We don't, however, expect the conflict to derail the equity rally, given strong 
earnings growth and healthy economic data in much of the world.
•
Recent comments from Federal Reserve officials suggest policymakers are not 
in a hurry to tighten again, while the recovery in energy flows could ease one 
source of inflation pressure.
New this week
Gulf crude exports rose above pre-conflict levels for part 
of September, even as attacks on tankers continued and a 
durable political settlement remained elusive.
One liner
The energy system has adapted faster than the conflict has 
been resolved, supporting our constructive view on equities 
and fixed income while reinforcing the case for diversified 
commodity exposure.
Did you know?
•
Before the conflict, the Strait of Hormuz typically 
handled around 125 large commercial vessels a day 
and accounted for about 20% of global crude and 
liquefied natural gas supply.
•
Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline stretches roughly 
1,200 kilometers and can transport up to 7 million 
barrels a day from the country’s eastern oil fields to 
the Red Sea.
•
Recent inflation data from the US and Eurozone 
suggest that underlying inflation remains contained, 
despite higher energy prices.
Investment view
We remain constructive on global equities, supported by 
resilient economic growth, robust earnings, and sustained 
AI investment. We also rate fixed income as Attractive, with 
higher yields providing income and creating opportunities 
in high-quality bonds. Diversified commodity exposure can 
add portfolio protection when inflation challenges equities 
and bonds, while structural demand, geopolitical risks, and 
constrained supply support the case for a broader commodity 
upcycle.

How can I invest in transformational innovation?
Key message
We believe investors can complement diversified equity portfolios with 
targeted exposure to AI, Power and resources, and Longevity. AI remains a 
powerful driver of earnings growth and investment, while electrification and 
rising data-center demand should continue to support Power and resources. 
Clinical innovation, meanwhile, underpins long-term growth potential in 
Longevity.
House view
01
We have strong conviction in the long-term potential 
gains offered by AI.
•
We estimate that overall AI-related capex will rise to USD 1.2tr in 2027, with 
agentic AI the next growth leg.
•
The heightened volatility in semiconductor shares this year has underscored the 
risk of single-stock concentration.
•
We favor diversified and balanced exposure across the AI value chain—from select 
semiconductors and hardware to megacap tech and more defensive areas of the 
industry.
02
Structural demand and the energy transition support 
long-term investment in power and resources.
•
Opportunities to invest in the power and resources value chain span grid resilience, 
renewables, nuclear, industrial automation, and critical minerals.
•
Recent earnings point to sustained demand for electrification, grid modernization, 
data center infrastructure, automation, and clean-power capacity, with strong 
order trends and guidance supporting the outlook for equipment and 
infrastructure providers.
•
Exposure to copper and aluminum is backed by structural demand drivers, 
while utilities offer visible earnings growth, dividend support, and a constructive 
medium-term outlook.
03
Companies offering products and services to extend 
healthy lifespans should continue to gain traction.
•
We are constructive on the long-term outlook for obesity treatments as greater 
supply and lower pricing are enabling broader patient access.
•
Multiple late-stage trial results in cancer, cardio-metabolic conditions, and 
neurology in the coming months could provide further catalysts.
•
By 2030, we estimate annual revenues in the global longevity market could reach 
USD 8tr, up from USD 5.3tr in 2023.
New this week
US President Donald Trump on 30 September announced 
a voluntary AI safety accord with six major companies, 
including Google, Meta, NVIDIA, and SpaceX, committing 
them to measures such as internal controls and independent 
audits of advanced AI systems. The agreement, which 
Trump described as “morally binding,” does not include 
enforcement mechanisms or penalties for non-compliance.
One liner
We favor a diversified approach to investing in 
transformational innovation, enabling investors to stay at the 
forefront of change, seek long-term growth, and manage 
specific risks.
Did you know?
•
With hyperscalers raising their 2026 capital expenditure 
estimates and projecting further increases in 2027, we 
now expect overall AI spending to hit USD 900bn this 
year and USD 1.2tr in 2027.
•
Data centers could account for up to 9% of total US 
electricity use by 2035 (versus around 4% currently), 
based on Bloomberg New Energy Finance forecasts.
•
For global obesity drugs (GLP-1s), we forecast 12% 
revenue growth per year on average through 2030, 
driven by rapid adoption, potential clinical benefits 
stretching beyond weight-loss treatment, expanding 
insurance coverage, and potential launches of oral 
GLP-1s.
Investment view
We believe that exposure to structural trends will remain a 
key differentiator for equity market performance over the 
long run. We see transformational innovation opportunities in 
Artificial intelligence, Power and resources, and Longevity.

How to diversify with alternatives?
Key message
As stocks and bonds move in lockstep, we believe alternative investments 
have a role to play in long-term portfolios as both a source of differentiated 
returns and as potential portfolio diversifiers. But geopolitical uncertainty, 
pockets of credit market stress, and withdrawal limits warrant a review of 
positioning, including liquidity, risk tolerance, and a bias toward diversification 
and selectivity across strategies, sectors, and regions.
House view
01
Hedge funds can be a fresh return source and portfolio 
stabilizer.
•
We continue to favor discretionary macro for its ability to navigate policy 
divergence and to diversify traditional equity and bond exposure at times when 
government bonds and stocks sell off together.
•
We also prefer lower net and equity market neutral strategies, alongside multi-
strategy funds. High dispersion and low correlations between stocks support alpha 
generation while diversified return sources and risk management can navigate 
market rotations.
02
Private markets still offer opportunities for 
diversification, return generation, and income.
•
In private equity, we favor value-oriented strategies, diversification into Europe 
and Asia (e.g., Japan), and secondaries, with technology, health care, energy, and 
defense offering structural growth.
•
In private debt, we continue to see merits for the asset class in a diversified 
long-term-oriented portfolio, but in the near term, the risk-return outlook has 
diminished, underscoring the need for greater selectivity and caution.
•
In real estate and infrastructure, we favor existing assets with visible income, 
resilient cash flows, and disciplined interest-rate risk management, particularly 
those benefiting from deglobalization, the energy transition, and digitalization.
03
We see numerous paths to invest in alternatives, 
subject to careful risk management.
•
Investors should carefully weigh potential investments against their needs for 
liquidity, especially in an environment where exits may be delayed or restructured 
amid uncertainty.
New this week
A 30 September Reuters article reported that the US 
Securities and Exchange Commission was proposing 
legislative changes to "democratize" access to private 
markets for retail investors. These include opening up 
accredited investor status to a wider group and changing 
advisor fees and share redemption requirements on certain 
types of vehicles.
One liner
Including an allocation to alternatives can improve 
diversification, growth potential, and portfolio resilience 
through the market cycle.
Did you know?
In the current climate, we believe the investment 
backdrop for infrastructure is solid. Investor appetite is 
strong: PitchBook data show that trailing 12-month net 
flows into open-ended infrastructure funds increased 
30% year over year through the first quarter of 2026. 
Performance moderated at the start of 2026, with the 
asset class returning 1.2% in the first quarter, potentially 
reflecting weaker sentiment in March amid heightened 
geopolitical concerns. Nevertheless, strong conviction in 
long-term secular themes, combined with solid year-to-
date returns from listed infrastructure equities, supports 
continued optimism.
Investment view
Incorporating alternatives into a diversified portfolio may 
enhance returns, reduce volatility, and generate income 
through the market cycle. Alternatives such as hedge funds 
and private markets can provide unique return streams that 
complement traditional assets. However, investors should 
be willing and able to bear the unique risks of alternative 
investments, including illiquidity, strategy complexity, and 
potential lockup periods on invested capital.

What's next for commodities?
Key message
While shifting conditions in the Middle East continue to impact oil and refined 
product markets, we believe the investment case for broad commodity 
exposure goes well beyond energy, offering both return potential and 
inflation-hedging benefits. Industrial metals should stay supported by 
investment in AI and electrification. Agricultural commodities have potential 
for gains given El Niño-related risks. And gold remains a useful strategic 
diversifier. We favor active management because commodity leadership 
is likely to rotate as geopolitical conditions, inventories, and demand 
expectations change.
House view
01
Middle East tensions are impacting energy markets.
•
European natural gas prices (Dutch TTF futures) hit EUR 84.5 per megawatt-hour
on 14 September, the highest level since December 2022.
•
Brent crude oil prices remain volatile, trading at around USD 102/bbl at the time
of writing.
•
Military exchanges between the US and Iran are continuing, with both sides
recently targeting oil tankers.
02
But fundamentals are supportive for broad
commodities.
•
AI infrastructure and electrification underpin the long-term outlook for industrial 
metals like copper.
•
Forecasts suggest a near 80% probability this year's El Niño reaches "very 
strong" or "super" rating by year-end and a 97% chance that it persists into 
2027.
•
Gold should remain supported by central bank demand and reserve 
diversification, despite a more challenging near-term outlook.
03
So, we continue to favor commodities, with a focus on
active management.
•
Commodities have historically shown low correlations with equities and bonds,
making them a useful portfolio diversifier.
•
A diversified, regularly rebalanced exposure is the simplest way to capture most
of the asset class’s benefits, while selective tilts can add value when conviction is
high.
•
However, investors need to be aware of unique risks such as price swings and
costs associated with futures or physical holdings.
New this week
G7 countries on 2 October agreed to release 100 million 
barrels of crude oil and diesel from emergency reserves, 
and pledged to avoid energy export restrictions, following 
reported US pressure to help ease global fuel supply concern.
One liner
Broad commodity exposure can enhance portfolio 
diversification, improve portfolios' inflation resilience, and 
provide access to structural demand trends.
Did you know?
•
Investors can access commodities through diversified
indices, exchange-traded funds (ETFs), exchange-traded
commodities (ETCs), or structured investments.
•
From January 1999 through May 2026, commodities
showed a correlation of 0.44 with global equities
and -0.04 with US bonds, supporting their role as a
differentiated return source.
•
For investors with substantial allocations and significant
unrealized profits in gold, broadening commodity
exposure to include copper, aluminum, and agricultural
assets can help diversify sources of future returns, in our
view.
Investment view
Commodities should continue to play a prominent role in 
portfolios, offering diversification amid supply-demand 
imbalances, geopolitical risks, and the global energy 
transition. We like broad commodity exposure, with an active 
approach amid still-elevated volatility.

Should investors pay more attention to cash?
Key message
With investment opportunities abundant and the Fed embarking on a new 
hiking cycle, investors should manage their cash holdings strategically. First, 
they should optimize returns on necessary liquidity. They should then establish 
a plan to deploy excess cash into markets, retaining sufficient liquidity for 
expected withdrawals and near-term commitments. Phasing strategies can 
help put capital to work while reducing the risk of mistiming markets.
House view
01
Investors should begin by actively planning how much
cash they really need.
•
CIO recommends that investors maintain enough cash to cover 2-5 years of net
expected portfolio withdrawals.
•
If an investor holds more than this, their excess cash may not be working
efficiently.
•
A thoughtful cash management strategy involves segmenting liquidity into three
categories: everyday cash, core liquidity, and investment cash. It is crucial to
optimize diversification, liquidity, and returns within each.
02
Focusing on high fixed rates may overlook risks.
•
The Federal Reserve raised rates in September. We believe it will hike again in 
December, then hold, whereas markets expect around three hikes in the next 12 
months. A wider range of outcomes speaks to greater flexibility.
•
Investors should consider a more deliberate mix of short-term liquidity, fixed-
term deposits, and high-quality bonds across maturities for income and 
diversification.
03
Investing surplus cash systematically can overcome
emotional pitfalls.
•
Investors today may be loath to invest near all-time highs or fearful of a
substantial correction. But history suggests market timing seldom pays.
•
Making a plan to phase into core portfolios when stocks decline by a certain
threshold—or delegating the management to professionals—may help
overcome bias and help investors focus on the constructive growth, earnings,
and Fed rate outlook.
New this week
Renewed inflation concerns, fiscal risks, and widening 
sovereign spreads have pushed French borrowing costs 
to multi-decade highs. The yield spread of 10-year French 
government bonds over German government bonds briefly 
surpassed 150 basis points on Friday, its highest level since 
late 2011.
One liner
In an uncertain world, and yet one where we see compelling 
investment opportunities across asset classes, we believe 
treating cash as a residual may be a mistake.
Did you know?
•
History shows that phasing excess cash into diversified
portfolios can deliver more robust long-term outcomes.
Over 10- and 20-year holding periods, US stocks have
beaten cash in 97% and 100% of cases, respectively,
between 1945 and July of this year, based on our
analysis.
•
CIO analysis of S&P 500 price data since 1954 finds that,
12 months following the first Fed rate hike of a cycle,
the S&P 500 has tended to rise about 10%.
Investment view
We believe investors should optimize returns on necessary 
cash, using a variety of tools to balance returns against 
managing interest rate, credit, FX, and market risk over a 5-
year horizon. Phasing excess capital into diversified portfolios 
of stocks, bonds, commodities, and alternatives can maximize 
chances of achieving financial goals, seize current tactical 
opportunities in stocks, and reduce timing risk.

How can I broaden beyond tech in US equities?
Key message
We expect US equity markets to move higher over the next 12 months, driven 
by robust AI investment, broad-based earnings growth, and gradually easing 
inflation. But investors should take steps to manage concentration risks by 
ensuring single-stock exposures are diversified. Investors should combine a 
diversified core equity allocation with targeted exposure to transformational 
innovation and cyclical opportunities across sectors including industrials, 
financials, and consumer discretionary.

Demand for compute remains robust and continues to exceed available supply.

Constraints in semiconductors, power, and related infrastructure are unlikely to

We expect cloud revenue growth—an indicator of how companies are

But we expect future gains to come from a broader

•
A robust US economy, a solid labor market, strong credit creation, and 
continued fiscal support should all support earnings outside the AI complex, 
helping the rally to broaden further.
•
Consumer spending remains resilient and cyclical areas of the market are seeing 
a pickup in activity. We believe this cyclical strength should persist well into 2027.
•
We see the S&P 500 reaching 8,400 by June 2027. We forecast earnings per share 
of USD 350 (+25%) and USD 400 (+14%) for this year and next.
03
We believe investors should take steps to rebalance 
concentrated positions.
•
Investors should ensure their core equity allocation is broadly diversified. Our 
preferred US sectors are consumer discretionary, financials, health care, industrials, 
and utilities.
•
We also favor targeted exposure to structural growth trends—not only AI, but also 
power and resources and longevity.
•
Investors with concentrated positions in megacap tech can consider using rallies to 
rebalance into structured investments that offer a degree of capital preservation.

Investment view
The medium-term outlook for equities is positive, in our view, 
supported by strong earnings growth. However, high market 
concentration and uncertainty over how AI-related value 
creation will ultimately be distributed mean diversification 
is key. We recommend that investors broaden their equity 
holdings, rebalance concentrated positions, and consider 
hedging strategies (e.g., capital preservation) to navigate 
specific risks.
