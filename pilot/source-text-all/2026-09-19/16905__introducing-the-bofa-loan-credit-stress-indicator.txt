The BofA Loan Credit Stress Indicator 
We introduce the BofA Loan Credit Stress Indicator, or BofA Loan CSI, developed to 
represent credit conditions in the Broadly Syndicated Loan market. What started as an 
indicator for the HY asset class years ago, has now been adapted to Loans, helping 
investors understand market conditions specific to the Loan asset class. Diverging credit 
quality and increasing rates uncertainty has recently catalyzed a structural disparity in 
bond-loan performance, making it necessary to look at the two asset classes separately. 
The objective of the BofA Loan CSI is to capture a full spectrum of credit inputs and 
create a unified measure of aggregate financial conditions in the leveraged loan market. 
We do so through three broad categories of factors that cover critical aspects of market 
functioning, namely 1) Volatility, 2) Market access & fundamentals, and 3) Credit Stress. 
Within each category, we select factors that are directly observable from the market and 
have good leading relationships to Loan spreads, including measures of volatility, market 
dispersion, distress, funding accessibility, credit quality, and fundamentals. The BofA 
Loan CSI will be refreshed weekly in our strategy reports, and is intended to be an 
indicative metric only, not to be used as a benchmark. 
Not as loose as you suspect 
The BofA Loan CSI is quoted as a percentile representing how tight credit conditions are 
in the loan market compared to its post-GFC history. The indicator is able to explain a 
large majority of forward-looking spread variation in loans, and holds an r-squared of 
66% to avg next-3m Loan 3yrDM (72% post Covid). The indicator is currently at 49% 
implying conditions in the loan market are historically neutral- contrary to the general 
narrative of tight valuations that currently exist in the credit space. In contrast, our HY 
CSI sits at 34%, more in line with that assessment.

 BofA Loan Credit Stress Indicator 
Financial conditions play a key role in determining phases of a credit cycle- one of the 
key questions in LevFin. The bust phase is always preempted by a sharp tightening in 
financial conditions; and the recovery phase starts only after financial conditions have 
started to improve. As such, the real-time tracking of financial conditions is key to 
understanding the current phase of a cycle and its likely direction. This was the general 
goal of our baseline CSI created several years ago- to track credit conditions and overall 
level of stress in the HY market. 
That said, credit conditions can often diverge across LevFin. Factors such as credit 
quality, sector mix, rates sensitivity, and ownership structure have been creating 
meaningful divergence between HY and Leveraged Loan performance, as seen over the 
last year. It is therefore the right time to enhance our toolbox to include a version of the 
CSI catered specifically to the Broadly Syndicated Leveraged Loan (BSL) market. 
Construction of the Indicator 
Our BofA Loan Credit Stress Indicator (BofA Loan CSI) has been developed to represent 
those conditions with a focus on the leveraged loan space, as opposed to other broadly 
available financial condition indices. The objective of the BofA Loan CSI is to capture a 
full spectrum of credit stress inputs and represent a unified aggregate measure of 
financial conditions in the leveraged loan market. 
To do so, we identified several categories of factors that cover all critical aspects of 
loan market functioning:  
• 
volatility: measures of implied/realized vols in credit and equities; 
• 
market access & fundamentals: measures of funding access, leverage and 
risk tolerance; 
• 
credit stress: measures of distress, dispersion, and diffusion.

Each input was chosen to satisfy two requirements: (1) being a high-frequency, market-
observed value as opposed to estimates, forecasts, and stale inputs; and (2) having a 
strong leading relationship to Loan spreads as measured by 3y discounted margin 
(3yDM). By design, it is a more comprehensive measure of credit stress and market 
function than simplistic factors such as the dollar price or spread. 
 
Specific factors included in the BofA Loan CSI are listed in Exhibit 1, where: 
• 
Distress is the proportion of the Loan Index1 trading below 80pts in price.  
• 
Dispersion represents the proportion of Loan Index trading at least a +/-100bps 
away from the overall Index level. 
• 
Diffusion is the spread standard deviation of individual loans to their 
quality/sector/duration cohorts. 
• 
Credit access represents primary market health, and is the weighted average 
percentile score of (1) credit quality of new issues; (2) their absolute pricing; (3) 
new issue yield premium vs. Loan Index; (4) new issuance volume as % of Index 
size. CAS of 1 = max openness. 
• 
Risk tolerance is indicated by the proportion of loans trading >100 pts in price, 
and highlighting investor risk appetite to push prices above par. 
• 
Debt leverage is the ratio of Debt to Enterprise Value measured in percentage 
points. 
• 
VIX Index is used for equity vol, 3M price vol of Loan Index for Loan vol, and 3M 
call implied vol of HYG bond ETF for HY vol. 
Most input factors are market-observable on a daily basis. The only exception is debt 
leverage, which is derived from the fundamental data of public issuers that report on a 
quarterly basis, which coincides with its lower r-squared. BofA Loan CSI is intended to 
be updated weekly in our strategy reports, and the latest data will be used wherever 
available. Otherwise, the most recent observations of inputs will be carried forward until 
new data becomes available. 
Construction methodology 
To calculate the BofA Loan CSI, we first fit a univariate regression of each factor against 
the N3M average loan spread. We then calculate fitted values for each factor, and 
convert them into percentiles based on their history since July 2008, when constituent-
level data for the Morningstar LSTA Loan Index became available. These percentiles are 
expressed such that for each factor 0 represents the loosest credit conditions and 100 
represents the tightest credit conditions. 
The aggregate BofA Loan CSI is then calculated as a weighted average of the percentile 
ranks of all factors, with weights proportional to the statistical strength of their leading 
relationships with loan spreads, as measured by the R-squared of corresponding 
univariate regression. For example, as shown in Exhibit 1, Distress exhibits an R-squared 
of 58% in its regression against the N3M average loan spread. Dividing this value by the 
sum of the R-squared values across all input factors results in a weight of 15%.

BofA Loan CSI vs HY CSI: structurally divergent 
Exhibit 5 shows the illustrative evolution of the BofA Loan CSI vs the HY CSI since 2008. 
Financial conditions in both Levfin markets generally move together as expected, except 
for macro backdrops which are especially punitive to one asset class vs the other- the 
exact goal of having two differentiated indicators.  
We see divergences pop up amid rising recession fears (2011, 2012, 2020, 2025) due to 
Loans’ higher beta and lower liquidity. In contrast, conditions for HY were tighter in 
2016 in the aftermath of the Energy crisis. In 2022, BofA Loan CSI finished higher than 
HY as floating rate issuers bore the immediate brunt of Fed’s aggressive hiking cycle. 
Recently, the gap between the two has widened massively, more so than any historical 
time period, perhaps indicating a structural transformation in the way these two markets 
are currently operating.  
The point at which this transition took place offers value- Sep 2025 was the month of 
the unexpected twin bankruptcies that rattled the BSL and PC markets and brought to 
the forefront under-appreciated risks of fragility in some pockets of secured loan 
lending on the back of years of high interest rates. This quickly grew into concerns 
around private credit asset quality, further exacerbated by concerns around AI 
obsolescence risk which affects a bigger proportion of loans and PC than HY.

CLO Spreads and Arbitrage 
As of Sep 17th, AAA CLO primary spreads are at 125bps, at similar levels as compared to 
start of year (125bps) (Exhibit 43). Arbitrage as of Sep 11th is 202bps, higher than its 
190bps level heading into the year, but down slightly from early March peak of 231bps 
(Exhibit 44).  
Exhibit 44 shows CLO spread levels by tranches. CLO arbitrage is a widely followed 
statistic in the loan market and represents the theoretical spread that managers can 
capture by issuing CLOs. Exhibit 44 compares CLO asset (Loan) spreads to the weighted 
average spreads of CLO liabilities. The difference between these two values is 
theoretical arbitrage and represents the current attractiveness of creating new CLOs. 
Our asset pricing is based on both primary (higher weight) and secondary loans, and 
liability pricing is based on the most current CLO primary pipeline from LevFin Insights.
