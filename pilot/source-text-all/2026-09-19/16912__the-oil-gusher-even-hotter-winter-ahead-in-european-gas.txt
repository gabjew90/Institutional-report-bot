Even ‘hotter’ winter ahead in European gas 
Rating Change 
   
 
 
Key takeaways 
• Upgrade EQNR + HBR from Neutral to Buy on greater uplift to estimates on above-
strip gas price forecast into winter and 2027 
• EUR25/MWh and $70/bbl Brent mid-cycle unchanged; we also reiterate Buy ratings on 
TTE, GALP, ITH, ENOG + preference for OFS 
• More upside risks for Europe's TTF gas price driven by Qatari LNG start-up delays, low 
seasonal storage and winter elasticity 
We see upside to Europe’s gas price strip into winter 
Europe’s gas storage remains at historical lows just weeks before heating season kicks 
off – at 68% full vs. 84% 10-year average. Europe’s TTF gas spot prices are up 178% 
YTD and currently trade at >$150/boe (as well as ~9x Henry Hub). However, TTF remains 
backwardated into the winter and below Asia’s JKM hub gas price. Our Commodities 
Research team now expects TTF gas prices to increase by another 25% this winter – in 
our view with risks stacked in favour of Hormuz upside vs. El Niño downside. 
Four years later: Another ‘hot winter’ ahead in Europe 
We note today’s elevated TTF gas prices in Europe only trade ~75% below 2022 peaks – 
partly thanks to not repeating panic-buying on the loss of Russian pipeline gas imports 
then. But the latter was not just offset by increasing LNG imports by ~50%: Europe’s 
gas demand today still stands >16% below 2021 levels – with incremental demand 
destruction in our view even more expensive today given both nuclear and coal power 
plant shutdowns as well as industrial activity losses recorded since. But without lifting 
our mid-cycle forecast from 2028 onwards, what’s already priced into stocks? 
Upgrade Equinor and Harbour Energy to Buy 
Our new TTF gas price deck drives our annual average FCF estimates up by ~5% into 
2027 – with greatest aggregate 2026-27 upgrades among Big Oils at Equinor (+22%) 
and among E&Ps at Harbour (+66%). As a result, we now see even more upside to 
consensus estimates – as high as 143% on Equinor’s 2027 FCF. The resultant changes 
to our POs leave TTE, EQNR, GALP, HBR, ITH and ENOG as the only Big Oils / E&Ps 
under our coverage not already discounting more than our (unchanged) mid-cycle 
$70/bbl Brent and <$9/mbtu TTF assumptions from 2028 onwards. 
Reiterate Buy on GTT as key LNG beneficiary 
GTT’s order intake has accelerated as we anticipated: 72 LNGC membrane orders YTD 
surpassing the FY25 result of 37 – with more to come, underpinned by record LNG 
project approvals in 2025. Next? Our global LNG project tracker adds to our conviction 
for further records ahead with 85mtpa potential FIDs in each 2026 and 2027 (see 
Exhibit 24). GTT is our key LNG supply growth beneficiary, with 100% market share.

 TTF forecast ahead of strip into winter 
We raise our winter TTF gas price forecast 
Winter is nearly here – and however warm it may end up being, we believe it 
could be an even hotter winter for European TTF gas prices: Missing Qatari LNG 
exports mixed with European gas storage on pace to enter winter at record lows 
leaves hopes for warmer El Niño weather to drive TTF prices back <EUR50/MWh. 
However, our Commodities Research team believes a cold winter could drive TTF 
to the 2022 highs leaving physical consumers and governments to scramble for 
LNG and spur additional demand destruction.  
Higher 2026-27 gas macro deck, but 2028 unchanged 
We raise our baseline European TTF gas assumptions behind our company estimates: 
• 
EUR100/MWh for 4Q26 means we see FY26 prices average ~EUR63/MWh (from 
~EUR52/MWh).  
• 
EUR55/MWh for 2027 (from <EUR40/MWh) assuming a return to EUR40/MWh 
average prices in 2H27 
• 
We leave our mid-cycle assumption from 2028 onwards unchanged at EUR25/MWh.

Investment Rationale 
Equinor ASA 
We rate Equinor Buy. Equinor is among the most exposed to European spot gas prices 
(>30% of its hydrocarbon production), which we see further upside to heading into this 
winter. We see further upside potential to shareholder returns given Equinor will exit 
2027 with >$10bn net cash. 
 
Price objective basis & risk 
Aker BP (DETNF) 
Our NOK215 price objective is based on a full asset life discounted cash flow (DCF). We 
derive our equity valuation once deducting net financial debt as well as decommissioning 
liabilities. 
 
Our DCF reflects a long-term oil price assumption of $70. We use a 7% discount rate. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: A lower oil price than forecast, cost inflation, project delays or 
unexpected production issues. 
 
Upside risks to our PO: A higher oil price than forecast, faster than expected 
development timelines, cost savings or exploration success.

BP plc (BPAQF / BP) 
Our PO of 500p/share (US$41/ADR) is based on our bottom-up cash flow model and 
resultant sum-of-the-parts valuation. Our SOTP valuation uses DCF valuation for BP's 
sub-divisions based on differentiated discount rates: 9.2% for Downstream, 5.1% for 
Renewables as well as 8.8% for Corporate (assuming a zero perpetuity growth rate for 
all). Our divisional DCF valuations are usually based on perpetuity assumptions beyond 
2030 - except for BP's E&P assets: Here we run a "depletion DCF" (at a 8.6% discount 
rate) and disregard both the income and capex attributable to future prospects 
(effectively assuming these are value neutral) while based on our long-term Brent oil 
price assumption of $70/bbl. This E&P analysis excludes Russia. 
 
Risks (upward and downward) to our PO are sharp moves in refining margins, oil and gas 
prices as well as the USD. Other risks are unanticipated government intervention and 
regulation, expropriation risk, project execution/oil spill/environmental risk, bankruptcy 
risk, litigation risk, M&A risk and the general risk of increased taxes and tariffs. In 
addition, our PO is subject to significant exploration success or lack thereof, general 
risks of changes in taxes and tariffs as well as fluctuating capex costs. 
Energean (EERGF / XMQFF) 
Our 930GBp price objective (ILS 37) is based on blended value from a dividend discount 
model (DDM) and underlying DCF over the full asset life valuation method. For the DDM 
we explicitly discount dividends modelled out to 2028 and apply a terminal growth rate 
of -2% thereafter. We blend the DDM and DCF with weights of 30% and 70%, 
respectively. 
 
We use a long-term oil price of $70. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: operational issues at its key Karish asset, cost inflation, and a 
weaker than expected commodity price environment for spot price exposed liquid sales. 
 
Upside risks to our PO: gas sales ahead of expectations via spot sales or new sales 
agreements, further exploration success and a stronger than expected commodity price 
environment for spot price exposed liquid sales. 
Eni (EIPAF / E) 
Our Eni PO is €24 (US$55/ADR). Our price objective is derived from divisional 
discounted cash flow (DCF) models, where we use discount rates between 6.5-8.5%, 
before deducting financial net debt and other balance sheet provisions to arrive at our 
equity value. Our long-term Brent price is $70/bbl. 
 
Upside risks to our PO: (1) upside to our Brent price forecast (2) upside to our European 
gas forecast (3) operational upsides primarily within the Upstream or gas trading 
divisions (4) favourable USD/EUR exchange rate movements (5) favourable value 
crystallisation for its renewable division. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: (1) downside to our Brent price forecast (2) downside to our 
European gas forecast (3) operational downsides primarily within the Upstream or gas 
trading divisions (4) unfavourable USD/EUR exchange rate movements. 
EnQuest (ENQUF) 
Our 24GBp price objective is based on a full asset life DCF. We derive our equity 
valuation once deducting net financial debt including lease liabilities. We use a long-term 
oil price of $70/bbl and a 12% WACC reflecting EnQuest's higher risk equity profile 
versus our industry default of 10%. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: A lower oil price than forecast, operational issues that lower 
production and/or recoverable resources.

Equinor ASA (STOHF / EQNR)

objective (PO) of NOK465 (USD49). 
 
Downside risks to our price objective are a significant change in the price of oil or

circumstances with operation.

liabilities and provisions from our calculated enterprise value. 
 
Upside risks to our PO: (1) higher LNG demand / prices than expected which could spur 
more LNG project approvals, (2) faster vessel project execution than anticipated, (3) 
higher vessel scrappage rates than expected, (4) faster growth than anticipated in GTT's 
Digital business. 
 
Downside risks to our PO: (1) lower LNG demand / prices than expected which could spur 
more LNG project approvals, (2) slower vessel project execution than anticipated, (3) 
higher vessel scrappage rates than expected, (4) slower growth than anticipated in GTT's 
Digital business.
