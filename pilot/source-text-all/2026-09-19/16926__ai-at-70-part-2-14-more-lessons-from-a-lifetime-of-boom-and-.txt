Adrian Cox | AI at 70 (Part 2): 14 more lessons from a lifetime of boom and bust | September 2026
The term “artificial intelligence” almost didn’t exist. Dartmouth College 
Professor John McCarthy invented it to attract scientists to come to his 
landmark summer workshop 70 years ago after his first attempt, “automata 
studies”, failed to excite much interest.
This is the second of our two reports looking at what the past 70 years of 
stunning breakthroughs and false dawns since then can tell us about what 
to expect from the current technology and investment boom. 
The first, “AI at 70: 14 lessons from a lifetime of boom and bust”, reviewed 
themes including non-linear growth to infrastructure constraints and 
valuation booms and busts. 
This one builds on those themes and gives them a spin forward to the 
future, including the speed of investment compared with past booms, a 
snapshot of AI’s strengths and weaknesses, a brief history of previous tech 
IPOs, and an overview of the prospects for jobs, trust and risks.
The stakes look higher than ever.
The leading companies have released even more capable models in the past 
week; there has been a series of security breaches where AI agents 
appeared to collaborate to evade detection; and a senior government 
official who joined OpenAI warned yesterday that “most people could die” if 
superintelligence is built without more robust safeguards.
