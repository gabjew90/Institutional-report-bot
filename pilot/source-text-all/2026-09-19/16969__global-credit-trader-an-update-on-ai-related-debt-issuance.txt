An Update on AI-Related Debt Issuance  
 
 
In late July, we outlined our top-down framework for AI-related debt issuance from the 
hyperscalers, using the CapEx estimates from our colleagues in GIR equity research as a 
key input. At the time, our estimate for 35% of 2027 CapEx to be financed with debt 
mapped to roughly $400 billion of gross debt issuance, globally, directly issued from the 
group. (Note: this excludes project finance issuance for data center and chip financing, 
which we forecast separately, expecting $300 billion in 2027). 
Several weeks later—and with the benefit of the most recent round of earnings results—
we refresh the gross hyperscaler issuance implied by our forecasts. As Exhibit 1 
illustrates, we now expect $420 billion of gross debt issuance, which reflects some 
modest upward revisions in CapEx forecasts. Our colleagues in GIR equity research have 
recently highlighted the ongoing supply-demand imbalance related to the AI build out 
and expect the CapEx cycle through the end of 2027 to remain elevated. 
 
This forecast would represent more than a 60% increase vs. our full-year 2026 estimate 
of $250 billion of direct global hyperscaler IG debt issuance. So far this year, $229 billion 
has been issued, across the range of currencies outlined in Exhibit 2. 
We continue to expect 65% to 75% of the 2027 hyperscaler gross debt issuance to price 
in the USD IG bond market, subject to market conditions and investor receptivity in light 
of issuer concentration considerations. As Exhibit 3 illustrates, there is still meaningful 
capacity for the group to grow their debt levels before reaching the size of the largest 
issuers.

A diverse mix of global syndicated credit markets have participated in the AI-related 
financing, as shown in Exhibit 6. We continue to expect the share of AI-related issuance 
from the EUR market will increase, considering its year-to-date share has lagged a range 
of other global IG markets. Moreover, as Exhibit 7 illustrates, the other non-USD global 
IG corporate credit markets pale in comparison to the size of the EUR IG market, limiting 
the amount of ‘heavy lifting’ they might do. Away from the traditional credit markets, we 
also see a larger role for convertible bond issuance in financing the AI buildout. 
 
The performance bifurcation between AI-related spreads and the rest of the market 
persists, as shown in Exhibit 8 and Exhibit 9. We believe the relative resilience of the 
broader USD IG market reflects two key factors. First is the combination of a supportive 
macro backdrop, including solid economic activity, generally constructive credit 
fundamentals, and an attractive all-in yield opportunity that continues to generate 
demand from yield-based investors. The second factor is the diversification value 
provided by bonds in larger sectors more directly removed from the AI theme, including 
Banks, Energy, and Healthcare, among others. 
While spreads for AI-related names, especially hyperscalers, have found some relief 
locally, we remain cautious on adding meaningful exposure to the segment. Near term, 
our supply estimates imply that much of hyperscaler funding this year has been 
completed, but the nature of the multi-year build-out keeps risks skewed to the upside, 
as sanguine sentiment could instead catalyze issuance. As the build-out has grown in 
scope, we continue to think that hardware names will remain less insulated from credit 
pressure as compute shipments, and related financing, grows. 
Issuance for the data center and compute ‘project finance’ segment remains even more 
difficult to assess, but we continue to think that it marches forward. We maintain that 
investors should be selective when investing in this segment, underwriting 
project-related risks in the face of a technical that is unlikely to abate in the near term.

As Exhibit 10 and Exhibit 11 highlight, the share of AI-related notional across the IG and

exposure is in the USD IG market, at 12% of total notional, followed by USD HY at 6%. In 
the EUR market, the figures are more contained at 3% for IG and 1% for HY. We see 
meaningful scope for this to grow as the multi-year issuance cycle extends. For context, 
the largest sector in the USD IG Corporate Index—Banking—is 22% of the total market 
value. In the EUR IG Corporate Index, Banks are even larger at 31%.

At current valuations and given our expectation for heightened dispersion, we prefer moving 
up in quality within EUR HY. 
Similar to the US, we prefer HY bonds given the loan market's weighting toward software 
(and the potential negative sentiment overhang). The reaction function of the ECB is 
increasing borrowing costs for floating rate issuers, given the central bank's focus on 
inflationary pressures.
EUR IG tranches offer carry pickup relative to USD IG CLO tranches. While the policy and 
macroeconomic picture is less supportive in Europe, subordination should protect tranches 
up in the capital structure from a continued idiosyncratic default picture. 
While we previously had a preference for IG vs. HY, we now view the case for an outright 
overweight in IG as less obvious based on valuations, rate sensitivity and supply related 
technicals. 
For floating rate investors, we prefer CLO mezzanine tranches vs. leveraged loans. 
Historical data points to low impairment rates for BB and BBB CLO tranches, and current 
valuations offer attractive carry and a cushion against moderate to elevated default 
outcomes.  Elsewhere, we are neutral between senior CLO tranches and USD IG; we would 
use spread widening to add exposure in CLOs. We are also neutral between CLO AAA and 
CMO floaters. 
While we previously had a slight preference for IG vs. HY, we now view the case for an 
outright overweight in IG as less obvious based on valuations, rate sensitivity and supply 
related technicals. 
The substantial software sector exposure in the loan market leaves us favoring the HY 
market, at least until we receive more clarity on the refinancing of the 2028 loan maturity 
walls. The lower-rated distribution of loans and our economists' base case for no rate hikes 
in 2026 are also contributing factors. 
Agency MBS offers an attractive diversification benefit vs. a portfolio of IG corporate bonds.
We view the additional spread compensation of hybrids vs. BBBs as moderately attractive in 
the context of the tight spread environment. We also expect the hybrid spread premium to 
compress vs. BBBs, as the hybrid market grows further.
Regional
We prefer owning BBBs over the higher-rated segments of USD IG (As and AAs), as a way 
to capture additional spread. We also see scope for balance sheet deterioration at the 
highest end of the rating spectrum in IG. 
We recently shifted our view to favor BBBs over both AAs and As in the EUR market, owing 
to an improved macroeconomic backdrop which now favors a broad-based, down-in-quality 
stance within IG. 
We expect BBs will continue to encounter a challenging supply technical due to AI-related 
supply. As a result, we favor the B rated cohort. The CCC universe is highly idiosyncratic and 
requires careful credit selection, in our view.
