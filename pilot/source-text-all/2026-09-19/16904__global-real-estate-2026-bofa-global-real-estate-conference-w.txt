Real estate momentum builds despite macro uncertainty 
The 20th Annual BofA Global Real Estate Conference came to a close this week. We met 
with 130+ global real estate companies from 15 countries. Attendance was in line with 
last year’s record levels. Our 2027 Save the Date will be out soon. 
Secular tailwinds are outweighing higher rates, for now 
Strong demand and declining new supply continue to outweigh higher financing costs 
across much of real estate. This was evident across meetings and reinforced by our 
Blackstone Real Estate Strategy panel, which characterized real estate as emerging from 
a prolonged downturn as supply declines, demand improves and capital returns. Higher-
for-longer rates remain a risk to balance sheets and external growth, but multi-year 
supply tailwinds support occupancy, pricing power and earnings visibility. The REIT 
compounding FCF story remains intact, highlighted in our Compounding Score analysis. 
Lower supply has become a multi-year growth tailwind 
Apartment operators pointed to stronger 2027 earn-in as deliveries decline, storage sees 
moderating ‘26-28 supply as a key recovery catalyst, and office landlords reported 
stronger leasing with little new construction. Our data center panel emphasized that 
power availability, rather than capital, is increasingly constraining development. In rapid-
fire questions, 72% of mgmt teams expect 2027 sector SS NOI growth to exceed 2026. 
Refis manageable, but higher-for-longer is a growth risk  
Our forecasts have not been assuming lower rates, and YTD refinancings have generally 
come in better than our expectations. Balance sheets remain positioned to extend 
maturities and fund investment, but our Navigating What’s Next panel stressed that 
long-term yields ultimately drive cap rates and valuations. 47% of management teams 
expect refinancing costs to have the largest impact on real estate from higher-for-
longer rates, ahead of lower supply at 42% and fewer transactions at 11%. 
Capital is broadening as investment pipelines build 
Capital favors high quality assets and proven operators, with investment pipelines 
expanding most visibly across senior housing, retail and net lease. REITs are combining 
balance sheet capacity with JVs, private capital, dispositions and retained cash flow. 72% 
of management teams expect third party capital to become a more important growth 
driver over the next three years. See our Third Party Capital note for more. 
Financing constraints could shift the advantage to REITs 
Higher rates could further extend the supply tailwind by making new development 
increasingly difficult to underwrite. Higher rates may also improve the competitive 
position of well-capitalized public REITs on external growth, as elevated financing costs 
make it more difficult for leveraged private buyers to acquire assets. Together, these 
dynamics could increase barriers to entry and create more favorable acquisition opps. for 
public REITs with available capital and/or help grow 3rd party capital platforms.

 Panel takeaways 
Blackstone’s Global Real Estate Strategy 
Jeff Horowitz, Managing Director and Global Head of Real Estate, Gaming and Lodging at 
BofA Securities GCIB, hosted a discussion with Ken Caplan, Global Co-Chief Investment 
Officer at Blackstone, focused on Blackstone's investment outlook, capital markets, and 
the implications of AI-driven demand across real estate and infrastructure. 
Blackstone (BX) remains constructive on the economy and real estate fundamentals, 
citing healthy consumers, strong corporate balance sheets, and the potential for AI-
driven productivity gains to be disinflationary. Caplan noted that Blackstone's global 
private equity portfolio continues to generate strong revenue growth, with Asia 
remaining particularly strong, the U.S. resilient, and Europe experiencing a slower 
environment. 
AI and digital infrastructure remain the firm's highest conviction investment themes. 
Caplan highlighted the unprecedented level of capital required to support AI 
development, with hyperscalers expected to invest roughly $800bn this year and 
potentially over $1tn next year. Blackstone believes this surge in demand is reshaping 
capital markets and creating significant opportunities across data centers, power 
infrastructure, and related real estate. Caplan compared today's AI-driven data center 
demand to the e-commerce boom that fueled logistics growth following the Global 
Financial Crisis, noting that Blackstone's investment in QTS five years ago has grown 
substantially as AI adoption accelerates.  
Beyond AI, Blackstone sees improving conditions across traditional real estate sectors. 
Caplan noted that real estate is emerging from a prolonged downturn, with new supply 
declining and demand improving across several property types. Investor interest has 
begun to recover alongside healthier capital markets, while industrial fundamentals 
continue to benefit from data center-related activity. Caplan also pointed to multifamily 
as an area where supply pressures should ease over time, creating a more favorable 
operating environment. More broadly, Blackstone believes real estate remains attractive 
given improving fundamentals, growing access to capital, and the sector's resilience 
despite ongoing uncertainty surrounding interest rates. 
While the outlook remains favorable, Caplan highlighted several risks to monitor, 
including the U.S. fiscal deficit, geopolitical tensions, energy price volatility, and the 
disruptive effects of AI. Even so, Blackstone views capital markets as broadly healthy, 
with strong lending activity, active M&A markets, and continued demand for high-quality 
assets. The firm remains constructive on transaction activity despite higher rates, noting 
that capital continues to flow toward the best assets and strongest operators. 
Regionally, Blackstone continues to favor the U.S. because of its economic strength and 
property market fundamentals, while remaining active in Europe and Asia where select 
opportunities continue to emerge. 
Global Forces Shaping Real Estate Strategy 
Jeffrey Spector, Head of U.S. REITs at BofA Global Research, moderated a discussion 
with Aditya Bhave, Senior U.S. Economist; Savita Subramanian, Head of U.S. Equity & 
Quantitative Strategy; and David Tinsley, Senior Economist at the Bank of America 
Institute, focused on the macroeconomic, consumer, and capital market forces shaping 
the outlook for real estate. 
Aditya Bhave described the U.S. economy as resilient, supported by consumer spending 
and AI-related capital investment. Labor market and spending trends continue to exceed 
expectations, with growth broadening beyond higher-income households, while inflation 
remains above the Fed's target. As a result, BofA expects additional policy tightening, 
though Aditya noted that fiscal policy and the sustainability of the AI investment cycle 
remain key risks to the outlook.

Around the World in Data Centers 
Marc Mozzi, Head of EMEA REITs at BofA Global Research, moderated a discussion on 
the global datacentre market with Andrew Power, President and CEO of Digital Realty, 
and Ismael Clemente, Vice Chairman, CEO and CIO of Merlin Properties. 
Importantly, both Andrew Power and Ismael Clemente emphasised that datacentre 
demand is increasingly multifaceted, with AI, cloud migration and the long-term 
digitalisation of the economy collectively driving capacity requirements. At the same 
time, new supply continues to be constrained by limited power availability globally and 
an aging energy grid. While capital remains available to fund development, the key 
limiting factor is increasingly access to power rather than access to financing. Andrew 
Powers highlighted that despite the noise of a slowdown, its "no pencils down" in AI and 
Ismael Clemente argued in favour of an independent regulatory body to oversee the 
evolution and capabilities of agentic AI. 
This constraint is particularly relevant in Europe, where historic underinvestment in grid 
infrastructure and the difficulty of securing appropriately powered sites are restricting 
development. Another constraint is the regulatory landscape: in Spain, the proposed 
Royal Decree would require new facilities to source 80% renewable energy on an hourly 
basis. Merlin Properties estimated that supporting Spain’s targeted 10GW of data-centre 
capacity by 2040 could require approximately €110bn of investment. Against this 
backdrop, expectations for growth in the Iberian market may be running ahead of the 
power and grid infrastructure available to support the current development pipeline. 
The panellists also highlighted an increasing shift towards rural data-centre 
developments, where power availability and expansion capacity can be more readily 
secured. Improvements in transmission technology and lower-latency cables are 
reducing the effective distance between facilities and their end markets, with 
transatlantic latency expected to fall from 60 to 30 milliseconds. Developers are 
therefore increasingly prioritising locations offering visibility over long-term power 
supply and sufficient capacity for future expansion. Once a hyperscale customer anchors 
a campus, additional buildings and customers typically follow, supporting the progressive 
scaling of the site. 
Global Real Estate: Navigating What's Next 
Sarah Cooper, Managing Director and Global Head of Real Estate Equity Sales at BofA 
Securities, hosted Sonny Kalsi, Chairman, President & CEO of SLC Management, and 
Goodwin Gaw, Chairman of Gaw Capital, for a discussion on the macro outlook, AI, and 
global real estate opportunities. 
The discussion opened with the resilience of U.S. REITs despite a challenging rate 
backdrop. Sonny highlighted the inflationary impact of energy prices, trade policy, and 
AI-related investment, while Goodwin pointed to uncertainty around the U.S. dollar and 
global capital flows. Both emphasized that long-term Treasury yields matter more than

Fed policy rates, as the long end of the curve ultimately drives cap rates and real estate 
valuations. 
The panel also discussed AI's impact on real estate. Goodwin noted AI-related leasing 
demand in markets such as San Francisco and Seattle, while Sonny pointed to improving 
fundamentals in premium New York City office assets. Both agreed AI's impact on 
employment and real estate remains in the early stages, with adoption likely to occur as 
a "glide path, not a cliff." 
Data Centers were a major focus of the discussion. While both executives acknowledged 
strong AI-driven demand for computing power and digital infrastructure, they expressed 
caution around the sector's economics. Goodwin remains constructive on data centers 
but noted that eventual exit opportunities remain an important consideration. Sonny 
highlighted elevated costs of capital, large development requirements, and uncertainty 
around long-term returns. He also expressed a preference for Europe over the U.S., 
noting that roughly two-thirds of his firm's data center exposure is located in Europe. 
On capital allocation, Goodwin emphasized contrarian investing, highlighting Hong Kong, 
self-storage, student housing, and luxury hotels across Europe and Asia. Sonny's highest-
conviction theme remains U.S. multifamily housing, supported by affordability 
challenges, elevated mortgage rates, and limited supply. He also highlighted European 
industrial real estate, citing reshoring, energy investment, and defense spending as key 
demand drivers. 
Finally, the panel expressed a favorable view on real estate credit. Sonny argued that 
tighter banking regulation and continued financing needs have created attractive 
opportunities for private lenders, favoring senior real estate credit targeting consistent 
10-12% returns over higher-risk equity strategies.

3Q26 is tracking in line with CPT’s outlook as of 2Q26 earnings. CPT expects 3Q26 
blended rent spreads of 1-2% (up from -0.2% in 2Q26) with same store occupancy

• 
CPT has been active recycling capital throughout 2026, selling its California 
portfolio, acquiring roughly $800M of assets and buying back $423M of stock. CPT 
is open to leveraging the balance sheet for additional investments, including more 
share repurchases. 
• 
CPT’s retention is operating at historically high levels. While higher mortgage rates 
are keeping some people in place, CPT attributed higher retention primarily to 
demographic shifts, with people getting married and having children later in life. 
CPT’s median renter age of 32 years old has risen by roughly two years over the last 
10 years. 
Essex Property Trust Inc (ESS) 
• 
ESS has approximately 150bp of earn-in for 2027 based on YTD activity through 
August, which is 65bp above last year’s earn-in for 2026. There is potential for

• 
Green shoots are starting to emerge in Seattle from lower supply. Improvements in

than to home affordability pressures.

MAA continues to ramp Wi-Fi investments, representing a $45M NOI opportunity 
over the next 5 years. Supply in its markets is expected to remain below long-term 
average deliveries over the next 3-5 years.
