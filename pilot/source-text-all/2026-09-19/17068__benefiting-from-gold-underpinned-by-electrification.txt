CIO View: Silver
Dominic Schnider, CFA, CAIA, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
• Silver continues to track gold closely, with the gold-silver correlation 
near multi-year highs. We expect the metal to benefit from gold’s 
supportive backdrop, including fiscal concerns, and longer-term risks 
to the US dollar.
• Industrial demand remains structurally supportive despite substitution 
and thrifting in solar applications. Growth in data centers, AI 
infrastructure, grid investment, and electric vehicles should help 
offset this pressure.
• We see limited scope for a sustained rise in the gold-silver ratio above 
70x and would use periods of price weakness to build exposure.
Although silver’s industrial characteristics often attract investor attention, 
recent price action has once again highlighted that the metal primarily 
trades as a higher-beta version of gold. The correlation between the two 
metals remains elevated, with silver influenced by many of the same factors 
as gold, including shifts in real interest rates, the US dollar, and geopolitical 
risk. In the near term, a more hawkish Federal Reserve and the possibility 
of further rate hikes pose headwinds. Over the longer term, however, 
we expect higher gold prices, alongside solid investment and industrial 
demand, to support silver.
Beyond investment demand, silver continues to benefit from several 
structural industrial trends. While solar demand faces pressure from 
high silver prices and efforts to substitute or reduce the metal’s use, 
consumption in other sectors remains robust. Expanding investment 
in artificial intelligence, data centers, digitalization, grid upgrades, and 
electric vehicles should continue to underpin industrial demand. Supply 
growth also remains constrained because most silver is produced as a 
by-product of lead, zinc, copper, and gold mining, limiting the industry’s 
ability to respond quickly to higher prices. At the same time, investor 
demand should benefit from persistent fiscal concerns, questions about the 
US dollar’s long-term purchasing power, and expectations that monetary 
policy will become less restrictive over time.
From an investment perspective, we continue to favor accumulating silver 
during price pullbacks rather than chasing short-term rallies. Key risks to 
our constructive outlook include a more hawkish monetary policy path, 
a significant deterioration in global growth, and a sharper-than-expected 
decline in industrial demand. Nevertheless, we believe the balance of risks 
remains tilted toward higher silver prices over the medium term.
