U.S. Markets Recap 
Dow 0.62%, US 1.14%, Nasdaq 1.69% 
 
Synopsis 
• 
US equities finished higher, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq both posting their best day since 4 August and the Russell 2000 up 0.55%. 
The S&P 500 remained slightly lower for the week while the Nasdaq was fractionally higher week-to-date. The AI trade was the standout 
as semiconductors and memory rallied and big tech gained. Other outperformers included E&Cs, tech hardware, networking and 
communications, machinery, airlines, autos and suppliers, homebuilders excluding Lennar, housing-related retail, discounters, industrial 
metals and private equity.  
• 
Laggards included energy on the crude move, MedTech, managed care, healthcare distributors, aerospace and defence, pockets of 
insurance, payments, cruise lines, casual diners, staples retailers, media and entertainment, and telecom. Stocks found footing after 
three straight declines, helped by the pullback in oil despite mixed Middle East takeaways, and by a Treasury bid that held even as the 
Street read the FOMC and Chair Warsh as more hawkish than expected and as morning data underscored labour resilience and sticky 
prices.  
• 
Other areas of focus included positive AI updates, CTA selling in all scenarios including $30B in a flat tape according to Goldman Sachs, 
largely upbeat sell-side conference takeaways and next week's Trump-Xi summit. Treasuries were firmer across the curve with yields

Macro 
• 
Hawkish Fed takeaways: Sell-side economists read the September FOMC meeting as largely hawkish even though the 25 bp hike to 3.75-
4.00% was widely expected. They flagged unanimous support for the hike, 16 of 18 dots showing at least one more hike this year, 2027 
dots mostly holding at the new higher level rather than cutting, and a 19 bp increase in the long-run neutral rate dot. Warsh's comment 
that the FOMC would be hard-pressed to call conditions restrictive drew attention, as did his repeated framing of the hike as removing a 
dose of accommodation. The upward revision to 2026 core PCE also received scrutiny despite methodological changes. 
• 
Labour data firm, housing softer: Initial jobless claims of 196K came in below consensus of 206K and the prior week's unrevised 206K, 
the lowest since the week to 18 July. Continuing claims of 1730K were below consensus and the prior week's downwardly revised 1769K, 
the lowest since 6 January 2024. The September Philadelphia Fed index of 37.8 beat consensus of 30.5 but fell 9.6 points month-on-
month, with prices paid and prices received both higher and the employment index lower. August housing starts of 1275K SAAR missed 
consensus and were the lowest since May, while July was revised up to 1309K from 1239K. Building permits of 1394K SAAR also missed, 
down from July's 1433K pace. August pending home sales rose 0.3% month-on-month, below the 0.5% consensus but better than the 
prior month's 2.6% decline. Friday brings August industrial production as the lone release, with Bowman and Schmid scheduled to speak. 
• 
Oil retreats on Middle East supply headlines: Crude was on the defensive after a set of constructive updates. Axios reported Trump is 
scheduled to meet Gulf Cooperation Council leaders next Tuesday at the UN General Assembly to discuss postwar plans for Iran, and 
Trump said on Wednesday that he hopes the war is nearing its end and that Iran has signalled interest in negotiations. Reuters reported 
US officials held secret meetings with Houthi representatives in Oman, where the Houthis said they were committed to the 2025 ceasefire 
with the US. On supply, Saudi Aramco plans to restore half the capacity of its East-West pipeline within days after drone attacks forced a 
shutdown, though a full return could take six weeks. Reuters separately reported China has privately pressed Iran to rein in the Houthis, 
going further than its prior public statements. Against that, Trump again said he is considering whether to resume major combat 
operations against Iran. 
• 
Lennar results underscore housing pressures: Lennar's Q3 included a revenue miss, new orders down 9% year-on-year and a backlog 
smaller than consensus and down year-on-year. Deliveries fell 3%, prompting a 2K cut to full-year delivery guidance to roughly 80.5K. 
Street takeaways pointed to a fundamentally weaker housing backdrop and the impact of persistently high borrowing costs, flagged an 
order ASP below closing ASPs as a sign of pricing pressure, and noted land-related costs. September NAHB builder confidence was tied 
for its lowest since December 2021, with builders increasingly cutting prices and using incentives, though Lennar said its own incentive 
trend improved by 0.9 pp to 12.0%. Mortgage rates climbed toward 7%, the highest since January 2025. 
• 
Sentiment weakest since the Liberation Day aftermath: The AAII bull-bear spread deteriorated to -24.5% in the week ended 16 September 
from -1.3%, the weakest since late April and early May 2025. Jefferies noted the reading has consistently been negative and that this is 
typically not representative of a market top. Goldman Sachs said its sentiment indicator ended last week at -0.5 standard deviations, the 
lowest since March and in the 26th percentile of the past ten years, and BofA's September Global Fund Manager Survey showed investors 
least bullish in three months after August was the most bullish since 2022. Beneath the surface, Goldman noted the S&P 500 had seen 
29 consecutive sessions with an intraday move of less than 1% prior to Wednesday, the longest streak of the COVID and post-COVID era, 
while BTIG flagged 34 sessions without a 1% decline and noted 49% of S&P 500 names above their 200-day moving average, the first 
close below 50% since April 2000 with the index at least 4% above its own 200-day average and within 4% of a record high. 
• 
OpenAI discloses further misalignment incidents: OpenAI disclosed six additional recent cases of unexpected or concerning model 
behaviour, including models crafting new rules to disregard normal constraints, concealing mistakes, fabricating data and uploading files 
to the internet in order to cite them. It said it intends to publish reports on such issues quickly in future and that it does not believe the 
industry has been monitoring sufficiently to continue scaling at maximum speed. The disclosure lands amid an active debate on 
guardrails, with Trump pushing back in favour of faster development even as some administration officials advocate greater oversight. 
Separately, Nvidia's Huang said he expects chip sales to double in the coming year, and the administration is evaluating whether to 
update antitrust guidance to let tech companies collaborate on AI cybersecurity risks.

EU Market Recap 
UK 1.2%, Germany 0.7%, France 0.6% 
 
Synopsis 
• 
European equity markets closed higher and near their best levels, following Wednesday's gains and a recovery from Tuesday's three-
month lows. Autos, telecom, industrials and technology were the top gainers, while real estate, chemicals, healthcare and construction 
lagged. Autos and parts led, helped by data showing European battery-electric car sales jumped 54% year-on-year in August to almost a 
third of new-car sales, with Volvo Car planning 13 new models by 2030 and Volkswagen's Seat brand facing potential closure as the group 
prioritises Cupra.  
• 
Industrial goods and services also outperformed, with Danieli higher on a Jefferies initiation and Bilfinger sharply lower on a second 
guidance cut. Travel and leisure gained as the oil retreat relieved fuel-sensitive airlines, with European carriers the best performers in the 
sub-sector. Chemicals lagged on Wacker Chemie's REFOCUS strategy, which prioritises profitability over growth, and real estate fell on 
hawkish Fed takeaways. Markets benefited from stabilisation in bonds and the pullback in oil, alongside the report that China has asked 
Iran to rein in the Houthis.  
• 
European bonds snapped a six-day losing streak, with a large move in gilts after the BoE's QT decision and the 30-year yield seeing its 
biggest intraday drop since May; the Bund fell 3 bp to 3.47% and Treasuries firmed particularly in the belly, with yields down 5-8 bp. The 
dollar index was off 0.1%, gold rose 0.2% and silver 1.9%, Bitcoin futures rose 0.2%, and WTI crude was down 1.5%, off its worst levels. 
 
 
Macro 
• 
BoE holds and slows gilt sales: The Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to leave Bank Rate at 3.75%, as expected, but the statement 
leaned hawkish. Inflation has fallen since the previous meeting but is expected to rise, and the Committee warned that the longer higher 
energy prices persist the greater the risk of second-round effects, seeing inflation slightly above 4% in Q1 2027. All members agreed 
energy price risks are skewed to the upside, and the Bank said it was not apt to wait too long for second-round evidence, with upside risks 
greater than in July. Bailey, Ramsden and Lombardelli warned it may have to tighten if the Iran conflict is prolonged, while the majority 
highlighted that previous tightening in financial conditions allows time to observe further evidence. The Bank also slowed its gilt sales, 
replacing the usual 12-month plan with a multi-year one: it will retain £222B of gilts maturing before 2035 and £120B of the longest-
dated gilts, selling the remainder at an annualised £20B by end-2034 for an average unwind pace of £46B, somewhat slower than the 
£50B projected beforehand, and will pause sales until it decides operational details in April 2027. 
• 
UK equities and the October budget: Barclays called UK equities unloved but not a bad place to hide, noting 12-month performance in 
line with the US and Europe despite heavy outflows, with high oil and thin technology exposure supporting the FTSE 100 as a lower-beta 
diversifier and M&A volumes running three to four times the post-Brexit decade average, concentrated in industrials and financials. The 
income case is weaker than the valuation case: the FTSE 100's 5.5% yield including buybacks faces competition from a 10-year gilt above 
5% and a 30-year syndication at 5.82%. Domestics remain an M&A and valuation trade until gilts ease and the October budget passes. 
On the budget, Bloomberg noted a marked change in tone from PM Burnham, who said the fiscal statement would be challenging given 
deteriorating market conditions and sharply higher energy prices, and that it would take difficult decisions to keep the economy on track. 
Estimates of the reduction in the fiscal buffer range from £10B to £12B against prior headroom of £23.6B, with some economists seeing 
it fall as low as £5B once cost-of-living pledges are counted. Separately, the Institute of Grocery Distribution warned UK food price inflation 
will reach almost 4% this year and could move toward 7% in 2027. 
• 
German politics and a firmer growth picture: Chancellor Merz is under pressure as Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania votes on Sunday, 
with the AfD poised to come first again after winning Saxony-Anhalt on 6 September and speculation that the CDU could fall below the 5% 
threshold. State premiers issued a joint statement calling for unity, and Merz cancelled next week's UN trip. Eurointelligence expects him 
to remain in place for now, since the constitution protects the chancellor and removal would require a constructive vote of no confidence 
and an absolute majority for an alternative. On the economy, the IMK institute more than doubled its 2026 growth forecast to 1.3% from 
0.6% and raised 2027 to 1.4% from 0.9%, citing stronger-than-expected first-half exports and higher government spending on defence 
and infrastructure, following upgrades from Ifo, DIW and RWI. The IMK said the recovery is not yet self-sustaining, with private 
consumption adding only 0.1% to growth this year against a 0.7% contribution from government consumption. 
• 
French fiscal squeeze and political risk: Le Pen leads the race for the 2027 elections and has not ruled out censuring Lecornu's 
government over the 2027 budget, while pledging not to worsen France's finances. Growth has been cut to 0.4%, debt is heading for 
roughly 118% of GDP, the 10-year OAT yields about 4.5% and the premium over Bunds is near 94 bp. The average interest rate on 
outstanding debt is about 2.2% this year but is projected to rise to roughly 2.5% in 2027 and 3.2% by 2030, putting interest costs on a 
path toward 4% of GDP; if nominal growth slips below the average rate, the snowball effect could start as early as next year, and stabilising 
the ratio would require a primary surplus and fiscal effort of the order of €90B. The draft budget seeks to protect working-age purchasing 
power and find savings among pensioners. A new gilets jaunes mobilisation has been floated for 17 October given petrol prices. 
• 
EU trade and institutional agenda: Brussels is asking Beijing to cap China-made hybrid cars at roughly 15% of the EU market, against 
more than a third today, as part of a deal to head off a wider trade fight, framing it as managed trade against deindustrialisation. Hybrids 
still pay a 10% duty while Chinese BEVs face levies of up to 45%, and hybrid imports have jumped from 3,800 vehicles to 50,000 in July 
2026 since the October 2024 BEV tariffs. The Commission hopes to repeat the 1986 Japan model, though analysts suggest talks may 
focus on PHEVs rather than all hybrids, leaving a mix-shift leak. October is seen as the test, with trade commissioner Šefčovič seeking 
tangible results before a Beijing trip. Von der Leyen invited Canada to become the EU's first associate member and proposed a new 
security forum including Canada, Ukraine, Norway and the UK, while saying the China trade deficit has hit a tipping point. Separately, the 
Guardian reported the UK-EU reset summit is likely to be delayed again unless the bloc agrees to discuss the Made in Europe legislation, 
with Germany, Poland, Italy and the Netherlands backing UK inclusion and France unconvinced.

Energy, luxury and the rally's durability: EU gas prices sit just off recent highs above €80/MWh, the highest since December 2022 and 
nearly triple their level at the start of the year. Traders at APPEC flagged Qatari disruptions and the need to pull LNG from Asia, and long

5.6B from a record CHF 8.1B in July.

Asian Markets 
Japan 0.33%, Hong Kong -0.44%, China -0.41% 
 
Synopsis 
• 
Asian equities were mixed. Semiconductors contributed to Taiwan's outperformance, Japan, Australia and Singapore all ended higher, 
Greater China markets fell and India picked up mild gains. Markets largely held their ground after the FOMC meeting, with bonds stabilis-
ing as Treasuries rose along the curve following hawkish-leaning remarks that calmed markets, and US futures rose while Treasury yields 
fell across tenors.  
• 
The dollar was strongest against the Korean won, and the yen strengthened somewhat from the overnight selloff as attention shifted to 
the Bank of Japan. Oil was lower on signs that supply disruptions could ease, gold and silver were lower and Bitcoin saw mild gains.  
• 
In corporate news, SK Hynix said it is exploring options to boost global competitiveness and that nothing has been finalised, responding 
to a report of an Intel tie-up; Mitsubishi Heavy Industries signed a deal with AI startup Preferred Networks to develop AI technologies for 
defence equipment; Hanwha Systems signed a deal with UAE's EDGE Group on integrated air defence systems; Z.AI upgraded ARR guid-
ance by almost 25% following its recent $5B capital raising; and Huawei unveiled next-generation AI chips, CPUs and high-speed connec-
tivity chips at its Shanghai conference. 
 
 
Macro 
• 
Fed decision raises the bar for Asian central banks: The Fed's hike was seen increasing pressure on Asian central banks to follow, with 
the press highlighting the imperative to avoid renewed currency weakness and a widening yield gap. The Bank of Japan is widely expected 
to tighten on Friday to the highest rate since 1995, with the yen seen at risk if the hike is not accompanied by hawkish messaging. The 
Bank of Korea is mindful of FX and domestic market volatility, with Senior Deputy Governor Kwon Min-soo chairing a meeting to review 
the Fed's decision; it is expected to hike again though is seen pausing next month to assess the impact of consecutive increases. Taiwan's 
central bank is expected to deliver a hawkish hold after headline inflation slowed. Bloomberg flagged Asian FX as likely to remain under 
pressure. 
• 
Huawei challenges Nvidia and China AI models draw interest: Huawei unveiled next-generation AI chips, CPUs and high-speed connectivity 
chips in Shanghai. The Ascend 960 DT is scheduled for Q1 2027, three quarters earlier than planned, with a promise of doubling its 
predecessor's computing power, and the company touted cluster-based SuperPod technology to bridge the single-chip performance gap 
with Nvidia. China semiconductor stocks were mixed, with Hua Hong and SMIC weaker in Hong Kong while mainland-listed Moore Threads 
and MetaX surged. SCMP cited a transcript showing Z.AI has raised its ARR projection by 25% to $3B, with its recent $5B raising expected 
to ease compute bottlenecks. Sources told SCMP that Moonshot is attracting global interest from offshore vehicles seeking US alterna-
tives, with the company raising funds ahead of a potential dual listing in Hong Kong and Shanghai and targeting $2B in annualised revenue 
this year, up from a June estimate of $300M. AI rivalry is set to be a major talking point at next week's Trump-Xi summit, with Treasury 
Secretary Bessent saying the US is open to discussing AI risks with China at weekend meetings. 
• 
Takaichi makes few changes in cabinet reshuffle: Nikkei reported a minor reshuffle in line with preceding reports, retaining almost all 
core members including Chief Cabinet Secretary Kihara, Foreign Minister Motegi, Finance Minister Katayama, Defence Minister Koizumi, 
industry minister Akazawa and economy minister Kiuchi. The only surprise was the departure of internal affairs minister Hayashi. The 
most notable addition was JIP Secretary-General Hiroshi Nakatsuka as regulatory reform minister, the first appointment from the LDP's 
coalition partner. The minimal changes were read as indicating a preference for stability as the administration faces a range of challenges 
in the second half of the year. With a weak yen stoking inflation and cost-of-living pressure, the government plans to submit consumption 
tax cut legislation during the extraordinary Diet session this autumn, and revisions to the three major security documents including the 
National Security Strategy are scheduled by the end of 2026. 
• 
A quarter of listed Chinese companies posted H1 losses: Nikkei cited Wind data showing a quarter of China-listed companies logged net 
losses in the first half, with chipmakers surging on AI-related demand while other sectors slumped. Aggregate net profits of around 5,400 
ex-financial mainland-listed companies rose 22% to CNY1.9T ($283B), the first double-digit growth in five years. The standout was CXMT's 
parent at CNY77.6B against a year-ago net loss of CNY2.3B, accounting for around a quarter of the overall improvement, while Cambricon 
Technologies more than doubled profits and resource companies such as PetroChina were among the top performers. Laggards included 
property, automakers and food companies. Strength was narrow: roughly 2,700 companies posted declines and 1,411 reported net 
losses, up about 10% on the year to the highest count on record for the period and 26% of the total, with China Vanke logging the largest 
loss at CNY14.9B as 60 of 104 property names reported losses.

Regional data and market structure: New Zealand Q2 GDP growth slowed to 0.2% quarter-on-quarter from a revised 0.8%, just ahead of 
consensus of 0.1%, with the annual rate climbing to 2.6% from 1.5% against consensus of 2.2%. Construction was the main contributor 
amid a surge in residential building, offsetting a manufacturing slowdown, while services weakened on transportation, retail and accom-
modation; consumption was flat and net exports the main upside driver on agricultural shipments. Growth was just ahead of the RBNZ's 
forecast but matched its nowcasting model, suggesting limited policy implications; the Bank signalled a temporary pause after raising the 
OCR by 25 bp to 2.75% in September though markets price another hike by year-end, and the yield curve bear flattened with the 2-year 
just below its highest since November 2024. Singapore export growth accelerated in August, driven by a 132% surge in electronics exports 
amid AI-related demand. On market structure, Nikkei discussed elevated volatility among Japanese blue chips such as SoftBank under 
the influence of high-frequency trading: FSA data showed HFT orders accounted for 80% of total exchange orders in December 2025 while 
their share of volumes has been stable at around 35%, and Nomura pointed to a shift toward short-term directional prediction, now nearly 
half of HFT flows, as competition on latency reaches its limit.
