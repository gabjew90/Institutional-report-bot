Our view in summary
We attended a large energy conference in New York last 
week and came away with higher confidence in many of the 
themes supporting Power and resources. Our discussions 
with energy and power companies at the conference 
left us with increased confidence in AI power demand 
and associated power contracts growing through year-
end. Further, we found a firm belief in “all-of-the-above” 
solutions among companies and investors, including all 
generation technologies. Behind the meter solutions are 
gaining an edge, as speed-to-power remains critical for data 
center developers and regulatory and political constraints 
are growing.

Weekly performance update
In the week ending 4 September, the "Power and resources"

Thoughts of the week
A large Wall Street energy conference is held annually 
in New York City in September, immediately after the 
US Labor Day holiday. The conference is often a useful 
barometer of management sentiment, investor priorities, 
and emerging industry themes for the coming year. 
This year’s conference was particularly notable because 
discussions focused on how the industry could meet a 
potentially significant increase in power demand and 
infrastructure investment needs from artificial intelligence 
(AI) data centers.
We identified several recurring themes across 
presentations by energy, power, utility, energy 
infrastructure, oil and gas, and energy services companies.
Confidence in AI-driven power demand continues to 
rise
We noted in several presentations and one-on-one 
conversations that confidence in long-term electricity 
demand growth driven by AI and data center development 
continues to strengthen. In our view, discussions of this 
demand have moved beyond whether AI will create 
incremental demand—which we expect it will—and 
shifted toward questions of where the required power 
will come from and how quickly it can be delivered. 
Utilities continue to report significant interest from 
hyperscalers, while infrastructure developers, power 
providers, and energy companies increasingly view AI 
as a durable, multiyear demand catalyst rather than a 
cyclical phenomenon. Several companies expect additional 
power contracts with hyperscalers and major data center 
developers to be announced by year-end and into 2027. 
Investors appear focused on these announcements, but 
execution and power delivery will ultimately be the key 
factors.
Behind-the-meter solutions have an edge
Another consistent theme was growing enthusiasm for 
behind-the-meter (BTM) power solutions. BTM generally 
refers to temporary or permanent power generation at a 
customer site that either precedes or eliminates the need 
for a utility-grid interconnection. While few participants 
argued that BTM generation alone could satisfy long-term 
data center demand, many viewed it as an increasingly 
attractive tool to accelerate project timelines and alleviate 
near-term grid and regulatory constraints. BTM’s ability to 
improve speed to power while meeting state regulatory 
demands for new generation—sometimes described as 
“bring your own generation” (BYOG)—has made the 
approach appealing to investors.
Few participants viewed BTM as a replacement for utility-
grid expansion because most data centers want to 
connect to the grid for greater service reliability. However, 
BTM is increasingly viewed as a practical solution at the 
margin, particularly where transmission development 
and interconnection timelines remain lengthy. Here too, 
execution and power delivery will be critical, particularly 
because some BTM providers are smaller companies 
with less power-sector experience than utilities and 
independent power producers (IPPs).
‘All of the above’ is a necessity
Perhaps the strongest consensus across the conference 
was that growing power demand requires additional 
investment across a wide range of generation technologies
—a theme we have long embraced: “all of the above.” 
Whether discussing natural gas, renewables, nuclear 
power, storage, distributed generation, or transmission 
infrastructure, management teams generally emphasized 
that multiple technologies will be required simultaneously 
to support reliability, affordability, and economic growth. 
In our view, this speaks to the duration of the opportunity 
and management teams’ confidence in developing AI-
related power demand. The utility and power industry 
appears increasingly focused on expanding the system 
as a whole through a diverse set of generation and grid 
technologies. The practical investment implication is that 
power demand growth is likely to support a broader 
opportunity set than some investors may assume.
Company highlights
We attended several presentations and one-on-one 
meetings with companies. Overall, most meetings were 
upbeat, with management teams expecting improving 
business conditions in the energy and power sectors over 
the next six to 12 months. A few notable discussions are 
summarized below.
Constellation Energy (CEG)
Constellation Energy management was upbeat at the 
conference and highlighted expectations for additional 
power contracts with AI data center developers and 
hyperscalers over the next several months. Constellation 
is a major US nuclear power plant operator and also owns 
a large natural gas-fired generation fleet. Management 
said the company expects to participate in both bilateral

that power agreements remain complex and time-

restart of the Crane Clean Energy Center in Pennsylvania

directly exposed to large-load growth and emerging 
power opportunities associated with AI and data center

NextEra Energy (NEE)
NextEra Energy continues to advance its proposed 
merger with Dominion Energy, its data center power 
solutions business through NextEra Energy Resources, and 
opportunities to site new large data centers in Florida. 
At the conference, the company said that in August it 
re-contracted the Point Beach nuclear plant and entered 
into a firm contractual agreement with the Department 
of Commerce to develop power plants in Ohio and Texas. 
The company remains optimistic about the prospects for 
approval of its proposed merger with Dominion. NextEra’s 
long-standing exposure to renewable energy development 
and large-scale infrastructure investment remains aligned 
with investors’ interest in the “all-of-the-above” theme.
Xcel Energy (XEL)
We had a constructive meeting with Xcel Energy. The 
company highlighted its recent settlement of its Colorado 
electric rate case and said it still expects to update its 
capital spending plans with 3Q26 results. We expect this 
update to increase projected capital spending for data 
center power development and support the potential for 
higher earnings growth through the end of the decade 
and beyond. The company also highlighted its ongoing 
efforts to reduce wildfire risk across its operating footprint. 
As with many regulated utilities, investors remain focused 
on balancing large-scale investment opportunities with 
affordability and regulatory support. In our view, Xcel 
remains well positioned to manage this balance.

thematic benchmark. The chosen benchmark is disclosed on the front page of each EPL. It is also used to measure the 
performance of the EPL owner(s). Including a stock in the EPL constitutes neither a view on its expected, standalone absolute 
performance nor a price target. Rather, EPLs are meant to support the UBS House View, with the stocks included in them 
selected for their superior risk/return profiles.
Our selection is based on an assessment of the company's fundamental outlook and valuation, the risks owning the stock 
entails, including material sustainability risks and the diversification benefits it provides in an investment portfolio, among 
many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific 
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.

within an EPL. Stocks can be selected for multiple EPLs. For consistency's sake, a stock can only be selected as either Most 
Preferred or Least Preferred, not both simultaneously. As EPL benchmarks differ, stocks do not need to be included on every 
list to which they could theoretically be added.

of the underlying asset. Investors could also lose their entire investment if the issuer becomes insolvent. UBS does not 
guarantee in any way the obligations or the financial condition of any issuer or the accuracy of any financial information 
provided by any issuer. Structured investments are not traditional investments and investing in a structured investment is not 
equivalent to investing directly in the underlying asset. Structured investments may have limited or no liquidity, and investors 
should be prepared to hold their investment to maturity. The return of structured investments may be limited by a maximum 
gain, participation rate or other feature. Structured investments may include call features and, if a structured investment is 
called early, investors would not earn any further return and may not be able to reinvest in similar investments with similar
