Follow The Flow 
The streak is over 
     
   
 
 
First fixed income outflows in 24 weeks 
Fixed income funds recorded their first outflow after 23 consecutive weeks of inflows. 
Sentiment in credit markets is facing increasing headwinds, with both IG and HY funds 
experiencing outflows over the past week for the second consecutive week. Significant 
moves in rates markets, elevated rates volatility, and stretched valuations across risk 
assets are prompting investors to reduce risk exposure.  
As "risk-free" yields continue to rise, the incentive to take credit risk to generate 
additional yield diminishes. We expect pressure on credit markets to persist until rates 
volatility subsides and government bond yields stabilise. Rates vol above the 75-80pts 
level has been associated with outflows over the past two years. 
Exhibit 1: Elevated uncertainty in rates is having a notable impact on flows into credit funds 
The higher the level of rates vol, the weaker the flow into credit funds 
Source: EPFR Global.  
BofA GLOBAL RESEARCH 
Over the past week… 
Overall, high-grade funds extended their outflows streak to two weeks. Across 
maturities, short-term IG funds have recorded inflows over the previous week. However 
mid- and long-term funds have suffered from outflows. 
High yield funds suffered from a second consecutive outflow this week. For European-
domiciled funds, all sub-categories suffered from outflows last week, led by Euro-
focussed HY funds. Similarly, HY ETFs saw outflows over the past week marking two 
consecutive weeks of outflows. 
Government bond funds logged a second consecutive week of inflows. Money 
market funds flipped back into outflows, almost fully reversing last week's inflow. 
Global EM debt funds remained on track, as inflows continued for a seventh 
consecutive week.  
Overall, flows for fixed income funds reversed course last week, turning to an outflow. 
Equity funds have also returned to outflows territory, while equity ETFs posted inflows 
for a seventh straight week.
