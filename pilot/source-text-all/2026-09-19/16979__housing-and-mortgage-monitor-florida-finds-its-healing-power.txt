Florida finds its healing power
Base effects from a weak 2H’2025 could provide a 
technical boost to year-over-year HPA in the coming 
months 
Home price appreciation (HPA) generally follows a consistent 
seasonal pattern throughout the year. In most years, 
cumulative HPA accelerates sharply during the spring and 
early summer months, then begins to moderate and largely 
levels off as the market moves into the fall and winter. 
However, during the second half of 2025, the housing 
market experienced steeper-than-normal seasonal price 
declines (Exhibit 1).  This unseasonal softness was driven by 
a combination of a rising unemployment backdrop at the 
time and weak consumer confidence. If HPA follows more 
normal calendar patterns this year and due to the 
comparison period (2H’2025) representing a depressed 
baseline, YoY HPA figures in the latter half of 2026 could 
optically appear stronger, even if actual month-over-month 
price growth is flat or minimal, potentially reaching over 
2.5% by year-end 2026.

Availability of new housing units should moderate further in the coming years  
The number of housing units under construction as well as new building permits have declined in recent years across most of 
the country, including the South (Exhibit 5). Lower forward-looking supply should boost future HPA, all else equal. In addition 
to the notable improvement in single-family inventory and supply of housing units, industry forecasts point to a steady 
improvement in multifamily inventory as well in the coming years as the pace of completions falls below the pace of 
absorptions (Exhibit 6). According to projections from CoStar, multifamily vacancy rates in Florida are expected to decline 
from around 10.3% currently to just 8% by the end of the decade and nearly converge with the national average vacancy rate 
(Exhibit 7). Absorption is aided by continued population inflow into Florida, although the pace of inflow has moderated in 
2025 (Exhibit 8). All these factors point towards continued healing in Florida.

Borrower performance remains robust despite modest home price declines 
Despite home prices trending modestly lower, borrower on-time payment performance has remained resilient across various 
loan types—Conventional (i.e., Fannie and Freddie) loans, loans that are made to high credit quality borrowers but are 
underwritten using non-standard income/employment documentation (i.e., non-QM loans), or loans to weaker credit quality 
borrowers (such as FHA loans, Exhibit 9). This is largely a function of the large amounts of remaining home equity, following 
several years of above-average HPA, and a relatively healthy economy. New mortgages also have sufficient equity cushion in 
them, with the average loan amount to home value (LTV) ratio for Florida mortgages originated in 2025 at 83%. As of Q1 
2026, only 1.9% of all mortgaged properties nationwide and just 1.2% of properties in the Miami metro area were 
underwater. While market participants are keenly monitoring delinquency rates in Florida and Texas, the continued benign 
delinquency rates have provided sufficient comfort to investors to buy non-investment grade tranches of recently issued 
non-QM deals despite the tranches trading near their tightest levels since the COVID housing boom.

Potential property tax reduction in Florida can boost home prices 
In November, voters in Florida are set to vote on a Constitutional amendment that proposes to lower property taxes for 
homeowners (primary residence) by exempting a larger portion of their assessment value from certain taxes, among other 
changes. Specifically, if approved, the amendment would increase the dollar amount of the home’s tax assessment value that 
is exempt from non-school property taxes from $25k currently to $150k in 2027 and $250k in 2028. The tax exemption 
would primarily apply to current resident homeowners. New Florida residents would retain the existing lower exemption until 
they meet a 5-year minimum residency requirement. Voters will decide on this Constitutional amendment during the 
November 3, 2026 elections. The amendment requires approval by at least 60% of voters. The probability of the proposed 
amendment passing has climbed to just over 50% on Polymarket recently. 
Several studies have found a negative relationship between property tax rates and housing prices, via what is known as the 
standard capitalization theory. This theory states that when the burden of owning a home declines (such as through lower 
property taxes), home buyers would be willing to pay the net present value of the decline in the housing burden and 
therefore should boost home prices immediately. Alternatively, one could also view this via the affordability channel—where 
improved housing affordability due to lower property taxes should marginally increase homeownership demand and hence 
home prices. Several studies (here and here) have found a direct relationship between lower taxes and higher home prices. As 
a result, based on the outcome of the November election, we estimate that HPA in Florida may receive a small positive boost. 
The positive impact would be greater for lower valued homes. The magnitude of the boost is somewhat hard to estimate, as 
tax assessment values are generally below current market value of the home but could be adjusted up to market value for the 
new buyer. 
Revising mortgage rate and origination volume forecasts 
Following forecast revisions by our economics and rates team, we now revise our 2026 and 2027 mortgage rate and 
origination volume estimates. Our interest rates strategists expect 10-year Treasury yields to decline to 4.75% and 4.40% by 
end-2026 and end-2027, respectively. Consistent with this, we now believe 30-year mortgage rates should reach 6.75% and 
6.40% during the same periods. We expect total mortgage origination volumes of $2.08 trillion and $2.05 trillion in 2026 and 
2027, respectively, up from a revised figure of $1.95 trillion in 2025 (Exhibit 10).
