Energy, Utilities & Mining Pulse: Investors Asking: What Are Takeaways 
from the Road or Recent Management Meetings?
17 September 2026 | 8:44PM EDT
The Natural Resources complex has performed well in 2026 through the continued 
geopolitical and commodity volatility. With this in mind, our analysts have spent 
much of September on the road attending industry conferences and facilitating 
meetings with our management teams. This week in the Pulse, we ask our senior 
analyst team to comment on the key debates and takeaways from their 
conversations while on the road attending conferences or in recent management 
meetings.  
Topics and stocks discussed include:  
Integrated Oil: CVX

Midstream: KGS

Integrated Oil: 
This week, we hosted Chevron Corp (CVX) for a roundtable dinner in Houston on 
9/14, followed by a full-day event on 9/15 featuring a Q&A with executives and a 
technology showcase. Key debates on the company include: (a) whether 
international growth projects justify a geopolitical risk discount, (b) whether 
management intends to scale the Bakken, (c) whether Project Kilby represents a new 
growth platform for the company, and (d) whether contract renegotiations in 
Kazakhstan can resolve favorably. Management continues to stack high-margin 
international growth options, with opportunities for incremental free cash flow 
coming from Venezuela, Iraq, and Argentina, in addition to countries in West Africa 
and the Eastern Mediterranean. We expect gross production in Venezuela to more 
than double to ~600 kbd by 2031 across three joint ventures, while Argentina output 
more than triples to ~240 kbd by 2035. In Iraq, we look for updates on commercial 
discussions over West Qurna 2 and Nasiriyah. While some investors argue

Energy Services:  
On the back of our meetings with LBRT and PUMP management on 9/10, we continue to 
see key debates for the Behind-the-Meter theme focus on contract announcements, 
financing, ability to secure equipment, and associated costs for equipment and balance 
of plant (BoP). During the meetings, management discussed positive momentum around 
contract negotiations, particularly as customers continue to focus on time to power. 
LBRT and PUMP see the contract negotiations in the later stages as hyperscaler 
customers choose which data center sites to develop and make progress around 
additional items, such as water, mineral, and land management, as well as other factors. 
Debate has additionally been around the varying capital spend guides from the BTM 
players. We believe the higher capex guides are largely a result of increased project 
scope and inclusion of ancillary services. As such, while it will require higher capital 
spending, we expect the returns to be at par, if not larger, than original expectations. 
Both LBRT and PUMP continue to see high-teens returns over the 4-6 year pay back 
period as previously guided, with the potential for continued favorable economics given 
the tightness in power supply. While we remain Neutral on LBRT and PUMP, we see 
potential catalysts from power contract announcements, particularly around LBRT’s 500 
MW ESA guide by year-end 2026. In addition, we believe the recent pullback in the 
equities has created a more favorable risk/reward and we continue to monitor for 
incremental updates which can provide a clearer line-of-sight to project economics and 
associated long-term capital spend.

E&P:  
Across our shale oil E&P coverage, we have seen our diversified oil producers shift 
incremental capital toward the optimization of their existing unconventional shale asset 
portfolio relative to capital-intensive exploration programs in pursuit of new resource 
potential and operating regions. In contrast, MUR is in the midst of an exploration cycle 
as the company is investing capital behind two key exploration and development 
opportunities in Vietnam and Cote d’Ivoire. Last week, we attended a management 
dinner with MUR where exploration was a key area of focus. MUR has underperformed 
our oil E&P coverage year-to-date (MUR +22% vs Oil E&Ps +49%), partially driven by the 
mixed results the company has seen across its exploration program in Cote d’Ivoire and 
its appraisal program in Vietnam. Given the constructive 2026E/2027E free cash flow 
revisions seen across the oil E&P sub-sector year-to-date through an elevated oil pricing 
environment, we have seen market sentiment focus on upstream producers’ outlook for 
cash flow generation and capital allocation and both have been relatively more 
constrained for MUR through its current exploration cycle. We see a number of potential 
catalysts and risks for MUR, including in Vietnam, where MUR expects to achieve first oil 
in 4Q26 at the Lac Da Vang prospect, and in Cote d’Ivoire, where MUR is conducting a 
3-5 well appraisal program in 2H26/2027 for the Bubale prospect. While the Bubale 
appraisal program presents the potential for capital spending to exceed current 
estimates in 2027, any upward revision to the total resource potential (MUR saw a 
predrill range of 340-850 MMboe) would serve as a positive catalyst for MUR. 
Utilities:

highlight key debates around the regulatory and policy backdrop ahead of the

These debates have been reflected in stock performance where our regulated coverage

cancellations. We see states like PA and TX that have implemented executive orders 
around data center development as more focused on ensuring all the boxes are checked 
in terms of addressing local communities’ concerns. As for stock implications, we screen

Clean Tech: 
The outlook for large nuclear reactor deployment in the US is seeing renewed optimism 
following recent reports that the US and South Korea are working to final a $100 
billion-plus energy investment framework aimed at potentially constructing up to eight 
new large reactors. Our discussions at the World Nuclear Symposium suggest that 
although EPC firms are showing a greater willingness to provide 25%-40% of their scope 
of work at a fixed price, full construction wraps and robust cost-mitigation insurance 
products are still generally difficult to secure. Additionally, commercial traction is 
building for Generation III reactors (such as GE-Hitachi’s BWRX-300 and Rolls-Royce’s 
SMR) alongside constructive, albeit early, progress on select Gen-IV advanced reactors. 
While the timing for these new large reactors to reach Final Investment Decisions (FIDs) 
remains uncertain, this incremental momentum is highly positive for nuclear equipment 
providers, as well as upstream uranium developers like UEC (UEC, Buy-rated). 
Furthermore, we believe there are supply-side pressures developing, particularly 
regarding potential production cuts from leading global producer Kazatomprom given 
recent reports. This could reinforce a structural deficit in the uranium market and 
potentially provide a firm price floor that supports our bullish outlook for UEC. 
Metals & Mining: 
On the back of our trip to Atlanta, we met with management from WS (not covered) 
along with a number of other value chain participants. Key takeaways were that current 
demand remains strong across most end markets backed by strong orders books, 
backlogs into 2027, strong reshoring manufacturing activity and power demand. Data 
centers and border wall construction continue to drive incremental steel consumption 
balancing out weaker consumer related demand like resi-construction, appliance, etc. 
While domestic mills noted that despite flat automotive production, procurement has 
been shifting over to US from imports. This positive sentiment is reinforced by our 
monthly supply/demand tracker which was published yesterday, indicating we are 
tracking at 0.9% growth this year on a seasonally adjusted basis, and we believe growth 
ends up coming in around the ~2.5% mark for full year 2026. As for supply, customers at 
the conference all have been citing difficulty in getting product. Near term price is 
expected to increase from limited spot availability in an increasingly thin supply market. 
As for the longer term, we expect mills to offer less fixed contracts and reduce discounts 
offered so higher prices into 2027 could be a theme as well. We expect this to continue 
to benefit flat roll producers as 1) NUE continues to ramp volumes for its new mill 
starting in 2027, growing flat rolled exposure to 54% in 2027 vs. 49% in 2025; 2) STLD 
given its exposure to HRC and value-added steel; 3) CLF given the company’s exposure 
to flat rolled steel as it looks to renegotiate contracts in 1Q27.

Conversations we are having with investors 
 
 
E&P - Neil Mehta 
FANG. FANG has been a stock in focus this week following the news of 9.1mn shares, or 
~$1.9 bn, sold by SGF FANG Holdings, LP. Those more cautious on FANG note the 
company’s GOR trajectory with the expectation that the increased activity in the 
Barnett would gradually increase the company’s gas cut over time. However, investors 
have highlighted the potential for incremental value from Permian gas given the start-up 
of new Permian takeaway capacity, as Waha has begun to meaningfully tighten in 3Q26 
following the depressed pricing throughout 2Q26. Those more constructive on the stock 
highlight FANG’s strength in well productivity, noting the whitespace remaining with the 
ongoing surfactant program as a key driver following the positive execution of a 12-well 
test in 2Q26. We see FANG trading at a discount relative to peers based on our 
2027/2028 FCF yield estimates at 11%, versus large cap E&P peers at 9% (peers include 
COP, EOG, OXY, and DVN). 
Gas E&Ps. Inbound conversations on gassy E&Ps have increased as investors weigh the 
setup for the sub-sector given the discounted valuations and overall weakness in the US 
gas commodity. Those more cautious on the sub-sector highlight concerns around El 
Niño and the robust outlook for incremental Permian associated gas supply expected to 
leave the basin as new takeaway capacity comes online. Investors highlight the 
sub-sectors’ underperformance at -9% YTD versus the XLE at +43%, and gauge the 
potential for mean-reversion ahead of winter. Those more constructive on the gassy 
names highlight the upside potential to long-term demand as more data center 
developments gather momentum toward reaching a FID. Investors have highlighted AR 
as it currently trades at a discount with a 12% FCF yield on 2027/2028 estimates relative 
to Appalachian peer average at +9% (peers include EXE, EQT, and RRC).

Canadian Oils. Investors continue to discuss the outlook for Canadian Oils, with most 
inbounds focusing on CVE and CNQ. Sentiment remains broadly constructive on CVE as 
the company approaches first oil at West White Rose and benefits from incremental 
production growth at Christina Lake. Investors also highlight strong momentum on debt 
reduction and shareholder returns at current commodity prices, with the company 
roughly C$1.4 bn away from a long-term net debt target of C$4.0 bn, at which point 
management expects to return 100% of excess free funds flow to shareholders. 
Pushback centers around relative outperformance and greater exposure to light-heavy 
differentials. Investors see similar progress on debt reduction at CNQ, where 
management expects the company to hit C$13 bn of net debt around early 2027, 
unlocking 100% of free cash flow to shareholders. They also highlight exposure to strong 
SCO premiums, but monitor upcoming turnaround at Horizon and expectations for 
long-term capital spend. Separately, investors look for additional color on tax savings 
across the complex from the recent Productivity Mega Deduction.  
Refining. This week within the refining complex, we have received incremental investor 
inbounds around the potential risks and implications of a US product export ban on the 
Gulf Coast. Given the sharp rally in refined product prices YTD, dialogue evaluating a 
potential export ban note that keeping excess supply domestically could combat rising 
prices and soften inflation. In this scenario, investors have discussed diversified names 
such as Phillips 66 (PSX) and HF Sinclair (DINO) given each company’s non-refining 
earnings contributions such as midstream, marketing, chemicals, and lubricants to offset 
potential softer refining margins. On the other hand, we note other investors who view 
this scenario as unlikely given that in past cases, export bans resulted in higher refined 
product prices due to reduced utilization and the potential negative economic 
implications for countries in Latin America and Europe. Under this view, discussions 
continue to reiterate Valero Energy (VLO), Marathon Petroleum (MPC), and Par Pacific 
Holdings (PARR) as relative preferences given the higher beta to elevated refining 
margins.

Energy Services - Neil Mehta & Alexa Petrick Breno 
BKR. We continue to have conversations with investors on BKR after the company 
provided guidance for GTLS after the close of the acquisition. Investors continue to be 
focused on the revised backlog for GTLS BKR provided, and what future project awards 
could be, particularly within the LNG and data center end-markets. BKR guided to 
22-23% segment EBITDA margins for GTLS by 2028, an expansion from guided 17.5%

margin uplift for the business, and how BKR will integrate some of GTLS’ end-markets 
and business lines with the existing IET business over the next several years to realize 
customer and operational synergies. Separate from GTLS guide, investors we speak to 
are also focused on the outlook for turbine orders for BKR’s IET segment, and how 
increasing turbine manufacturing capacity will affect the company’s long-term IET 
earnings and orders. 
Pressure Pumping Pricing. Conversations with investors this week focused on the 
magnitude for increases in pressure pumping pricing amid continued higher commodity 
prices and supply disruption in the Middle East. US exposed pressure pumping 
companies such as LBRT and PTEN have messaged the ability to increase pricing amid a 
higher commodity environment over this past earnings season. With the supply 
disruption in the Middle East continuing longer than originally anticipated, investors are 
looking to size the potential magnitude of pricing increases for fracking in the US. As 
pressure pumping fleets typically run on shorter contracts (~6 months) compared to rigs 
(~12 months) in the US, fracking companies have the ability to raise prices for fleets 
more frequently than traditional US drilling companies. With the prolonged conflict 
preventing the Middle East from ramping activity and production back to pre-conflict 
levels, US pressure pumpers have experienced higher activity levels as operators look to 
complete previous DUCs (Drilled But Uncompleted Wells) amid higher commodity prices.

California. The utilities in California remain a key topic of our investor discussions this 
week. Investors remain focused on monitoring headlines around the potential for a 
special legislative session focused on wildfire reform to be called. Both the potential for

reform around wildfire liability in California. That said, those that are more optimistic,

magnitude of the sell off in the past month, and investors query if there are other near

the $2 bn reduction in the 2027 capital plan that has already been announced. There 
have also been questions on Sempra, largely around how the California exposure will 
impact overall sentiment given the generally more positive developments in recent 
weeks in Texas around 765-kV transmission lines and conditional Batch Zero 
classifications that showed higher proposed base load than many anticipated.  
IPPs. With the news from Anthropic this week on slowing AI’s progression, the IPPs were 
down this week. We continue to see steady incomings on the IPPs, but it is less clear that 
investors are looking to own the equities in the immediate term. There remains heavy 
trepidation on policy heading into November, but investors are watching progression on

headlines in PA, where investors remain focused on how the governor’s focus areas will

rate case activity. Conversations have started to shift slightly, with more of a focus on 
power markets and energy prices, rather than just when the next power company will

believe there is potential for positive earnings revisions for several of the IPPs in the

center PPAs signed. Interest on Talen and Vistra has been the strongest this week, largely

has garnered interest in investors focused on sustainability and lower coal exposure, and 
lower for NRG as well as investors wait for more details on the 1.2 GW potential data 
center project, though there was encouragement that the project advanced as studied 
load through batch zero.

MLPs/Pipelines - John Mackay & Olivia Foster 
PRB consolidation following PAA’s Silver Creek acquisition. This week, PAA 
announced an agreement to acquire Silver Creek Midstream for $585m, extending its 
service offering in the Powder River Basin (PRB), via a 49% interest in the Powder River 
Gateway JV. These assets would increase PAA’s footprint in the region, and through PAA’s 
existing pipeline network, would provide PRB producers with enhanced access to the 
Cushing crude market. We note similar interest in the basin and Cushing connectivity by 
ENB, which recently announced its $2.55b acquisition of Tallgrass Energy’s crude oil 
business, including a 75% stake in the Pony Express pipeline, and the other 51% interest 
in the Powder River Gateway JV. We fielded inbounds focused on the potential 
competitive dynamics between ENB and PAA, with investors asking whether the two are 
set up to compete for incremental volumes and what that implies for Rockies to Cushing 
tariffs. On one hand, investor feedback highlights the deals as strong integration plays, 
and a read-through to a step up in PRB development, while more cautious investors 
question the quality of growth drivers behind that view. Further, the transaction has 
focused market attention on PAA’s strategic approach to expand its scale and scope 
outside of the Permian. While management has highlighted organic growth 
opportunities in the Permian and Canada, investors are monitoring PAA’s willingness to 
expand its footprint elsewhere or further within the PRB. From here, focus remains on 
PRB rig activity and producer development plans as the key proof point behind the 
acquired system’s dedications, alongside any 2027 guidance contributions, with 
read-throughs for Rockies differentials and bolt-on multiples across the liquids group.  
Read-throughs from ENB’s ~2 bcf/d West Texas Express open season. This week, ENB 
launched a non-binding open season for West Texas Express (WTX), a proposed 
greenfield intrastate system providing 2 bcf/d of additional egress out of the Waha hub 
area and into the greater El Paso market, with a target in-service date of 4Q29. Investor 
inbounds centered on what the announcement implies for the Permian takeaway 
balance, particularly coming after the Solitude announcement, which most investors we 
spoke with assumed was sufficient to keep the basin adequately supplied through the

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
