Downgrading SMR to Underweight; Maintaining OKLO
at OW
We are downgrading SMR to UW as the investment case increasingly depends on commercial
milestones that continue to move to the right. A definitive TVA/ENTRA1 PPA has yet to
materialize, raising the risk of further project and revenue delays, while uncertainty around
ENTRA1’s execution capabilities and the broader EPC pathway adds another layer of risk. These
delays also bring SMR’s liquidity profile into sharper focus, with continued cash burn likely
necessitating meaningful additional equity issuance. In contrast, we maintain our OW rating on
OKLO, where recent progress is steadily de-risking the commercialization story: Groves provides
a tangible proof point of the company’s ability to move nuclear infrastructure from
development to operation on an accelerated timeline, Aurora continues to advance through the
DOE authorization process, and growing customer and commercial visibility gives us greater
confidence in the path toward deployment.
Limited progress on TVA/ENTRA1 for SMR raises potential for milestone
push-outs
Looking to SMR commercialization timelines, we expect investors to remain focused on the
status of discussions between TVA and developer ENTRA1 Energy regarding definitive PPAs
supporting the proposed deployment of up to 6GW of SMR technology. Despite prior
commentary suggesting discussions were progressing constructively, there has been no
announced definitive agreement to date, with SMR’s 2Q26 update continuing to characterize
the parties as advancing discussions toward a definitive PPA. The company had originally
signaled that it was hoping for a binding PPA by the end of FY26, but no announcement to date
raises the risk that these negotiations could leak into 2027.
On the whole, the absence of a more tangible commercial milestone increases the possibility
that project timelines, associated development milestones, and ultimately revenue recognition
shift to the right. This is particularly relevant given the potential for a definitive agreement to
unlock site-specific engineering, licensing and other services revenue. At the same time, the
broader 6GW program remains dependent on future PPAs, with ENTRA1 Energy expected to
develop, finance, own and manage the plants and sell power to TVA. Overall, visibility into the
timing of the TVA opportunity remains limited, reinforcing our broader concerns around the

No regulatory milestones likely for a while for OKLO
On the regulatory front, OKLO continues to advance Aurora-INL through the Department of
Energy authorization process, as the company announced it had received approval of its
Preliminary Documented Safety Analysis (PDSA) on the 2Q call. The milestone is broadly
analogous to an NRC construction-stage approval in that it supports continued development
but does not yet authorize fuel loading or operations. The PDSA represented one of the more
significant DOE gating items, with relatively limited regulatory risk expected before the facility
approaches substantial completion.
Looking ahead, the next major milestone is approval of the final Documented Safety Analysis
(DSA), which will involve a comprehensive review of the completed facility and its operational
readiness prior to startup authorization. More broadly, we view the recent PDSA approval,
alongside the successful deployment and criticality of the Groves reactor, as further evidence
that OKLO is gaining confidence in DOE-led authorization pathways as a means of advancing
near-term nuclear projects, while the NRC continues development of its next-generation
licensing frameworks. While management acknowledged that operational and safety readiness
evaluations remain important milestones as Aurora progresses toward commissioning, the
largest remaining regulatory scrutiny is likely to occur closer to commercial operation rather
than during facility construction.
Groves proves speed is possible in nuclear
OKLO recently hosted analysts to its Groves isotope facility in Texas, a pilot-scale reactor project
that serves as the company's most tangible demonstrations of execution. Perhaps the most
notable highlight is speed of deployment; the project progressed from groundbreaking in
September 2025 to first criticality in August 2026, with construction beginning just 229 days
after groundbreaking. In an industry often characterized by lengthy development schedules,
regulatory complexity, and multi-year construction timelines, Groves provides a real-world
example of OKLO's ability to move a nuclear asset from concept to operation on an accelerated
timeline.
Isotope reactors are smaller and less complex than commercial power reactors, but the project
nevertheless required the company to navigate fuel procurement, regulatory approvals, special
nuclear material handling, and operational startup. While based on different technologies (the
Groves pilot is a LWR reactor, as opposed to the fast reactor of the Aurora design), the
experience gained at Groves should provide lessons for future Aurora deployments and offers
investors an early proof point that OKLO can translate design concepts into operating nuclear
infrastructure.
Commercial opportunity grows as Groves 2 makes progress
The company is targeting a range of end markets including radiopharmaceuticals, industrial
applications, semiconductors, and space and defense, although management indicated
biopharma is the primary near-term focus. Isotope customers prioritize reliability above all else,
followed by quality and then price, as roughly 15% of usable isotope value can be lost each day
in transit and some products must move from reactor to patient within only a few days. As
radiopharmaceutical adoption continues to accelerate and pharmaceutical companies place
increasing emphasis on secure, diversified supply chains, OKLO believes its dedicated isotope-
production model may offer advantages relative to a market that today often relies on large
power reactors where isotope production is a secondary activity.  Customers are also
increasingly seeking multiple qualified suppliers, creating opportunities for new entrants that
can demonstrate reliability and consistent product quality.

Looking forward, Groves appears to be only the first step toward a broader, vertically integrated
isotope platform in OKLO's future. While today's isotope supply chain is often fragmented
across irradiation, chemical processing, purification, packaging, and transportation, OKLO's
recently announced Groves-2 Forge initiative provides a clearer roadmap for consolidating
those functions into a more integrated commercial model. Groves-2 Forge was selected as part
of the Department of Energy's Nuclear Energy Launch Pad program and will build upon the
Groves Isotope Test Reactor by adding isotope production, processing, purification, and
product preparation capabilities, initially focused on Lutetium-177 for radiopharmaceutical
applications. 
Groves 2 is expected to be substantially larger than the existing facility and may ultimately
incorporate multiple reactors alongside downstream processing infrastructure, reflecting the
company's goal of integrating historically disjointed portions of the isotope value chain. While
Groves 2 will remain in Texas, future site selection remains customer-driven, with proximity to
end users viewed as vital given that certain isotopes can lose meaningful value with each day
spent in transit. The Groves-2 Forge announcement provides an important proof point that
OKLO is moving beyond a standalone demonstration reactor toward a commercial isotope
platform that will finally allow notable revenue recognition. 
Supply chain continues to de-risk, but SMR contract timelines are in
trouble
SMR continues to point to manufacturing progress as it prepares its modules for potential
commercial deployment, including prior work with Paragon on the Highly Integrated Protection
System and production of certain long-lead components for 12 modules. Most recently, NuScale
Power and MillenniTEK successfully fabricated first-of-a-kind boron-oxide pellets for the passive
emergency core cooling system, advancing another specialized manufacturing process required
for deployment. While these announcements demonstrate continued engineering and supply-
chain activity, we are increasingly cautious about extrapolating component-level progress into
confidence around project-level milestones. We continue to see risks related to binding
customer contracts, manufacturing and construction readiness, and financing and regulatory
approvals. We therefore view recent manufacturing announcements as important but largely
prerequisite steps, with the more consequential test remaining whether NuScale Power and its
partners can translate an increasingly mature design and supply chain into firm orders,
financed projects and ultimately commercial deployment on the timelines currently
contemplated.
ENTRA1’s limited track record remains a question
Despite its central role in the proposed TVA deployment, ENTRA1 Energy maintains a relatively
limited public profile and lacks an established record of completed projects. SMR has previously
clarified that the 45 years of experience cited for ENTRA1 Energy relates to its principals,
specifically the Habboush Group, rather than the developer itself. This is particularly relevant
given ENTRA1’s role as exclusive developer of the TVA project and its responsibility for leading
commercial negotiations, effectively placing a critical component of project advancement
outside SMR’s direct control.
In our view, this introduces an additional layer of execution and timing risk, and ENTRA1 is
unlikely to commit significant capital to the project until commercial terms are sufficiently de-
risked, including through the execution of a PPA with TVA. As a result, ENTRA1’s ability to
progress negotiations remains an important dependency for the broader project timeline.
Additionally, an added layer of risk is that ENTRA1 will need to secure the appropriate NRC
licenses to construct and operate a nuclear power plant, a process that can take significant
resources and time (months to years).

OKLO
Capital expenditure assumptions continue to rise
Cash outflows continue to be pulled forward as OKLO accelerates spending ahead of Aurora-INL
deployment. The company increased its FY26 capital spending outlook on the 2Q call, raising
expected cash used for PP&E to $400-500mm from a prior $350-450mm, with management
emphasizing that the higher spending reflects timing rather than any change to the targeted
deployment schedule. We expect similar outflows in FY27. Specifically, the incremental
investment is being directed toward procurement of long-lead equipment, grid interconnection
activities, and other development work intended to reduce execution risk ahead of the
company's targeted 2028 Aurora startup date. Against that backdrop, and recognizing the
broader trend of advancing expenditures earlier in the project lifecycle, we are modestly
increasing our capital cost assumptions for Aurora. We now model approximately $500mm of
capex for initial 75MW deployments, up from $450mm previously, and increase our longer-term
mature-fleet assumption to $350mm per 75MW plant from $325mm (see Figure 1 below). These
revisions mostly reflect higher expectations of material costs, as opposed to fuel, which has
been more stable in 2026 after a significant run up in prices in 2025. We expect management to
provide a more comprehensive update on Aurora unit economics sometime next year, as the
company hasn't released anything concrete since its S-1 filing in June, 2024. 
Price Target: We lower our price target to $59 (from $76). Our price target is predicated on a DCF
analysis assuming that the company hits its run-rate of putting twelve 75 MW plants per year by
2035 and it continues at that rate through 2050. 
SMR
With delayed timelines, liquidity concerns surface
At the end of 2Q, SMR's total cash and short term investments stood at a combined $1.9bn, a
large chunk of which was made up of $947mm in net equity proceeds in the quarter. As a
reminder, SMR had fully executed its previous ATM of $750mm, and announced a incremental
$1bn ATM, which it mostly fulfilled via the aforementioned $947mm in proceeds during the
quarter. In August, it put another $750mm of ATM program in place. Operating cash outflows
have totaled -$373mm YTD (although there is significant fluctuation quarter to quarter
depending on the timing of PMA accrued liability and asset flows). Taking an average of the last

Sustainable Investing Related Research: There is currently no globally accepted framework or definition (legal, regulatory or otherwise) of, nor
market consensus as to what constitutes a ‘sustainable’, ‘ESG’, ‘green’, ‘climate-friendly’ or an equivalent company, investment, strategy or
consideration or what precise attributes are required to be eligible to be categorised by such terms. This means there are different ways to evaluate a
company or an investment and so different values may be placed on certain sustainability credentials as well as adverse ESG-related impacts of
companies and ESG controversies. The evolving nature of sustainable investing considerations, models and methodologies means it can be
challenging to definitively and universally classify a company or investment under a sustainable investing label and there may be areas where such
companies and investments could improve or where adverse ESG-related impacts or ESG controversies exist. The evolving nature of sustainable
finance related regulations and the development of jurisdiction-specific regulatory criteria also means that there is likely to be a degree of divergence
as to the interpretation of such terms in the market. We expect industry guidance, market practice, and regulations in this field to continue to evolve.
