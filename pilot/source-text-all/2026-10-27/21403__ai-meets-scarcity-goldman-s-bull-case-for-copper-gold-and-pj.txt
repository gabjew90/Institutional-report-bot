 
AI Meets Scarcity: Goldman's Bull Case For Copper, 
Gold And PJM Power In Four Charts 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 21, 2026 - 01:15 AM 
Tactical hedges come and go with the headlines out of Hormuz. Goldman's structural 
commodity calls are about something slower and harder to fix: rising demand running into 
supply that can't keep up. 
In its new note making the case for commodity diversification (available to pro subs), the bank's 
commodities team singles out three markets where that collision is already visible: copper, gold, 
and select power markets such as PJM. Here are the four charts that make the case. 
1. Copper And Gold: Up And To The Right 
Goldman expects LME copper to average $15,400/t next year, up from just under $10,000 last 
year and 7% above spot, driven by electrification, energy security, quasi-strategic stockpiling, 
data centers and AI, all against "fairly constrained" mine supply. On gold, it reiterates $5,400 by 
end-2027. 
 

 
 
Readers won't need reminding that copper hit an all-time high back in September (Sep 8), as 
the physical economy repriced scarcity:

2. The Copper Squeeze Is Happening Outside The US 
The key detail: visible copper cathode stocks outside the US have fallen to seasonal lows, as 
the US and China compete for constrained supply. Tariff uncertainty keeps pulling metal into US 
warehouses, tightening the ex-US market where the LME price is set, while tighter Chinese 
scrap-invoicing rules push fabricators toward cathode, "further drawing down China's cathode 
inventories." Goldman expects the dynamic to persist over the next year. 
 

 
 
Goldman is not alone here: UBS calls copper its "highest-conviction" theme (Oct 10) and BofA is 
betting on copper and uranium (Oct 12), while we noted the tumble in Shanghai inventories a 
month ago (Sep 22). 
3. Gold: Central Banks Keep Buying 
Gold has had a rougher few weeks: Goldman admits the September drop in gold call-option 
demand, as Fed pricing shifted and the "debasement" theme cooled, implies downside risk to its 
end-2026 forecast of $4,650. But the structural driver hasn't changed. Goldman assumes 
central banks keep buying at the last 12 months' pace of roughly 60 tonnes per month through 
2026-27, versus a pre-2022 average of 17, a regime shift that dates precisely to the 2022 freeze 
of Russia's reserves.

4. PJM: Where The AI Boom Hits The Grid 
Finally, power. Spanning 13 Mid-Atlantic states including Virginia, "the world's data center 
capital," PJM now accounts for roughly 38% of US and one-sixth of global data center capacity. 
The result: PJM data center capacity has gone from roughly 2 GW in 2012 to more than 20 GW, 
while the 12-23-month-ahead PJM power price, net of natural gas costs, has jumped from 
around $2-3/MMBtu for most of the last decade to roughly $10. 
 

 
 
Washington has noticed. The Energy Department has already ordered PJM to dispatch backup 
power (Oct 13) to keep the lights on. Which is why we keep saying the answer for hyperscalers 
is generation they own: behind-the-meter today and small modular reactors tomorrow, rather 
than waiting in line for a grid that is already maxed out. 
Bottom Line 
Commodities in which rising demand meets persistent supply constraints face structural upward 
price pressure, especially copper, gold, and select power markets. 
Strip away the war premium and you are left with three markets where the supply response is 
measured in years and the demand in quarters. You can print chips, deficits and data centers; 
you can't print copper mines, bullion or a substation.
