China: Fiscal revenue growth slowed in September, while spending 
remained subdued; lowering our 2026 full-year AFD forecast
23 October 2026 | 10:35PM HKT
Bottom line: 
On-budget fiscal revenue growth fell to -0.1% yoy in September from +4.7% yoy in 
August, driven mainly by slower tax revenue growth. On-budget fiscal expenditure 
growth remained subdued at 0% yoy in September (vs. -0.1% yoy in August), 
although it improved modestly in sequential terms. Property-related government 
revenue worsened in September, with land sales revenue contracting 23.5% yoy. 
Combining on-budget and off-budget financing channels, our proprietary 
“augmented fiscal deficit” (AFD) metric narrowed further in September on both a 
3-month and 12-month moving average basis, suggesting that fiscal policy remained 
a growth drag despite recent modest easing measures introduced since 
end-September. After flagging downside risk for several months, we lower our 2026 
AFD forecast to 10.0% of GDP from 11.5% previously, reflecting year-to-date fiscal 
data and delayed easing this year. 
Key numbers: 
Fiscal revenue growth: -0.1% yoy in September (-2.1% mom sa non-annualized, 
estimated by GS), vs. +4.7% yoy in August (-5.6% mom sa non-annualized). 
Fiscal expenditure growth: +0% yoy in September (+3.4% mom sa non-annualized), 
vs. -0.1% yoy in August (-2.3% mom sa non-annualized).

1. On-budget fiscal revenue growth fell to -0.1% yoy in September from +4.7% yoy in 
August (Exhibit 1), driven mainly by tax revenue growth which slowed to +0.2% yoy from 
+5.6% yoy. Non-tax revenue growth remained subdued at -1.0% yoy in September (vs. 
+0.3% yoy in August). The August-to-September deceleration in year-on-year tax 
revenue growth was led by corporate income tax and property-related taxes, more than 
offsetting faster VAT revenue growth. On a quarterly basis, individual income tax 
revenue growth remained elevated at +16.3% yoy in Q3 (vs. +16.4% yoy in Q2), whereas 
growth in household disposable income per capita moderated to +5.4% yoy from +5.6% 
yoy (Exhibit 2). This disconnect may partly reflect tighter tax collection in recent 
quarters, including taxes on offshore trusts and insurance products, in our view.  
2. On-budget fiscal expenditure growth remained weak at 0% yoy in September (vs. 
-0.1% yoy in August), as faster spending growth in urban & rural community affairs, and 
energy saving & environmental protection largely offset slower spending growth in social 
security & employment, healthcare, and transportation. Based on our estimates, growth 
in infrastructure-related on-budget fiscal spending2 rose modestly to -1.3% yoy in 
September from -3.7% yoy in August, broadly consistent with the modest improvement 
in infrastructure investment growth (rising to -11.6% yoy in September from -16.8% yoy 
in August, based on GS definition and estimates).  
3. Property-related government revenue worsened in September — year-on-year 
contraction in off-budget land sales revenue widened to -23.5% in September from 
-12.3% in August, and on-budget property-related tax revenue growth dropped to 
-14.6% yoy from -1.9% yoy (Exhibit 3).3 Combining these two items, we estimate that 
government revenue directly from the property sector contracted 21.1% yoy in 
September (vs. -9.7% yoy in August). Despite recent green shoots in home prices in some 
large cities, most property activity indicators such as land sales, new home starts and 
property investment were weaker than market expectations so far this year. We 
maintain our forecast that land sales revenue will fall by 30% yoy or more this year, as 
recent property policies accelerating the shift to a completed-home sales model may 
further strain developers’ cash flows amid the prolonged property downturn.  
4. Taking into consideration the general public budget and government-managed fund 
budget, we estimate year-on-year growth in total government revenue slowed to -3.5% 
in September from +3.0% in August, while the year-on-year contraction in total 
government expenditure narrowed to -3.6% from -6.6%. Further incorporating more 
off-budget financing channels, our proprietary “augmented fiscal deficit” (AFD) metric 
continued to narrow in September on both a 3-month and 12-month moving average 
(12mma) basis, suggesting that fiscal policy remained a growth drag despite recent 
modest easing measures introduced since end-September (Exhibit 4).  
5. In the first nine months of this year, on-budget fiscal revenue grew 5.1% yoy (vs. -1.7% 
yoy in 2025), exceeding the MOF’s full-year budget projection of a 2.2% yoy gain thanks 
to a notable improvement in tax revenue growth amid higher global energy prices and 
domestic PPI inflation, while anecdotal evidence suggests that some local governments, 
under financial strain, have collected tax revenues more aggressively. Specifically, tax

this year’s growth target largely secured, we see a possibility that some proceeds from 
this year’s government bond issuance may remain unspent until early next year. Fiscal
