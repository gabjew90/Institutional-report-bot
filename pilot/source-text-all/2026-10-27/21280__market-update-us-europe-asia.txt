U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.58% | Nasdaq +1.05% | Dow +0.69%
Synopsis

US equities finished higher on Wednesday, ending not far from best levels. The upside reversed Tuesday's mostly lower finish, with the
major averages all pacing for solid weekly gains. The AI trade led, with semiconductors and memory higher and neoclouds largely better,
though CRWV-US was lower. Big tech was mostly higher, with GOOGL-US the only Mag 7 decliner. Space names rallied, led by SPCX-US.
Other outperformers included tech hardware, cybersecurity (PHP vulnerability headlines), multis, machinery, airlines, larger-cap banks,
IBs, credit cards (COF-US), hotels and department stores. Laggards included tobacco (PM-US), telecom (T-US), entertainment (NFLX-US),
MedTech (ISRG-US), China tech, integrated energy, managed care, apparel retail, restaurants, casinos, food/beverage and media. Treas-
uries were narrowly mixed with the curve flattening, the dollar index was flat, gold finished up 0.2%, silver was down 0.5%, Bitcoin futures
were up 2.5% and WTI crude settled down 0.7% in choppy trading. The focus on the AI trade followed blowout results from SPCX-US and
a positive SMCI-US preannouncement, with some attention on NVDA-US supply chain agreements. Compute demand and expansive capex
plans were the big talking points, while competitive challenges and funding dynamics also remained in the narrative. Attention was pulled

Macro

AI demand narrative: Positive AI demand takeaways came from SpaceX and Super Micro Computer. SpaceX's SI segment now represents
just over half of total revenue, and 90%+ came from compute sales and leasing. Pricing was slightly above $30-50 per watt, paybacks
are now under one year, and the company is prioritizing short-term contracts due to a supply deficit. It raised its 2027 compute target to
13GW+ from ~10GW (and highlighted a 20GW upside case), guided 1Q27 revenue to $33-37B vs ~$22B+ consensus and now sees
$500B-$1T in ARR exiting 2027. Super Micro Computer preannounced Sep Q (fiscal 1Q27) revenue of $18.5-$19.5B vs prior $14.5-
15.5B, citing stronger AI-platform demand and a larger enterprise mix. Separately, a report said Anthropic could spend up to $750B on
computing power over the next few years.

Middle East risk and oil: Reuters reported on Wednesday afternoon that the US State Department is preparing to order the departure of
some personnel and family members from US embassies across the Gulf, with officials citing the risk of renewed hostilities, particularly
involving Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen. The development comes despite President Trump saying earlier this month the US would not
attack Iran before the 3-Nov midterms amid "productive" talks. US-Iran headlines remained murky after reports that Qatar and Pakistan
had brokered a proposal addressing both sides' demands were later denied, and Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi said Tehran remains
open to diplomacy but will defend itself, while Iran maintains there will be no direct talks until its demands are met. Several Gulf diplomats
dismissed Tuesday's reports of progress in peace talks, and Brent was back above $110/bl in Asian trade after paring overnight losses.
Uncertainty about the stability of the status quo seemed to outweigh positive headlines on energy flows, including a media report that
Persian Gulf oil exports are beginning to top prewar levels despite an uptick in ship strikes. Kpler estimated Middle East flows averaged
>24M bpd in the week through 18-Oct vs 23.3M bpd before the war. US crude oil inventories fell 3.1M barrels in the week ending 16-Oct,
more than expected, and the Trump administration will loan 315K barrels from the emergency heating oil reserve to curb rising fuel costs.

Earnings themes: AI compute demand was the big theme, with focus on SpaceX results and guidance, which also played into elevated AI-
related capex. There was still little AI quantification, though Thermo Fisher noted a ~90% reduction in manual setup time related to a new
AI-based clinical research assistant. Pegasystems results played into the AI disruption theme in software. Pricing power was a takeaway
from United Airlines and, to a lesser extent, Netflix, while a benign credit backdrop was highlighted by Capital One. Winnebago noted
affordability pressures, elevated financing costs and macro uncertainty, but United reported strong travel demand, with premium a stand-
out, and was seemingly the most explicit example of input price pressure. Commentary was mixed on tariffs (refunds vs higher aluminum)
and China demand (seemingly industry specific). Netflix was the high-profile laggard from Tuesday afternoon's earnings batch: Q3 was
largely in line, but Q4 revenue missed consensus by ~0.6% and the company cut FY26 ad revenue guidance by ~10%, blaming a slower
rollout of ad-product capabilities. Cautious takeaways focused on weak US engagement and the content slate, with uncertainty over 2027
content spend and margins, and the stock is down 40%+ from its peak. More positive commentary centred on the core subscription
business, retention, pricing power, margins and international growth.

Afternoon headlines: On AI, Nscale's CEO told employees contracts to deliver computing power have doubled since March, a Nebius
executive reported rising AI computing prices as scrutiny of neocloud business models intensifies, Nvidia introduced containerized Vera
Rubin AI racks, OpenAI is bringing its models to Google Cloud, and SpaceX CDS spreads widened after it said it is investing aggressively.
A new AI-discovered vulnerability puts 70% of global websites at risk. On corporates, Intel is advancing factory expansion in Arizona and
Ohio, Abbott Laboratories will spin off its nutrition unit following costly baby formula litigation, a judge ordered Meta to pay New Mexico
$25B in the Cambridge Analytica case, eBay will cut 1K jobs, or 9% of headcount, and Getty Images filed for chapter 11 bankruptcy to
facilitate a move to private ownership. Companies have postponed more than $5B in debt sales this month amid rising rates.

EU Market Recap
Germany +1.3% | France +1% | UK +0.2%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished higher, near best levels. Tech, energy and real estate advanced, while autos, retail and telecom were
the big laggards. The session followed softer performance on Tuesday, though stocks had closed well above worst levels. European yields
backed up, with the French 10-year up 1.8 bps to 5.012%, moving above the 5% threshold, while German Bunds were broadly flat and UK
Gilts outperformed, with the 10-year yield down 3.4 bps. The dollar index was up 0.1%, gold up 0.1%, silver down 0.6% and Bitcoin futures
up 1.9%. Technology was the strongest sector, with the AI trade attempting a rebound, although neoclouds were weaker again and the
broader AI funding scrutiny theme remained in play. French yields returned to the focus, though there was no apparent spillover into
equities, albeit CA-FR cut its FY26 outlook citing the impact of French civil unrest and curfews. In ECB-speak, Kazaks said further rate
hikes may be needed if inflation stays elevated, and Lane said greater global euro use could help the ECB control inflation. Hawkish BoE
official Greene said the UK labor market is not as weak as thought after Tuesday's official data. Macro data focus was on UK updates,
with no Eurozone data of note: headline CPI and services prices came in ahead of forecast and core firmed as expected, borrowing in-
creased ahead of the October budget, and Brightmine showed UK pay awards edging up to 3.1%. The UK government approved the con-
troversial Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea, with gas supply starting from the end of this month, and Energy Secretary Fahnbulleh said
an energy fund will be set up by the government with a £2B down payment to reinvest revenues into the climate transition. Elsewhere,
the EU and Canada plan to flesh out closer associate membership by 2027. The Oil & Gas sector was another big outperformer, with the
European Energy basket up ~1.6%. Real Estate was stronger, with Aroundtown (AT1-DE) a notable outperformer after its subsidiary Grand
City Properties acquired a 20.04% stake in LEG Immobilien (LEG-DE), and Health Care was firmer on results from EssilorLuxottica (EL-FR),
Getinge (GETI.B-SE) and Eurofins Scientific (ERF-FR). On the corporate front, strength was seen in Industrials and Services, with RAND-
NL Q3 results influencing recruitment peers, while weakness was evident in Retail and Autos, with the CA-FR guidance cut and ML-FR
disappointing 9M sales. Michelin (ML-FR) cited lower original-equipment sales and Elisa (ELISA-FI) dropped sharply on a Q3 comparable
EPS miss, while Adidas (ADS-DE) rose after record Q3 operating profit. Mercedes-Benz (MBG-DE) CEO warned that EU protectionism
against China is triggering higher costs.
Macro

French bond rout: Press attention stayed on France following the weekend's civil unrest and violence, which spread across the country.
French 10-year yields were back above 5% (2002 highs) by the European close, while the French-German bond spread reached levels last
seen during the Eurozone debt crisis. The FT highlighted the risk of further spread widening ahead of the 2027 presidential election, citing
limited political appetite for fiscal consolidation and a potential Moody's downgrade due this Friday. The NYT said France's bond turmoil
reflects a broader structural risk of a self-reinforcing doom loop between fiscal pressures, political fragmentation and rising borrowing
costs, with investors demanding higher yields from governments perceived as unwilling or unable to make difficult spending or tax
decisions while voters increasingly favor populist parties. The article said this dynamic could generate a prolonged economic drag rather
than an immediate sovereign default. The FT highlighted contagion already visible in Italian and Belgian bonds, while ECB intervention
may require broader deterioration across Eurozone sovereign markets. Separately, Reuters said Eurozone banks are better prepared for
sovereign debt volatility than during the previous crisis, noting that lenders are better capitalized and supervised than during the 2010-
12 crisis.

UK inflation, labor market and BoE pricing: UK inflation accelerated in September to an eleven-month high of 3.5% y/y versus consensus
3.4% and prior 3.1%, while core inflation increased from 2.6% y/y to 2.8%, as expected, and services prices came in at 3.7% y/y versus
3.6% forecast and prior 3.4%, reinforcing worries of domestically generated inflation. Notable upward pressure came from transport
prices, particularly motor fuel, due to ongoing Middle East tensions, with housing and household services, airfares and communication
also contributing. Output PPI was 4.6% y/y versus consensus 4.4% and prior 3.8% revised from 3.7%, and input PPI was 8.3% y/y versus
7.6% forecast and prior 6.7% revised from 6.1%. The market is almost fully priced for a November BoE rate hike, and Reuters cited LSEG
data showing market expectations for four rate increases by the middle of next year, while sell-side economists have been less aggressive,
leaning towards one or two moves. Brightmine showed UK pay settlements edging up to 3.1% in the three months to September versus
3.0% in the prior three-month periods in July and August, with most awards expected to stay at 3% in 2027. Yesterday's official data
showed private sector wage growth edging up from 2.9% to 3.0% for the first time since the end of 2024, and average weekly earnings
excluding bonuses rose to 3.7% from 3.6%, in line with expectations. The cost of hedging sterling versus the euro rose to a five-month
high ahead of next Wednesday's UK Budget, with one-week implied volatility at 4.88%, the strongest level since May.

UK public finances and the Budget: UK government borrowing hit its highest level so far this year ahead of the 28-Oct Budget, at £24.5B
in September versus consensus £23.9B and prior £18.3B. Between April and September the government has borrowed £100B, £6B more
than the OBR forecast in the March spring statement, though £4.4B lower than a year ago, and total government debt exceeded £3T for
the first time. Debt interest costs were £10.9B on the month, which is £1.6B more than September 2025 and £2B above the OBR forecast,
and spending increases overwhelmed strong growth in income-related taxes. The government is under pressure to cut spending amid
expectations for a sharp reduction in fiscal space, and is not expected to tackle welfare reform until next year, when it will carry out a
departmental spending review. Bank tax rises are feared ahead of the Budget: European lenders are ~10% below their August peak and
UK banks nearly 14% off highs, and UBS noted that doubling the 3% surcharge would trim profits by ~2% on average. British gambling
operators may get relative relief, with reports that machine games duty may rise only from 20% to 25% in the Budget, well below the 40%
rate urged by Gordon Brown and the Social Market Foundation. Goodbody estimates a 5% rise, with 30% mitigation, would cut FY27


Ukraine peace diplomacy: US envoy Witkoff will meet Putin after "constructive" Oslo talks with Zelenskiy and European leaders on a peace
framework, including security guarantees and a post-war economic plan (Reuters), and Putin agreed to resume US-mediated peace talks

Policy and calendar watch: The EU and Canada will endorse an unprecedented alliance at a summit next week to reinforce plans for EU
associate membership for Ottawa, with the draft communique saying the full scope will be defined by the 2027 Canada-EU summit; the

roughly 2% of the UK's projected gas imports between 2025 and 2050, and the government will use revenues from North Sea oil and gas
to fund renewable schemes. BCA Research suggested investors should "buy Europe," naming banks as its preferred route, while
Morningstar is more cautious, seeing stocks as fully priced and profitability near its peak. Friday's Eurozone flash PMIs for October are
expected to show activity easing modestly from September's strong readings, with consensus seeing the composite at 52.7 from 53.1
prior, services at 52.5 from 53.0 and manufacturing at 52.7 versus 52.9 prior. Goldman Sachs sees Germany at 53.3 and France at 50.5.
The ECB meets 28-29 Oct, with markets pricing almost no chance of a hike but nearly two by mid-2027.

Synopsis

Asia equities ended mixed on Wednesday. The biggest gains were in South Korea and Taiwan, while Japan's Nikkei also ended higher,
although all three were well off their peaks. Australia's ASX and Singapore made small gains. India pared early gains to trade lower, and
Greater China indexes lost ground. The dollar edged higher, with the Australian dollar stronger on hawkish RBA comments but little move-
ment of note elsewhere. Treasury yields were higher across tenors and JGBs were mixed. Precious metals retreated further and industrial
metals were flat. Most technology-weighted benchmarks rose, with Samsung Electronics (005930-KR), SK Hynix (000660-KR) and Kioxia
(285A-JP) leading, while SK Inc (034730-KR) outperformed on news that an activist investor group had acquired a stake. RBA Deputy
Governor Hauser said inflation was "materially worse" than August's forecasts, lowering odds of a rate hike next month and triggering an
uptick in Australian bond yields and the Australian dollar. Japan's September exports grew 16.7% y/y but missed estimates and, while
imports grew, they were also below expectations. South Korea's exports for the first 20 days were again robust, but growth slowed some-
what against August's surge. Bank Indonesia held its base interest rate unchanged but cut its banks' RRR to attract foreign capital. On
corporate news, Tokyo Ohka Kogyo (4186-JP) and Amechatec (6227-JP) are to form a 51-49% joint venture to supply the semiconductor
sector. CATL (3750-CH) posted Q3 earnings that missed market estimates on higher costs and compressed margins across its main
business units. Activist investor Align Partners said it had acquired a 1.5% stake in SK Inc (034730-KR), adding that it saw the company
as an attractive investment opportunity. NextDC (NXT-AU) cut its earnings forecast after reporting delays to power supply in one of its
Melbourne data centers, and shares were down sharply. Jio Platforms began its public bidding process in India's record $3.2B IPO after
confirming cornerstone investors.
Macro

China export outlook: Caixin reported that economists warned China's export boom may start to slow down from 2027. Speaking at the
China Macroeconomic Forum, Changjiang Securities chief economist Wu Ge highlighted that September's 36.3% y/y export growth is rare
even by the standard since China's accession to the WTO in 2001, which he attributed to robust global manufacturing demand, seen from
strong PMIs. He pointed to two factors that markets may be underpricing. The first is changes in export tax rebates, which Beijing has cut
or scrapped for products including solar, batteries and EVs in different phases: the rebate for battery products, reduced in April this year,
will be abolished from Jan-27, and that for EVs will be reduced from 13% to 9% from Apr-27, which prompted front-loaded shipments.
Bloomberg noted the sharp drop in CATL (3750-HK) shares on Wednesday as analysts warned of downward revisions to forecasts, citing
margin pressure due to the rebate changes. The second is China-EU trade talks, as Brussels tends to use quotas rather than US-style
tariffs, which are likely harder to dodge through re-routing, prompting exporters to ship early. Export growth is likely to slow from 2027 as
front-loading fades and the base effect bites. Goldman Sachs chief China economist Hui Shan said at the same forum that much of
China's AI export strength was price-driven, citing only a 1% gain in volume but a 130% surge in value in September.

Regional central banks: RBA Deputy Governor Hauser said in a fireside chat that the inflation outlook looks "materially worse" than August
forecasts and that it was "pretty clear" inflation would likely be higher than expected, citing higher oil and tech prices as well as persistent
domestic pressures. He discussed the de-anchoring of inflation expectations, detecting signs of doubt in the public that inflation will
return to the target range, and said a growing share of companies consider 3.0-3.5% inflation as the new normal, yet acknowledged the
softer than expected labour market data complicating the dual mandate. RBA November rate pricing rose to about 50% from 30%. In
Japan, an October Reuters consensus poll found 54 of 78 predicting the next BOJ rate hike in December, taking the policy rate to 1.50%,
a near 70% proportion that almost doubled the share in last month's poll. October calls were all but ruled out, consistent with OIS pricing,
and 60% see another move by June-end, with almost half not seeing further hikes while those forecasting 2.00% or higher grew to 51%
from 40%. Almost two thirds anticipate FY27 JGB issuance will be kept below JPY40T ($253B). Bank Indonesia kept its 7D repo rate steady
at 5.0% and its lending facility at 6.5%, but raised its deposit facility rate 25 bps to 5.0% to attract fresh foreign capital inflow. Consensus
had expected a hold, though several economists had opted for a base rate rise in the wake of MSCI's proposal to double-downgrade its
equity market. It will lower banks' RRR by 1ppt from November and a further 1ppt in December, and made no changes to its FY26 GDP
forecast of 4.9-5.7%.

Japan and South Korea trade data: Japan's customs exports rose 16.7% y/y in September, below consensus of 21.3% and following
19.3% in the previous month, marking the softest growth since April and the first headline miss this year. Chip-related goods and autos
remained the positive drivers, while shipments to China sharply decelerated to 8.6% from 20.5%. Imports increased 18.9%, below con-
sensus of 20.4% and following 28.0% in the prior month, dominated by fossil fuels. Total crude oil imports rose 3.1% by volume and 51.1%
by value, with crude shipments from the US surging 513.8% by volume and 819.7% by value, in stark contrast to the Middle East at -
30.0% and +1.5%. South Korea's seasonally adjusted exports in the first 20 days of October rose 65.9% y/y, fueled once again by semi-
conductor shipments, versus September's 89.8% y/y growth. Per-workday exports grew just 0.9% m/m following a 7.0% m/m gain in
August, though data were distorted by fewer workdays amid the Chuseok holiday, and the BOK will note the slowdown ahead of its rate
decision due Thursday. Unadjusted total exports contracted by 3.9% m/m, and on a y/y basis total exports rose 105% and imports 43%,
giving a narrower trade surplus of $14.5B, down from August's record $23B.
