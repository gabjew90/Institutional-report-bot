Kering
Model update post 3Q26
Our view: Kering's 3Q print is difficult to interpret. At the group level,
revenues are in-line with consensus, whilst for Gucci, retail was softer than
expected, and offset by outsized wholesale growth. This makes the starting
point for 4Q, where accelerating newness from November (Gucci Core)
as well as brand investments in marketing and store renovations, more
challenging than we previously thought, and we reduce our 4Q Gucci retail
estimate to -5% organic as a result (from -1% previously). The demand
environment is challenging, which was always going to be a factor in
Kering's equity story, even if it's outside of management's control. RBC EPS
estimates are 7%/15% below consensus FY27E/28E.
Key points:
Conference call key takeaways - 1) ) October current trading broadly
in-line with 3Q, whilst September slightly better than the 3Q average
2) Sales growth target for FY26 removed from slides, given difficult
trading environment, and management focus on quality of revenue rather
than near term top line growth, 3) 3Q Group American and Chinese
cluster growth rates decelerated by 5ppt vs 2Q run-rate (-HSD and -
low teens respectively in 2Q), 4) 2H26 and FY26E opex will be lower
yoy in absolute terms, and confirms FY26E margin improvement vs FY25
despite gross margin pressures from regional mix, gold/raw materials
prices and investment in product quality, 5) Gucci 4Q26E wholesale may
be slightly negative given delivery phasing, 6) 3Q26 largely impacted by
low traffic, whilst conversion and AUR are up, notably for Gucci. 7) 2 new
Creative Directors will be announced (for either Saint Laurent, McQueen
or Balenciaga). 8) Store closures targeting at least 125 and accelerating
refurbishment program including new store concept roll-out. 9) Jewellery N
American deceleration (from +10% in 2Q to -10% in 3Q) due to Boucheron
high jewellery sales that did not repeat.
3Q26 revenues of €3.34bn (-1% organic) are in line with consensus, with
Gucci revenues €1.29bn (-6% organic vs cons -5%) slightly missed in organic
terms. Gucci retail -9% is softer than consensus (-6%) offset by strong
wholesale +45% (cons +3%). Jewellery €239m (+11% organic) and Eyewear
€373m (+4% organic) revenues are in line with consensus.
Change in estimates - For FY26E, We lower group organic growth by
130bps reflecting Gucci 3Q26 retail miss (-9% vs -6% consensus), and
lower our 4Q26 estimate partly offset by higher wholesale. Positive FX
translation benefit results in stable reported revenues and EBIT estimates
are unchanged, whilst we adjust net finance expenses and tax rate, which
results in 5% EPS reduction. RBC vs consensus - RBC EBIT/EPS estimates are
3%/7% below consensus for FY27E and 7%/15% below for FY28E.
Valuation - KER trades at a 25x FY27E P/E and 2.1x EV/sales on RBC
estimates which does not offer sufficient downside protection in our view,
in the event that the Gucci turnaround takes longer than expected.

3Q26 results quick take 
Quick Take 
Bottom line - 3Q26 revenues of €3.34bn (-1% organic) are in line with consensus, however the 
focus will be on Gucci which at the headline level delivered 3Q revenues in line with consensus 
but the shape is unusual – with retail (-9%) missing consensus (-6%) by a margin, and offset by 
notably high wholesale growth (+45%) reflecting a low comparison base and new collection 
deliveries. 
On balance, we do not believe Kering will be judged on 3Q performance which at the group 
level is broadly in line with lowered expectations coming into the print. Rather, Gucci 
turnaround is likely to be judged against 4Q performance which should see better product 
newness (Gucci Core) and commercialisation actions including brand investment and new 
store concept roll-out. As such, we believe investors will be keen to hear details on the exit 
rate and October current trading (including China Golden Week performance). 
Details 
Kering delivered 3Q26 revenues of €3.34bn (-1% organic) which are in line with consensus in 
reported and organic terms. By region, N America (+1% organic), W Europe (flat), APAC (-8%), 
Japan (+4%) and RoW (-5%). 
Fashion & Leather revenues of €2.74bn (-3% organic vs cons -3%) are in line with consensus. 
Gucci revenues of €1.29bn (-6% organic vs cons -5%) slightly missed in organic terms. Gucci 
retail -9% is softer than consensus (-6%) offset by strong wholesale +45% (cons +3%) which 
reflects order-book effects and timing of new collection deliveries. However, 3Q is a transitory 
quarter ahead of 4Q where the breadth of new product (Gucci Core), marketing investments 
and initial new store concept roll-out should be more visible in our view. 
Jewellery €239m (+11% organic) and Eyewear revenues of €373m (+4% organic) are also in 
line with consensus.

Valuation charts 
Kering shares currently trade at 24x 12-month forward P/E and 15x EV/EBIT which are above 
the 10yr historical average valuation, largely reflecting depressed earnings base. Shares trade 
at 2.2x 12-month forward EV/sales which is below the 10yr historic average (3.3x). 
On a relative basis, Kering trades at an 17% P/E premium relative to the luxury sector (vs 19% 
discount historically) and at 8% EV/EBIT premium (vs 16% discount historically) which reflect 
depressed earnings base.  
For Kering to re-rate would require an acceleration in revenue growth momentum, largely 
driven by Gucci in our view, and for which visibility is low at present. Gucci trajectory should 
be better understood post 4Q26E and into 1H27E by which point some of Gucci’s strategic 
changes including product and price architecture repositioning, newness and refreshed store 
concept and marketing approach should be better understood.

Valuation
We use a DCF analysis to derive a 12-month price target of
€260. At our price target, Kering would trade at 28x FY27E P/E,
and the implied upside from current levels justifies our Sector
Perform rating in view of our relative ratings framework.
The core assumptions behind our DCF valuation are a WACC of
8.5% (incorporating a beta of 1.0, risk-free rate of 4.0%, equity
risk premium of 5.0%, and pre-tax cost of debt of 3.0%), our
explicit five-year forecasts, +3% FCF CAGR in the medium term
(years 5-10) and 2.5% terminal growth rate.
Upside scenario
We estimate an upside DCF-based value of €400 assuming
Kering can deliver 6% sales CAGR in years 5-10 and 22% margin
by FY35E, resulting in 9% FCF CAGR (vs +3% in our base case).
Downside scenario
We estimate a downside DCF-based value of €160 assuming
Kering can deliver -1% sales CAGR in years 5-10 and 15%
margin by FY35E, resulting in -6% FCF CAGR (vs +3% in our base
case).
Investment summary
We rate Kering Sector Perform with a DCF-derived price
target of €260.
Kering is under fairly significant pressure to stabilise revenue
trends at Gucci principally, and Saint Laurent and Other
Houses to a lesser extent which management has confirmed
should take place in 2026 at group level.
We believe it is in the early innings of its turnaround, with
management and creative director changes largely in place.
There has already been a raft of initial changes at Kering since
new CEO Luca da Meo took the reins (Sept '25), including
streamlining the leadership structure, appointing Francesca
Bellettini as CEO of Gucci, renegotiating Valentino option
terms and the sale of Kering Beauty to L'Oreal.
New strategy formed by the new CEO, and presented during
Kering's 2026 CMD is detailed and well constructed, with focus
now on execution and time required for improvement in brand
heat especially as that relates to Gucci and the financials
(revenue acceleration and margin recovery).
Risks to rating and price target
In addition to sector-wide risks (macro + FX), the main
company-specific risks include: 1) Lack of improvement in
momentum at Gucci under new leadership and creative
direction; 2) Saint Laurent price elasticity stretched too far
and its growth profile remains subdued; 3) Balenciaga brand
equity is impaired; 4) too much change too fast under new
leadership; 5) Valentino put options exert additional pressure
on leverage and balance sheet in 2028 and 2029.

Kering
Company description
Kering is one of the largest luxury conglomerates globally, with FY25 revenues of €15bn and EBIT of €1.8bn. Its brand portfolio
primarily includes Gucci (c. 40% revenue, 60% EBIT), Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta and Balenciaga as well as smaller brands in
soft luxury and Boucheron and Pomellato in hard luxury amongst others. The group is controlled by the Pinault family via Artemis,
which owns 42% of Kering’s share capital.
