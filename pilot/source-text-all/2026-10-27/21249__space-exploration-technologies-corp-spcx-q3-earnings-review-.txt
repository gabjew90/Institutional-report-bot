GS Positive & Negative Takeaways from Earnings Call
Quarterly Takeaways (Positives & Negatives)

Positives

Q3’26 revenue and GAAP EBIT came in ahead of Street estimates, largely driven by SI segment revenues (cloud service agreements) and continued 
adoption of Starlink (across both consumer and enterprise & government);

Positive commentary around ability to bring compute capacity online, with management highlighting line of sight into scaling to 2.5-3GW of 
nameplate compute by YE26 and the potential to bring an incremental 10GW of compute online in 2027 (with an upside node of SpaceX having >20GW 
by YE27);

Given increased visibility into compute capacity coming online, management provided Q1’27 revenue guidance ($33-37bn) which came in well 
ahead of GSe/Street estimates – while also implying solid sequential growth (vs. management’s December ARR target of $100bn); &

Continued optimism around Starship’s long-term ability to scale mass to orbit, with management also providing additional commentary around the 
timing of goals (incl. Flight 15 goal of capturing booster and ship) and launch cadence.

Negatives

Q4’26 capex guidance came in ahead of GSe/Street estimates, primarily due to management’s expectation of bringing additional compute capacity 
coming online (to ~2.5-3GW by YE26);

GAAP Super Intelligence EBIT came in below GSe/Street expectations, largely driven by elevated levels of SBC related to the Cursor acquisition; &

Given the elevated levels of capital intensity across 2H26 & 2027 (implied in our updated operating estimates), potential future funding needs will 
likely act as an overhang on the stock until visibility around potential future capital raises is achieved.

Continued Focus on the Drivers of Revenue Growth (Both Near & Longer-Term), Key Operational Milestones & 
Investments Needed to Support Operating Trajectory
Key Investor Questions  

Revenue Growth Drivers. On the back of management’s $100bn ARR target (December 2026) and Q1 2027 revenue guidance 
(of $33-37bn), is there any framing around how to think through the composition of revenue growth drivers between SI (hosting 
& inference), Connectivity (across Enterprise/Government and Consumer) and Launch? 

Compute Buildout. What is SpaceX’s multi-year roadmap for bringing multi-gigawatt compute capacity online – and what level 
of visibility does the company have into managing power grid access & scaling the necessary infrastructure? In terms of the costs 
associated with bringing compute online, how should investors think through the key cost advantages that SpaceX has (currently 
and longer-term) on a per GW basis? 

Compute Monetization. Given management commentary around the monetization rate of hosting deals, paired with the 
continued progress around Grok model release deployment (and introduction of Grok Bot), how should investors think through 
both the near-term allocation of compute (across internal & external use cases), along with the monetization rates (on a per watt 
basis) of capacity? 

Starship Execution & Economics. How should investors think about the operational timeline to achieve full Starship reusability, 
launch cadence, and unit economics over time? With respect to upcoming launches (i.e. Flight 15/16), what are the key goals that 
SpaceX remains focused on achieving and is there any way to think through the importance of the upcoming launches as a 
mechanism to drive increased confidence in the company’s ability to achieve the launch cadence outlined during earnings (across 
both 1H27 & exiting FY27)? Beyond 2027, is there any framing around how to think through the trajectory of refurbishment 
vehicle timelines during 2027 and beyond? 

Connectivity. Away from SpaceX’s recently announced agreement to acquire licenses related to 800 MHz spectrum, what 
additional investments need to be made to scale the Connectivity business? Any additional framing as to how to think about 
Starlink’s ability to scale capacity (both terrestrially & through satellites) to support the business?

Outlining SpaceX’s Segment Specific & Overall Risks 
Key Risks

Overall

a) Governance risks given majority voting power concentrated in founder & CEO; b) existing related-party transactions across various 
companies/entities associated with Founder & CEO; c) high capital intensity forecasts & FCF burn, requiring sizable future fund raises; d) potential for 
future equity dilution from either stock grants/other forms of compensation (incl. sizable management stock brands based on performance targets) 
and/or potential equity fund raises

Space

a) Competition from other commercial launch companies; b) higher than expected allocation of launches to internal use cases; c) reliance on 
government contracts for revenues; d) any delays or difficulties in maintaining/renewing required regulatory approvals for space-related activities; & e) 
lower than expected profitability (given the fixed-price nature of contracts)

Connectivity

a) Competition from other broadband internet and mobile service providers; b) reliance on significant growth of internal launch capacity through 
Starship, which has yet to prove out full reusability/rapid launch schedules; c) ability to scale enterprise & government Starlink contracts; d) reliance on 
3P partners for go-to-market/distribution and spectrum; e) any delays or difficulties in acquiring regulatory approvals & licenses for satellites 

SI

a) reliance on developing, scaling and maintaining orbital datacenter compute; b) competition from other frontier AI models as it relates to both model 
performance and consumer/enterprise demand for model access; c) ability to scale consumer AI revenues; d) reliance on significant growth of internal 
launch capacity through Starship, which has yet to prove out full reusability/rapid launch schedules; e) supply chain risks & reliance on 3P partners for 
key inputs (i.e. NVDA GPUs) 

In addition, the company is exposed to broader macroeconomic conditions which could adversely impact the business

GS Factor Proﬁle
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: 
Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for 
specific metrics for each stock. The normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows:
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial 
Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s 
forward-looking P/E, P/B, price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile indicating a stock trading at a 
higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile).
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future 
compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.

an acquisition target, 2 representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard departmental guidelines we incorporate
