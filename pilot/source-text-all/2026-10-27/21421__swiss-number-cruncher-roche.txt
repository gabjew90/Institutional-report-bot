Outlook: Roche has conﬁrmed its FY26 guidance: revenue growth in 
the mid-single-digit percentage range, core EPS growth in the high-
single-digit percentage range; impact of patent expiries: around CHF 
0.6 bn. The currency impact is expected to be -3% on revenue and -
5% on core EBIT and core EPS. The dividend is set to be increased 
further. Over the medium to long term, the core EBIT margin is ex­
pected to rise further. 
The ﬁnal weeks of FY26 and the start of FY27 are set to remain 
eventful. Tomorrow evening (23 October), detailed competitive data 
on remibrutinib (Novartis) in multiple sclerosis will be presented. Over 
the weekend, Europe’s largest cancer conference, ESMO, will take 
place, at which details of Roche’s Divarasib (a KRAS G12C inhibitor 
for various types of cancer) will be presented at one of three Presi­
dential Symposia in Madrid. On 30 November and 18 December, reg­
ulatory decisions on giredestrant for early- and late-stage breast can­
cer will follow; the pricing strategy for giredestrant will be a key fac­
tor here. 
At the start of FY27, we expect an update on the survival data from 
the lidERA trial for giredestrant. The FY26 results, including initial 
giredestrant launch data, will follow as early as the beginning of 
February. Approval for fenebrutinib is then expected by the end of 
March. 
Japan (3Q26: +49% year-on-year) beneﬁted from one-off effects to­
talling CHF 180 mn, which will cease in 4Q26. A new IT system was 
introduced and, to ensure supply security, stock levels were increased 
as a precautionary measure.  
On the pipeline front, there have been only minor changes. The 
CDK4/2 programme is entering Phase 2, whilst the Phase 2 pro­
gramme for aﬁmkibart for atopic dermatitis has been halted.  
Conclusion: Roche has reported strong sales ﬁgures in its pharma­
ceuticals division and presented the expected stabilisation in diagnos­
tics. This means that, building on a solid foundation, Roche can 
tackle the coming months with promising launches.  
In addition to strong operational performance in terms of revenue 
and proﬁtability, the investment case for FY27 will be driven primarily 
by the launches of giredestrant and fenebrutinib as well as further 
pipeline news.

Explanation of the rating system 
The Research team of Zürcher Kantonalbank does not publish absolute recommendations such as “Buy”, “Hold” or “Sell”. Its ratings are relative to a benchmark (“Outper­
form”, “Market Perform”, “Underperform”). For Swiss equities, the Swiss Performance Index (SPI) serves as a benchmark. The expected relative performance is deﬁned as 
the anticipated total return over the coming 12 months, which consists of the share price performance (price gain or loss) plus distributions (e.g. dividends). Compared to 
the expected total return of the benchmark, the “Outperform” rating implies a considerable outperformance. “Market Perform” refers to a comparable performance (+/–
5%), whereas “Underperform” predicts an overall performance which is markedly lower than that of the benchmark. “Restricted” indicates, that for legal reasons, re­
search coverage of the corresponding security has been temporarily suspended. It cannot be ruled out that a temporary suspension may extend over a longer period or 
even lead to the deﬁnitive discontinuation of coverage.
