Investors pulling back a little bit  
◼ 
At -0.8982 (vs -0.9638 last week) our Risk Index has further retreated from 
risk-seeking territory but is still signalling investor optimism. The Index 
is above its 100-day moving average for the first time since April, during 
the height of uncertainty around the US-Iran war. Breaches of the Risk 
Index above its 100-day moving average can signal more sustained 
retreats in risk sentiment.  
◼ 
The beginning of the earnings season has led to investors pulling back 
a little bit from risk-seeking behaviour. This pullback is despite the fact 
that, of the 165 five S&P 500 companies reporting so far, nearly 98% have 
beat earnings expectations. But investors are especially focused on 
technology companies given that the AI boom is the biggest driver of 
equity markets. When focusing on technology companies, the earnings 
beat ratio drops to a still-healthy 81%.  
◼ 
About 60% of technology companies have reported so far. Later today, 
SAP, IBM, Texas Instruments, Meta & Telsa will report their earnings, and 
these reports will be critical for investment sentiment.   
◼ 
Investors are also keeping an eye on oil prices. While there has been an 
increase in the number of attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz, oil 
flows out of the Gulf continue leaving investor concerns quelled for now. 
Investors are also awaiting the Israeli general election on 27 October as 
well as the US midterm elections on 3 November.  
◼ 
Rising equity and FX market volatility as well as EM-Sovereign and 
private credit spreads were the main drivers of the push higher in our 
Risk Index over the past week. Capping the rise in the Index was the 
outperformance of defensive stocks by cyclical stocks. 
◼ 
Among G10 currencies, only the CHF, CAD and SEK have significant 
positive correlations with our Risk Index. The GBP, USD and NOK have 
significant negative correlations with the Index.
