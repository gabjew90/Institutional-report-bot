Luxury & Lifestyles: Quick Pulse – L'Oréal: 
Beauty(ful) beat
Luxury & Lifestyles
 
Author: Thomas Parmentier, CIO Equity Sector Strategist - Consumers, UBS Switzerland AG
• L’Oréal reported 7.7% LFL sales growth in 3Q26, ahead 
of expectations and its strongest quarterly performance 
since 1Q24. Growth was broad-based across regions, 
marking a third consecutive quarter above 6% LFL 
growth.
• We see three key takeaways from the quarter: 1) 
growth remained broad-based across divisions and 
geographies, supporting another quarter of strong 
market share gains; 2) execution was particularly strong 
in Dermatological Beauty and emerging markets, while 
underlying demand remained robust despite some 
benefits from inventory build-up and favorable seasonal 
factors; and 3) innovation, AI-enabled marketing and 
portfolio breadth continue to support above-market 
growth.
• We continue to view the beauty category as one of 
the most attractive segments within Luxury & Lifestyles, 
benefiting from structural growth drivers linked to 
wellness, self-care and premiumization, as well as 
resilient consumer demand across economic cycles.
Source: UBS, Gettyimages
 
Our view
L'Oréal delivered a very strong performance in 3Q26, with 
like-for-like (LFL) sales growth of 7.7% y/y, comfortably 
ahead of consensus expectations. A key positive was the 
broad-based nature of the growth: every major region 
delivered LFL growth above 5.5%, except for Latin America, 
where temporary execution issues in Mexico weighed on 
performance. This marked the third consecutive quarter 
of LFL growth above 6%, reinforcing our view that the 
company's strong first-half performance was not merely 
driven by easier comparisons but also reflected structurally 
stronger growth drivers.
Looking ahead, management indicated that the global 
beauty market should continue to expand by around 5% 
in 4Q26, although L'Oréal's outperformance is expected to 
moderate from the exceptionally strong level achieved in 
3Q26. Several temporary tailwinds supported the quarter, 
including a particularly favorable sun-care season in Europe, 
inventory build-up in Asia Travel Retail, fragrance channel 
loading ahead of the year-end season, and an earlier start 
to Double 11 in China.
Beyond its unparalleled brand portfolio and category 
leadership, we see growing evidence that artificial 
intelligence 
is 
becoming 
an 
additional 
source 
of 
competitive advantage for the group. AI-enhanced 
consumer engagement, personalization, innovation and 
marketing effectiveness should further strengthen L'Oréal's 
ability to outperform the global beauty market consistently. 
We expect the upcoming Capital Markets Day (30 Nov-1 
Dec) to provide additional evidence supporting this thesis.

Here are our three key takeaways 
from the 3Q26 results:
Brand momentum remains exceptionally strong across the 
portfolio.
The most encouraging aspect of the quarter was sustained 
strong growth, with LFL sales increasing by 7.7% y/y, the 
company's strongest quarterly performance since 1Q24. 
Growth was broad-based across geographies and divisions, 
suggesting that the performance was not dependent on a 
single category or region. Several of L'Oréal's largest brands 
continued to gain momentum. In Professional Products, 
hair-care brands Kérastase, Redken, and Matrix maintained 
strong growth, helping the division outperform the global 
hair-care category. Management highlighted particularly 
strong momentum for Kérastase in Europe and North Asia. 
Premium skincare also remained robust, supported by strong 
performances from Helena Rubinstein and SkinCeuticals in 
North Asia.
Strong execution across regions highlights continued 
market share gains.
Regional performance remained impressive. All major 
geographies except Latin America delivered LFL growth 
above 5.5%, demonstrating strong operational execution 
and brand momentum. In Latin America, sales declined 
3% LFL owing primarily to company specific disruptions 
in Mexico. Given that Latin America represents only 
around 7.5% of group sales, the impact on the group 
overall was limited. Management also highlighted several 
temporary factors that supported growth during the quarter. 
These included stronger-than-normal inventory build-up 
in Asia Travel Retail as new operators came online, 
fragrance replenishment ahead of the important year-
end selling season, and an earlier start of the Double 
11 promotional period in Mainland China. Additionally, 
an exceptionally strong summer season boosted sun-care 
demand, particularly within Dermatological Beauty, where 
sun-care sales reportedly grew by around 20% LFL. Despite 
these temporary benefits, underlying momentum across the 
broader business remains robust. Excluding the areas most 
affected by inventory and seasonal factors, approximately 
60% of group sales still grew by around 6.2% LFL during 
the quarter.
AI, innovation and brand scale increasingly reinforce 
L'Oréal's competitive advantage.
While management expects some moderation in L'Oréal's 
outperformance during 4Q26, the broader message 
from the quarter was that the company's competitive 
advantages continue to strengthen. The global beauty 
market remains healthy, growing by around 5%, while 
L'Oréal consistently outgrows the industry through superior 
innovation, portfolio management and execution. Artificial 
intelligence is playing an increasingly important role in this 
process. Management highlighted how proprietary tools 
such as BetIQ are improving media allocation, content 
creation and marketing effectiveness across markets. We 
expect the upcoming Capital Markets Day to provide further 
evidence that AI is not only driving incremental demand 
for beauty products but also enhancing L'Oréal's ability 
to innovate faster, improve consumer engagement and 
consistently outperform the global beauty market.
Risks to our view:
Unfavorable economic developments in key markets, 
weaker consumer confidence, intensifying competition, 
increased promotional intensity, unsuccessful innovation 
and M&A, changing beauty trends, and unfavorable FX 
effects.

Equity selection: An assessment relative to a benchmark
Equity selections in Equity Preferences lists (EPLs) are assessments made relative to a sector/industry, country/regional or 
thematic benchmark. The chosen benchmark is disclosed on the front page of each EPL. It is also used to measure the 
performance of the EPL owner(s). Including a stock in the EPL constitutes neither a view on its expected, standalone absolute 
performance nor a price target. Rather, EPLs are meant to support the UBS House View, with the stocks included in them 
selected for their superior risk/return profiles.
Our selection is based on an assessment of the company's fundamental outlook and valuation, the risks owning the stock 
entails, including material sustainability risks and the diversification benefits it provides in an investment portfolio, among 
many other factors. UBS GWM CIO‘s selection methodology enables wealth management clients to invest in a specific 
investment theme or focus on a sector/industry or country/region.
The lead strategist is responsible for the CIO view on a stock, while the EPL owner(s) is/are responsible for the stock selection 
within an EPL. Stocks can be selected for multiple EPLs. For consistency's sake, a stock can only be selected as either Most 
Preferred or Least Preferred, not both simultaneously. As EPL benchmarks differ, stocks do not need to be included on every 
list to which they could theoretically be added.
Only stock views prepared by UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBS FS) which are compatible with the above equity selection 
system are provided. A stock cannot be selected as Most Preferred if it is rated Sell by UBS Investment Bank, while a Buy-
rated stock by UBS Investment Bank cannot be selected as Least Preferred.
