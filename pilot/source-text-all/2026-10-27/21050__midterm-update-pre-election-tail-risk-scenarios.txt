Macro Research 
 
    
With two weeks until the US midterm elections on 3 November, early voting 
has already opened in more than 40 states. Prediction markets are broadly 
unchanged since we published our Midterm update, 13 October. The 
prediction market Polymarket continues to see Democrats winning both 
chambers as the most likely outcome, with a 62.5% probability.  
This has created a growing sense of urgency, with President Trump and the 
administration appearing ready to 1) implement policies before Republicans 
potentially lose their majorities in both chambers of Congress and 2) pursue 
policies aimed at swaying voters towards Republican candidates. 
1) became clear when the White House formed a committee to probe Fed 
Governor Cook, whom Trump has repeatedly tried to remove from the Fed 
Board of Governors. Trump appears pressured to remove Cook before the 
new Senate takes office in early January, as a Democratic majority could 
block his Fed Board nominations even if he removed Cook later. We do not 
expect him to succeed before the new Congress takes office. The prediction 
market Kalshi put the probability of Cook leaving or being removed before 
January at below 10%, and only a 25% chance of it happening before 2029. 
The first point could also become clear if Republicans seek to pass the full-
year government funding package when the current stopgap bill expires on 
11 December. We doubt any spending increases would materially affect the 
US macro-outlook. The early framework passed by the House in July included 
USD 95 billion in new spending, or 0.3% of GDP, and was already criticised by 
Republican fiscal hardliners. For details, see RtM USD, 20 October.   
2) The first example is the suggestion for a payment to every adult American 
if Republicans win the midterms. Furthermore, 2) became clear when Trump 
signalled willingness to strike a diesel deal favourable to Russia, under which 
Russia would release diesel to world markets to help ease US diesel prices 
ahead of the midterms. So far, the decline in US spot and retail diesel prices 
has been limited, with most of it attributable to seasonal patterns. The 
probability that Trump would impose a problematic diesel export is now only 
at 5% at Kalshi, which we have flagged several times. 
Trump could try to interfere directly with voting on Election Day. Kalshi 
assigns a 13% probability to Trump declaring a national emergency before 
Election Day and a 29% probability before the end of 2026. However, such a 
declaration should not give the president power over elections, as these are 
managed at state level.  
An even more severe measure would be for Trump to invoke the Insurrection 
Act or declare martial law. The former could be used to deploy the military at 
polling stations, which Kalshi assigns a 17% probability before the end of 
2026. Martial law would mean military authority replacing civilian 
government and would be even more extreme. A low volume Kalshi markets 
estimates 24% probability of this happening in 2026. Trump should not legally 
be able to use these powers to take control of elections, but he could still try.
