MENSCH UND MASCHINE  (MUMG.DE) - Risk to FY26 guidance – downgrade to Hold; Buy to Hold, Tgt EUR45 to 
EUR38. Last Close EUR33.75. 
Nicolas Herms: MuM delivered a soft Q3/26, with gross profit down 6% y/y as solid growth in Software was more than offset 
by a 22% decline in Digitization. The shortfall was again driven by the Autodesk business, mainly reflecting a smaller cohort 
of 3-year renewals and pressure on renewal commissions under the new Autodesk compensation model. Unlike in previous 
quarters, weaker top-line growth also weighed on profitability, with Q3 EBIT down 16% and EPS 18%, as higher D&A more 
than offset cost-saving measures (Q3 OpEx: -5%). Despite the weak quarter, management remains confident that MuM can 
achieve the lower half of its 11–19% EBIT and EPS growth guidance, supported by a strong Q4 recovery. We are more 
cautious and see downside risk to the guidance, as a meaningful share of the Autodesk business remains dependent on 
(now lower-margin) renewals, and we expect the higher-margin new-business contribution to take longer to offset pressure 
on renewal commissions, weighing on FY26 growth.

BANKS MONITOR  - Risk weights & the headline risk 
Benjamin Goy: Last week, SEB and Nordea reported higher risk weights in their corporate portfolios due to regulatory 
reviews within 24 hours of each other. While both banks are at the lower end of the range for corporate risk weights, we 
believe identifying the next potential regulatory impact is considerably more complex than a headline comparison suggests. 
Even when examining corporate models and probability-of default buckets more closely, there are valid reasons for 
differences in risk weights, including business model, portfolio and regional mix, collateral, seniority, duration and historical 
loss experience. Hence, our base case is that SEB and Nordea— supervised by different regulators—do not mark the start 
of a broader trend towards higher risk weights for European banks. By contrast, we expect a select number of banks to 

benefit from model updates in 3Q26 and 4Q26, and we broadly expect regulatory stability in Europe, similar to prior years, 
when the aggregate risk weight across our coverage universe was essentially unchanged.

ESSENTIAL PROPERTIES REALTY TRUST  (EPRT.N) - Downgrading To Hold: Risk/Reward Looks Balanced 
Heading Into 2027; Buy to Hold. Tgt USD38 to USD28. Last Close USD25.76. 
Omotayo Okusanya: We are downgrading EPRT to Hold from Buy. EPRT historically leveraged an attractive cost of capital 
to generate industry-leading earnings growth through acquisitions, despite underwriting tenants further out on the credit 
spectrum, but we believe that growth advantage is moderating against a rising interest rate backdrop. While we continue to 
expect external growth to be accretive, recent weakness in the shares has narrowed EPRT’s cost of capital advantage and 
reduced the earnings benefit from incremental investments, leaving the company’s growth outlook less differentiated at a 
time when tenant-credit concerns are becoming a greater investor focus. Compounding these concerns, refinancing will 
become a meaningful headwind after being largely absent in recent years. Given the combination of lower acquisition 
accretion, the emergence of refinancing headwinds, and rising tenant credit concerns, we view risk/reward as balanced and 
downgrade EPRT to Hold.
