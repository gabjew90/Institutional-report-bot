The Conference Board Leading Economic 
Index Was Flat in September 
Headlines 
 
The Conference Board Leading Economic Index (LEI) was unchanged in 
September. 
 
 
The LEI has declined year-on-year for 51 consecutive months.  
 
 
The improvement in the LEI’s six-month growth rate in September points 
to continued near-term growth.  
 
 
PNC expects the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) to hike the fed 
funds rate by 25 basis points in December and another 25 basis points in 
March 2027, taking it to a range of 4.25% to 4.50%.  
 
Details
The Conference Board Leading Economic Index 
(LEI) was unchanged in September at 99.6 (2016=100) 
after slipping 0.1% in August (Figure 1). That was 
slightly above the consensus forecast of -0.1%. The 
LEI’s six-month growth rate improved to 0.6% 
between March and September from -1.1% over the 
previous six months. 
 
The year earlier growth rate improved for the ninth 
straight month to -0.5%, the smallest decline since 
July 2022. Nonetheless, the LEI has declined annually 
for 51 consecutive months (Figure 2).  
 
Half of the 10 components increased, led by the 
Interest Rate Spread Between the 10-Year Bond and 
the Fed Funds Rate (+0.14%), Average Weekly Hours 
Manufacturing (+0.12%), the Leading Credit Index 
(+0.08%) and Average Weekly Initial Jobless Claims 
(+0.07%). The only notable decrease was in Average 
Consumer Expectations for Business Conditions (-
0.29%) with three other components falling slightly.  
 
The Conference Board Coincident Economic Index 
(CEI) rose 0.3% in September on the heels of a 0.1% 
increase in August. The CEI’s six-month growth rate 
improved to 0.9%, well above the 0.2% increase over 
the prior six months. The CEI’s four components - 
payroll employment, personal income less transfer 
payments, manufacturing and trade sales and 
industrial production - are among the data used to 
determine U.S. recessions. All four components 
made positive contributions in September. 
 
The 
Conference 
Board 
Lagging 
Economic 
Index (LAG) increased 0.5%, up from 0.2% in August. 
The LAG’s six-month growth rate improved to 1.0% 
over the last six months through September, twice 
the 0.5% increase over the previous six months.

The improvement in the LEI’s six-month growth rate 
in September is consistent with continued near-
term growth. This aligns with PNC’s outlook, which 
sees growth being supported by continued AI-
related spending and resilient consumer spending. 
PNC’s baseline forecast has the FOMC lifting the fed 
funds rate by 25 basis points in December and by 
another 25 basis points in March 2027 with inflation 
still well above the Fed’s 2% objective.
