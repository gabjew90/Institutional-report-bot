CIO View: Emerging market equities
Suresh Tantia, Head CIO Asia Equity Strategy, UBS AG Singapore Branch
Clement Chen, Head CIO Equities APAC
Alejo Czerwonko, Chief Investment Officer Emerging Markets, UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBS FS)
Xingchen Yu, Emerging Markets Strategist, CIO Americas, UBS Financial Services Inc. (UBS FS)
• We rate EM equities as Attractive, thanks to their resilient earnings, 
attractive valuations, and continued AI investment and improving 
monetization trends. Our June 2027 target for the MSCI EM index 
is 1,920.
• We remain Neutral on China H-shares and continue to prefer China 
A-shares, which retain an Attractive rating. We downgrade Indonesia 
from Neutral to Unattractive and Malaysian equities from Attractive 
to Neutral.
• Latin America, including Brazil, offers exposure to commodities, 
infrastructure, and domestic cyclicals, which, in our view, not 
only help diversify portfolios beyond technology-heavy markets but 
should add performance to portfolios amid the region's ongoing 
technological and geopolitical transformation. In the near term, 
Brazil’s presidential election in October is a key signpost for the 
country’s economic and reform outlook.
• Absolute view: Attractive; Relative preference (in comparison to other 
equity regions): Preferred
Asian and emerging market equities traded within a relatively narrow 
range over the past month as concerns over higher oil prices, elevated 
bond yields, and a stronger US dollar weighed on investor sentiment. 
Despite these macroeconomic headwinds, the artificial intelligence (AI) 
theme remains a key driver across the region and continues to provide 
strong fundamental support.
Looking ahead, market volatility will likely persist through the fourth 
quarter as investors adjust to a higher-for-longer interest rate environment, 
rising bond yields, and ongoing geopolitical uncertainty. However, we 
believe much of this pessimism is already reflected in valuations. The MSCI 
Asia ex-Japan and MSCI Emerging Markets indices are currently trading at 
12-month forward price-to-earnings ratios of 8.9x and 8.8x, respectively, 
their lowest since 2011. At the same time, AI monetization trends remain 
firmly intact, and we expect Asia to deliver 20% earnings growth in 2027, 
the strongest among major regions globally. This earnings outlook should 
provide further support for regional equities. We therefore maintain our 
Attractive view on Asian and Emerging markets and continue to expect 
mid-teen upside through June 2027.

Taiwan outperformed Asia ex-Japan by more than 4% in September, 
primarily driven by improved earnings visibility across its technology sector. 
Investor confidence was reinforced by technology roadmaps presented at 
the SEMICON industry event in early September, highlighting multi-year 
growth opportunities in AI chip manufacturing and advanced packaging. 
Taiwan’s semiconductor exports rose by approximately 60% year over 
year, reflecting robust external demand, while TSMC reported monthly 
sales growth of 53% year over year, underscoring continued momentum 
in AI and high-performance computing. These developments supported 
Taiwan’s strong relative performance versus the broader region. In our 
view, Taiwan’s comprehensive semiconductor and hardware ecosystem 
positions the market to be more resilient than many Asia Pacific peers in an 
environment of higher rates and bond yields, allowing for healthier sector 
rotation and potentially sustaining its upward market trajectory.
Within ASEAN, we downgrade Indonesian equities from Neutral to 
Unattractive and Malaysian equities from Attractive to Neutral. While 
Malaysia continues to benefit from resilient economic growth, ongoing 
data center investment and improving corporate governance initiatives 
under the MY Value Up program, we believe further upside is becoming 
increasingly constrained. First, political uncertainty is likely to rise ahead 
of the next general election. Second, persistent foreign investor outflows 
and historically low foreign ownership levels continue to reflect concerns 
around policy consistency. Third, the global liquidity backdrop remains 
challenging as the Federal Reserve maintains a restrictive stance and 
interest rates stay elevated. Taken together, these factors are likely to keep 
Malaysian equities range-bound over the next 6 to 12 months and limit the 
potential for a meaningful valuation re-rating. As a result, we see a more 
balanced risk-reward profile and move our rating to Neutral.
In Latam, Brazilian equities have rallied over the past month, supported 
by the first round of the presidential election, which strongly favored 
right-wing candidate Flavio Bolsonaro. We believe that greater clarity on 
the country’s medium-term economic outlook, along with a clear path 
toward fiscal consolidation following the 25 October run-off election, 
could further improve investor sentiment and support equity valuations 
over the medium term. More broadly, Latin America provides exposure 
to commodities, infrastructure, and domestic cyclical sectors, offering 
diversification benefits relative to North Asia’s technology-heavy markets.

Upside scenario
MSCI EM June 2027 target: 2,050
Supportive global policy and growth backdrop: Further central bank 
easing would boost global liquidity, risk appetite, and EM financing 
conditions, especially if global growth remains resilient or moderates in an 
orderly manner.
Geopolitical tensions and energy supply disruptions ease: Swift 
resolution to energy supply issues as well as reduced geopolitical trade 
tensions would ease inflation expectations, support global growth, and 
boost sentiment toward risk assets.
Significant targeted policy support in China: Large, targeted fiscal 
measures and clear policy implementation, focused on consumption and 
property stability, would reinforce confidence in China’s growth and 
earnings outlook.
Stronger earnings, cyclical and AI monetization trends: Improved 
margins, continued demand for memory, clearer progress on AI 
monetization among leading Chinese companies, and EPS revisions 
across sectors would support EM. A cyclical global upturn and firmer 
commodity prices could strengthen earnings resilience across EM, notably 
in manufacturing sectors as well as EMEA and LatAm equities.
Renewed foreign investor inflows: Renewed foreign inflows into 
broader and key EM regions would further amplify the upside and narrow 
the valuation gap relative to US equities.
Downside scenario
MSCI EM June 2027 target: 1,300
Global economic weakness and tighter financial conditions: A 
weaker global economy, labor market weakness, or setback in corporate 
capex plans could dampen risk appetite and weigh on EM equities. Tighter 
financing conditions or a persistently stronger dollar could soften flows and 
sentiment across EM.
Escalating geopolitical tensions and persistent energy supply 
disruptions: Further tariffs, export controls, or policy uncertainty could 
disrupt trade and amplify volatility. Prolonged energy supply shocks would 
keep inflation high, hurt global growth, and weigh on energy-importing 
EMs.
Decelerating tech capex and delayed AI monetization: Weaker tech 
investment or demand could disrupt EM supply chains and soften earnings, 
especially in South Korea and Taiwan. Limited progress in AI monetization 
would add uncertainty for EM tech leaders.
Limited mainland China policy support: Weak or narrowly targeted 
fiscal support could undermine confidence in mainland China’s growth, 
leading to equity derating and outflows.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the 
collective judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total 
returns on liquid and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
