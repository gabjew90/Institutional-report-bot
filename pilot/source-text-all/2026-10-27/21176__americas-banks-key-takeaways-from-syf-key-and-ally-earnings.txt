SYF: Guide points to slight 4Q downside; double digit EPS growth framework for 27 
remains intact 
SYF reported mixed 3Q26 results as a bottom-line EPS beat was driven by a credit 
beat (lower NCOs, lower ACL %) which made up for the PPNR softness as higher 
expenses (higher op losses) was partially offset by higher revenue (higher NII and 
lower fees). The focus was on SYF’s updated 2026 outlook, where SYF moved its EPS 
expectations to $9.50 (from $9.25-$9.50) which we think largely reflects the EPS 
beat in 3Q, and implies $1.97 of EPS in 4Q (Visible Alpha Consensus Data of $2.14) 
with the downside driven by higher reserve build than the street is currently 
assuming. So while the guide pointed to EPS downside, it could end up being 
conservative given continued improvement in credit.  
Shares underperformed (-1.2% vs. +0.6% card peers) as the market digested the 
results and mixed guide. Looking ahead, despite the mixed guide we are somewhat 
upbeat on the outlook, although the market may wait for greater clarity on the path 
of 2027 EPS growth as well as SYF reaching its MSD loan growth guide before getting 
more upbeat. In particular: 1) purchase volume growth was solid, up +8% YoY in 3Q 
(relatively stable with 2Q) where it noted stable to improving spend volume across 
credit cohorts (non-prime +12% YoY; super-prime +7%; prime +7%), and while loan 
growth is not following at the same magnitude given an elevated payment rate 
(+60bps YoY to 16.9%), it noted a) 35bps of the YoY increase is from new portfolios, 
which should decline as seasoning occurs, b) as credit normalizes (higher NCOs and 
higher promo balances), it expects both of these should be a tailwind to the payment 
rate and thus loan growth, 2) while SYF’s updated guide points to $1.97 of EPS in 4Q, 
we think there could be some conservatism as it assumes a slower than seasonal 
decline in the ALL (normal seasonality is – 25bps QoQ; expect significantly less than 
that), which along with the continued outperformance of DQs (+10bp MoM in Sept. 
vs. +20bps MoM expected) points to strong credit performance into 2027.  While 
losses may begin to rise in 2027, it will likely be met by other tailwinds on the PnL

KEY: Still sees path to 3.25% NIM (w/ 70% mechanical); confident deposit growth 
should outpace loan growth 
KEY reported solid 3Q26 results as core PPNR was in-line with street expectations 
(Visible Alpha Consensus Data), with higher NII offset by a touch softer fees and a tiny bit 
higher expenses, while EPS came in ahead on improved credit costs (lower NCOs and a 
reserve release). The focus was on its updated 4Q26 outlook and commentary around 
2027 targets, where KEY pointed to higher NII on a larger balance sheet despite moving 
its exit NIM outlook to ~3.00% (from 3.00%-3.05%; street at 3.01%), with average 
earning asset growth of +$1.5-$2.0bn in 4Q (+50bps vs. street) which along with 
unchanged expense pointed to ~3% PPNR upside. In terms of the medium-term outlook, 
KEY reiterated its 3.25%+ 4Q27 target, with ~70% of the expansion driven by 
‘mechanical’ fixed-rate asset repricing and ~30% coming from business performance 
(strong client deposit growth and potential paydown of wholesale funding other levers) 
and noted it continues to expect to reach a ~15%+ ROTCE by 4Q27 (though it was ~15% 
this quarter as well). Lastly, it sounded upbeat on capital markets into 2027 as despite 
higher rates potentially weighing on CRE cap markets, it sounded like the outlook for 
M&A is constructive.

the upbeat 4Q guide, as well as the reiterated medium-term outlook. Coming into the

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
