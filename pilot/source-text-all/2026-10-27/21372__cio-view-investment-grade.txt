CIO View: Investment grade
Antoine Geiller, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
Frederick Mellors, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
Carolina Corvalan, CFA, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
• We hold an Attractive view on investment grade bonds. We find the 
outright level of yields appealing (5.7% in USD and 4.2% in EUR).
• Our core preference remains for short- to medium-duration 
investment grade bonds, where we continue to see the most 
attractive balance between yield and interest rate risk. However, the 
rise in long-dated government bond yields has created opportunities 
to selectively extend duration through high-quality investment grade 
issuers.
• Investor demand for high-quality credit should remain strong in 
light of elevated yield levels and solid investment grade corporate 
fundamentals.
• Absolute view: Attractive; Relative preference (in comparison to other 
fixed income segments): Neutral
Central scenario
Global bond yields rose to new highs in September, driven primarily by 
expectations of higher short-term interest rates. The increase in yields 
this year has largely reflected a reassessment of policy rate expectations 
following the onset of the US-Iran conflict. At the same time, considerable 
uncertainty remains around energy prices and the extent of future 
policy tightening. Against this backdrop, higher funding costs and capital 
expenditure concerns weighed on AI-related credit, contributing to wider 
spreads across the broader credit universe. Investment-grade credit has 
proven more resilient, with index spreads widening by only 10bps over 
the past month, compared with a 50bps widening in US high-yield bonds 
over the same period. Despite the recent volatility, investment-grade bond 
yields remain attractive at 5.7% for US IG (1-10 years) and 4.2% for EUR 
IG, representing increases of roughly 100bps since the start of the year. 
In our view, investors should focus on these elevated yield levels, as the 
higher income available can support positive total returns for the asset 
class. Given the sharp rise in yields across the curve, we see a tactical 
opportunity to selectively extend duration. Medium- to long-duration 
bonds issued by high-quality investment-grade companies offer attractive 
carry opportunities across intermediate maturities while providing the 
potential to benefit from any future decline in yields.
Both headline and core September CPI and PPI came in above expectations. 
The increase in headline inflation was driven in part by higher energy prices, 
while the upside surprise in core CPI was broad-based across categories.

Despite the stronger-than-expected inflation data, recent comments from 
Federal Reserve officials suggest little urgency to raise rates further, 
reducing pressure on markets to price in an October hike. In our view, 
markets remain overly hawkish, pricing in around 75-80bps of additional 
tightening across the US and Europe over the next 12 months. While 
we expect a further 50bps of US tightening by 1Q27, we do not see 
this as the start of a new tightening cycle similar to 2022. Importantly, 
higher policy rates are not, in isolation, a credit-negative development. 
For credit markets, the growth outlook remains the key determinant of 
performance. As long as economic activity remains broadly resilient, and 
financing conditions stay supportive, we believe credit spreads should 
remain well anchored despite a modestly higher rate environment.
On the demand side, investor appetite for high-quality bonds has remained 
generally robust this year in the context of appealing yields, resilient 
growth, solid earnings, as evidenced by continued solid fund inflows. 
Strong demand has helped absorb elevated bond supply. A large portion of 
the increased supply came from US hyperscalers funding AI-related capex, 
a trend that is likely to continue in the year ahead given ongoing elevated 
capital spending needs.
Our view has been that a sustained elevated pace of bond supply from 
hyperscalers could lead to some supply indigestion and moderately wider 
spreads, which we have seen since the start of the year. Hyperscaler 
bonds currently represent about 4% of the US IG Intermediate index. 
Large tech firms have increasingly used debt rather than operating cash 
flow to fund capex, leading to wider credit spreads that imply multiple-
notch downgrades. Many projects are financed off-balance sheet, and 
because they are not yet operational, future lease obligations are not 
fully reflected in reported liabilities. While credit spreads partly price in 
these commitments, limited transparency, and long project timelines may 
mean the market is demanding an excessive risk premium. However, from 
a fundamental perspective, we note that hyperscalers have considerable 
debt capacity under their current ratings, meaning they can increase 
debt levels without compromising their current credit ratings. Over the 
medium term, we expect current elevated capex levels to boost operating 
free cash flow as investments in AI infrastructure are monetized over 
time, particularly as AI adoption increases. With earnings season now 
underway, cloud revenue growth, management guidance on future capex, 
and commentary on its financial impact will be key in shaping expectations 
for 2027 and beyond.
In aggregate, fundamentals remain solid. Based on 2Q26 earnings 
(according to data from BofA), median y/y EBITDA growth accelerated to 
9.1% in 2Q, the highest level since 2021, while net debt growth was muted 
at -0.2% y/y. Median net leverage for US IG issuers (excluding financials 
and utilities) fell to 1.97x from 2.05x in 1Q. Over the last three months, 
upgrades have continued to outpace downgrades on average.
In our base case, we believe continued solid investor demand amid 
good fundamentals and resilient growth should continue to underpin the 
market. All-in yields of over 5.0% for US IG and 4.0% for EUR IG remain 
elevated relative to historical levels based on data since 2010. In our view, 
these elevated yield levels should support positive total returns for high-
grade bonds. While spreads could temporarily overshoot our target levels, 
our base case remains one of a benign macroeconomic environment, with 
rates gradually declining and no material deterioration in growth. We 
therefore continue to favor short- to medium-maturity bonds, where risk-

Key risks to our view include a meaningful slowdown or an outright 
recession, though we believe spread widening would be offset to a 
significant extent by falling interest rates in this scenario. If interest rate 
volatility rises again, particularly if driven by concerns about reaccelerating 
inflation, this could eventually place renewed upward pressure on credit 
spreads. Finally, periodic episodes of supply indigestion, particularly driven 
by AI-related technology sector issuance, continue to represent a risk factor 
that could lead to modestly wider spreads.

Goldilocks
A swift resolution to the conflict in the Middle East would allow oil prices 
to stabilize below USD 90/bbl. Disinflation resumes in the US once the 
tariff and energy impacts have been passed through. Investment spending 
and AI adoption exceed expectations. Healthy consumption, buoyant 
investment, and strong productivity growth allow the US economy to grow 
by 2.5-3% in 2026 and 2027. Residual negative tariff effects are offset by 
business-friendly policies in the US, Europe, and China.
Downside scenario
Yield Pressure: Bloomberg Barclays US Int. Corp. June 2027 target: 
90bps, Bloomberg Barclays Euro-Agg. Corp. June 2027 target: 
100bps
Oil prices remain under upward pressure due to prolonged disruptions of 
flows through the Strait of Hormuz or widespread destruction of energy 
infrastructure in the Middle East. Business investment and hiring remain 
strong in a concentrated number of economic sectors. Concerns rise about 
longer-term inflation and the credibility of monetary and fiscal policy. 
Longer-term bond yields drift higher. Central banks face pressure to raise 
interest rates despite pockets of weakness in the economy. Labor market 
softness leads to further wage growth slowing.
AI bear market: Bloomberg Barclays US Int. Corp. June 2027 target: 
180bps, Bloomberg Barclays Euro-Agg. Corp. June 2027 target: 
190bps
AI stocks deflate due to growing concerns over low returns on investment 
and unsustainable commitments by large AI companies. Worsening 
investor sentiment leads to a broader market decline. The downward trend 
in equity markets feeds through to the real economy via the sentiment 
and wealth channels. Declining investment in AI infrastructure allows for a 
redistribution of resources into other segments of the economy, allowing 
US GDP to grow by 0.5-1% in 2026 and 2027. Inflation concerns give way 
to fears of a global economic decline, allowing central banks to cut rates 
aggressively in 2027.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the 
collective judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total 
returns on liquid and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
