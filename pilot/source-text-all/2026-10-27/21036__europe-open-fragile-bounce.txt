Europe Open - Fragile bounce
By Varshi Karamsetty
The Europe Open will now be published from London and is abbreviated.
Strong Q3 earnings from SpaceX and Super Micro Computer is providing support to the AI/tech sector overnight, with
Kospi and Nikkei gaining though China stocks still trading under pressure. Nasdaq futures up 0.6% but conviction may
stall here as more megacap tech report after the close tonight (Meta, IBM, Texas Instruments). AU assets in focus after
RBA Hauser warned that the inflation outlook was "materially worse" than Aug forecasts - Nov hike OIS odds are now
50/50 with CPI next week the final metric. FX pairs trade rangebound, oil prices rallied overnight. Iran Foreign Minister
Araghchi said that talks were continuing, and that the US simply needed to agree with conditions set out in June, pushing
back against Tuesday Qatari media reports that a deal had already been reached.
Limited catalysts ahead for the US, but plenty of EM events - Indo rate decision due where our economists expect a
50bp pre-emptive hike though market consensus sees a smaller hike. Israel BoI expected to hold rates. South Africa
watches CPI ahead of the MTBPS. The latter should indicate a smaller deficit vs Feb. Watch out for Mexico retail sales,
Colombia trade balance and Argentina economic activity. South Korea 20d exports surged once again, printing at a
whopping 105.4% YoY.

In Focus‌
Rates: AU front-end sold off after RBA Hauser said that Australia's inflation outlook looks "materially worse" than what the
bank had predicted in Aug forecasts, clearly hawkish versus his colleague Kent earlier this week. Nov OIS odds jump to
+12.9bp post comments, final expectations will rely on CPI next week. USTs drift slightly weaker, 10s trading at 5.125% at
print. No data ahead, and Fed remain on blackout, so moves will follow risk sentiment. As we go to print, UK CPI comes in
line with expectations, with services tad stronger than Aug, coming at 3.7% YoY and core also higher at 2.8%. Put together
with a strong labor market report, a Nov 25bp hike remains base case. BoE Bailey speaks later today at 14:00. In France,
Finmin Lescure said that they have instruments to pass the budget without a vote, citing constitutional levers. 

Equities: Strong Q3 earnings from SpaceX and Super Micro Computer (up 16% in post market trading) revived AI sentiment
and boosted US equity futures higher overnight. Asian stocks still trade mixed, with Nikkei and Kospi recovering but China
stocks in the red. Meta, IBM and Texas Instruments report after the US cash close tonight as investors remain concerned
about funding the AI buildout. Elsewhere, we also flag EU CETS | Earnings - Positioning Problems. Our strategist highlights
that 22% of the EU market cap reports this week (12% on Thu), with largest moves expected in semis, software, defensive
and expensive stocks. So far, spot reactions have been negatively correlated with positioning. Crowding on long books
capping upside convexity, while crowded short books dampening moves lower on poor results + prompting squeezes on
good/passable reports. 
FX: Tech FX top the currency list, with TWD leading gains against USD overnight. G10 pairs trade in tight ranges - implied
vols are now pricing out risk premiums and returning to the lower end of recent ranges, our options desk writes. AUD in focus
following Hauser comments, we currently see a 30bp jump risk on CPI next Wed. We like owning event weight and preferably
in AUD crosses, e.g. EURAUD and AUDCHF. Overall, volumes in the Asia session were 17% higher than 30d averages. 
Commodities: Brent rally picking up overnight, returning to the $110 handle. Late Tue, Qatari newspaper Al-Araby Al-Jadeed
reported that Qatar and Pakistan have presented a mediation proposal that addresses the demands of the US and Iran. The
proposal received American approval as well as Iran's consent. Overnight, Iran Foreign Minister Araghchi told Iraqi media that
"they (US) simply need to implement the conditions that were previously set out and to which they committed", and says that
talks were continuing. ICYMI, our sales desk think further risk premium needs to be added to the curve and like buying early
expiry Jan27 Brent 110/120 Call Spread (expires 30 Oct, immediately following the Israeli election). Interesting note here on a
Cheap Way To Position For The Oil Trade After The Trade, where buying downside protection now expecting a later reversal
looks appropriate.
Elsewhere‌
EUR: Eurozone ECB's Cipollone speaks at 09:00 BST; ECB's Lagarde speaks in Wiesbaden at 17:00 BST. Recent Reuters
reports suggest that Lagarde could leave the ECB to join the WEF as early as Nov. She has previously stated intention to
leave before her term expires in Dec 2027. Watch for further hints. In the background, watch France Retail Sales ex-Auto
MoM at 07:45 BST.
NZD: CPI YoY at 22:45 BST for Q3. Lower fuel prices should help drive a softer print at 0.8% QoQ/3.9% YoY vs 1.5%/4.1%
prior (Citi e.). This is in line with RBNZ forecasts so shouldn’t surprise markets. Upside risks remain for a higher terminal OCR
in the near future.
 
Don't miss in EM:
IDR: BI-Rate Decision at 08:20 BST. The possibility of an equity downgrade has prompted our economists to change their
call, now expecting a 50bp hike versus previous expectations of a pause. The purpose would be pre-emptive, to prevent the
expected equity foreign outflows from widening to the fixed income side and be exacerbated by resident capital outflows. Dig
deeper.
ZAR: MTBPS is the main event - Citi Economics preview here. Treasury's cautious track record suggests it will continue to
present cautious revenue projections, leaving the MTBPS budget deficit smaller, at around 3.5% of GDP vs 3.7% in the Feb-
2026 Budget. Attention is also on the shape of the fiscal anchor i.e. whether it is a legislated primary surplus or a debt ceiling,
or a principles based anchor. Flatteners have been a popular trade. Before that, Sep CPI due at 09:00 ET. Our economists
see fuel price hikes increasing headline CPI to 4.9% from 4.4% previously, with core unchanged at 4.1%. Food and fuel are
the largest drivers of the monthly inflation rise.
ILS: Base Rate Decision at 14:00 BST. Our economists expect a hold at 3.25%. Updated forecasts, and commentary around
them might be most interesting. For now, we maintain our forecast for one more rate cut, with risks around our 3% terminal
rate assumption balanced. Full Econ preview, our trading desk also review expected outcomes here. 2s are expected to move
2bp higher should the BoI hold (75% chance), they place a 24% probability for a 25bp cut which should move 2s down 10bp.
Ahead of that, Citi Research is hosting a call at 13:00 BST - Israel Elections Outlook with Ilan Jonas.

MXN: Retail Sales YoY at 13:00 BST for August. August retail sales are likely to remain low after misses in consecutive
months.
COP: Trade Balance at 16:00 BST for August. July trade balance stood at nearly -2.8bn, the widest in recent months. With no
consensus for August, the deficit is likely to remain wide reflecting softer crude exports.
ARS: Economic Activity Index MoM at 20:00 BST for August. A pivotal print in confirming whether the recent slowdown
solidifies into an economic contraction, as structural asymmetries persist between resilient , capital intensive sectors and
stagnant labor, intensive industries weighing on domestic demand. Last month’s print came at -1.4% YoY.
KRW: 20d export data surged to a whopping 105.4% YoY vs 78% in 10d data, with imports also rising towards 42.8% YoY. A
record trade surplus in Sep saw Citi Economics revise their long term USDKRW forecasts to 1250 (previously 1400) given
structurally large current account surpluses.
