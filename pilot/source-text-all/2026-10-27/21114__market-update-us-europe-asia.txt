U.S. Markets Recap
US +0.28% | Nasdaq -0.02% | Dow +1.02%
Synopsis

US equities were mostly higher in Tuesday trading, with the Russell 2000 up 1.65% and the equal-weight S&P among the notable gainers.
Outperformers included banks, private equity, insurers, payments, investment banks, software (on M&A), pharma/biotech, med tech,
managed care, airlines, trucking, building products, quick-service restaurants, travel and tourism, homebuilders, staples, telecom and
media. AI plays finished modestly lower though off their worst levels, and Big Tech was also mostly lower; energy, engineering and con-
struction, multi-industrials and off-price retail lagged. Treasuries were mixed with some curve flattening, with yields down 5-6 bp at the
long end. The dollar index fell 0.3%, gold finished down 0.4%, silver rose 1.3% and bitcoin futures gained 1%. WTI crude (Nov) finished
down 1.8%, near its worst levels, amid US-Iran diplomacy chatter; earlier, Brent breached $110/bl in Asian trading for the first time since
May. The AI trade stabilised after an earlier pullback, and the market took little from the scrutiny of Anthropic's S-1, which media reports
put at a net loss of about $80B in 1H. Breadth was strong, with a mix of defensives and select cyclicals doing well, and attention on the
lower long-end rate backdrop and positioning. Investors looked ahead to hyperscaler and semiconductor earnings and Nvidia's GTC Berlin.

On earnings, GE beat and raised with commercial engines margins and orders a bright spot, though valuation drew focus; RTX beat and
raised; 3M's organic growth was light but the stock was helped by an expanded commercial deal with Amazon; GM was weaker after in-

Salesforce agreed to acquire Braze in a $5.6B+ deal, and Solstice was boosted by activist headlines. On data, ADP weekly private payrolls
cooled to a four-week average of 18.5K from 24.75K, September housing starts missed, building permits beat, and September pending
home sales hit a record low, down 7.3% m/m against a +0.1% consensus.
Macro

AI funding and OpenAI's raise: Reuters reported that OpenAI is working to secure about $30B in new investor commitments at a valuation
of more than $1.4T, on a rapid timetable aimed at closing the round before Anthropic's expected mid-November IPO. The article says the
speed reflects a desire to get ahead of Anthropic, since the two firms tap a similar investor base, and a wish to fund infrastructure com-
mitments and bolster the balance sheet in case a weaker IPO market delays its listing. OpenAI confidentially filed its prospectus in June,
and its CFO said in August it was targeting a 2027 IPO. Separately, Amazon is reported to be buying Nvidia AI hardware faster than it can
deploy it amid power constraints, OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio, and a Citadel strategist expects investors to pile back into the AI trade
after the midterms.

Oil volatile on conflicting US-Iran reports: Crude initially rallied before reversing sharply lower around midday on an unconfirmed Al-Araby
report that Qatar and Pakistan had brokered a mediation proposal addressing key US and Iranian demands, then recovered somewhat
after a subsequent report denied the claims. The alleged proposal included a partial release of frozen Iranian funds in exchange for IAEA
inspections of Iranian nuclear sites. Iran reportedly told Pakistan it has "nothing new to say" to the US, and Reuters reported Iran is
prolonging talks in stalemate to weaken President Trump ahead of the midterms. Iranian officials said the US will not earn Tehran's trust
until it agrees to the conditions Iran has laid out, the UK is to commit fighter jets to a defensive mission to help protect Saudi cities from
Houthi attacks, and a Trump envoy warned Gulf allies the US will no longer guarantee regional security. A US energy official said Wash-
ington is working with allies on diesel reserve releases and ways to raise refinery throughput.

Housing data weak: September pending home sales fell 7.3% m/m versus expectations of a 0.1% rise, the steepest drop since Dec-25,
pushing the index to a record low of 66.0, with all four regions lower, led by the West (-9.2%) and South (-8.2%). October NAHB homebuilder
sentiment had fallen to its lowest since Jun-2012 on high mortgage rates, construction costs and weak consumer confidence. September
housing starts fell 5.2% m/m to a 1.221M SAAR against a 1.300M consensus, down from a revised 1.288M in August and the third
straight monthly decline, and 7.4% lower y/y. Building permits came in at a 1.400M SAAR versus a 1.390M consensus, down 0.2% m/m
and 3.0% y/y. The calendar is empty on Wednesday apart from a $13B 20-year note sale; initial claims are the Thursday highlight, and
Friday brings October flash PMIs and final University of Michigan sentiment and inflation expectations.

Earnings themes and deals: Q3 earnings pointed to strong and durable data center and electrification demand, a resilient consumer
despite weak sentiment (KeyCorp, Synchrony, Hasbro and GM all described demand as stable or resilient), and cost inflation from com-
modities, fuel, logistics and memory. Pockets of soft discretionary demand and pressure on lower-end, rate-sensitive cohorts remain
under scrutiny. Northrop Grumman fit the national security capex theme and Halliburton the energy security theme. In deals, Salesforce
is to buy Braze for $5.6B in cash, Apollo's Legendary Entertainment revived talks to acquire Lionsgate Studios with a takeover offer, and
activist Jana built a stake in Casella Waste. Kroger plans a $5B investment to lower prices on staples, Qualcomm is cutting headcount in
a performance-based reduction, and Amazon plans to hire more than 300K holiday workers, up 20% from 2025.

EU Market Recap
Germany -0.06% | France -0.38% | UK -0.29%
Synopsis

European equity markets finished lower but off their worst levels, following a negative session on Monday. Miners, AI-linked industrials
and tech were the biggest decliners, while defensive sectors gained. UK Gilts outperformed, with 10-year yields down near 3 bp; German
Bund yields edged up slightly, while French, Spanish and Italian yields were broadly flat. Sterling saw strong gains. Scrutiny of the AI trade
drove risk-off price action again, with financing concerns centred on neoclouds, and CoreWeave bond yields stayed elevated. Reuters
sources said OpenAI is looking to speed up fundraising ahead of Anthropic's mid-November IPO, and a report that Nscale is in talks for
another $45B deal with Anthropic kept compute demand in focus. Elsewhere, Trump said he is open to meeting Xi and Putin in China for
nuclear talks and has not approved Patriot missiles for Ukraine after recent criticism of Zelensky, and France imposed curfews in parts of
Paris and other cities after civil unrest. On the macro data front, German ZEW sentiment pulled back after hitting a seven-month high in
September, UK jobless claims rose by more than expected but the number of payrolled workers unexpectedly increased and the unem-
ployment rate dipped, German producer prices accelerated in September, and the Swiss trade surplus narrowed sharply on a big jump in
imports. German Chancellor Merz talked up investment in AI and reform of the social welfare system, and highlighted that a rapid decision
can be made on senior ECB vacancies if needed, which could include a successor to president Lagarde. UK Chancellor Healey ruled out
a capital gains tax increase because of the damage to investment.
Macro

Defensives lead as miners, AI-linked industrials and tech fall: Basic Resources was the biggest laggard on weak corporate updates: Anglo
American reported lower copper production alongside higher diamond guidance, while BHP saw output plunge on operational disruptions.
Industrial Goods & Services also fell, with ABB slumping as AI volatility and costs overshadowed record Q3 data-center-fuelled orders of
$12.1B, up 40% organically, and Schneider Electric declining after raising €6.0B through a share placement to help finance its PTC
acquisition. Technology fell as the AI rebound faded; Barco slumped after forecasting FY sales below prior-year levels and an EBITDA
margin of ~11%, while OVHcloud traded higher after FY revenue of €1.15B was broadly in line and adjusted EBITDA of €480.5M beat
expectations, with healthy FY27 growth and free cash flow targets. Health Care was the strongest sector: Roche rose after Oddo upgraded
it to outperform from underperform, AstraZeneca gained after launching a global AI collaboration with Oracle on clinical-trial optimization,
and Saniona rose after submitting an FDA investigational new drug application. Food & Beverage, Utilities and Personal & Household
Goods were the other top gainers. EssilorLuxottica posted better-than-expected Q3 revenue helped by its core eyewear business. HSBC
luxury analysts concluded that a recovery in Chinese demand is no longer in sight after meetings in Hong Kong, Xiamen and Shanghai
produced "no timeline or evidence of recovery"; LVMH shares hit a six-year low after a fashion and leather-goods miss linked to weak
China demand and now trade at ~16x forward earnings, the lowest in a decade, and Barclays cut its medium-term sales growth
assumption for the group to 4% a year.

European AI headlines: Anthropic is pursuing a second multibillion-dollar Nscale deal to secure more UK computing capacity (FT). Nvidia
is backing four German AI startups as Chancellor Merz courts investment to revive Germany's economy, ABB shares fell nearly 6% as AI
investment concerns overshadowed record data center orders, and OVHcloud targets a 2027 AI launch, using capacity to onboard clients
without construction. Paris has surpassed London as Europe's leading AI hub in a new city ranking, a Google executive downplayed
European AI-dependence fears, and advisers warned the UK risks AI dependence without building domestic expertise and infrastructure.
The BoE is weighing an AI-turmoil stress test for UK financial resilience, residents in Devon oppose an AI data center over environmental
fears, and UK banks and businesses faced service disruptions following a Google Cloud outage.

German ZEW, UK labour data and the BoE: The German ZEW investor sentiment indicator fell in October to 28.1 versus consensus 32.0
after a seven-month high of 34.7 in September; the current situation indicator rose to -35.9 versus a forecast of -44.5 and prior -47.1,
while the Eurozone reading fell to 18.3 from 25.8. ZEW said the German economy is slowly emerging from stagnation but the recovery is
not self-sustaining, and that the outlook for private consumption deteriorated on higher energy prices. UK September claimant count
jumped 30.3K versus a consensus 19.4K rise, while payrolled workers unexpectedly rose 13K versus a forecast 8K fall, the ILO
unemployment rate was 4.7% versus consensus 4.9%, and average weekly earnings were 3.7% versus 3.8% forecast, with private sector
wages at 3.0%; vacancies fell 21K on the quarter. UK inflation is forecast to accelerate in Wednesday's September publication, with
consensus for headline CPI at 3.4% y/y versus 3.1% prior, core at 2.7% and services at 3.7%, on higher energy, food and rents, which
would raise expectations of a November BoE rate hike; the market prices up to four increases over the next year, while most sell-side
forecasts are for one to two. S&P Global's UK Consumer Sentiment Index fell to 41.3 from 42.7, its lowest since July 2023. The Swiss
trade surplus narrowed to CHF1.4B, the smallest since November 2022, versus consensus CHF4.3B, as imports jumped 24.9% m/m on
chemical and pharmaceutical purchases while exports rose 1.5%. The BoE looks set to tighten in November, while the odds of an October
ECB rate hike continue to ease amid little evidence of second-round effects.

UK Budget, French fiscal risk and Spanish election: A Bloomberg survey of Gilt dealers expects UK Gilt sales to rise by £3.4B this fiscal
year to a median of £249.6B versus the DMO's £246.2B. Chancellor Healey has ruled out a capital gains tax rise at the 28-Oct Budget,
with the main revenue raiser expected to be a tax on banks to fund a targeted, temporary cost of living package, and is reportedly prepared
to cut the fiscal buffer from ~£24B to ~£15B. Some large investors are positioning for a Gilt rally after the Budget, and UBS sees up to a
20 bp fall in the 10-year yield if it is well received. In France, the National Assembly postponed the vote on the revenue section of the
2027 state budget with more than 2,800 amendments pending; the Assembly is widely expected to reject the revenue section, leaving
options including Article 49.3, a special law or ordinances, and French bond yields hit a 24-year high as civil unrest complicates deficit
reduction. A Moody's ratings review this Friday is the next catalyst, with a cut to A1 and a stable outlook the base case. Spain will hold a

Macro

AI financing concerns persist: The AI trade is under pressure, with neoclouds at the centre of concerns about financing. CoreWeave bond
yields remained elevated, its CDS spreads held at distressed levels and the stock fell on Monday despite more bullish sell-side commen-
tary. Sentiment towards other neoclouds turned more cautious, with South Korea's Elice down more than 30% at one stage in its Kosdaq
debut after a retail tranche more than 400x oversubscribed and pricing at the top end of the marketed range. The weak debut follows


PBOC moves towards an overnight policy rate; China data: Local media published a readout of a speech by PBOC Governor Pan signalling
that the overnight reverse repo rate will replace the 7-day rate as the new policy benchmark, with the PBOC to study a migration at the
appropriate time. The overnight rate is currently 1.25% versus 1.4% for the 7-day, prompting debate over whether the shift amounts to
an effective rate cut. Pan said overnight repos (DR001) are the most heavily traded product in the interbank market, that policy guidance
will push loan pricing reform away from reliance on LPRs, and that eligibility for the Standing Lending Facility will be expanded alongside
a liquidity support tool for non-bank financial institutions. Overnight interbank rates fell below 1.3% in October, the lowest since May,
widening the gap against the 7-day rate. China held its 1Y and 5Y LPRs for a 17th straight month. Economists said the stronger-than-
expected, if marginal, Q3 GDP print and better September activity moved most 2026 estimates off the 4.5% lower bound of the govern-
ment's 4.5-5.0% target, though supply-side strength continues to diverge from weak domestic demand, capacity utilization is depressed,
and 2027 GDP forecasts of ~4% point to slowing growth.

and maintain a hawkish stance following consecutive rate hikes in Q3. September CPI eased to 2.9% from 3.1% but remains above the
2.0% target, with elevated oil prices and strong technology-led exports expected to keep price pressure in place over the medium term
despite subdued consumer activity. Expectations are for inflation to reach target by Q2 on subdued wage growth, won stabilization and
Seoul's steps to cap cost pass-through. House price increases and lending metrics improved after the summer hikes, giving the bank
time to pause, and economists say it is wary of a third hike in a matter of months given weak consumers and household debt burdens,
though a Fed rate hike before year-end would renew pressure to tighten once this year and possibly once more in Q1-27. The bank is also
aware of financial stability risks in an overheated housing market and in financial markets, where a continued equity correction could
affect the pace of tightening.

after the initial funding. The Japan Growth Strategy implementation plan, due towards year-end, is expected to reveal the five-year invest-
ment path. Separately, Nikkei reported that the BOJ sees a chance to shift to sustaining inflation as the 2% goal approaches.
