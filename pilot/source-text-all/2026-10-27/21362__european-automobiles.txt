Positioning and key trends
We recommend a more diversified exposure to companies with pricing power and
strong brand positioning, but also some companies with restructuring potential.
We also prefer companies that appear better positioned in light of the US-EU tariff
deal, which favors companies that already have a strong US production footprint
or are more exposed to Europe, while at the same time looking at some suppliers
that develop beyond the traditional automotive space.

The market focus keeps shifting between US tariffs, “Made in EU,” and Middle

profits settled at an annualized 12-15% level, which was also one of the reasons
for an overall weak 2026 financial guidance of various auto companies in the
beginning of the year.
The next big thing is the EU Industrial Acceleration Act (“Made in EU”), which
seem to gain speed as EU regulators notice the urgency to address competitveness
which could end-up determining a minimum value-added being generated in
Europe or face higher tariffs, e.g., for Chinese vehicles. Depending on the
outcome and the timing, it could limit the growth potential of imported Chinese
cars, with a positive impact on European car stocks. The agreed Chinese plug-in
hybrid vehicle sales quota in Europe is the first step, the potential relief in overly
tough CO2 2035 regulation is another relief. Last but not least, the still-ongoing
Middle East conflict is affecting the sector—to a lesser extent directly via lower
sales and the risk of supply interruptions, but indirectly via dented consumer
confidence and potentially lower consumer spending due to higher inflation and
economic concerns, but at the same time resulting in an overall boost to electrified
vehicles sales as consumers try to shop less “oil dependent” internal combustion
engine vehicles.
For the time being, we expect volatility and the valuation discount to stay elevated
amid higher commodity and component costs, while currency effects have
become less of a drag. Consensus top-line and earnings forecasts have been
continuously adjusted downward and are now at a realistic starting point. In our
view, there is now a fairer chance that the worst for the sector in terms of
earnings downgrades is behind us, as signaled by overall decent 2Q26 results.
How are we positioned? We have the highest conviction in Ferrari as a high-end-
wealth growth story, committed to maintaining its scarcity value rather than
focusing on unsustainable growth, and the brand enjoys second-to-none pricing
power, as evidenced once more by good 2Q26 results and a revenue guidance
upgrade. We are positive on premium manufacturer BMW and we thus
recommend to be overweight. We like Continental (Conti), as the company is
becoming a pure tire company, which should lead to a rerating of the stock. We
also like Michelin, in spite of some sobbering 3Q26 results, as tires are less
exposed to a weak Chinese end-market and growing Chinese competition in
Europe. In addition, we like Volkswagen (VW), whose surprising recent
restructuring announcements offer optionality. After years of absence, we recently
added Porsche AG (P911) after new management has seemed willing to address
the severe challenges, as well as Schaeffler—for their beyond-traditional
automotive supplier business opportunities.

Most Preferred

Why it is Most Preferred
Of the established automakers, we continue to believe that BMW has
one of the strongest positions in electrified vehicles. Its 2Q26 results
were in line with its recently reduced 1-3% EBIT margin range for this
year. We believe the stock is trading at reasonably attractive multiples
of below 6.5x 2027E P/E and around 0.4x P/B. The next capital
market day, with BMW’s new CEO, is scheduled for 30 September.
This could act as a catalyst to move the stock higher, and hence we
overweight the stock.
Company profile
BMW is a leading global premium car manufacturer. In 2025, it
delivered 2.5m cars under the BMW, Mini and Rolls-Royce brands.
BMW also runs a motorcycle business and financial services
operations. In China, it has a joint venture with Brilliance, in which it
has a 75% shareholding, which has been fully consolidated in the
group’s financial statements since February 2022. Some 40% of
2025 unit sales were in western Europe, 20% in the Americas, 35%
in Asia, and 3% in other regions. The Quandt family owns 49% of
BMW’s stock.
What drives our opinion
In the last few years, BMW has made significant progress on its
electrification strategy while ensuring that its internal combustion
engine technology—including its famous inline six—remains state-of-
the-art and is further developed to serve this part of the market as
well. However, changes in the auto industry and BMW’s strengths go
far beyond electrification. BMW’s highly anticipated “Neue Klasse”
iX3 SUV was presented in September 2025, and the sedan version
was just unveiled, proving the company is still one of the most
innovative in the higher-end premium car segment, in our view. These
models showcase BMW’s ability to successfully combine hardware
and software solutions that consumers appreciate, and BMW has a
strong product line-up, including its new 3-series and its highly
lucrative X5 SUV. With the new “Neue Klasse” key platform and
products being launched, R&D and capital expenditures should fall
from peak levels, which should support free cash flow generation.
Regarding US tariffs, we believe BMW’s strong US production
footprint and its ability to increase US production by around 10% in
the short run—and, if required, even more over time—should offer
some mitigation. In terms of end-markets, the US and Europe are
doing fine. However, China remains a highly challenging market for
Western brands, including BMW. While list price cuts were
implemented recently resulting in increasing showroom traffic,
BMW’s 75%-owned and fully consolidated China business is facing
severe headwinds, as proven by the recent profit warning, and the
overall low margins confirmed with 2Q26. On the positive side,
renewed share buybacks and a very solid balance sheet offer the
opportunity for ongoing cash returns to shareholders. Key risks
include any changes to the current EU-US tariff situation, which could
result in an inability to pass on higher costs to consumers. Other risks
include the inability to stabilize and right-size its important China
business and production capacity there; any supply constraints (e.g.,
access to rare earths); geopolitical events like the Middle East war
that limit its ability to produce and sell its high-margin models as well
as affecting consumer confidence; major new trade tariffs or other
trade restrictions; and outstanding diesel and emission-related
concerns and other lawsuits. Additional legal risks are related to
brake-failure product recalls. Furthermore, costly capacity
restructuring programs, adverse currency movements, rising
commodity and component costs, and erratic price cuts by key
competitors for electric vehicles or new competitors entering the
market would increase the risk of additional pressure on BMW’s
profitability.

Why it is Most Preferred
Management announced the final disposal of ContiTech in July to
occur before the end of 2026. Although market expectations for the
deal value were slightly higher, we appreciate that the company has
gone ahead. Reported 2Q26 results came in above consensus
expectations, with full focus on the tire segment. We believe the risk-
reward looks compelling for a stock with strong exposure to the
lucrative replacement market and trading at a 2027E P/E of around
10x—i.e., below its tire sector peers—and we see room for a rerating.
While Conti remains one of our Top Picks in the Automobile sector,
with a lot of news out, we reduce our overweight. We leave it
unchanged as part of the well-diversified Germany EPL.
Company profile
Continental AG (Conti) is a leading premium tyre manufacturer. The
company generates sales of some EUR20bn and employs around
78,000 people in 54 countries.
What drives our opinion
Continental (Conti) is transforming itself. With the spin-off of its auto
supplier business into a new entity called Aumovio in 2025, and the
announced disposal of ContiTech by year-end 2026 with at least
partial redistribution of the proceeds to shareholders, we believe the
investment case for Conti has changed significantly. Conti will end up
as a pure tire manufacturer, and we believe this move will be
rewarded, given that the tire business is highly successful. In our view,
this should lead to a rerating versus its (European) tire peers. Tires rely
on the substantially more lucrative replacement market, which enjoys
decent pricing power. The recently announced European tariffs on
imported Chinese tires are supportive for established European tire
companies as well. The structural trend toward wider and larger-
dimension tires and more high-performance tires is a key positive
driver for the company. Tire production is a scale business, and Conti
is heavily engaged in ongoing restructuring efforts to support
profitability and free cash flow generation. In summary, we like
Conti’s story and recommend it for investors interested in the
automotive industry. Key risks to our positive view on Conti include
failure to complete the ContiTech sale at the stated price. Other risks
are geopolitical events like the Middle East war affecting consumer
confidence, willingness to spend on premium tires, and miles driven;
the impact of US tariffs on volume and profitability, as well as
potential tariffs from other regions; a general deterioration in auto
production; pricing pressure from auto manufacturers; raw material
cost developments, including higher-for-longer oil prices; and
currency risks. Further risks comprise product liability and other
corporate legal-related issues, as well as increasing competition from
established and new players, including the effective control and
sustainable implementation of tariffs to address Chinese low-cost tire
imports.

Why it is Most Preferred
With the stock having come down significantly from its May 2023
peaks, we believe the risk-reward now looks interesting again.
Porsche trades at a 2027E P/E of around 20x on depressed earnings.
While the process of paving the way back to double-digit operating
profit margins will likely take until at least 2028, 2Q26 already
offered a glimpse of hope for this journey and we believe that
starting to add some exposure to Porsche makes sense. The
upcoming capital market day in October should shed more light on
the new management team’s plans to bring the company “back on
the race track.”
Company profile
Headquartered in Stuttgart-Zuffenhausen, Porsche AG is a producer
of sports cars and SUVs. Porsche is one of the most desired luxury car
brands and sits between premium car brands and ultra-luxury brands,
also in terms of profitability. Volkswagen AG owns 75% minus one
share; the domination agreement between VW and Porsche has been
terminated in the context of the IPO. Porsche AG is not to be
confused with Porsche Automobile Holding SE, which is the financial
holding company controlled by the Porsche and Piëch families.
What drives our opinion
The Porsche brand is synonymous with sports cars and German
autobahns, and Porsche AG, which listed in 2022, is, in our view, the
purest way to invest in the brand. Over the past decade, Porsche AG
has shifted from a strong growth trajectory—broadening its product
portfolio and growing its volume substantially—to an ailing company
hit by misguided product decisions, severe sales volume drops in
China, overcapacity (i.e., too high fixed costs), and a series of
disappointing quarter-over-quarter results. However, Porsche’s
products beyond EVs are still in high demand, as performance,
technology, longevity, and reliability—on and off the track—as well as
relatively high residual values, continue to attract high-end car
enthusiasts. Most of Porsche’s rivals cannot match its clientele. While
Volkswagen (VW) remains the majority shareholder, Porsche AG
enjoys full operational flexibility and still has access to the scale
benefits of VW—a strong combination for Porsche AG shareholders.
Porsche proved its resilience in the past, for example during the
global financial crisis, when it maintained solid profit margins. With
substantial changes to the top management team, we believe the
company is committed to finally addressing its product and cost
challenges to return to previous success, and the just agreed 5,000
headcount reduction is part of this restructuring efforts. While
suffering from very weak Chinese market demand, given its still
superior brand positioning, Porsche AG should remain substantially
more insulated from the competitive pricing pressure that volume-
focused car manufacturers are facing from Chinese competition in
other parts of the world. Key risks are corporate governance-related,
given the complex shareholder and management structure, as well as
foreign exchange rate risks, given its large global sales versus its
German production footprint. Other risks include ongoing shortages
of parts due to potential supply chain disruptions, geopolitical
tensions (e.g., in the Middle East or Russia-Ukraine war) affecting end
markets and consumer confidence, any additional Chinese end-
market weakness for Porsche, potential commercial failure of
upcoming new products, the risk of even higher trade tariffs with the
US or other markets, and any remaining outstanding diesel and
emission-related concerns and lawsuits.

Why it is Most Preferred
After the October 2025 disappointment on 2030 financial guidance,
consensus top-line and earnings expectations rebased to more
realistic levels. Its 2Q26 results were once again good, with an
upgrade of the full-year revenue guidance, proving once again the
strength of the brand and its business model. We believe Ferrari will
continue to deliver on margins and its 2026 margin guidance serves
as a floor, leaving room for consensus earnings beats as the year
progresses. However, we also believe that the Ferrari Luce failed not
only to inspire brand enthusiasts, but also might limit the further
medium-term earnings growth expectations we originally had in
mind. Nevertheless, Ferrari remains one of our top picks and we
maintain an above-benchmark weight within the automobile sector
as we believe that thanks to its unique positioning, Ferrari is not
affected by the issue of Chinese competition like other brands. As a
side note, its plug-in hybrid models are in high demand, reaching
42% of total shipments in FY25 (down to 30% in 2Q26), and we
expect this to evolve over time. Its electrification strategy—aiming for
40% hybrids and 20% pure-EVs by 2030—looks reasonable to
maintain the unique character of the Ferrari brand, in our view, but
more emotional EV products might be needed.
Company profile
Ferrari is among the world’s leading luxury brands, focused on
design, engineering, production, and sales of the world’s most
recognizable luxury performance sports cars. The company’s name
and history are closely associated with its Formula 1 racing team,
Scuderia Ferrari, the most successful team in Formula 1 history. The
company operates in four segments: Cars and Spare Parts; Engines;
Sponsorship, Commercial and Brand; and Other. The company is
headquartered in Maranello, Italy.
What drives our opinion
Ferrari is one of the world’s most prestigious brands, with a unique
and superior business model. Ferrari’s aim is to keep supply tight to
preserve its brand value and pricing power. Volume growth will likely
not serve as a major driver of the stock. However, a strong product
portfolio, an increasing level of vehicle customization, and more
limited editions are keys to success, in our view. The introduction of
the brand-new 849 Testarossa and the recently presented 12Cilindri,
296 Speciale, Amalfi, and F80 all confirm the success of this strategy
and ensure continuing earnings momentum, in our view. On its
capital market day (CMD) on 9 October 2025, Ferrari presented its
new 2030 strategy and financial guidance. While it disappointed
those who had hoped for aggressive growth forecasts, we think it
confirmed the ongoing successful strategy of focusing on the brand’s
scarcity value. At the end of May, the first fully electric Ferrari model
was finally presented—a very atypical design for Ferrari, and priced at
EUR 550k, which is well above the company’s current average selling
price. While we had expected overall volumes to remain decent to
not overstretch the brand, we were nevertheless surprised by the
progressive design, which in our view is likely to “limit interest by
design.” The valuation (in P/E terms) is at a substantial premium to its
auto peers, and while it has strongly derated in the last few months,
the stock is still trading closer to those in the luxury sector. Ferrari
enjoys decent visibility on its sales, with long lead times and an order
book that extends toward the end of 2027, resulting in relatively
stable and visible earnings and cash flows, which is highly atypical for
an auto company. The main risks are substantial wealth destruction
that impacts demand from Ferrari’s high-end clientele; the length of
the current geopolitical situation and its effect on demand in the
Middle East; societal or regulatory pressure to address wealth
inequality in certain markets; unfavorable emission-related
regulations; very high trade tariffs with the inability to pass them on
via higher prices (mitigated by Ferrari immediately announcing up to
10% price increases on the initial US tariff announcements); a
substantial strengthening of the euro; supply chain disruptions; any
dent to the brand, including the market reception of the just
introduced EV, and any major disappointment in financial results, as
well as way too high (growth) expectations by investors as seen on
various times in recent past, which would pressure its still-high
valuation multiples.

Why it is Most Preferred
Michelin delivered mixed 3Q26 results. In spite of the disappointing
volume growth guidance, in the current environment we prefer
automobile-related companies that are less exposed to a weak
Chinese end-market and growing Chinese competition in Europe.
Following recent tariff rulings on low-cost Chinese tire imports,
Michelin fits this profile. Given its high-quality business, and it focus
on reducing its environmental impact, we believe Michelin serves as a
suitable enabler in our Greentech theme. Trading at around 10-11x
2027E P/E is not necessarily cheap versus peers, but leaves room for
expansion, in our view.
Company profile
Michelin is one of the world’s largest tyre producers. The company’s
main business is in passenger car and truck tyres, selling tyres under
the Michelin, BFGoodrich, Uniroyal and Kleber brands. Approximately
80% of the company’s business is in the replacement market.
Michelin has its own tyre distribution business for passenger car and
heavy truck tyres (under the Euromaster brand in Europe), and
manufactures speciality tyres for two-wheelers, aircraft, construction
equipment, agricultural equipment and earthmovers.
What drives our opinion
Tire companies enjoy different dynamics than traditional auto
suppliers and auto manufacturers. They depend on the highly
lucrative replacement markets. We like Michelin’s strong focus on
lucrative +18-inch premium passenger tires, which now represent
more than half of Michelin brand sales, and high-margin specialty
tires. Michelin’s strong pricing power, with good pricing discipline, as
continuously proven with a strong price/mix contribution, and
ongoing cost savings with ongoing plant optimizations, including
closures, should help to cope with ongoing industry pressure in terms
of volumes and raw material costs. This supports free cash flow
generation and higher dividend payouts. We also like Michelin’s focus
on reducing its environmental impact, using either renewable or
recycled material, and its tires showing superior abrasion
characteristics to other tire brands. Risks to our positive view on
Michelin are geopolitical events like the Middle East war affecting
consumer confidence, willingness to spend on premium tires, and
miles driven; the impact of US tariffs on volume and profitability, as
well as potential tariffs from other regions; a general deterioration in
auto production; pricing pressure from auto manufacturers; raw
material cost developments, including higher-for-longer oil prices; and
currency risks. Further risks comprise product liability and other
corporate legal-related issues, as well as increasing competition from
established and new players, including the effective control and
sustainable implementation of tariffs to address Chinese low-cost tire
imports as well as major M&A activity.

Why it is Most Preferred
We believe Schaeffler offers appealing cost efficiency and
technological innovation in the automotive industry and beyond, and
it is also one of the few suppliers we have in our EPL. Since we
removed the stock at the end of January 2026, it has the declined,
now trading at around 12x 2027 P/E. The profit warning on 31 July
took the market by surprise. That said, the market reaction has been
too harsh, in our view. While still not cheap after the correction, we
believe earnings progress on rebased expectations leave room for
some upside. We keep the stock on our list as we believe reduced
guidance and its 2Q26 results were clearing events from which the
stock can recover again.
Company profile
Schaeffler is a European automotive and industrial supplier that is
active globally. It is a top-25 global automotive supplier and the
second-largest ball bearing producer. Its automotive division makes
and supplies engine, transmission and chassis parts to OEMs directly,
to tier 1 suppliers and to after-market customers. Its industrial division
manufactures and supplies bearings and related parts into a wide
array of end markets. Some 75% of its shares (100% of voting rights)
are held by a holding company owned by the Schaeffler family, which
also holds a 46% stake in Conti’s share capital.
What drives our opinion
For auto suppliers like Schaeffler, auto production is normally the
main driver for the business and, ultimately, the stock price. As a
result of the challenging situation for global auto production as well
as certain industrial applications we have seen in recent years,
Schaeffler’s stock has been quite volatile, with the successful and
stable “Vehicle Lifetime Solutions” after-market business rarely
getting the credit it deserves. The integration of the Vitesco auto
supplier acquisition has been well on track, and with its capital
market day in September 2025, Schaeffler announced promising
2028 medium-term targets, i.e., a doubling of margins. While
demanding, with the financial market taking a more conservative
stance, management seems to be highly determined to make this
happen, and we think it can and will pull various levers to move
toward achieving this. The combination of reducing capacity in
Germany, a turnaround in its still unprofitable E-mobility (EV)
business, with R&D expenses having peaked, and a strong product
line-up with a lot of exposure to Chinese auto manufacturers look
promising. We also like its technology-agnostic hedge—i.e., in case
EVs do not grow strongly enough, the internal combustion engine
business can make up for it. What makes Schaeffler very appealing to
us is its exposure to humanoids, where its production and product
competence around bearings, joints and actuators offer a strong
future growth story, while potentially commercializing its abundance
of production-related data to train humanoids could become a nice
add-on. Furthermore, its activities in defense, drones, and space
shows Schaeffler’s willingness and capabilities go beyond its
traditional bearings for industrial applications and its auto supplier
business. Risks to our positive view on Schaeffler are a general
deterioration in auto and industrial production, supply chain
interruptions, the impact of announced US tariffs on volume and
profitability as well as potential tariffs from other regions, pricing
pressure by auto manufacturers, EVs failing to gain traction,
unfavorable raw material cost developments, and currency risks. Its
expansion into the news business opportunities beyond automotive
and industrial applications bring execution risks. Furthermore, product
liability and other corporate legal related issues might also arise.

Why it is Most Preferred
VW as a company, as well as its stock price, has had a tough ride in
the last few years. We removed the stock in June 2026 due to a lack
of near-term catalysts. We now add the stock back as we view the 3
September announcement indicating a broad-based willingness
across all stakeholders to restructure the company as supportive of
the name. This could trigger renewed interest in VW’s stock. Trading
at 3.5x 2027E P/E and 0.2x 2027E P/B, the latest news offers a lot of
optionality for a re-rating, in our view.
Company profile
Volkswagen is Europe’s largest automaker, and a leading producer of
commercial vehicles. In 2025, it sold 9.1m cars, making it the second-
largest OEM by unit sales globally. Its portfolio comprises 12 brands
covering all segments, from motorcycles to supercars and heavy
trucks, and includes VW, Audi, Skoda, Seat, Porsche, Bentley,
Lamborghini and Traton. VW’s main voting shareholders are Porsche
SE (32%), Lower Saxony (12%) and Qatar (10%). VW also operates
two joint ventures in China, with SAIC and FAW, respectively.
What drives our opinion
Volkswagen (VW) Group surprised the market by announcing
sweeping restructuring measures, including a significant headcount
reduction, plant capacity adjustments, consolidation in brands and
products, and reduced capital expenditure spending in order to
address its uncompetitive cost position (mainly) in Germany. While
this will take time, we see it as a step in the right direction to curb
the gradual decline VW has been facing. We think Volkswagen (VW)
Group has the right brands and is once again on the path to having
the right products. It is committed to electrification—while not
neglecting internal combustion engines—and we believe it will be
able to achieve the demanding EU-wide CO₂ targets. Concerns
around Chinese manufacturers making inroads into European
markets have hit the attention of EU regulators. There are indications
that this is finally being addressed with the EU’s Industrial
Acceleration Act (IAA) most likely ending up offering support to
ensure competitiveness at VW can be maintained. Other concerns
about the deterioration in the Chinese market are not only well
known but are also being addressed with the help of its partner
Xpeng and other local partners on the technology front. Furthermore,
the increasing localization and operational independence of its
Chinese operations should help cater to the needs of the demanding
Chinese auto market. As for its Western markets, the software-
related issues that have burdened the overall VW Group have also
been addressed with VW’s engagement with US-based Rivian. Key
risks include the inability to execute on the announced restructuring
measures as well as US tariffs, which could result in an inability to sell
certain products like the Audi Q5 to the extent planned, or the
inability to pass on higher costs to consumers across its various group
brands, hitting overall profitability. Other risks include VW’s important
China business, such as a further loss of market share, inability to
launch appealing products for Chinese consumers, and inability to
right-size the cost structure in the Chinese market. Further risks are
any supply constraints (e.g., access to rare earths) or other
geopolitical events resulting in the inability to produce and sell its
high-margin models, geopolitical tensions like the Middle East war
affecting consumer confidence, and the risk of other trade tariffs or
other trade restrictions beyond the US, as well as any remaining
outstanding diesel- and emission-related concerns and other lawsuits.
In addition, adverse currency movements and rising commodity and
component costs, erratic price cuts by key competitors (namely in
China, but also in Europe) for electric vehicles, as well as new
competitors entering the European market, increase the risk of
additional pressure on VW’s profitability.
