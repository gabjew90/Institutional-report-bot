Following a series of regasification terminal announcements in recent months, 
we update our global LNG regasification supply/demand model. We update our 
model to reflect the recent uptick in global regasification project announcements, 
projects that have recently entered service, and the latest regional LNG demand 
forecasts from our GS Commodities research colleagues. We now estimate ~130-365 
mtpa of incremental regasification capacity (+10-30% vs the current market, 
ex-China) will be needed by 2035 to absorb the upcoming LNG supply wave, of 
which we estimate ~30-115 mtpa is likely to be captured by new floating 
regasification units, net of projects already under construction. On a headline basis, 
our regasification capacity estimate (ex-China) is largely unchanged versus our prior 
model, as slightly higher Asia ex-China demand (+10 mtpa vs our prior model) and 
Europe demand (+15 mtpa) is balanced by lower Middle East and Other demand. 
Elsewhere, although excluded from our forecast, we reflect lower illustrative China 
regas demand following the approval of the country’s 15th 5-Year Plan, which 
maintains a focus on its coal-plus-renewables strategy. In summary, we expect: 1) 
despite near-term global gas price volatility, the pre-sanctioned LNG supply wave 
will still support lower global gas prices, price-elastic LNG demand growth, and 
associated LNG import infrastructure demand in emerging markets later this decade; 
2) floating regasification units (FSRUs) will take market share vs land-based 
alternatives; and 3) price-elastic demand across Southeast Asia and the Middle East 
will drive import growth once post-2027 supply additions ease near-term price 
volatility. Overall, recent project announcements support our view that regasification 
capacity must expand to prevent import bottlenecks and that floating regasification 
will capture an outsized share of new additions given its lower capital intensity, 
greater speed to market, and inherent operational flexibility for emerging markets. 
We highlight Buy-rated Excelerate Energy (EE) as a clear beneficiary going forward: 
holding a ~25% global FSRU market share, EE is uniquely positioned to capture tight 
regasification supply dynamics via both organic growth and operating leverage on its 
existing fleet. See within for details.

Floating regas should continue to take market share — we estimate ~30-115 mtpa 
will be needed. FSRUs have historically made up ~15-20% of the global regasification 
mix relative to larger, fixed, land-based regasification terminals. We note FSRUs offer 
lower capex (on a $/ton basis), faster 12- to 24-month deployment timelines (vs 4-6 
years for onshore infrastructure), and greater flexibility for emerging markets where 
demand certainty and counterparty credit ratings may be lower than what is required by 
fixed, onshore terminals. Thus, given the GS Commodities research team’s expectation 
that price-elastic markets will make up a significant portion of demand growth, ex-China 
(~123 mtpa in Asia ex-China, ~24 mtpa in the Middle East, ~14 mtpa in the Americas 
and other), we would expect flexible floating regasification to be deployed at a higher 
rate vs onshore capacity. Specifically, we estimate FSRUs will account for 20-25% of the 
total regas market by 2035 vs ~15-20% historically. In our view, the recent uptick in 
FSRU announcements vs land-based terminals is a key proof point of the relative appeal 
of fast-to-market, lower-cost, flexible floating infrastructure, particularly for emerging 
markets.

Still confident in price-elastic demand growth into the end of the decade despite 
elevated near-term pricing. Amid continued LNG supply disruptions from the conflict 
in the Middle East, global gas prices continue to rise. Higher global LNG prices and 
physical LNG supply disruptions have introduced questions around the magnitude and 
timing of price-elastic LNG import demand and the associated need for regasification 
infrastructure additions. On the one hand, higher near-term prices and price volatility 
pose questions on supply security and long-term affordability for price-sensitive 
emerging markets, which, if sustained, may encourage substitution toward a 
combination of coal-plus-renewables approach vs LNG. On the other hand, elevated 
substitution fuel prices (crude and products pricing) and the still-resilient LNG supply 
outlook 2H27+ support our view that LNG prices will be lower later this decade, allowing 
for price-elastic demand growth to emerge. Consequently, even amid elevated 
near-term spot pricing, we remain confident in price-elastic demand growth 
materializing into the middle of next decade, supporting our view of long-term 
regasification infrastructure development needs.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
