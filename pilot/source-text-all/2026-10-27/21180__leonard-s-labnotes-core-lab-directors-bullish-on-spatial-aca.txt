Leonard's LabNotes: Core Lab Directors Bullish on
Spatial; Aca/Gov Stabilization?
Our view: Last we attended a regional core lab director conference. Takeaways were incrementally
positive for Outperform rated Waters (WAT) and Illumina (ILMN), and mixed for Sector Perform rated
10x Genomics (TXG). The read for 10x Genomics reinforces our view that spatial interest remains healthy
while single-cell is a mature line of business. Overall aca/gov sentiment has improved; stabilization
remains the operative word, with no one declaring victory. Genomics commanded the majority of the
floor traffic and mindshare at the conference relative to flow cytometry and other technologies, though
this may partly reflect the research focus of the attendee mix.
Instrument Utilization and Foot Traffic
• Waters and Illumina had notable foot traffic at the conference. The busiest booths of the conference
were Waters and Illumina. Waters showcased its BD FACSDiscover A7, while Illumina showcased its
single-cell and spatial offerings. StrataMap garnered interest, with core lab directors wanting to better
understand its capabilities.
• A tour of one academic cell analysis lab offered useful perspective on real-world instrument utilization.
The lab's Agilent BioTek Cytation 5 reader and a Keyence fluorescent microscope were the most
utilized instruments. The low-volume liquid handler, by contrast, was the least utilized-- a function of
the fact that this instrument is most applicable to genomics research, and the lab lacks an adjacent
genomics core to drive its pull-through.
Trending Themes in Research
• Multiomic workflows: Vendors note that researchers are increasingly looking in incorporate
multiomic (e.g. proteomic + genomic) data into one workflow. We believe Outperform rated Illumina,
and Sector Perform rated 10x Genomics and Thermo-Fisher (TMO) are well positioned in the theme.
• Organoid-based assays: One vendor noted a recent uptick the utilization of organoid-based and 3D
cellular assays, which we believe is constructive to high content screening platforms like the ones
provided by Sector Perform rated Revvity (RVTY), Outperform-rated Danaher (DHR), and Agilent (A).
Sanguine on Spatial, Single-Cell Slumps
• Atera continues to diligence well. Most conversations suggest +DD% growth in Spatial, with 10x's
Atera continuing to be the hottest new instrument in the field. General excitement around its
capabilities continued at the conference.
• Single-cell remains a mature market. Asking about growth opportunities in single-cell felt like pulling
teeth, as directors expect their single cell revenue to be ~flat at the median.
• Many prefer 3' versus Flex for Chromium. Several directors noted that customers are unwilling to
switch their validated 3' assay to Flex, particularly for clinical research. Based on the feedback we
received, we believe there will still be a meaningful customer base that does not switch over to Flex
or Flex Apex. On one hand, this is a positive for 10x's pricing power, as the stickiness of the validated
3' assay limits the downward pressure that Flex adoption might otherwise exert. On the other hand, it
suggests that demand elasticity for single-cell workflows may be lower than the market appreciates.
• Waters BD Rhapsody has a niche. It was noted that the BD Rhapsody is the best choice for lower
cell count samples (<1,000 cells). One director noted that the BD Rhapsody has seen an increased
utilization because it is an easier entry into single-cell for new researchers. This feedback was a
surprise, as the Rhapsody doesn't always diligence well.

Aca/Gov Environment Stabilizing?
• Conversations with core lab directors and private vendors (n = 7) suggest things are stabilizing
relative to three months ago, though the prevailing tone was one with caution rather than confident
recovery, and tracks with the muted NIH outlays. One vendor flagged improvement beginning in
September; others noted spending normalized around the academic fiscal year-end in June. In our
coverage Illumina (ILMN), 10x Genomics (TXG), and Bio-Rad (BIO) have the most torque to an
improving aca/gov research environment.
• New facilities emerge, a positive demand signal. One director highlighted that her institution is
standing up a new facility dedicated entirely to single-cell (and spatial) sample prep, utilizing 10x's
platforms. While a single data point, the sentiment is that research institutions are leaning into
more shared resource and co-opt models in the current belt tightening environment, where principal
investigators (PIs) might not have the means to purchase new capital equipment. We view this
dynamic as a positive for Tools demand in a largely muted environment.
