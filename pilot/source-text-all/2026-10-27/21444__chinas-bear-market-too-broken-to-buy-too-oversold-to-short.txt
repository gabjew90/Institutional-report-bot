China in meltdown
CSI 300 has entered bear market territory, down 20% from its June peak after 10 consecutive weeks of
declines. The sell-off has accelerated since the late-August death cross, driven by the global AI unwind,
fading stimulus hopes and deteriorating domestic fundamentals. Even better-than-expected Q3 GDP
failed to lift sentiment, while rising oil prices add another headwind. The 4,000 level is next to watch,
but there is little urgency to buy China.

The earnings problem
China's equity sell-off is not just about sentiment. Consensus 2026 earnings growth for MSCI China has
collapsed from more than 11% at the start of the year to barely 1%, according to BofA. The
fundamental backdrop offers little support for a sustained recovery.

The policy put is losing its punch
Beijing is stepping up support through buybacks, liquidity injections and restrictions on institutional
selling. Yet BofA argues that national-team buying is more likely to establish a floor than ignite a
sustainable rally. History offers little comfort: the average one-month CSI 300 gain following the past
29 RRR cuts was barely 1%.

China's two economies
China's economic divergence has become extreme. Exports and industrial production continue to
power ahead, while domestic consumption struggles and real estate investment remains in a
prolonged collapse. Headline GDP growth is masking a deeply fractured domestic economy.
JPM
China's broken housing engine
China's property market remains the economy's biggest structural drag. New home sales have
collapsed to roughly 40% of their 2019 level, even as real retail sales continue to rise. The divergence
illustrates how deeply the housing downturn has reshaped China's domestic economy.
GS
China's giant savings glut
Chinese households are sitting on an estimated RMB 63 trillion in excess bank deposits, according to
Goldman Sachs. The savings rate has climbed to 33.4%, yet consumption continues to disappoint.
China has plenty of household liquidity, but very little willingness to spend it.

GS
The missing borrower
China's households are not just reluctant to spend. They are increasingly reluctant to borrow. Goldman
Sachs data show short-term household loan flows deeply negative, while longer-term borrowing
remains exceptionally weak. The household deleveraging cycle continues despite Beijing's efforts to
revive domestic demand.
GS
Oil matters
Chinese equities have tracked inverted oil remarkably closely. With energy prices surging, another
potential headwind is emerging for an economy already struggling with weak domestic demand.

The missing buyer
