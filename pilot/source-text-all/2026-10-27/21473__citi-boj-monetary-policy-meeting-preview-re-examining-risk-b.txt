BoJ Monetary Policy Meeting Preview
Re-examining risk balance between growth and prices
CITI'S TAKE 
The BoJ is widely expected to keep the policy rate unchanged next week. 
Market pricing for a second consecutive rate hike in October, which had at 
one point reached around 40%, has since receded. The inflation forecast for 
next fiscal year in the BoJ Outlook Report could be revised up, reflecting the 
lag in pass-through of higher oil prices. The characterization of underlying 
inflation could also advance from "approaching 2%," although the 
assessment that financial conditions remain accommodative is unlikely to 
change, and we think the BoJ will maintain its stance of continuing rate 
hikes. We maintain our base-case scenario of quarterly rate hikes through 
mid-next year with a terminal rate of 2%, and expect the next hike in 
December. That said, market developments such as the correction in AI-
related stocks and spillover from rising French yields could influence the 
pace of hikes and warrant monitoring.
Japan Economics — The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate unchanged 
next week. We expect hawkish Board Member Hajime Takata to once again submit 
a rate hike proposal at this meeting, but we continue to forecast the next hike in 
December. The characterization of underlying inflation could also advance from 
"approaching 2%," though the assessment that financial conditions remain 
accommodative is unlikely to change, and we think the BoJ will maintain its stance 
of continuing rate hikes..
Japan Rates Strategy — Rate hike pricing appears broadly fair and we think range-
bound trading is likely for the time being. While upside risks to interest rates are 
receding, there is still a lack of catalysts to justify aggressive buying. The market is 
likely to hold firm while monitoring equity markets, foreign bonds, and commodity 
prices.
Japan FX Strategy — We believe the USDJPY is likely to remain in the ¥155/$–¥160/$ 
range near term. However, the currency policy of the Japanese MoF is clearly 
becoming more hawkish. We would expect the next round of intervention to buy the 
JPY if the USDJPY tops ¥160/$, with the aim of pushing it below ¥155/$. Also, we 
focus on developments in the EURJPY to gauge the likelihood of another round of 
intervention to buy the JPY by the US.
Japan Equity Strategy — We expect the BoJ to keep its policy rate unchanged at the 
forthcoming meeting. We believe the market has already largely priced in the BoJ’s 
future interest rate hike path and do not anticipate anything dramatic at this 
meeting. Rather than monetary policy, for now the equity market's attention will 
likely be focused on earnings trends in Japan and the US, as well as trends in AI and 
data center investment. With the business sentiment DI surprise in the September 
Tankan survey in positive territory for most sectors and remaining at an elevated 
level overall, July-September earnings at Japanese firms are likely to be generally 
strong with many positive surprises. If hikes to guidance predominate in response to 
strong results while fundamental factors grow in importance, we would regard these 
developments as positive for Japan equities as a whole.

The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate unchanged next week. Following 
the rate hike at the September meeting, market pricing for a second consecutive 
hike at the October meeting had at one point reached around 40%, but neither the 
subsequent Tankan survey nor the Summary of Opinions contained anything that 
would support consecutive hikes. If anything, pricing for hikes over the longer term 
has recently declined amidst an equity market correction. We expect Board 
member Hajime Takata—seen as the most hawkish among Policy Board 
members—to once again submit a rate hike proposal at this meeting, but this 
would not be a surprise. We continue to expect the next rate hike to come in 
December.
Since BoJ Governor Ueda stated at his September press conference that "the policy 
phase has changed", recent BoJ official commentary has largely converged around 
the view that underlying inflation is approaching the 2% target. A key focus at this 
meeting will be whether there is any change to the previous characterization of 
underlying inflation as "approaching 2%." While some advancement to language 
such as "at broadly 2%" is possible, we think it would likely still be premature to 
declare outright that the price target has been "achieved." Moreover, even if 
inflation is judged to have broadly reached the target, the assessment that 
financial conditions remain accommodative is unlikely to change, and the BoJ will 
likely continue to signal its intent to proceed with further rate hikes.

While market pricing for rate hikes has pulled back from earlier levels amidst recent 
equity market weakness, continued rate hikes remain sufficiently justified from a 
price perspective. Citi also expects the Fed to raise rates in December, and should 
the BoJ fail to hike in December the risk of renewed yen weakness could resurface. 
On the other hand, looking at recent corporate goods price data and the Tankan 
price indicators, the pace of price increases appears to be leveling off, and there is 
little evidence of inflation accelerating to a degree that would necessitate 
consecutive rate hikes (Figures 5 and 6). We therefore continue to expect three 
further rate hikes at a quarterly pace through mid-next year, bringing the terminal 
rate for this cycle to 2%.
In terms of factors that could shift the near-term policy path, market developments 
warrant close attention. While the USDJPY appears to have calmed somewhat 
compared to earlier levels, the recent correction in AI-related stocks is likely also a 
source of concern for the BoJ. Governor Ueda himself remarked at the press 
conference following the previous meeting that the BoJ wishes to avoid a pace of 
rate hikes so rapid as to trigger a significant correction in asset prices. Note that 
equities fell after Governor Ueda struck a more hawkish tone at the July 2024 
meeting press conference, and should the equity market correction deepen the 
possibility of a slower pace of rate hikes cannot be ruled out. The spillover from 
rising French yields also warrants close attention. Should global financial 
conditions tighten and rate hikes by the Fed and ECB be pushed back, the urgency 
for the BoJ to hike quickly could also diminish.

At the September meeting the market saw two votes against the rate hike as 
dovish. However, with the subsequent weakening of the yen and rising global 
interest rates, rate hike pricings accelerated again. The OIS market once priced in 
about a 40% probability of back-to-back rate hikes in October. However, this 
turned out to be the peak of hawkish pricing, as a series of strong auction results, 
concerns about the AI boom, and turmoil in OATs led to significant short covering. 
We think it too early to conclude the upward trend in interest rates has ended, but 
there are clear changes. 
There were views that the terminal rate may be above 2% when interest rates were 
rising. At present caution seems to be mounting again regarding the rate 
potentially being 2% or lower. If tightening financial conditions cause the Fed and 
ECB to delay rate hikes pressure on the yen should ease, and the likelihood that the 
BoJ will rush to raise rates would also recede. If the Bank finds it hard to hike 
further from the latter half of next year onward, it is reasonable to expect the 
terminal rate to be 2% or lower. Of course, if the yen continues to weaken or 
inflation remains high at that point further tightening would be necessary, 
although the hudle is high given the composition of Policy Committee members. 
As noted above, a December rate hike still looks highly likely. However, uncertainty 
is increasing beyond that. OIS market pricing is roughly the same as after the 
September meeting and looks fair. We maintain our terminal rate forecast at 2%, as 
there is still a lack of catalysts for market pricing to decline further. On the other 
hand, it is likely to take time before the rate resumes rising. A rebound in the stock 
market is a fundamental prerequisite for a positive outlook on the economy. 
However, if the situation in the Middle East worsens, the economy may face 
stagflation-like headwinds via higher commodity prices. In that case uncertainty 
about the rate hike path would increase and be bearish for bonds.

Shift from constraining JPY weakness to actively boosting 
the JPY
The Japanese MoF and the BoJ conducted rate checks directly after the last BoJ 
monetary policy meeting (MPM) on September 18. This was when the USDJPY rose 
to about ¥158/$. What stands out is that the rate checks occurred at a level 
significantly lower than the previous round of intervention to buy the JPY at end-
July, when the USDJPY had climbed to close to ¥164/$ (Figure 13). 
During this time the demands of US authorities under Treasury Secretary Scott 
Bessent have changed from preventing further JPY deprecation to actively 
promoting appreciation. At a September 22 summit President Donald Trump 
expressed US concern about JPY weakness directly to Prime Minister Sanae 
Takaichi. 
We expect the USDJPY to remain in the ¥155/$–¥160/$ range in the near term 
(Figure 14) and believe there will be a next round of intervention to buy the JPY 
when it tests ¥160/$. In this case, we believe the Japanese government will likely 
want to push the USDJPY down to below ¥155/$, or perhaps even close to ¥150/$.

We note the potential for there to again be concerted intervention with the US. 
When intervening in the EURJPY at end-July, Treasury Secretary Bessent indicated

Debate regarding neutral interest rate
In the near term, the neutral interest rates indicted by the Taylor Rule and the like is 
2%–3% (Figure 17), while the BoJ estimates Japan’s potential growth rate at around 
1% (Figure 18). Even so, Japanese companies have in recent years been bringing 
capital expenditure back to domestic sites, which is now expected to contribute to 
raising the potential growth rate after a period of around 30 years during which 
capital deepening has been the biggest factor depressing growth. 
Private sector debt is smaller among companies and households than before, while 
deposits have been growing due to previous fiscal expenditure. Looking at the 
macro balance of the Japanese economy from the perspective of fund flows alone, 
rate hikes could even be positive for the private sector. 
We do not that the rate hikes to c2% that we expect will have a marked adverse 
effect on the Japanese economy and equity market.

Fundamentals-related factors a major negative in Jul-Sep 
Japanese stocks have been range-bound in a TOPIX range of 3,900 to 4,200 from 
the start of July to the present. Particularly striking during this period has been the 
major negative stemming from fundamental factors such as forecast revisions and 
EPS growth rates (Figures 21 and 22). While it is customary for investors to focus on 
fundamentals as they increasingly price in this year's forecast earnings during the 
period from Q1 to the H1 announcements of Japanese firms, this year we have seen 
the exactly opposite development. We see the reasons fundamental factors such 
as earnings forecast revisions and EPS growth rates did not function well in the 
July-September quarter as follows: 1) a move to anticipate strong earnings and 
lofty revisions in the April-June quarter, mainly in AI and semiconductor-related 
sectors, with a backlash to this move appearing in the July-September quarter; 2) 
the further steepening of the yield curve, which led to a superior showing for value 
stocks, including finance, and weaker performance for growth stocks; and 3) the 
rise in crude prices due to the turmoil in the Middle East, which led to impressive 
performance in sectors such as energy, trading companies, and shipping, contrary 
to their recent earnings.

Citi Research stock recommendations include an investment rating and an optional risk rating to highlight high risk stocks.

expectations of expected total return ("ETR") and risk. ETR is the sum of the forecast price appreciation (or depreciation) plus
