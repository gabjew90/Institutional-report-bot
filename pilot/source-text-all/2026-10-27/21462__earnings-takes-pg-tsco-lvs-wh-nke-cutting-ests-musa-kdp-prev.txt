PG (+4% pre mkt) – Clears the bar. Organic sales +4% should be well met, ahead of the street +2.1% and our sense of 
buyside expectations of ~3%. Upside across all key segments and total volumes solid at +2.4% (st +1%). Buyside sentiment 
had improved recently on the back of accelerating consumption data and improved mgmt tone on the road; our flows have 
been better to buy [both HF and LO]. One of Andrea’s top picks into the qtr from her sector preview. Following the EPS beat 
($2.00 vs st $1.89), they reiterate both the FY OSG and EPS outlooks [while total sales is raised +100bps to reflect the Thorne 
acquisition]. 
•
TSCO (-5% pre mkt) – Interested to hear thoughts here… While we do thing positioning had moved higher recently I’m a 
bit surprised to see shares this weak off the print given the multiple compression and underperformance in the stock (-40% 
from Feb highs). Comps +0.4% land ahead (st -0.9%) and this is within the realm of buyside expectations (though perhaps a 
tick light of some looking for 1%). Their comments on comp linearity are positive  “comparable store sales improved 
sequentially through the quarter, moving from negative in July to modestly positive in August to further strengthening in 
September” which might help and probably suggests ~2% in Sept (?) GPM miss probably not helping the stock either (50bps 
below the street). They reiterate the outlook which brackets the street on implied Q4 EPS and puts the street at the high-end on 
comps. 
Research Takes: LVS, WH, PG
•
PG – Expected Stock Reaction: Positive, Proving Sales (+2% Volumes) Momentum We Anticipated as a Top Pick Into 
Print and from Feedback from Our Meetings at the All-Stars Conference in London. PG (OW-rated) beat core EPS by 11 cents 
to $2.00 and across the board. Sales growth of +4.0% beat JPMe/consensus +1.2%/+1.9% and was driven by +2.4% organic 
volumes, +1.0% in mix and just a modest help from pricing (+0.4%). Total sales came in well ahead of including OSG +4.0% 
(vs. JPMe/CM +1.6%/+2.2%). Gross margin also positively surprised despite the increased cost pressures. We believe 
management’s decision to keep the guidance range despite the beat is prudent given it is the start of the fiscal year and amid 
the uncertainties from a macro/geopolitical standpoint. With the momentum in volumes and core productivity savings 
(+250bps benefit to core operating margin) that helped to partially offset the higher commodity and logistics costs and fuel 
growth reinvestments, we believe the company is set to continue to beat estimates through the balance of the fiscal year.
•
LVS (-2% pre mkt) – Q3 downside reflects softness among high-end players in both Macau and Singapore. On a hold-adj 
basis, Macau EBITDA of $518m was similar to our $529m (Str: $541m), but the bigger downward surprise was Singapore’s 
$594m (hold-adj), 13-14% below JPMe/Str’s $680-685m. While we think investors were positioned for misses across both 
segments, it’s worth noting that Singapore was adversely impacted by low ~19% mass hold (adjusting for this, MBS EBITDA 
may have been closer to ~$635m); mass table vols were decent +13% y/y (vs. +10% in 2Q26), and non-gaming spend was 
+5% y/y. We interpret LVS’ comments that “there are a bunch of things going on globally that may have [had] an impact on 
visitation” as pertaining to recent efforts by the Chinese government’s offshore tax campaign (filing deadline is Oct. 22), but 
it’s unclear how quickly high-end gamblers will return to Macau/Singapore. Combined with the challenging macro backdrop 
in China, not much is working in LVS’ favor right now. Operationally, LVS believes its Macau promotions and opex/service 
levels are pretty much where they want them to be, but for margin expansion and/or EBITDA growth, market GGR will need 
to grow. As LVS waits for the backdrop to improve, it’s aggressively repurchasing stock ($950m in 3Q), and its $5b 
authorization is sizable relative to the ~$10b market value of its outstanding float, potentially putting a floor under the stock. 
Our PT goes to $50 (-$4), but we stay OW as we think Singapore is still a high-quality asset worth closer to 11x than the 8.5x 
currently embedded in LVS shares. 
•
WH (unch pre mkt) – 3Q adj. EBITDA of $207m was +3% vs. our $201m/Str’s $200m, with upside attributable to both 
strong US RevPAR, higher management fees, and lower than forecast corporate/other opex. Heading into WH’s report, 
we thought the two focal points were (1) algo working; and (2) path to positive US NUG. On (1), WH’s algo worked as it 
should (RevPAR+NUG = 6% fee growth), but on (2), WH lowered its FY26 NUG to ~4% (-25bps at MP), with the 
implication that US is unlikely to grow this year (tougher transaction/interest rate environment, but does this remove the 
overhang?). During the 3Q, WH’s US RevPAR grew 6% (outperforming its ~5.3% chainscale-weighted index), and we’re 
encouraged that WH sees US RevPAR growing a similar 6% in 4Q26. Together, this RevPAR strength was enough for WH to 
raise its system-wide FY26 RevPAR guide (+25bps vs. prior) and FY26 adj. EBITDA guide +$2.5m. Lastly, we note FY26

NKE: NYC Mgmt Takes; Cuting Ests on 2H27 + FY28 Downside Risk
M. Boss out w/ key takes from NYC CFO meetings; maintains UW/$33 PT while cutting FY28 EPS by ~9% to $1.13 
(~25% below the street). Will focus on key call outs to the numbers here; full detail in the note: 
(1) incremental headwinds to the Q2 revenue outlook from strategic actions on Jordan and China where our math points to revs -
13.2% (< st -10.7% and an 800bps q/q decel) and EPS 30c (< st 37c), 
(2) Incremental downside risk to 2H ests which are implied -7% more/less matching Q1 and would need a big re-accel to hit 
which we don’t see as likely,
 (3) At the Analyst Day we expect them to put finer points on the financial impacts anticipated to right size Jordan and China 
confirmed by mgmt will extend beyond FY27 and bleed into FY28 w/ mgmt taking as aggressive actions as possible near-term to 
work w/ marketplace partners. 
 All of this points to FY28 revenue constraints where we model -2.9% YoY (< st +0.3%) with inventory overhang across key 
geographies from strategic actions as a GPM% constraint in FY28  (offsetting pace program flow-thru which will have outsized 
NT benefits) and we model FY28 EPS $1.13 (< st $1.47) on GPMs +25bps YoY (st +60bps) and OPM 5.0% (< st 6.5%).
 Multi-year, our model math points to FY31 EPS power “opportunity” of ~$2.35 with a 10% operating margin and mid-single-
digit top-line growth profile pointing to a mid-teens multiple per peer regression (= $25 equity value risk profile).

DAR Update
From Tom Palmer: This morning, Darling Ingredients (DAR - Overweight) issued a press release with 3Q26 results for its 
Diamond Green Diesel joint venture and provided a 2026 EBITDA outlook (something it had not previously given). The release 
followed the earnings report of JV partner Valero (VLO, covered by Arun Jayaram). In our view, the headlines could be seen as 
disappointing in that (a) DGD’s $408MM EBITDA in 3Q was well below the Bloomberg consensus estimate of ~$537MM and 
(b) DAR’s newly issued annual 2026 EBITDA outlook of $2.2-2.3 billion is below the consensus estimate of $2,317MM and, at 
least at the low end, would seem to suggest that the timing headwinds that weighed on DGD’s 3Q results will not be fully made 
up for in 4Q. At the same time, we think investors were probably braced for a 3Q EBITDA shortfall at DGD (even if consensus 
estimates were slow to reflect it). Plus, there could be an element of conservatism baked into DAR’s annual outlook given how 
early it is in 4Q. After all, at its May 2026 investor day, DAR discussed an “upcycle” EBITDA outlook of ~$300MM quarterly 
EBITDA in the base business and $1.50 EBITDA per gallon at DGD and later reported 2Q results of $352MM and $2.23, 
respectively.
