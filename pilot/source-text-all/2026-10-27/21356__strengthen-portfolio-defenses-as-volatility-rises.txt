Thought of the day
Market volatility has increased over the past week as investors weigh 
questions about AI profitability in a higher-rate environment, while 
geopolitical conflicts remain unresolved. Brent crude oil was up 1% to near 
USD 110/bbl at the time of writing.
With the third-quarter earnings season now in full swing, further volatility is 
likely. Markets will be assessing hyperscalers’ ability to generate returns on 
their substantial AI investment, the financing and execution risks associated 
with the AI buildout, and whether higher interest rates are weighing on 
business activity and consumer spending.
We still expect a robust reporting season, with AI-related growth 
helping drive overall S&P 500 earnings growth of 28%. But increasingly 
concentrated market performance and the potential for stock-specific 
volatility reinforce the importance of portfolio resilience. We see ways 
investors can further diversify their sources of return and strengthen 
portfolio defenses.
Structured strategies can tailor equity exposure to different market 
outcomes. Investors concerned about a correction but keen to retain 
equity exposure can consider put warrants, which may help limit losses 
on stocks they already own. For those looking to add exposure cautiously, 
capital preservation strategies can provide participation in a market recovery 
while seeking to protect some or all of the initial investment at maturity. 
Income-seeking investors may consider reverse convertibles in parts of the 
equity market where implied volatility remains elevated. These strategies 
can be tailored to investors’ outlook, objectives, and risk appetite, but they

Hedge funds may add differentiated sources of return as market 
dispersion increases. Higher interest rates can widen differences among 
companies, economies, and securities, giving active managers more 
opportunities to identify relative leaders and laggards. This may benefit 
equity market neutral strategies, while diverging central bank policies can 
expand the opportunity set for discretionary macro managers. Uneven 
moves across yield curves may also create opportunities for fixed income 
relative value strategies. We therefore believe diversified exposure to select 
hedge fund strategies can complement traditional portfolios, although 
manager and vehicle selection remain important given risks including 
leverage, limited transparency, higher fees, and illiquidity.
Commodities can diversify portfolios and provide insulation against 
supply-driven inflation. Their historically low correlations with equities 
and bonds can offer a differentiated source of return, while exposure 
to energy may help protect against supply disruptions and the resulting 
inflationary pressures. The longer-term outlook for broad commodities 
is also supported by rising power demand, AI infrastructure investment, 
electrification, and constrained supply across several markets, particularly 
copper. We favor diversified exposure across precious metals, energy, 
industrial metals, and agriculture, with an actively managed approach 
helping to navigate shifts in market leadership.
While we remain constructive on equities and believe investors should 
retain diversified core exposure, the case for broadening portfolio sources 
of return has strengthened amid concentrated market leadership, higher 
financing costs, and ongoing geopolitical risks. Investors should also take 
into account their objectives, risk tolerance, and liquidity needs.
Caught our attention
Meta raises capex forecast. Meta share price was up 2.5% in post-
market trading after the company posted revenue and earnings beats 
for the September quarter and boosted its capital spending outlook. It 
now expects capital expenditure for 2026 to be USD 135-145bn, raising 
the bottom end of the range from USD 130bn. “Our investments are 
paying off, the core business is strong, and we’re seeing velocity across the 
board,” CEO Mark Zuckerberg said. The tech company also announced 
plans for a new class of non-voting stocks, which can be a tool to help 
further Meta’s expansion, including capital fundraising.
Our view: Without commenting on individual companies, recent results 
from AI-related firms underscore continued industry commitment in AI 
investment amid robust demand. We stay constructive on the structural 
growth of AI, and recommend a diversified exposure to companies with 
better earnings visibility and risk-reward. Investors should also consider 
exposure to defensive areas of tech, while those with concentrated 
positions tech stocks should consider using periods of market strength to 
rebalance across sectors and regions.
UK inflation strengthens the case for a rate hike. UK consumer 
price inflation rose to 3.5% year over year in September from 3.1% y/y 
in August, exceeding the 3.4% y/y consensus forecast and reaching its 
highest level since October 2025. Services inflation, closely watched by the 
Bank of England as an indicator of domestic price pressures, increased to 
3.7% y/y from 3.4% y/y, while core inflation rose to 2.8% y/y from 2.6% y/

Our view: The stronger inflation reading reinforces our base case for the 
Bank of England to raise its policy rate in November, followed by another 
hike in the first quarter of 2027 to 4.25%. The breadth of the increase 
matters: Higher services and core inflation suggest the impact is not 
confined to energy, increasing the risk that higher costs feed into wages 
and business pricing. The government therefore faces a difficult balancing 
act in next week’s budget between supporting households and businesses 
and maintaining confidence in the path of public borrowing. Higher UK 
rates for longer could support sterling against the Swiss franc through its 
relative yield advantage, consistent with our GBPCHF carry-trade view.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the collective 
judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total returns on liquid 
and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
