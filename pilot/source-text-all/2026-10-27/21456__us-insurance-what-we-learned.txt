Thoughts from early 3Q26 earnings and a look ahead at EG, HIG, BRO
and RYAN
Our view: Earnings season will accelerate over the next few weeks, following an initial tranche of MRSH
and several large non-covered names (CB, TRV, WRB). In this note, we take a moment to highlight
the week ahead, with particular focus on upcoming earnings from EG, HIG, BRO and RYAN (plus 7
other names set to report in coverage by next Friday). Additionally, we take a look at two key topics
that have come up repeatedly on the initial tranche of 3Q reports: pricing competition and artificial
intelligence. On pricing, there are no signs of relief and CB once again sounded the alarm on Casualty
rate below trend (WRB did not indicate much positivity as well). Pricing commentary is likely to remain
negative as underwriting margins remain strong, investment yields push higher and major reinsurance
renewals (at January 1) approach. On AI, companies are increasingly citing tangible benefits, primarily
on underwriting efficiency (submission turnaround, quoting) and claims. It appears some of the gains
will be leveraged to drive incremental growth and could compound the existing competitive dynamics.
The Week Ahead: Earnings season accelerates next week, with 9 covered names (ACGL, AJG, AXS, BRO,
EG, HIG, RNR, RYAN, THG plus KNSL and SIGI tomorrow) set to report in addition to a range of non-
covered names (AON, CINF, WTW most notably). We provide updated preview tables (in the appendix
on pages 11-16) and incremental thoughts. In coverage, we are paying closest attention to EG, where
we expect a reserve charge (link), and HIG, which had a busy intra-quarter with the NICO settlement
(link) and recently announced CEO transition. Elsewhere, we are watching BRO and RYAN closely (AJG
effectively pre-announced 3Q at their quarterly IR day - link). On recent marketing (link) BRO came up
as a name where people saw limited downside and were incrementally positive, though we note the
stock has bounced 15% since. Beyond individual names, we are hyper-focused on capital deployment,
particularly at sub-1.5x BVPS names (ACGL, AXS, EG, RNR), and signs that investment yields are lowering
underwriting margin targets / pressuring pricing in long-tail lines.
Pricing Appears to Decelerate, with Some Exceptions: As expected, we heard some commentary about
continued pressure in long-tail lines of business, with CB in particular calling out pricing below loss
trend in casualty lines, partially derived from a variety of new entrants into the market. Similarly, pricing
results from TRV's BI units showed sequential declines in all three categories and exposure growth not
the primary driver across all three of its BI subsets. WRB indicated more pain to come in property at
1/1 renewals, and MRSH added there was adequate capacity in the reinsurance space, helping their
clients achieve better rate outcomes as buyers. Despite all this commentary, the impact on margins has
been fairly benign and results have been strong. Estimate revisions from non-covered names appear to
reflect the shift higher in investment yields, which continues to improve core earnings for the industry.
AI Gains Increasingly in Focus: The topic of AI liability has been in the news and came up on a handful of
calls, but what we believe is more important were the incremental data points on AI efficiencies. Carriers
cited improved speed of turnaround in recent quarterly calls / investor days (we highlight THG's IR day
slide on the topic on page 8). There seems to be a consensus around roughly 20% quoting efficiency
gains, with WRB and several others citing a number in that range. TRV noted they could leverage some
of their expense efficiencies and scale advantages into pricing to grow, and MRSH noted they have
been successful in participating alongside clients, particularly in Marsh Management Consulting, with a
shift towards success-driven fees. All-in, the AI narrative continues to gain traction and becomes more
tangible as we look at outlooks for companies. While many are not yet highlighting financial targets,
some have and we expect continued interest in specific financial impacts as tech investments mature.

Everest Group (EG)
We are re-highlighting our recent initiation of Everest Group at Outperform (link) and our view
that we expect a 3Q26 reserve charge, which we believe could be a clearing event and refocus
investors on meaningful reductions in volatility due to the shift higher in investment yields. We
note the company re-cast financials in an 8-K this week, providing further support to our view
that management is preparing to signal a clean break in results, elevating the chance of a reserve
charge and shift to a more conservative stance. Our expectation is driven by 4 factors:
1. Everest hired a new outside CFO (Elias Habayeb effective May 2026) offering fresh eyes on a
US reserve portfolio subject to high loss trend and prior reserve challenges.
○On the 2Q26 earnings call, new CFO Elias Habayeb indicated "the annual reserve studies
for most of our long tail lines, including Reinsurance Treaty business, are scheduled to be
completed later in the third quarter. And Katy and I will be heavily involved in that process."
2. The company completes its long-tail reserve studies in the third quarter and has been
shrinking Treaty lines with US casualty exposure.
3. In our experience, Adverse Development Covers tend to be utilized and EG has co-
participation on its 2025 ADC of $100M/layer.
4. We believe the lack of major catastrophic losses in the last 18 months creates some
opportunity to take a more cautious view on long-tail lines.
EG shares are widely discounted (at 0.9x trailing BVPS, ~6.5x 2027e consensus EPS) to both peers
(which trade closer to 1.2x trailing BVPS, 8.5x 2027e consensus) and the company's own history
(~1.15x trailing BVPS, ~8x earnings). This discount to recent history is notable as the company's
earnings are largely driven by fixed income investments (NII is 86% of pre-tax income in our
2027 estimates). We believe higher yields have meaningfully lowered volatility in results, which
is underappreciated at the current valuation:

Hartford (HIG)
We believe Hartford has one of the more complicated earnings set-ups. Intra-quarter the
company announced the acquisition of EQH's Employee Benefits Business (8/4, closed earlier
this month) and the resolution of the NICO ADC dispute (9/25 via 8-k: link). Additionally, in early
October the company announced a CEO transition and we continue to note consensus appears
to mis-model cat losses in forward annuals.
As we head into the print, we think there is some risk of a negative BI reserve surprise, but note
the NICO resolution is likely to drive accelerated buybacks and there is incremental upside to
consensus next year on the cat XOL treaty. In this tape, we expect a BI reserve charge would
result in a pullback, though we generally believe interest rate moves and accelerated buybacks
could result in upwards revisions. Quick thoughts on the 4 moving items:
1. NICO Resolution Could Skew Headline C/R: The headline combined ratio in the quarter is
likely to be fairly useless, as we expect the NICO ADC gain ($497M pre-tax) will flow through as
PYD (in Other P&C) but be adjusted out of core earnings. We estimate 3Q26 earned premiums
of $4,645M, which means the NICO gain is a roughly 10.7% release on the consolidated
combined ratio (we do not model it in the C/R as it is not a part of core earnings).
2. Seasonality Could Drive BI Reserve Issues: Ex-NICO gain, we remain worried on seasonality
of releases in BI and believe core favorable development in the unit could be lower than
modeled by peers (we model 0.5 points favorable vs 1.2 point consensus). HIG appears to
review Bond and Catastrophe reserves in 2Q/4Q and releases have been significant in those
lines. To the extent General Liability reserve challenges continue ($116M charges in 1H26)
or Auto Liability repeats ($26M charge in 2Q26), we believe there is a risk that BI takes a
charge in 3Q26.
3. NICO Cash Could Drive Accelerated Buyback: To the positive, while we believe there is a risk
of a BI reserve charge, the NICO gain should drive healthy BVPS growth (~$1.45/share in our
model) and the incremental $1.12B of cash from the transaction should allow the company
to accelerate buybacks.
4. Consensus 2027/28 EPS Models Cats Wrong, Worth ~0.5x on Forward P/E: HIG's aggregate
catastrophe XOL treaty attaches at $750M of losses and has $200M of coverage. With
consensus estimates north of $900M in both 2027 and 2028, it would imply the company
either blows through the XOL treaty (which it has never done) or moves the attachment point
up (which doesn't make a lot of sense in softening reinsurance market). In our view, this
mis-modeling leaves about $0.45/share of upside to 2027e consensus and ~$0.60/share of
upside to 2028e consensus.

Pricing Appears to Decelerate, with Some Exceptions
Unsurprisingly, pricing has been a key focus on 3Q26 calls in the P&C universe. Little appears
changed as property pricing remains negative and there are incremental signs of deceleration in
longer-tail lines of business. Capital is abundant and there were signs of incremental pressure,
whether it was CB again sounding the alarm on casualty, TRV indicating yields and AI could
impact their pricing decisions, and WRB expecting more pain at 1/1.
This all said, we remind investors that the pickup in investment yields (we think the 5-year
Treasury is the best single yield to monitor as a proxy) is often more than offsetting underwriting
pressures in terms of earnings outlook for many carriers.
Chubb Sees Rate Below Trend in Casualty, Naive Capital
Chubb once again warned that pricing competition is spreading to casualty lines as new capital
enters the space and returns remain high for traditional carriers. While Evan Greenberg did not
go as far as saying these competitive dynamics represented a return to cash flow underwriting,
he did refer to the new capital behind fast-follow facilities, sidecars and MGAs as naive and
setting up a hardening cycle in the future.
"In terms of pricing, the market continues to grow more competitive in commercial P&C across
the globe. In particular, the US and London. Beyond property, competitive market conditions are
spreading to more casualty lines, where pricing in numerous areas is failing to keep pace with
loss costs, which while steady continue to well outpace general inflation. There is a lot of hungry
and extremely naive capacity available." - Evan Greenberg, Chairman & CEO of CB
"So sidecars and broker facilities, blindfollow facilities, more MGAs with capital behind them
other than professional insurance and frankly, a lot with professional insurance behind it is all
throwing out of balance. The supply relative to demand and it's a lot of very naive capacity." -
Evan Greenberg, Chairman & CEO of CB
"I think a lot of that clever capital doesn't understand the pricing versus the loss cost environment.
And I don't think they're going to end up making the turn, but they think they might make. But in
a word, we've been living in a world where the cost of money is cheap and there's a lot of money
looking for absolute return not risk adjusted. As you know, generally the world is beginning to
change that way. But in the meantime, a lot of it is found its way or a fraction of it is found its
way into insurance as sort of an alternative investment asset class." - Evan Greenberg, Chairman
& CEO of CB

Unsurprisingly, one of the key themes this earnings season is a continued refining of AI
integration expectations. Companies are citing increased efficiency in intake, triage and quoting
of submissions, and an improvement in claims efficiency as key areas of tangible AI benefits
today. Compute costs have not come up as an issue, but companies remain reticent to cite
specific financial impacts (primarily margins) from AI rollout.

could drive incremental competition to the extent more carriers are able to quote business faster.
