Fed in the spotlight in a busier week 
Week ahead 
◼ 
We are set for a much busier week containing the October FOMC meeting 
plus a slew of notable data releases. Highlights for the latter include PCE 
inflation, the advance Q326 GDP report and personal income & spending, 
among others. We would caveat up front that we are writing this on 
16 October as we will be traveling the week of 19-23 October, so we 
reserve the right to change forecasts once we return based on data 
released in the interim. 
◼ 
The October FOMC meeting has become a closer call following upside 
surprises for both CPI and PPI in September, though we continue to lean 
towards a skip in October followed by another hike in December. We tend 
to think that consecutive hikes would be the better approach for the Fed 
to take, but our job is to project what the Fed will do and not what we 
think the Fed should do. 
◼ 
Yet several influential Fed members including New York Fed President 
John Williams, Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and Governor Christopher 
Waller have hinted at a preference to stay on hold in October to gather 
more information about how the economy and data evolves. Waller 
explicitly said that while he thinks the Fed will likely need to hike more, 
it has “flexibility” on the timing as hikes do not need to come at 
consecutive meetings. Thus, we think that this group has a bias towards 
holding and would need to be convinced to act in October, with the 
inflation data not quite reaching that bar, even if we do not think the 
decision is a given. We will offer more thoughts (and a final call) in our 
Fed preview to be published when we return on Monday. 
◼ 
We will not see September PCE until the day after the Fed meeting, but 
expectations are for a firm report based on CPI and PPI. Here we look for 
core PCE to rise around 0.33% MoM, so rounding up to 0.4% MoM cannot 
be ruled out, with the YoY pace edging up to 3.1% from 3.0%. We expect 
headline PCE to be up 0.5% MoM to take the YoY pace up to 3.6% from 
3.4%. This is clearly not ideal and should keep additional hikes on the 
table later on, such as in December, though we are not sure it quite 
reaches the bar for the broader committee to agree on an October hike. 
◼ 
Also in this report, we look for personal income to rise 0.2% MoM and 
personal spending to rise 0.5% MoM, meaning that real spending would 
be relatively soft given expectations for the PCE deflator. That said, a 
softer month would be coming on the heels of a very strong August, so 
overall consumption is on track for another solid quarter in the 3% range. 
◼ 
With consumption likely to remain strong and non-residential investment 
tracking another robust quarter, we look for headline GDP growth to 
accelerate to 3.0% QoQ SAAR compared to 2.2% in Q226, though 
underlying details were stronger than the headline last quarter. This 
would indicate that the economy has remained resilient overall through 
Q326, despite the ongoing war in Iran. 
◼ 
Elsewhere, we look for a strong increase in headline durable goods 
orders given a surge in Boeing orders, though the headline figure can be 
noisy with underlying details generally having been solid this year. 
Consumer confidence is likely to be weaker, though this has not 
provided a great signal for actual spending recently, with new home 
sales expected on the softer side as well.

US Economic Outlook 
◼ 
Despite large amounts of noise created by the somewhat chaotic 
approach to policy shifts emanating from Washington, the US economy 
generally held up in 2025, even if the pace of growth slowed from 2024. 
Going forward, we think the policy backdrop may turn more constructive 
in 2026, helping to drive a similar pace of growth this year despite 
headwinds from the Iran war. 
Note: Any changes in the view from last week in italics 
Annual average growth came in at 2.3% in 2025, modestly above our estimate 
heading into the year. Moving forward, we look for a similar pace of 2.3% in 
2026 before a small step down to 2.1% in 2027. Still, we have taken 2026 growth 
down from 2.5% due to the Iran war, which should provide some drag, even if the 
US is less vulnerable than many others. 
Outside of the war, policy changes continue to play a key role in our outlook, 
as we think fiscal support from the Big, Beautiful Bill and a diminishing drag 
from trade policy & related uncertainty create a more constructive backdrop 
in 2026. Continued strength in AI-related investment and a potential boost to 
productivity could also provide some support, though we acknowledge there are 
risks from the more concentrated nature of recent growth, referred to as the 
K-shaped economy. 
With growth slowing but still solidly positive and net immigration dropping 
sharply, the labour market has cooled but not fallen off a cliff. We continue to 
see the unemployment rate’s 4.5% print last November as representing a peak and 
look for stabilisation in the low-4% range to persist through yearend and into 2027. 
However, the possibility of AI beginning to replace certain job functions could act 
as a headwind and contributes to only a slow grind lower following the peak. 
We had already expected inflation to struggle to return to 2% over our 
forecast horizon, and the spike in energy due to the Iran war adds further 
upside risks to forecasts that we have already revised higher. For now, we 
have headline CPI peaking at 4.2% YoY in May and remaining above 3.0% into 
early 2027, before dipping back to the low 2.0% YoY range starting in Q227. We 
see less of an impact on core but still have core hovering between about 2.4-2.7% 
YoY through early 2027 and only slowing to around 2.3% by end-2027. 
Given the resilience of the economy and stickiness of inflation, which has 
been more acute with regards to PCE inflation, the Fed delivered a hike in 
September, and we expect more to come. We see two additional 25bp hikes in 
December and March to take the upper bound to back 4.50%, reversing the late 
2025 insurance cuts, though see consecutive hikes as a risk. 
That said, the improvement in our inflation forecast that materialises in H227 
could open up some room for a return to cuts, though only modest easing 
given that we see inflation stabilising slightly above 2% and believe the 
neutral rate has moved higher as well. Specifically, we project two 25bp cuts in 
Q427 to take the upper bound to 4.00%, before the Fed then pauses once again.
