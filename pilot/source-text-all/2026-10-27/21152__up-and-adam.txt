IG credit spreads remain low by historical standards at 85 bps but have widened 5 bps from last month’s lows amid higher rate 
volatility, softer fund flows, and increased scrutiny of AI financing. Notably, Technology led the move, with spreads widening 13 bps 
MTD to 100 bps, their widest since April 2025. Still, moderating Treasury yields have cushioned the impact, helping the IG benchmark 
index gain 1.1% MTD, outperforming high-yield. With volatility rising, we continue to favor staying up in quality.

Last week, we highlighted improving S&P 500 breadth, with October gains led by value-centric sectors. However, participation remains 
uneven: 37% of S&P 500 stocks are still negative YTD, 44% trade below their 50-day moving average, and the average stock remains 
22% below its record high. Here is another way to frame it: the S&P 500 is up 12.7% YTD, but the median stock is up just 6.3%. For broad-
based strength to return, we believe that bond yields would need to post additional declines from currently elevated level.
As the world’s largest producer of semiconductors, Taiwan plays a key role in the AI infrastructure buildout. Case in point: Taiwan 
exported a record $87.2B in September—up 61% YoY—with IT-related products more than doubling. Likewise, new orders—a gauge of 
future exports—rose 62% to a record $114B, with IT accounting for 80% of the total. Orders from US buyers climbed to a record $45B, 
reflecting robust data center demand for advanced microchips. This data reinforces our positive outlook on the Technology sector.
