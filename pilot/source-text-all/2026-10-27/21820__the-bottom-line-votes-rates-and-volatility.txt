TOP STORIES OF THE UPCOMING WEEK
Israel election: a referendum on Netanyahu? 
On 27 October, 7.3mn Israelis will elect a new parliament. 38 parties are competing for 120 seats in the 
Knesset albeit only around 11 are expected to enter it. To enter parliament, a party needs 3.25% of the votes, 
which gives smaller parties leverage in the coalition bargaining process. The two main competing blocs are 
the incumbent alliance of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu – his right-wing Likud party, two ultra-orthodox 
parties (Shas and United Torah Judaism) as well as the religious nationalist parties Otzma Yehudit (led by 
Itamar Ben-Gvir) and the Religious Zionist Party (led by Bezalel Smotrich) – and the “change bloc”, led by 
former IDF chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot and his recently founded Yashar party, as well as former PM Naftali 
Bennett’s centre-right B’Yachad, Avigdor Liberman’s secular nationalist right-wing Yisrael Beiteinu and Yair 
Golan’s centre-left The Democrats. The election outcome is highly uncertain: latest polls by Channel 11 point 
to 51 seats for the Netanyahu block vs 52 for the Eisenkot bloc, while a Channel 14 poll suggests 61 seats for 
Netanyahu vs 46 seats for the Eisenkot bloc. While the vote may be a referendum on Netanyahu, the 
ramifications are likely to extend beyond domestic politics. A victory for Netanyahu’s bloc would most likely 
imply continuity in security policy and expansion of the West Bank settlements.

Fed on hold, waiting for December   
We expect the Fed to keep interest rates unchanged on Wednesday. The probability of a rate hike is low. 
Recent comments by Fed Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams indicate that 
the Fed can remain patient in its tightening as pressure at the long end of the UST curve builds. A weak NFP 
report for September strengthens the case for no action at this meeting. Although the FOMC statement and 
Fed Chair Kevin Warsh will likely refrain from providing forward guidance, we expect the tone of the press 
conference to validate our and market forecasts that the next rate move will occur in December. Next week’s 
data will probably confirm that higher policy rates are warranted. We forecast solid GDP growth of about 3% 
annualised for 3Q26, while core PCE prices for September are likely to have increased by 0.3% mom and 3.0% 
yoy. 
October pause for the ECB  
The ECB will almost certainly stand pat at its meeting next Thursday, remaining on track for a further increase 
in interest rates at its December meeting, as inflation risks continue to point to the upside. The decision is 
likely to be unanimous. Rising long-term rates have tightened financial conditions, buying the ECB time to 
better assess incoming data. The reference macro picture is one of intensifying energy pressure on headline 
inflation, broadly stable core inflation, ongoing moderate economic growth and no evidence of second-round 
effects through wages. Sovereign tensions have increased, but have not escalated, remaining far from levels 
that would push the ECB to seriously consider action. At the press conference, ECB President Christine Lagarde 
is likely to show confidence in the central bank’s toolbox to cope with risks to the transmission mechanism 
of monetary policy.  
Euro-area growth expected to have held up in 3Q26  
Setting aside the volatility of Irish GDP data, we expect euro-area activity to have expanded by 0.2% qoq in the third 
quarter, supported mainly by fixed investment, particularly capital goods and construction. Risks to our forecast 
appear broadly balanced amid relatively weak hard data and more resilient survey indicators. At the country level, 
a divergence between hard and soft data is particularly evident in Italy. Leading indicators based on the monthly 
data available so far suggest a broad stabilisation in activity following two quarters of moderate growth (around 
0.2% qoq). However, industrial value added and services activity may turn out stronger than currently anticipated, 
implying upside risks to our forecast. In Germany and France, GDP likely expanded by 0.1% qoq in the third quarter, 
while Spain is expected to have continued to outperform its main euro-area peers, posting growth of 0.6% qoq. 
RBNZ to continue tightening, Boc and BoJ will likely remain on hold 
Three other central banks are scheduled to meet in the coming days. The RBNZ is set to continue on its 
tightening path due to persisting inflation pressure in New Zealand, probably lifting the official cash rate 
(OCR) by another 25bp to 3.00%. By contrast, the BoC and the BoJ are likely to remain on hold, at 2.25% and 
1.25%, respectively. The BoC prefers to wait for further developments regarding prices and geopolitical risks. 
With Governor Kazuo Ueda warning that underlying inflation is approaching the 2% target, the BoJ is set to 
call for another rate hike by December. 
Brazil: a narrow margin ahead of Sunday’s runoff; BRL to react sharply, regardless of the outcome  
The second round of Brazil’s presidential election will be held on 25 October. The latest polls show a very tight 
margin between the two candidates, with right-wing candidate Flavio Bolsonaro at 50.5% and incumbent 
President Inácio Lula da Silva, who has gained ground by proposing more (costly) labour market reforms, at 49.5%. 
USD/BRL has rebounded to 5.0650 from the low of 4.9452 reached after the first round of voting. The outcome is 
binary: a victory for Bolsonaro would diminish the political risk premium associated with loose fiscal policy, drag 
USD-BRL below the 4.8858 low seen on 11 May and support Brazilian stocks and bonds. By contrast, a victory for 
Lula would push USD/BRL to its pre-election range of 5.20-5.30, potentially reopening the path toward a technical 
target of 5.50, and Brazilian assets would come under pressure. As discussed in our Long View, the election in 
Brazil will also have an impact on the US-China rivalry in the Western Hemisphere.

EU-Canada summit: a digital trade deal within reach? 
At their 29-30 October summit in Montreal, the EU and Canada aim to deepen their economic ties and reduce 
strategic dependencies on the US and China. One potential deliverable is a digital trade agreement (DTA), 
complementing the Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA), with rules on cross-border data 
flows, data localisation and software protection. Negotiators have been seeking to resolve outstanding 
differences before the summit. Other priorities include securing European access to Canadian critical minerals, 
particularly nickel, graphite and rare earths, and closer defence industrial cooperation. The summit will also 
explore European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen’s proposal to make Canada the EU’s first-ever 
associate member – an entirely new form of partnership whose legal framework and economic implications 
are to be defined. For Prime Minister Mark Carney, the broader objective is to diversify Canadian trade away 
from the US. The key question is whether the summit will produce binding commitments rather than broad 
political declarations.

WHAT WE ARE READING AND LISTENING TO 
The Architecture of Money – A European Strategy for Tokenized Finance › 
In this French Institute of International Relations (Ifri) report, Bastien Buchwalter 
argues that tokenization could transform European financial sovereignty by 
enabling new forms of financial integration while also creating risks of 
dependence on non-European infrastructures and dollar-based stablecoins. He 
states that Europe needs robust settlement systems, competitive digital euros 
and effective oversight to maintain sovereignty as finance fragments into distinct 
tokenized ecosystems. 
Money and the architecture of trust › 
According to this BIS Papers analysis, money is a social institution built on 
interconnected promises and trust. It operates through a hierarchy of money and 
credit, anchored by central banks, with elastic credit constrained by funding and 
public backstops. The author argues that money is neither a commodity nor 
technology, and contrasts this framework with gold, bitcoin and stablecoins. 
Israel Election Special, Bibi at the abyss or another victory? › 
This podcast discusses whether Benjamin Netanyahu ("Bibi") is facing a genuine 
risk of losing power or can secure another mandate despite political 
fragmentation and ongoing security challenges. This episode examines electoral 
arithmetic, coalition-building prospects, the balance between ideological and 
pragmatic alliances, and the implications for Israeli governance after the vote.
