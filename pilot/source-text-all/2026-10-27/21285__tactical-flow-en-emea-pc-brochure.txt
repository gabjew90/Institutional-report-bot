Equity ETPs attracted $6.1B last week, down from $9.6B during the week prior. Developed market equities once again 
lead inflows with a net buy of $6B relative to $103m into EM exposures.
Within Developed Market equities, inflows were led by Global exposures which recorded a total net buy of $3.9B, albeit 
a reduction relative to the $4.8B seen during the week prior, while North American exposures trailed behind with 
$1.8B of net buying. Further inflows were also seen into DM APAC ($547m) and DM EU ($73m). 
On a single-country level, inflows were dominated by US equities, which recorded a total net buy of $1.8B, a 
moderation from the $3.4B of inflows seen during the week prior, bringing total YTD inflows to $64.4B for the 
exposure and representing the third straight week of net inflows. Further inflows were also seen into Japanese 
($281m), Swiss ($246m), and Swedish ($64m) equities. In contrast, outflows were seen from French equities, with a 
net sell of -$79m, for the third straight week.

Emerging market inflows remained relatively muted last week with a net buy of $103m, down slightly from the $193m 
of inflows seen during the week prior. Inflows were led by Broad EM exposures, which saw $278m of inflows, 
representing a moderate rise from the $191m of inflows seen during the week prior. In contrast, outflows were seen 
from EM single country exposures which experienced a net sell of -$100m while regional exposures followed closely 
behind with a  net sell of -$75m. 
From a regional perspective, outflows were led by Latin American exposures, which recorded a net sell of -$69m, 
representing the eighth straight week of outflows and bringing YTD net outflows to -$101m. Further outflows were 
also seen from APAC with a net sell of -$8m. Minor inflows were seen into EM Europe ($2m).
From a single country perspective, outflows were led by China which saw a net sell of -$340m, representing a sharp 
reversal from the $30m of inflows seen during the week prior. Outflows were also seen from Indian (-$13m) and 
Mexican (-$2m) equities. In contrast, inflows were seen into Brazilian ($20m) and South Korean ($64m) equities.
