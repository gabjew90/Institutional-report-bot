Volatility under the surface  
Like on Monday, IG credit remained volatile on Tuesday despite the calmer equity and 
rates markets. The liquid IG index was little changed on the day. However, the intra-day 
range was a big 3bps. That’s 2.2 standard deviations of the liquid index daily spread 
move so far in 2026 (the YtD std is 1.3bps). On a 10d MA basis, the intraday range for IG 
doubled from 1.0bps in early September to 2.3bps currently (Exhibit 1).  
IG lagging 
While volatility increased across the board over the past few months, it has been 
declining since early October for interest rates, but not credit. For example, both rates 
implied vol and the intra-day range for 10yr Treasury yields peaked around October 5th 
(Exhibit 4). The intra-day range for IG spreads, by contrast, remains near the recent peak 
levels (Exhibit 3). That’s likely due to 1) poorer liquidity in IG, 2) market being driven by 
the very volatile AI sector where investors are not yet willing to step in, 3) upcoming 
potential hyperscaler supply. IG volatility is slowing down with a lag, but it should catch 
up to the broader markets eventually. A less volatile credit market would be bullish for 
spreads.

Daily HG fund flows

income funds and ETFs domiciled in the US (and investing in any currency, although the 
majority is invested in USD). We also exclude funds primarily focused on Government, 
HY, EM and MBS from the sample. The "bond" category refers to the broad high grade 
market, which includes Treasuries, mortgages and agencies in addition to corporate 
bonds. This category accounts for the majority of AUM and flows.

Asian demand) while Exhibit 15 and Exhibit 16 show net dealer-to-affiliates volumes 
between 8am and noon (biased toward European demand). Exhibit 17 shows the 
relationship between foreign buying and change in treasury yields. A drop in yield 
typically leads to lower foreign demand, though not always.
