When US equity leadership narrows, US dollar rally broadens 
The S&P 500 has been largely rangebound since August but far more ink has been 
spilled on the decline in market breadth. Poor equity breadth has connotations for the 
durability of the risk rally but may also matter for the broader direction of the US dollar. 
Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 2 show loose correlation between the DXY index and measures of 
equity breadth. A narrow rally – a smaller proportion of stocks trading above 200dma 
and/or the underperformance of an equally-weighted S&P 500 index – is often 
associated with a stronger USD.

Equity breadth a barometer of US exceptionalism vs. global convergence 
Intuitively, such breadth measures tell us about the US vs. rest of the world prospects, 
which are key for the USD exchange rate. A broad rally typically reflects a global growth 
upswing, with cyclical sectors outperforming and growth differentials versus the US 
narrowing. In contrast, a technology-led rally reinforces the US exceptionalism narrative, 
attracting capital inflows and supporting the USD.  
USD has been supported by the confluence of US technology exceptionalism and 
(related) growth outperformance vs. the rest of the world in recent months. But the 
stable-to-weaker USD as equities have sold off in October is consistent with AI risks 
undermining the exceptionalism narrative. We test this hypothesis more robustly below.  
Breadth matters for USD beyond level and rates effect 
We regress weekly FX returns since 2015 on the relative performance of SPW (equally 
weighted S&P 500) vs. SPX. We control for the S&P 500 index move, as well as UST 2y 
rates, to help judge whether breadth matters beyond these traditional drivers. Exhibit 3 
shows the estimated coefficients (betas) to these factors. We highlight the following. 
• 
The sensitivities to the traditional drivers are the “correct” sign and all statistically 
significant, except for JPY’s beta to S&P 500. We have long noted that the changing 
structure of Japan’s net foreign assets (skewing toward FDI rather than portfolio 
investments) means the yen no longer benefits as much from risk-off episodes. 
Instead, front-end US rates are the dominant driver. 
• 
Equity breadth has its own effect – it matters beyond the level changes & 2y rates, 
and the betas are consistent with the explanation above, i.e. USD outperforms on a 
narrow equity rally. JPY is again the only currency where the breadth beta is not 
statistically significant, although the relative performance of Japan vs. global stocks 
is still key in our view (see report: FX Viewpoint 15 June 2026). For DXY, a 1% rise in 
SPW vs. SPX is associated with 0.2% depreciation.

The most cyclical currencies – the Nordics & Antipodeans – have the highest betas

read-through for global growth divergence/convergence vs. the US. 
USD & risk off – breadth nuances 
Looking ahead, what would a transition away from a narrow rally regime imply? A 
broadening would be USD negative from both the equity level & breadth effects, most 
clearly against high beta FX. An equity correction would be more nuanced: the level 
effect would be USD positive (except vs. JPY) but dampened if the sell-off is 
concentrated in large cap technology stocks. The breadth sensitivity exceeds level 
sensitivity for most pairs, especially EUR/USD.  
For illustration, a 10% SPX decline with 6pp outperformance of SPW is broadly neutral 
for DXY. In this scenario, the rates channel would likely dominate: a correction driven by 
US growth concerns and lower front-end rates would favour JPY and CHF longs, while a 
correction driven by higher yields, like 2022, would be more broadly USD positive.
