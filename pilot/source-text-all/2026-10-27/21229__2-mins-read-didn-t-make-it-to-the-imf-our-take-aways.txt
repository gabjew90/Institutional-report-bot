Didn’t make it to the IMF?
 
Major take-aways from the GS team that met policymakers + investors in Bangkok:
 
8 bullets:
 
1/ "Uneasy resilience”: Surprise on how well DM + EM absorbed the energy shock, but scepticism this resilience
lasts. Consensus: Inflation risks skew higher; central banks lean hawkish; investors more hawkish on DM monetary
policy than GS.
2/ Geo-Politics ->  Middle East re-escalation a clear, imminent concern; little pushback on GS view that more oil is
getting through Hormuz; no-one expects any break-through on Russia/Ukraine. 
3/ AI’s Impact on Macro a big point of discussion: Conversation centred around: Capex-driven inflation;
productivity; labour-market politics; AI's role in driving rates + USD (especially last week's Rates-rally / dollar-
weakness. Real Money clients have cautious bias to add US duration. However, fiscal dynamics, hyperscaler issuance
+ non-US yields seen as capping any rally. What could cause the Fed to turn? "when US equities break."
4/ Fiscal: UK looks good; France + Poland on watch:
-> France: Consensus that the market will force adjustment, not politics; absence of spread fragmentation raises the
bar for ECB/EU intervention.
-> UK: Positive vibes on UK, as adjustment is already underway; landing a credible Budget could extend UK asset
outperformance.
-> Poland: Consensus too many hikes priced, but worry inflation is about more than energy; Consolidation hard given
standoff between President + Government
5/ EM: Still constructive: Anchored inflation, stable FX + credible central banks justify EM outperformance, but
spreads to the US are now tight + policy is diverging:
-> India + South Africa are turning hawkish to protect their currencies, while Mexico stays dovish
-> Long BRL (FX + rates) strongest consensus trade. 
-> Other focus areas were Colombia (IMF programme speculation) + Hungary
-> Key risk seen as more robust Fed hikes + stronger USD.
6/ Asia: Rare pessimism on China/India, Korea the favourite.
-> China + India: For the first time in recent memory, the room was gloomy on both. China: Expectations have shifted
from a fiscal push to spark demand to Beijing raising revenue for mild consolidation. India: AI is seen as a headwind to
growth; foreign equity positioning remains heavy.
-> Korea: KRW the top Asia long, supported by exports, fiscal revenues + repatriation. KRW rates the only Asian rates
market clients like, via front-end receivers + flatteners.
-> High-yielders: Consensus bearish on PHP, IDR + INR, and on EM Asia rates broadly, since US yields above 5%
exceed most local yields.
7/ Turkey vs Nigeria: Frontier carry – Diverging Views:
-> Turkey: Consensus notably more cautious, TRY positioning lightened. Most agree now is not the time to cut but
expect the central bank to cut anyway, given its dovish guidance + history of easing early. 
-> Nigeria: Central bank more willing to let NGN appreciate while guiding rates lower. Reserves are on track to be well
above USD 60bn by year-end, FX backlog to foreigners is essentially cleared.

-> Other: Consensus less constructive on Egypt; Kazakhstan still constructive but less so on FX tactically. Policymaker
feedback on Lebanon and Ukraine was negative.
8/ South Africa: Rating upgrades ahead, January hike in play. No pushback on our constructive fiscal view; we
expect upgrades from S&P first, then Moody's. November hike looks unlikely if the Fed holds in October, but January
hike is plausible, and the SARB sounded balanced. On politics, ANC losses in Johannesburg + other key areas could
strain the Coalition. 
 
Thoughts welcome – Adam

Andrew Tilton (Chief Asia Pacific Economist) + Danny Suwanapruti (Head of Asia EM FX & Rates Strategy) +
Santanu Sengupta (Chief India Economist): “Key takeaways:
 
i) Policy-makers - Resilient growth so far, but headwinds to growth and upside inflation risks are emerging amid
elevated energy prices,
ii) Risk-takers - AI capex cycle remains at the center of the debate
iii) Real money funds - Increasing intent to add US duration risk
iv) Global EM - Strong consensus in favour of Brazil
v) Asia – Bullish KRW, bearish Asian high-yield (PHP, IDR and INR).
 
The feedback from our own GS forum, as well as broader client feedback was that policy makers have been
surprised by the relative resilience of growth around the world, despite the large energy shock. However, going
forward, they did not want to become too complacent that the stronger than expected growth will last, while inflation
risks are still to the upside. Global central banks leaned slightly more hawkish. Mood on the Middle East is that
investors were worried that re-escalation is more likely than de-escalation -- hence energy price and inflation
worries.
 
On US rates, clients appeared increasingly interested in adding US duration at current levels, while remaining
mindful that several structural factors — including US fiscal dynamics, hyperscaler debt issuance, productivity, and
non-US core yields — could limit the scope for a significant Treasury rally. As a result, while yields are viewed as
elevated, the prevailing mood is one of selective buying rather than strong conviction to be long. 
 
Several discussions revolved around “what could turn US yields lower” and most seemed to agree that “when US
equities break” is what would prompt the Fed to change direction. This comes back to AI capex, which is inter-linked
with US yields.
 
On global EM, we had representation of clients from LATAM, US, EU, CEMEEA and Asia at our lunch/dinner
roundtables. Strong consensus on being long BRL (FX and rates). In Asia, most still believed in KRW FX (exports,
excess fiscal revenues, repatriation), while bearish across on high-yielders (PHP, IDR and INR). 
 
By the end of week, the focus had shifted to news that MSCI is considering lowering Indonesia’s equity index
classification from EM to standalone (a double downgrade rather than a single downgrade to frontier), clearly
negative for the IDR FX and bonds. Our Asia equity strategists just published on this, highlighting that it could prompt
around USD 5.6bn of passive outflows from MSCI tracked indices (another USD 2.7bn from FTSE, if they follow),
whilst there is another USD 8bn of active funds in Indonesia equities.
 

 On EM Asia rates, investors were bearish across all markets, since US yields at 5%+ are above most EM Asia
markets, while central banks are neutral / hawkish (with more hikes in the pipeline). The exception is KRW rates,
where clients like both front-end receivers and/or flatteners.”
 
Andrew Matheny (CEEMEA Senior Economist): “Broad feedback across EMs was constructive, justifying the EM-
DM yield compression on the grounds of well-anchored inflation, stable FX and central bank credibility as well as
improving balance sheets (in select EMs). 
 
Market concerns on US rate cycle and growth boost from AI capex boom justify more hawkish pricing for the DM
policy rate outlook in the eyes of investors than our baseline house forecast. In particular, Warsh emphasized the
“massive investment surge” and “synchronous monetary tightening around the world”. A hiking cycle from the Fed and
strengthening of the USD represent the key risk to our broadly dovish EM inflation and rates outlook. 
 
In terms of specific EMs, there was a big focus on Brazil (elections), Colombia (speculation on IMF program) and
Hungary (Euro convergence). Feedback in South Africa was balanced but nonetheless incrementally hawkish from
the SARB (we met with three MPC members). 
 
Additional focus on frontier carry trades: Nigeria (more conviction on stronger FX), Egypt (turning less constructive),
Turkey (more cautious), Kazakhstan (still constructive, but less so on FX tactically). Finally, among distressed credits,
broadly negative feedback from policymaker meetings including Lebanon and Ukraine (although our outlook for
financing in the latter is more constructive).”
 
Kamakshya Trivedi (Chief FX & EM Strategist): “Uneasy Resilience: There was both surprise at how well the
global economy, both DM and EM, had held up in the face of supply shocks so far, but also a clear sense of unease
at whether a combination of higher energy and tighter financial conditions were now beginning to cause cracks in
places where fundamentals were questionable and valuations rich (examples included Euro Area sovereign
spreads, parts of AI complex, MXN).
 
Focus on Fiscal/UK: Given that the meetings were happening against the backdrop of rising sovereign yields, no
surprise that there was much discussion on ‘rebuilding fiscal space’. Contrary to some of the markets conversation in
London, the UK actually came out well in this discussion, mostly because of the fiscal adjustment already
underway. Still even here much of the necessary adjustment is backloaded so its reasonable that not all plans are
taken at face value; but if the government can rebuild some headroom in the upcoming budget, there is scope for UK
local assets to extend their recent outperformance.
 
EM Outperformance: The IMF showed a chart (see below) on how EM bond yields had risen much less than DM
yields. That’s remarkable, but the reality is that the outperformance has been going on since the post pandemic
surge in rates in 2021-22. So the real question is how much further this can extend given that EM rate spreads to the
US are now approaching very tight levels in a number of places. While domestic fundamentals like inflation are still
generally pretty benign, we are beginning to see a differentiation between places which don’t want to take more risk
with the currency and are hiking (India/South Africa) versus those who are still willing to take that risk and stay
dovish (Mexico).

AI – Macro as much as Micro: AI and its implications were the elephant in most rooms. No longer just a micro
earnings story, much of the macro policy and asset market debate centred on AI as well. On the policy side, short and
long-term inflation effects of the investment boom, the productivity implications, and whether politicians are ready
to manage the social and political implications of AI impact on the labour market were key debates. On the market
side, apart from equities, even the rates and the dollar debates now squarely have an AI component, with a good part
of the Dollar’s underperformance last week reflecting AI concerns as we discussed in our latest FX trader.”
 
Clemens Grafe (Co-Head of CEEMEA Economics): “On France, most clients and officials agree that it will need to
be the market that forces the adjustment and that the lack of fragmentation raises the hurdle before the ECB/EU is
forced to intervene. Despite all the euphoria over Korea/Taiwan, it is notable that the for the first time I can remember
everybody is quite pessimistic on the two heavyweights (China and India). Expectations on China have moved
from looking for the fiscal push to demand towards the central government trying to raise revenue to mildly
consolidate. AI is widely seen as a headwind for the Indian growth story against still meaningful foreign positioning
in Indian equities. 
 
Little push back on the GS view that over time more oil is getting through Hormuz despite it being hard to see how a
settlement to the conflict can be found. Expectations on a resolution of Russia/Ukraine are very low, but at the same
time risks of an escalation are also seen as too much of a tail risks to be relevant. 
 
Turkey: Clients have turned notably more cautious and generally agree with us that this is not the time to cut the
rate, but most believe the Bank will cut given its dovish guidance and history of cutting too early. Split opinions
on the Governor’s guidance on exchange rate policy. Many agree with us that his comments were: a) Justifying the
faster depreciation since the energy shock and the simple math that a given rate of depreciation requires larger daily
increments as the currency depreciates, but b) Also putting a higher weight on the need to 
keep competitiveness not only to protect exporters but also to contain expectations of a disorderly depreciation in the
future. However there were also some who interpreted the governor’s comments as suggesting the Bank would raise
the rate of depreciation going forward. Generally clients claim they have significantly lightened up on TRY
positions.

South Africa: No pushback on our constructive view on the fiscal path, likely to be acknowledged by the rating
agencies (S&P first, followed by Moody’s) with further upgrades. On rates, clients see November hike as unlikely as
long as Fed holds in October, but a Jan hike as quite plausible. The governor sounded quite balanced in line with that
view. On politics, there is some concern that the ANC loosing control of Jo’burg and other important constituencies to
the DA (outside KZN) and to MK party in KZN could lead to tensions in the ANC/DA + coalition but most clients
question how a poor showing of the ANC might affect the choice at the ANC leadership contest in Dec 27. 
 
Nigeria: The Bank appears more willing than in the past to allow the currency to appreciate while guiding rates
lower. Gross reserves are likely to be well above USD60bns by year end while the clearing FX liabilities to foreigners is
essentially completed with the exception of one transaction that will only roll off in 2031.
 
CEE: Client focus is on Hungary and Poland. Deputy governor Kurali, generally seen as the best in giving guidance
on the Euro adoption, was not in Bangkok, but client’s main question is now what could go wrong in the adoption of
the Euro. On Poland, some concern on fiscal and how and when there will some consolidation given the stand off
between the President and the government and what it means for rates. Generally clients agree with us that there are
too many hikes priced but wonder if the inflation is not about more than energy given the fiscal backdrop.”
