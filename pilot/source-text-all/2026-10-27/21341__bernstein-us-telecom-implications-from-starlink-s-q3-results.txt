US Telecom: Implications from Starlink's Q3 results - from
broadband threat to wireless question

SpaceX pulled forward its Q3 earnings call (covered by Harned), and given the overhang
Starlink has created for US Telecom stocks YTD, investors were watching closely.
Connectivity results came in ahead of what already appeared to be a high bar.
Connectivity segment revenue grew 69% YoY while EBITDA increased 82% YoY and
EBITDA margin of 67%, up from 61% in Q2, implying expanding operating leverage.
Revenue and EBITDA exceeded consensus by 7% and 16%, respectively, continuing a
pattern of upside surprises.
That said, the quarter was not without nuance for Telecom investors. Subscriber
growth, arguably the most important metric for assessing competitive pressure
on incumbent broadband providers, came in shy of expectations. Connectivity
subscribers reached 13.6M, below 14.2M expected, following a small miss in Q2 (12M
vs. 12.2M expected). Sequential subscriber growth slowed to 13% from 17% in the prior
quarter, adding to suspicion that subscriber growth may be decelerating despite strong
financial performance. The key takeaway, in our view, is that Starlink’s economics are
improving faster than its subscriber base.
For incumbent broadband operators, that distinction matters. A slowing subscriber
trajectory suggests Starlink is not yet taking share aggressively enough to materially
alter the competitive landscape for FWA or Cable BB operators. Near-term implications
for incumbents therefore remain limited, but the combination of rising ARPU ($69/
month), expanding margins, and continued capacity expansion changes the long-
term setup. Starlink is becoming increasingly profitable before the arrival of its next major
capacity step-up with V3 satellites, allowing Starlink to consider aggressive pricing to
compete for share. We continue to believe the question is not whether Starlink competes
on price, but when. Capacity remains the gating factor, but that constraint is gradually being
addressed, and SpaceX may choose to compete aggressively to seed share before fully
deploying V3 constellation.
While Starlink BB is the threat investors can see today, Starlink Mobile remains
the bigger concern. The exact route to market remains unclear, but SpaceX’s actions
are not. Between Echostar and Grain spectrum, the company has committed roughly
$30B to wireless assets, clearly indicating their ambitions that extend well beyond
satellite broadband. The impact is unlikely anytime soon. Build a fourth carrier takes years
regardless of the route taken, but the issue is not whether Starlink becomes a fourth carrier
in the future, but whether it eventually becomes a credible one. As we’ve argued before,
the impact may be years away, but the anticipation alone is likely to remain an overhang ,
particularly as the industry approaches the next C-band auction.
The quarter does little to change that narrative. Broadband subscriber growth remains
slower than feared, but improving economics point to a better-funded and potentially more
aggressive competitor over time.

VALUATION METHODOLOGY
Space Exploration Technologies Corporation
We base our valuation approach on 12-month forward EV/EBITDA applied to 2031 EBITDA. We believe it is necessary to use a
long-term period (i.e., 2031) because the cornerstone of the value is in the orbital data centers. Applying a multiple to, say, 2028
EBITDA would not capture any of that business - i.e., it would simply be space launch, terrestrial compute and near-term Starlink.
We use a sum of the parts methodology to give an EV/EBITDA valuation in 2030 (based on 2031 values). We then discount that
valuation backward for each segment to mid-2027 in order to reach a one year target price of $248.

two key assumptions in the model are (1) terminal growth rate of 0.0%, and (2) a WACC of 8%.

T-Mobile US Inc

over time, which depends on full reusability. SpaceX must be able to operationalize its orbital data centers to deliver on the

risks associated with potential launch incidents or regulatory restrictions. Should compute demand be more limited than most
expectations, that would form another risk.

AT&T Inc
Upside risks to our target price include: (1) a delay in SPCX’s attempt to enter the wireless market and/or a delay in the Starlink V3
constellation deployment; (2) fiber-wireless bundling resulting in even lower churn and/or incremental wireless share; (3) faster
than expected fiber passing build and higher penetration rate.
Downside risks to our target price include: (1) irrational wireless pricing by T-Mobile and/or Cable MVNO providers can lead to
intensifying competition; (2) irrational broadband pricing by CableCos and changing consumer perception can deteriorate the
market competitiveness of AT&T’s fiber offering; (3) fiber-wireless bundling may not result in lower churn and/or incremental
wireless share; (4) Capex can increase as AT&T expands its fiber passings and participate in future spectrum acquisition.
Verizon Communications Inc
Downside risks to our price target include: (1) irrational wireless pricing by T-Mobile and/or Cable MVNO providers can lead to
intensifying competition; (2) irrational broadband pricing by CableCos and changing consumer perception can deteriorate the
market competitiveness of Verizon’s fiber offering; (3) fiber-wireless bundling may not result in lower churn and/or incremental
wireless share; (4) Capex can increase as Verizon expands its fiber passings and participate in future spectrum acquisition.
Upside risks to our price target include: (1) higher penetration of fiber that would lead to greater expansion of overall near-term
margins; (2) FWA growth outpacing their guidance; (3) higher share of wireless post-paid net adds driven by higher-than-expected
FWA-wireless bundling; and (4) decrease in Capex vs. guidance
T-Mobile US Inc
Upside risks to our price target include: (1) Sustained FWA growth with higher contribution margin; (2) faster fiber roll-out and
take rate; and (3) even greater share of postpaid net adds at increased retail prices.
Downside risks to our price target include:1) A deterioration in the US competitive environment, which could negatively impact
the industry's long-term outlook and weigh on TMUS' growth momentum; 2) A significant rise in capex to fund deployment of fibre
infrastructure would lead to reduction in cash flow and could negatively impact valuations; 3) Objections to the TMUS-USCellular’s
deal raised by antitrust authorities 4) top management has been pivotal to TMUS' successful turn-around, so the departure of key
executives could represent a risk to the equity story and have a negative impact on our target price.
Comcast Corp
Upside risks to our price target include: (1) FWA and Fiber deployment slow down, leading to more subs for Comcast; (2) Wireless
ARPU could grow faster, increasing EBITDA; and (3) Peacock margins could turn positive earlier and expand faster.
Downside risks to our CMCSA price target include: the competitive pricing environment could be more aggressive than we
expect. Longer term, broadband regulation could become more onerous. 5G and fixed wireless could continue to take broadband
customers sooner and/or to greater degrees than we expect. NBCU, like other media businesses, is under pressure from
streaming services and performance could deteriorate relative to expectations.
Charter Communications Inc
Upside risks to our target price include: (1) FWA growth decelerates faster leaving room for Charter to grow BB subscribers; (2)
Telcos slow down fiber expansion resulting in Charter to sustain its current market position, or decelerate subscriber losses; and
(3) overall market competition alleviates allowing CHTR to increase prices to drive revenue and EBITDA growth.
Downside risks to our target price include: (1) Intensifying competition from both FWA and Fiber could (a) negatively impact
Charter’s ability to increase ARPU and retain/acquire subscribers, and (b) force Charter to spend on Capex on additional passing
with below-average returns; (2) Mobile subscriber growth could slow substantially driven by MNO pricing actions; and (3) Charter
could fail to optimally manage its heavy debt load
