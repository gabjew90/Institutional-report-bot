Will it spread?
French yields are marching towards the magical 5% level, sovereign spreads continue to blow out, and
European banks are suddenly looking vulnerable. Yet contagion across the eurozone remains
surprisingly contained. The real risk may be hiding outside the banking system, while France's
stretched public and private balance sheets leave little room for maneuver. The euro is trying to
stabilize, but the French mess is far from over.
Let the trend be your friend
France's 10-year yield continues to trade above its 21-day moving average, following a steep upward
trendline. Note just how far below the 50-day moving average remains. The big question now is
whether we get a print above the magical 5% level or whether the move pauses here.

France is the problem, not Europe
French sovereign stress continues to intensify, but the contagion story is surprisingly muted according
to Authers. While French OAT spreads have widened sharply, Italian and Greek spreads versus
Germany have actually narrowed this month.
This is a very different setup from the 2010–12 eurozone crisis. Investors are increasingly treating
France as an isolated sovereign problem rather than a systemic European event.
On the other hand, France is part of the core, so we wouldn't take anything for granted.

The hidden contagion risk
The real vulnerability may not be European banks, but foreign institutional holders of French
government debt.
According to Absolute Strategy Research, foreign institutions own 57.5% of French government debt,
compared with just 35.9% of Italian government bonds. With French banks already under significant
pressure, the next potential transmission mechanism could be insurers and other institutional
investors.
ASR
France has nowhere to hide
France's fiscal problem extends well beyond government borrowing. Public debt is approaching 110%
of GDP, while private-sector leverage is already substantially higher than in the US and much of
Europe.
This creates an uncomfortable dynamic: the government has limited fiscal room, while the domestic
private sector has little capacity to absorb significantly more debt. France is increasingly running out of
balance-sheet flexibility.

The French connection
The soggy euro has managed to pause its meltdown over the past few sessions, despite French
spreads continuing to blow out. There is more to the euro than France, but don't expect much more
than a pause unless the French mess takes a breather.
