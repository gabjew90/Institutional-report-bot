International Business Machines Corp. (IBM): Solid quarter, with upside 
in Software and inflection in Consulting as execution improves
21 October 2026 | 8:21PM EDT
Key stock takeaways: We expect the stock to trade higher following a solid quarter 
driven by the modest beat in Software and improvement in Consulting. We believe 
investor sentiment on IBM has begun to recover somewhat following the company’s 
2Q guide down and better-than-expected results from peer Accenture. We were 
also encouraged by the modest inflection in Consulting revenue, which suggests this 
business has also started to trough. Overall, we believe the company’s improved 
execution this quarter is likely to begin to restore investor confidence in IBM’s 
software growth trajectory and overall financial execution - but note that it is likely 
to take multiple proof points for the stock to fully re-rate. We are Buy rated on IBM 
based our view that the company remains on track to deliver improving software 
revenue growth and consulting market share gains over time.  
3Q revenue results are above the Street: IBM reported 3Q26 revenue of $17.1 bn 
vs. GS/Street at ~$16.77 bn. Total revenue from continuing operations was up 6% in 
constant currency (up ~5% in nominal terms), above the Street and our estimate of 
$16.8bn. Software revenue growth of 8.7% in CC was well above the Street at ~6.2%, 
Consulting revenue was up 1.8% in CC and was in line with the Street at ~1.8%. 
Infrastructure revenue increased by 4.9% in CC and was above the Street estimate of 
~2.0%. Non-GAAP gross margin of 58.5% came in below the Street at 59.1% and our 
estimate of 59.0%. Pre-tax margin of 19.3% was slightly above the Street and our 
estimate at 19.1%. EPS from continuing operations of $2.95 was above the Street 
and our estimate at $2.85. Free cash flow as of 3Q26 of $3.03 bn was slightly below 
the Street at $3.16 bn. By segment, IBM now expects Software revenue growth of 7% 
- 8% (up from 6% - 8% prior), with Hybrid Cloud growth in the double digits and 
Transaction Processing flat (from down low-to-mid single digits prior). For 
Consulting, IBM continues to expect low-to-mid-single digit growth. Management 
continues to expect Infrastructure to grow low-single digits driven by stronger power 
and storage demand. 
Software: Software revenue was $7.80 bn (up 8.7% yoy in CC, including an 
n
estimated 500bps impact from M&A). IBM posted better growth in Hybrid Cloud 
(up 11% in CC), and a significant improvement in Transaction Processing (up 10% 
in CC) following last quarter’s shortfall in mainframe. Automation (which includes 
HashiCorp) declined 3% in CC and Data was up 20% in CC (driven mainly by 
Confluent). IBM now expects Software revenue growth of 7% - 8% (up from 6% - 
8% prior) for 2026.

Consulting: Consulting revenue was $5.34 bn (up 1.8% in CC). Total consulting 
n

Intelligent Operations (previously Application Operations and BPO services) was flat. 
Management continues to expect low- to mid-single digit growth in Consulting in

Infrastructure: Infrastructure revenue was $3.71 bn (up 4.9% yoy in CC). Hybrid 
n
Infrastructure was up 9% in CC (with IBM Z-series down 19% in CC given difficult

CC), while Infrastructure Support was down 2% in CC. Management reiterated its 
Infrastructure revenue to be up low-single digits in 2026. 
Read-through to our coverage: We see IBM’s results as a modestly positive read-across 
for our IT Services coverage, as consulting results in the quarter improved against a 
challenging macro backdrop. 
2026 guidance reiterated, FCF ticked up: For 2026, IBM reiterated guidance for 
constant currency revenue growth to 4% - 5%, (we maintain a 4.4% CC growth or $70.6 
bn), along with a neutral FX impact. IBM modestly increased its FCF guidance for more 
than $1bn increase (or ~$16.0 bn (vs. GS at ~$15.97bn and the Street at $15.96 bn). 
Estimate adjustments: Our EPS estimates are essentially unchanged given little change 
to our revenue and margin assumptions: FY26/27/28 move to $12.40/$13.35/$15.00 
from $12.40/$13.35/$14.95 (see detailed estimates in Exhibit 2). 
Price target update: On essentially unchanged estimates, we increase our 12-month 
price target to $300 (from $270 prior), which is based on 22X (up from 20X given higher 
peer multiples) our one-year forward EPS estimate. Downside risks: 1) cyclical 
headwinds in software, 2) slowdown in consulting, 3) AI bookings deceleration, 4) 
dilutive M&A. 
We maintain our Buy rating on IBM, as we believe the company is on track to complete 
its pivot to long-term growth driven by long-term improvements in software growth and 
consulting market share gains.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
