COMMODITIES / ENERGY 
Oil holds near highs as Saudi attack risks sustain geopolitical premium. Oil prices 
remain supported by geopolitical risks with Brent futures just under USD 114/b and 
WTI trading over USD 100/b, both holding onto large overnight gains. Houthi militants 
claimed an attack on the oil loading port at Yanbu in Saudi Arabia along with attacks 
at Aramco facilities and the Dammam airport in the last several days. Production and 
export flows through the Strait of Hormuz are still holding at close to pre-conflict levels 
though at elevated shipping rates. The upcoming Israeli election (October 27) 
represents the next geopolitical anchor moment while media reports suggest that both 
Israel and the US have laid out plans to resume attacks against Iran. Refining margins 
have narrowed in October – front month gasoil/Brent is less than USD 80/b – after 
having surged to more than USD 90/b in mid-September. 
Gold slides as rising energy prices fuel inflation fears. Gold prices sold off heavily 
overnight, mirroring the move higher in energy prices. Gold is trading at USD 4,013/oz 
and tracking energy for its threat to the inflation outlook along with a broader sell-off in 
risk assets. The S&P 500 fell more than 1.2% overnight while the tech-oriented 
NASDAQ saw even larger losses. A rising inflation backdrop along with selling 
pressure to cover losses in equity or bond market positions is a negative combination 
for gold in the short-term even as the Fed is looking assured to keep rates unchanged 
at next week’s FOMC meeting.

MIDDLE EAST – CREDIT TRADING 
End of day comment – 22 October 2026. Big spread move wider today. Volumes 
increased with sellers: buyers ratio 2:1 and ETFs becoming more sizeable sellers of 
bonds. There was a bit of pushback into the close on ever widening spreads but overall 
we closing at the wides. In terms of performance the only area where cash prices were 
higher was ADGB/ QATAR long end which closed +0.125pt/+10bp led by some early 
morning buying in ADGB 54s. The front end and belly close unch/+12bp in both curves. 
The KSA complex differed from this as concerns over the overnight air attack/ Aramco 
HQ evacuation brought sellers out in long end bonds, ARAMCO 54s closed -
0.25pt/+15bp. Then came the credits that closed lower in cash prices today, mainly 
higher beta sovgn and corps/fins. OMAN led the weakness in higher betas with 48s 
getting sold closing -0.50pt/+17bp. In fins new DHBKQD led the weakness, having 
priced at T+140 (99.733) it traded all the way down to 99.00 and closed 99.125/375 -
0.5pt/+20bp. MASDAR announced a new 5y blue bond which will price at T+115bp in 
600mm. On the back of it the outstanding bonds widened up to 20bp with 35s closing 
-0.5pt/+20bp. Not many places to hide. (Source: Dominik Roth, Credit Trader) 
MIDDLE EAST – MACRO / MARKETS 
Turkey delivers first rate since January as inflation ease. Turkey’s central bank 
(CBRT) cut policy rates by 100bps to take the one-week repo rate to 36%. The move 
follows a better-than-expected inflation print for September (at 29.7% y/y), cooling 
growth and a resumption of repo auctions at the official policy rate several weeks ago. 
This was the first cut since January this year as the central bank held policy unchanged 
amid an uncertain global inflation environment caused by elevated energy prices and 
a changed outlook for Fed rates from the US. The bank noted that inflation may 
continue to ease thanks to waning domestic demand but also noted that future 
decisions will be “decided prudently on a meeting-by-meeting basis.” That suggests 
the bank will not necessarily adopt sequential path lower in rates but will opt for pauses 
to evaluate the inflation environment around each meeting in 2027. 
MENA growth hit by prolonged conflict recovery expected in 2027. The IMF's 
October 2026 Regional Economic Outlook projects MENA GDP to contract by 2.0% in 
2026 before rebounding by 7.0% in 2027, reflecting severe disruptions to hydrocarbon 
production, trade, logistics and tourism from the prolonged Middle East conflict. The 
downturn is concentrated in GCC economies, where GDP is forecast to shrink by 4.8% 
in 2026 before recovering by 9.1% in 2027, driven by the gradual restoration of energy 
production and exports. Kuwait (-16.7%), Qatar (-15.6%) and Bahrain (-11.3%) face 
particularly sharp contractions, while Saudi Arabia (-3.3%) and the UAE (-3.9%) are 
also expected to enter recession. In contrast, oil-importing economies have remained 
relatively resilient, supported by domestic demand, tourism, remittances and policy 
measures. Regional inflation is projected to rise to 14.9% in 2026 before easing to 
11.0% in 2027, while fiscal and external buffers face increasing pressure. Risks remain 
firmly tilted to the downside, particularly from renewed geopolitical escalation, 
prolonged energy disruptions and tighter global financial conditions.
