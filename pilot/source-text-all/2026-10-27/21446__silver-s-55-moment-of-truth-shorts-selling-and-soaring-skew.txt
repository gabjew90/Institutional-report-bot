Silver's make-or-break moment
Silver is trading around the critical $55 level, arguably the ultimate make-or-break for the metal.
Momentum remains poor, and a break below could trigger an aggressive move lower, with little
meaningful support in the vacuum beneath.
LSEG Workspace
The silver selling continues
Managed Money sold another $565 million in silver futures during October 6–13, marking the third
consecutive week of selling. Cumulative selling has now reached $2.2 billion. Of the latest selling, $160
million came from long liquidation and $405 million from fresh shorts. The bears are increasingly
taking control.

New shorts are arriving
Silver futures aggregate open interest increased by $1.6 billion, with much of the increase occurring
during the heaviest selling session. Combined with falling prices, this suggests fresh short positions are
being established rather than simply existing longs heading for the exits.

The dollar problem
Silver continues to struggle against the dollar backdrop. During October 6–13, the dollar gained 0.3%
while December silver futures lost 3.4%. Interestingly, the dollar subsequently retraced 0.5%, yet silver
fell another 2.6%. Dollar weakness alone is no longer enough to revive the silver trade.
GS
The missing industrial bid
Silver's industrial demand proxies are flashing warning signs. US solar equities fell 4.7%, Chinese solar
declined 1.6%, and data center stocks plunged 7.9% during October 6–13. The weakness in solar and
AI-related equities adds another headwind to an already struggling silver market.
GS

Downside fear is getting expensive
Silver's normalized 25-delta put-call skew briefly reached the 98th percentile of its one-year range.
Demand for downside protection is becoming increasingly aggressive, reinforcing the bearish
message from futures positioning.
