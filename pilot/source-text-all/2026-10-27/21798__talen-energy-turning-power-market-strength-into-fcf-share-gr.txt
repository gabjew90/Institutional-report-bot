Our Conclusion 
As of October 15, 2026, we initiate coverage of Talen Energy (TLN) with an 
Outperformer rating and $499 price target. We view TLN as one of the more 
compelling ways to gain exposure to tightening U.S. power markets, with its 
concentrated PJM position, Susquehanna nuclear facility and strong FCF/sh 
growth outlook. We believe evolving/delayed policy changes in PJM have 
weighed on TLN and its peers, but recent contracts signed by a peer bring 
some renewed optimism. That said, we believe the current valuation implies 
minimal upside from data centre deals and we continue to see an attractive 
risk/reward even without perfect policy outcomes, while TLN’s increased 
commitment to an aggressive buyback should help unlock shareholder value. 
Key Points 
Strong FCF Supported By PJM Exposure. TLN is entering a period of 
materially stronger earnings as higher PJM energy and capacity pricing flows 
through the portfolio. We forecast ~41% EBITDA and ~60% FCF/sh CAGRs 
from 2025 to 2028E, which by our estimates is the strongest forward-looking 
growth among the U.S. IPP peer group. Improving basis and higher realized 
power prices could provide additional upside beyond our current estimates.  
Susquehanna + Data Centres Provide Contracting Upside. The 1.9GW 
Amazon agreement establishes a key value creation source for 
Susquehanna, but we do not view it as the end of TLN’s data centre 
contracting opportunity. The company continues to advance ~4GW of land 
and data centre opportunities, although uncertainty around PJM policies 
(IRAS, RBP) could affect contracting timelines and investor conviction. We 
expect greater policy clarity over time. Further agreements would increase 
earnings visibility, but are not required to support our base case outlook—
rather they represent upside on FCF and a potential re-rating.  
Capital Allocation Is Central To The Story. The combination of rising FCF 
and a shrinking share count should continue to allow FCF/sh to grow 
considerably faster than EBITDA. TLN has demonstrated a willingness to 
opportunistically pursue M&A when returns are attractive, but has recently 
leaned more heavily into buybacks. TLN’s capacity payment monetization 
funds a ~$1.5B accelerated repurchase that should retire ~10% of shares 
over the next two quarters. In effect, TLN is pulling forward future capacity 
payments through a levered buyback at what we view as a discounted 
valuation. This accelerates FCF/sh growth and returns value to investors 
sooner, while retaining flexibility for regular buybacks and accretive M&A. 
Valuation. We derive our $499 target price using an equal weighting of 
EV/EBITDA (10.0x target multiple) and FCF yield (9.75% target yield) 
methodologies, with both applied to our 2028 estimates. TLN currently trades

on our 2027/2028 FCF estimates, respectively. Our upside/downside 
scenario valuations are $747/$274.

Executive Summary 
We initiate coverage of Talen Energy (TLN) with an Outperformer rating. Our target price 
of $499 implies potential upside of approximately 31% from the current share price. Our 
scenario analysis implies a valuation range of $274 to $747 per share, implying 45% 
downside and 50% upside from the current share price. 
Our positive outlook on the company reflects TLN's exposure to the structurally tighter PJM 
power market and a quality portfolio of generation assets enhanced through recent 
acquisitions. We believe TLN is well positioned to deliver upside from its fleet, while strong 
FCF conversion supports capital deployment for further upside and a significant return of 
capital for investors. TLN’s 2.5GW Susquehanna nuclear facility is central to the thesis, with a 
contract in place with AWS that provides steady embedded FCF growth. We believe recent 
policy uncertainty around the siting and contracting of data centres in the Pennsylvania-New 
Jersey-Maryland Interconnection (PJM) created an attractive entry point, although the recent 
rebound has started to erase the valuation discount. Overall, we continue to see a strong 
growth outlook from existing contracts, buybacks and normalization of the PPL zonal 
discount, with additional upside from new contracts/projects and potential M&A. 
Key Investment Drivers 
• 
Concentrated PJM Exposure Creating Some Angst Today But Provides Long-
term Upside. With ~98% of its generation exposed to PJM, TLN provides investors 
with a largely pure-play exposure to a market with strong fundamentals, albeit 
currently clouded by policy/contracting uncertainty. Tightening supply-demand 
fundamentals, a 6.8GW shortfall in the latest capacity auction, expected strong load 
growth (~5.2% through 2030) and limited new supply support a constructive long-
term outlook, while strength in PJM power prices in recent months supports the tight 
supply-demand thesis. However, TLN’s high PJM exposure increases its 
concentration risk. Key market structure items in PJM, notably the proposed residual 
base auction (RBP) delay and Interim Resource Adequacy Service (IRAS), have 
created uncertainty and angst for investors. We could get greater clarity over the next 
several quarters. Additionally, a significant portion of TLN’s fleet is exposed to the 
PPL pricing hub, where local oversupply and transmission constraints have weighed 
on pricing. We expect constraints to ease, although we do not expect meaningful 
relief until H2/28 or 2029, which will likely weigh on realized upside for TLN. Each 
$1/MWh improvement in PPL zonal pricing adds ~$1/sh to FCF. 
• 
Nuclear & Dispatchable Fleet Position TLN To Capture Data Centre Demand. 
TLN’s Susquehanna nuclear facility has a 17-year, 1.9GW PPA with Amazon that is 
expected to ramp through 2032, providing TLN with a growing source of long-term 
contracted cash flows and the potential to layer on more contracts. TLN is also 
progressing ~4 GW of land and data centre development opportunities, which will 
provide additional opportunities to contract with large-load customers and monetize 
sites across its portfolio. We believe deals can still be struck as PJM works through 
ongoing policy uncertainty and evolving market rules. 
• 
Capacity Monetization Accelerates An Already Strong FCF/Sh Outlook. TLN’s 
~$1.5B monetization of 2027-2029 PJM capacity payments funds an accelerated 
repurchase plan that is expected to retire ~10% of shares over the next two quarters. 
We view this as a quasi-levered buyback that pulls forward future cash receipts to 
repurchase shares at a discounted valuation—approximately a 13%-15% 2028E FCF 
yield—accelerating FCF/sh growth and rewarding investors sooner. While the 
transaction adds leverage and reduces future capacity cash receipts, TLN’s 
underlying FCF generation should support continued regular buybacks and its 
commitment to return 70% of FCF to shareholders yet maintain financial flexibility for 
other growth efforts. We forecast a 60% CAGR in FCF/sh from 2025-2028E.

Growth Drivers 
TLN's growth outlook is driven by a combination of previously announced highly accretive 
acquisitions, improving PJM market fundamentals, strong buyback activity, and embedded 
optionality from data centre contracting and powered land development. Together, these 
drivers more than double our EBITDA forecast between 2025 and 2028 while driving 
substantial FCF growth, positioning TLN to compound value through further reinvestment, 
buybacks, and future load-serving opportunities, as highlighted in the Exhibit 4 bar charts. 
• 
Recent M&A Establishes A Materially Larger Earnings Base; Tightening Power 
Markets & Capacity Prices Support Meaningful Organic Growth. TLN’s acquisition 
strategy has been the largest near-term EBITDA and FCF growth driver, with the 
Freedom, Guernsey and Cornerstone transactions expanding the portfolio to ~15.7GW 
and adding an estimated ~$1.0B of EBITDA. The Cornerstone acquisition alone added 
~2.6GW of efficient baseload and peaking generation, increasing TLN’s exposure to 
tightening PJM markets and diversifying its earnings base across additional assets and 
locations. Beyond acquisitions, higher wholesale margins and fleet utilization, along with 
higher capacity revenues, are expected to drive ~$860MM of incremental growth through 
2028, split roughly $510MM/$350MM between incremental wholesale and capacity 
EBITDA, respectively. Combined with stronger contributions from the existing fleet, we 
forecast EBITDA increasing from ~$1.0B in 2025 to ~$2.9B in 2028 (41% CAGR). 
• 
High FCF Conversion Enables More Growth And Capital Returns. We forecast FCF 
increasing from ~$524MM in 2025 to ~$1.9B by 2028 (52% CAGR), as EBITDA growth is 
partially offset by higher acquisition-related interest expense but benefits from relatively 
modest incremental sustaining capital and cash taxes. FCF conversion consequently 
improves from approximately 51% in 2025 to 64% by 2028. 
• 
FCF/Sh Growth Boosted By Aggressive Buyback. TLN recently announced a $1.5B 
monetization/securitization of 2027-29 PJM capacity payments, with proceeds to support 
a ~$1.5B accelerated buyback expected to retire ~10% of shares over the next two 
quarters. This pulls forward future FCF for TLN to opportunistically purchase shares 
currently trading at a ~12%-15% FCF yield. Note that foregone payments won’t hit TLN’s 
Adj. FCF as debt/liability payments are not included. As such, we see an acceleration of 
FCF/sh growth (60% three-year CAGR). TLN still has $2.8B to support its regular 
buyback ($1.5B authorization through 2028) and other value-enhancing opportunities. 
Further, TLN is also progressing ~4GW of powered land and data centre contracting 
opportunities, giving the company a potentially material growth avenue beyond what is 
embedded in our base forecast.

margins, contracts, hedge positions and typical asset/portfolio operating costs (our pricing

estimate TLN will generate ~$2.1B of cumulative retained FCF over 2026-2028, providing 
additional capacity for debt reduction, organic investment and incremental M&A. Finally, we 
assume TLN remains sub-investment grade, but we do not see this as an impediment to 
unlocking value for shareholders, particularly with no refinancing requirements over the next

Valuation  
TLN's valuation is best assessed, in our view, through a combination of EV/EBITDA and FCF 
yield, which together capture the value of its generation fleet, long-term power market 
exposure, and growing data centre-related cash flow opportunities. We view the two metrics 
as complementary, with EV/EBITDA reflecting asset and earnings value, while FCF yield 
captures the company's ability to convert those earnings into shareholder returns.

EV/EBITDA. TLN currently trades at 9.4x EV/EBITDA (FY2), below its 5-year average of

multiple expansion over time.

case is increasingly driven by the monetization of its power assets and long-term free 
cash flow growth. TLN currently offers a 10.3% FCF yield (FY2 or 2027 estimate), well

the market is discounting execution and merchant market risks despite TLN’s strong FCF/sh 
growth outlook.

Key Risks To Price Target 
Concentration Risk—Market & Assets. TLN's earnings profile is significantly more 
concentrated than its peers. The Susquehanna nuclear facility represents a substantial 
portion of enterprise value and earnings generation, while much of the company's economic 
exposure is tied to PJM market fundamentals. This concentration amplifies both upside and 
downside outcomes. Operational issues at Susquehanna, adverse PJM market 
developments, transmission changes, or unfavourable regulatory outcomes could have an 
outsized effect on earnings. 
Dependence On Data Centre Load Realization. Among U.S. IPPs, TLN has historically had 
one of the most direct links between its valuation and the data centre buildout narrative. 
While we think the pullback in the stocks reflects less optimism on data centre load growth 
and continued monetizations/contracts, we do believe delays in hyperscaler development, 
lower-than-expected utilization rates, changes in customer procurement strategies, or slower 
AI adoption could materially affect growth expectations and sentiment.  
Merchant Power Market Exposure. TLN possesses significant exposure to merchant power 
pricing, causing earnings to be highly sensitive to changes in forward energy and capacity 
markets. New generation additions, weaker demand growth, improved weather conditions, 
lower natural gas prices, or market design changes could pressure long-term pricing 
assumptions and reduce cash flow expectations. 
Nuclear Operational And Regulatory Risk. Because Susquehanna contributes such a 
large share of earnings, nuclear operational risks are unusually important for TLN. Prolonged 
outages, major capital projects, safety-related issues, regulatory actions, or lower-than-
expected capacity factors could have a disproportionate impact relative to more diversified 
peers. The concentration of value in a single asset increases earnings volatility compared 
with companies that own broader generation portfolios.
