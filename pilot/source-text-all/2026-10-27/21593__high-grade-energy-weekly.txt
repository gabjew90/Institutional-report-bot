Industry Overview 
   
 
HG Energy spreads finish mixed w-o-w 
HG Energy spreads finished largely between 5 bps tighter to 5 bps wider w-o-w. At a 
sector level, HG Energy and HG Pipelines reached new record tight levels (since 2014) vs 
the Corp Index early this week, trading 5 bps through and 3 bps wide, respectively. We 
include a calendar of upcoming HG Energy earnings in Exhibit 22. 
KMI earnings: Growth outlook improves, capex to rise 
On Wednesday, KMI reported 3Q26 adjusted EBITDA and earnings above consensus 
expectations, while leverage remained near the low end of management's target range 
(3.5-4.5x). The company also raised its 2026 EBITDA outlook and reiterated expectations 
for year-end net leverage of 3.6x. More notably, KMI's project backlog increased to a 
record $14.4b from $9.6b last quarter following sanctioning of two large natural gas 
pipeline projects: the Southeast Connector and Permian Link projects, improving 
visibility into long-term EBITDA growth through the early 2030s. We view the projects 
favorably given their long-term take-or-pay contracts and creditworthy counterparties; 
however, the larger backlog and management's expectation for roughly $4b of annual 
growth capex over the next several years reinforces our view that a more capital-
intensive spending cycle lies ahead. While we do not view higher spending as an 
immediate credit concern given the projects' 2030 in-service dates and KMI's current 
financial flexibility, future capital budgets will remain an important area to monitor. 
Compared to peers, we view KMI’s spreads trading near ENBCN as fair and remain MW.  
EXE appoints new CEO, CHCOCH upgraded to A- at S&P 
On Monday, EXE announced the appointment of Danny Brown as President and CEO, 
effective October 26, 2026, with Interim CEO Michael Wichterich returning to his role as 
Non-Executive Chairman. Brown joins from Chord Energy, where he previously served as 
President and CEO, and brings extensive upstream and midstream operating experience, 
including nearly two decades at Anadarko Petroleum. Away from EXE, on Friday morning, 
S&P upgraded Cheniere Corpus Christi Holdings LLC (CHCOCH) to A- from BBB+, 
following substantial completion of its Stage 3 Project. 
Continued Middle Eastern strikes keep oil price high  
Oil prices were up w/w (December contracts), with Brent +5% to ~$115/bbl and WTI 
+2% to ~$101/bbl. Oil markets remained focused on Middle East developments, 
including Iranian military activity in and around the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi attacks 
on Saudi energy infrastructure, even as the IEA, EIA, and OPEC all lowered their global 
demand forecasts. Other notable developments included record tanker rates that 
contributed to a roughly 50% m-o-m decline in U.S. crude exports to Asia, Saudi Arabia's 
efforts to secure access to overseas strategic storage, the Trump administration's 
exchange from the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve, and India's September crude 
imports reaching a five-month high.

 News & Views 
KMI 3Q26 EBITDA beat; higher backlog points to larger 
capex cycle ahead 
On Wednesday, KMI reported 3Q26 adj. earnings of $0.35/share, above Bloomberg 
consensus estimates of $0.33/share. Reported adj EBITDA of $2,180mm was also above 
consensus estimates of $2,110mm. 
Cash flow and credit metrics 
Cash from operations of $1,590mm (including a $3mm working capital headwind) did 
not cover capex of $1,045mm and dividends of $665mm. There were no share 
repurchases in the quarter. Credit stats were largely unch’d q-o-q: adj. debt/adj. LTM 
EBITDA = 3.8x (3.7x at 2Q26), net adj. debt/adj. LTM EBITDA = 3.7x (3.6x) and adj. LTM 
EBITDA/interest expense = 5.0x (5.0x). 
2026 guidance 
KMI raised 2026 adj. EBITDA guidance to more than 6% above its initial budget of $8.6b 
(previously more than 5% above). KMI affirmed its YE26 net leverage outlook of 3.6x. 
KMI did not address its previously guided ~$3.35b growth capex budget for 2026, but on 
its call, said it expects capex to be “around $4b” for the next few years. Management 
also reiterated that it has roughly $3b of annual cash flow available to fund expansion 
capex while maintaining a strong balance sheet. 
Capital allocation 
KMI reported net leverage of 3.5x at 3Q26 (3.6x at 2Q26), at the low-end of its 3.5-4.5x 
long-term target range.  
Growth projects 
KMI reported a $14.4b project backlog at 3Q26, up from $9.6b at 2Q26, primarily due to 
the addition of Southeast Connector and Permian Link (more below). Natural gas 
projects ($13.2b) represent most of the backlog. More than 75% of backlog is associated 
with projects supporting power generation and local distribution company demand. 
Excluding CO₂ and certain gathering and processing projects, management expects the 
backlog to generate a first full-year project EBITDA multiple of approximately 5.7x. 
Management highlighted that Southeast Connector and Permian Link are supported by 
long-term (20+ year) take-or-pay agreements with creditworthy counterparties and 
extend KMI's visible backlog-driven growth runway into the early 2030s. More on these 
projects: 
• 
Southeast Connector: Designed to transport up to 2.6 Bcf/d and carry abundant 
Marcellus/Utica gas into the Southeast, with an expected $3.1b KMI share of project 
costs and service beginning in phases starting in 4Q30. Final capex is dependent on 
ultimate project scope and could increase capacity to around 3.5 Bcf/d. 
• 
Permian Link: A $4.2b project (~$1.6b KMI share) designed to transport ~2.8 Bcf/d 
from the Permian Basin to new power generation load, with an expected in-service 
date of 3Q30. 
Management also indicated it continues to see a substantial pipeline of additional 
natural gas opportunities beyond the current backlog, with recently sanctioned projects 
expected to create follow-on expansion opportunities across its network. On project 
funding, KMI indicated Southeast Connector and Permian Link are expected to be funded 
through a combination of JV cash flows, JV-level debt, and owner equity contributions. 
KMI noted it intends to preserve the existing ratings at the JV pipelines and retains the 
flexibility to fund the required capital directly from KMI, supported by its balance sheet 
and cash flow generation.

Western Gateway 
On 8/11, PSX, KMI, and DINO reached FID on the Western Gateway Pipeline, a 900-mile 
refined products pipeline connecting the Gulf Coast to Arizona and California via KMI's 
CALNEV system. The project has a $5b enterprise value, 230 mbbl/d capacity, and 
targets startup in late 2029. Ownership is 49.9% PSX, 35.1% KMI, and 15.0% DINO, with 
cash contributions of ~$2.5b, $250mm, and $750mm, respectively. KMI's lower 
contribution reflects assets contributed to the JV. The project is supported by long-term 
shipper agreements and follows the October 2025 open season announcement 
Segment Results 
Operationally, Natural Gas Pipelines adj EBDA increased 6% y-o-y to $1,489mm, driven 
by higher contributions from El Paso Natural Gas Pipeline, the KinderHawk gathering 
system, Tennessee Gas Pipeline, and the Texas Intrastate system. Natural gas transport 
volumes rose 3% y-o-y on LNG deliveries through TGP and stronger demand on the 
Texas Intrastate system, while gathering volumes increased 14% y-o-y, primarily due to 
KinderHawk. Other segments also posted growth, with Products Pipelines adj EBDA up 
9% y-o-y to $313mm, Terminals up 15% y-o-y to $314mm, and CO₂ up 35% y-o-y to 
$184mm. 
Record backlog supports LT growth outlook, but signals spending cycle ahead 
KMI's 3Q26 results were largely positive, supported by an EBITDA beat, higher full-year 
EBITDA guidance, and leverage remaining at the low end of management's target range. 
More importantly, the company sanctioned the Southeast Connector and Permian Link 
projects, driving backlog to a record $14.4b and further improving visibility into long-
term EBITDA growth. We view the projects favorably given their long-term take-or-pay 
contracts, creditworthy counterparties, and ability to extend KMI's growth runway into 
the early 2030s. At the same time, we believe these projects reinforce our view that a 
more intensive spending cycle lies ahead. The larger backlog, management's comments 
around ~$4b of annual growth capex over the next several years (which is a step up from 
current spending), and continued discussion of additional projects beyond the current 
backlog all point toward higher spending requirements over time. That said, we do not 
view this as a 2027 issue. The two largest projects announced this quarter carry 2030 
in-service dates, suggesting the heaviest spending burden remains several years away. 
As such, we view the current developments more as an early warning sign for higher 
future capex than an immediate credit concern, though future capital budgets will be an 
important area to monitor. From a relative value perspective, we continue to view KMI 
as fairly valued and remain comfortable with our MW recommendation. Spreads trade 
broadly in line with ENBCN and around WMB, which we view as reasonable given KMI's 
high-BBB ratings, current balance sheet strength and improved long-term growth 
visibility. While a larger spending cycle could ultimately pressure free cash flow, funding 
needs, and potentially leverage over time, we do not expect investors to focus heavily on 
those risks until higher spending begins to show up more clearly in forward budgets and 
capital plans.

Ratings Agency Actions 
S&P upgraded CHCOCH to A-; outlook stable 
On Thursday, S&P raised Cheniere Corpus Christi Holdings LLC’s (CHCOCH) rating to A- 
from BBB+, with a stable outlook. The upgrade reflects timely, below-budget substantial 
completion of the Stage 3 project, which increased CHCOCH’s total liquefaction capacity 
to 25 mtpa from 15 mtpa, as well as CHCOCH’s highly contracted take-or-pay cash 
flows, ~75% margins and modest leverage. S&P forecasts minimum Debt Service 
Coverage Ratio (DSCR) of 3.7x through 2042. Stage 3 expected cost of $7.0-$7.1b, was 
below its FID budgeted cost of $8.3b, supported by large equity funding and lower 
construction interest. The outlook assumes stable operations and prudent financing of 
future expansions, with DSCR maintained at or above 2.25x incorporating expansion debt 
but no associated cash flows. An upgrade is unlikely as the rating is capped at A- by 
counterparty credit quality, while a downgrade could arise from prolonged operational 
disruption or expansion-related financial/operating risks causing DSCR to remain below 
2.25x. Additionally, CHCOCH has $2.3b of debt maturing between 2027 and 2029, which 
if transferred to its parent company LNG, could create additional debt capacity.

Valuation & risk 
Kinder Morgan (KMI) 
Kinder Morgan Inc. (KMI) is a diversified midstream company which was formed in 
November 2014 after consolidation of its subsidiaries. 
 
KMI's recent results benefited from stronger-than-expected EBITDA, higher full-year 
guidance, and leverage near the low end of management's target range. The growth 
outlook remains favorable, supported by a substantial opportunity set in natural gas and 
power projects. Our primary concern is the potential for significantly higher capex and 
financing needs as the backlog expands and potential for M&A. With that said, 
management continues to emphasize maintaining leverage discipline. With spreads 
trading largely around fair value, we provide a MW rec. 
 
Risk to our rec: new project execution risk, spills and other operational disruptions that 
can cause environmental damage, changes in management's commitment to its ratings 
(positively or negatively), a renewed focus on strengthening the balance sheet, increased 
shareholder returns, event risk in the form of M&A, changing cost of capital, new 
issuance risk, and regulatory changes.
