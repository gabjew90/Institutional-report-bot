beat vs our estimate was driven by SPCX’s AI segment (renamed SI for Super 
Intelligence) – continuing 2Q26’s theme of AI-driven beats.  Management went out 
of its way to make clear how far ahead the company was tracking vs. consensus by 
issuing 1Q27 revenue $33-37B, which was >$10B higher than consensus of ~$23B.  
We raise our 2027 EPS estimates considerably ($2.14 to $3.25) as well as out years.  
See tables below.  Accordingly, we also raise our Price Target from $200 to $240. 
Separately, management acknowledged contemplating a tie-up with Tesla – no 
surprise as we’ve stated that we expect the relationship to continue to grow closer.  
But, perhaps most importantly, management confirmed that Starship Flight 15 
would involve a test of the ‘chopsticks’ catching the second stage – a monumental 
event that represents a critical unlock in future value toward the $900+ valuation 
level we’ve discussed since our initiation (see Space Exploration Technologies

Space Segment:
Flight 15 is likely a monumental event for SPCX and for the industry. Revenue of 
$1.8B exceeded VA consensus of $1.5B/Citi of $1.7B and Adj. EBITDA of $357M 
exceeded VA consensus of $115M and Citi of $257M. SPCX reported launch activity 
(113 total launches YTD) and mass to orbit (1,517mt YTD) – but we do not view this 
KPI as material to the stock’s valuation or trading until Starship begins regular 
operations. As noted in our preview, we believe the Space segment’s 3Q26 
financial results are likely to have little impact on shares (see 3Q26 Likely Another 
Strong Q Along Multi-Year Catalyst-Rich Arc).  More importantly, management 
confirmed that Flight 15 would represent a monumental test of the chopsticks.  
Recall that although a number of major technological milestones remain for 
Starship, the milestone that we are most focused on in launch remains the second 
stage ‘chopstick’ test: when the second stage (the ship) is caught by the 
‘chopsticks’ for the first time. We expected this capability to be demonstrated by 
year-end and management confirmed it would be tested on Flight 15 barring a 
major surprise.
Connectivity Segment: 
SpaceX reported strong financials with lighter than expected consumer 
subscriptions at 13.6M (1.6M net adds) vs our estimate of 14.4M and VA consensus 
of 14.3M. Total Connectivity revenue of $5.2B exceeded VA consensus of $4.9B 
and Citi of $5.0B partly on better broadband ARPU mix (as net adds were weighted 
to developed markets) as well as significantly better other revenues within the 
consumer sub-segment as well as better revenue from Enterprise and Government. 
Adj. EBITDA of $3.5B exceeded VA consensus $2.9B and Citi of $3.0B with 
favorable operating leverage and margin expansion in the period, although a 
reversal of tariff-related expenses helped the margin by approx. 300 bps, or ~$156 
million.  For SPCX, financial performance continues to perform well in the segment 
with respect to organic revenue growth and margin expansion and management 
commentary was positive on the ongoing trajectory, especially from enterprise and 
government segments.
More importantly, management reaffirmed plans to become a major mobile 
carrier with a specific opportunity in the US from the pending spectrum 
acquisitions leveraging a combination of satellites, towers, and locations already 
using Starlink broadband. Management also acknowledged that there is a path to 
annual revenue in connectivity of several hundred billion broadly from mobile and 
may look to a forum to delve further into the future opportunity given the 
discussion needs more time than they have during an earnings call.  With respect to 
future mobile entry, management expects to begin mobile service in early-2028 as 
it begins to launch its V2 satellites next year and in the future. We believe an MVNO 
would still be the preferred method to enter the US mobile market, while a 
terrestrial build as well as a Telco acquisition remain viable alternatives, in our view. 
Management commentary continues to increase the likelihood that SpaceX will 
enter the US mobile market through a build scenario, while management 
acknowledged that the use of Towers would be a part of its real estate needs for the 
terrestrial portion of its network solution. We view the news as a net-positive for 
Tower stocks, while the risk from potential mobile entry may continue to remain a 
risk for the Telco stocks as we framed in our recent note (link). SpaceX was more 
optimistic on timing of mobile entry in 2028, although we still believe Starlink will

Super Intelligence Segment:
The SI segment drove most of the beat in 3Q26, as expected.  SI (renamed from 
AI) revenue of $7.6B (+195% QoQ, +830% YoY) exceeded VA consensus of $6.5B 
and Citi of $6.9B, and Adj. EBITDA of $5.0B exceeded VA consensus of $4.0B and 
Citi of $3.6B. The company lifted its outlook for compute capacity next year, 
aiming to bring on an incremental 10GW and end YE27 with around 13GW, up from 
its stated 5-10GW target as recently as last quarter and our expectation for 5.5GW. 
To support the incremental capacity, management shared they would aim to bring 
on 20GW of power, cooling and electrical equipment infrastructure, pointing to the 
advantages they have with casting blades and veins for turbines, and highlighting 
modular compute clusters as an opportunity. 3Q segment capex came in at 
$16.4B, below our expectation of $23B, while 4Q total capex guidance came in at 
$35-40B, well ahead of our $27.8B, reflecting pulled forward investments. SPCX 
management emphasized their strategy of dynamically allocating compute month 
to month (with a goal of splitting it roughly 50:50 for 3P vs. internal purposes) and 
renting compute at favorable spot market rates. The company noted a possibility 
that they could see a 6-month payback period for AI capex next year, which is more 
aggressive than the <1 year period rate they mentioned last quarter. Grok Bot was 
highlighted as the fastest growing product, with the product crossing paid weekly 
users of 800K earlier this month (more than double MoM) and potential near-term 
upside coming from scaled enterprise deployments in the coming weeks following 
the launch of the X Subscription last week, which brings together Grok, Grok Bot, 
Cursor and X Premium for teams and enterprise customers. Accordingly, raising our 
GW and $/watt assumptions in 2027 and beyond is the largest driver of change to 
our model.
Raising Price Target from $200 to $240
We’ve adjusted estimates higher and raised our price target.  We view SPCX as a 
highly vertically integrated disruptor leveraging unrivaled capabilities in launch to 
establish dominant positions in Global Connectivity and Super Intelligence. 
Because the company’s ultimate advantage is rooted in its ability to deliver mass to 
orbit at greater scale and lower unit cost than any other entity on the planet, if 
SPCX succeeds in demonstrating key technological and operational milestones in 
the upcoming, catalyst-rich ~2-3 years without faltering, then we see a path to 
generating over ~$1 trillion in revenue.  We had emphasized that our prior $200 
base-case price target is a heavily discounted version of the $900+/share that we 
believe can ultimately be achieved and with progress on terrestrial AI exceeding 
expectations and Flight 15 anticipated to be the long awaited “chopstick test” we 
are beginning the process of ratably revising our price target as SPCX delivers on 
milestones (see SPCX: Potential for $900+; Initiating at Buy). Our price target is 
primarily derived from 2027/28 growth adj. multiples (EV/Revenue/Growth and 
EV/EBITDA/Growth) for the “Trillion Dollar Comps” (a basket of similar trillion 
dollar+ market caps to SPCX).

Company description
SpaceX is a vertically integrated technology leader building the foundational 
hardware and software infrastructure for the future of space, global 
connectivity, and artificial intelligence. The company’s Launch segment 
pioneered full reusability to provide affordable access to orbit, while its 
Connectivity segment operates the world's largest satellite constellation to 
deliver high-speed internet globally. Following its acquisition of xAI, SpaceX 
has also established a dedicated AI segment that integrates frontier models 
like Grok with massive terrestrial and orbital compute infrastructure.
Investment strategy
We rate shares of SPCX a Buy due to the following points: 1) Unrivaled launch 
capabilities right now and for the foreseeable future; 2) Unique capability of 
scaling space infrastructure and unlocking trillion-dollar market 
opportunities (Orbital AI and Starlink); 3) Extreme vertical integration, which 
reinforces the company’s ability to drive costs down, throughput up, and 
deliver at a scale at which no competitor can replicate; and 4) Differentiated 
margin and growth profiles.
Valuation
We derive our $240 base-case price target for SPCX using the average of 
three valuation methods: 1) 2027E growth adj. multiples (EV/Revenue/Growth 
and EV/EBITDA/Growth) for the “Trillion-Dollar Comps” (a basket of similar 
trillion-dollar+ market caps); 2) sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuing Space, 
Connectivity, and AI separately using 2027E growth adj. multiples 
(EV/Revenue/Growth and EV/EBITDA/Growth) for industry-specific 
comparable companies; and 3) 2030E comparable company multiples 
(EV/Revenue and EV/EBITDA) for the “Trillion-Dollar Comps” (a basket of 
similar trillion dollar+ market caps).
Risks
Risks to our thesis, which could impede the shares from reaching our price 
target, include headwinds to the launch segment which reference the failure 
to demonstrate rapid reusability on Starship, lack of launch infrastructure, 
and regulatory headwinds from the FAA and other government organizations. 
The failure to rapidly gain market share in Starlink mobile, as well as 
demonstrate the ability to create AI orbital satellites capable of compute, also 
presents risks to SPCX.
