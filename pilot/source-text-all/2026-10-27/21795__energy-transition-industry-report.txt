WHAT YOU SHOULD KNOW:
■
Seeing if Money Can Solve The Problem. Yesterday, the DOE announced it is
awarding ~$967M in additional funding to help improve grid infrastructure while
simultaneously lowering power costs, something that has been an ongoing point of
emphasis from the federal government. Additionally, this week the DOE announced
it is providing ~$42M through the Defense Production Act (DPA) to support a
nat gas pipeline from Pennsylvania to New York. We note that New York has
the ~5th highest energy prices amongst US states (Pennsylvania ~12th), making
lower energy prices a major point of emphasis in the state. New York remains
the only state to have officially enacted a one-year data center moratorium as it
looks to address incorporating large-load data centers without harming residential
power users. Lastly, the DOE announced ~$50M in DPA funding for upgrades to
transmission lines in Virginia, the largest data center market in the US. We note
that a Virginia senator recently proposed a three-year data center moratorium for
the state, which would halt data center approvals starting next July.
■
More Attention for Nuclear. The Philippine Department of Energy aims to add
~1.2GW of capacity by 2032, growing to ~2.4GW by 2035 with a long-term goal
of ~4.8GW by 2050. We note the Philippines currently has no operating nuclear
facilities, with the country's plant, the Bataan Nuclear Power Plant, sitting dormant
since it was built in the 1980s. The program includes a technical evaluation of
this plant, although its main focus is on exploring existing SMR technologies that
the country believes is better-suited for the nation's geographical layout. The
government highlighted electricity demand growth and a renewed focus on energy
independence as key factors contributing to its new targets for nuclear energy.
We note that no Southeast Asian country currently operates a commercial reactor
however, Vietnam recently approved of a ~2.4GW plant that is expected to come
online in the early 2030s. We expect the growing global emphasis on energy
security and soaring energy demand (think AI data centers) will motivate more
countries to look to nuclear as a stable source of power.
■
Grants for the Energy Transition. The EU awarded EUR ~3.4B via its Innovation Fund
to 77 net zero technology projects this week. Nearly half of funding will go towards
12 large scale projects covering decarbonized fuels, industrial carbon management
(think CCS, efficiencies), chemicals, iron and steel, and a platinum group recycling
plant. This is also the first funding round to include SMRs and direct air carbon
capture (DAC), with awards to Calogena's (Private) ~30MWt NetZeroHeat heat-
only nuclear pilot in France and Sirona Technologies' Norwegian DAC ~4ktpa pilot.
Cash has been sourced from revenues from the bloc's Emissions Trading System, a
cap-and-trade CO2 emissions trading market that sets a declining ceiling on overall
carbon emissions, and will be disbursed to winning projects in the form of grants.
Next up, grant agreements for this funding round will be announced in 1Q27, and
the Innovation Fund plans to launch its next round of funding calls before year end.

■
Utility and Hyperscaler Advance SMR Tie-Up. AEP (AEP, Buy, $147 PT, Analyst: Alex Kania) signed a joint development
agreement with X-energy (XE, Not Rated) to evaluate feasibility of a ~960MW nuclear energy project in Indiana. The project is
backed by an Amazon (AMZN, Not Rated) funding agreement, and AMZN plans to enter a long-term PPA if the project reaches
positive FID. Located at a former coal plant, XE would initially install 12 SMRs (~80MW each), part of a broader agreement with
AMZN to add over 5GW of nuclear capacity to the US grid by 2039. XE's initial projects (reactor construction in Texas backed
by the DOE, TRISO fuel plant in Tennessee) are already progressing through the NRC licensing process, with the advanced fuel
plant expected to come online in 2028.
■
Supporting Data Center Deployments. Hitachi Energy (6501-JP, Not Rated) and Ansaldo Energia (Private) announced an MOU
to deploy integrated power solutions using Ansaldo's nat gas turbines and Hitachi's grid operation expertise to help meet
growing global power demand. Nat gas continues to be a preferred baseload power source for data center loads, but with
major turbine OEMs largely sold out through the end of the decade and grid constraints occurring globally (particularly in
the US), developers are seeking alternative solutions to secure prompt power. We note that earlier this year, Hitachi Energy
announced a ~$1B US grid manufacturing investment, another effort to help onboard large load data centers.
■
LNG Heavyweight Gets into the Compute Game. Venture Global (VG, Not Rated) signed an agreement with GE Vernova (GEV,
Not Rated) and Baker Hughes (BKR, Not Rated) to develop over 10GW of BtM power at sites the company owns. The sites
are split into 16 ~720MW units, which the company has earmarked for new compute capacity, leveraging ~4.5GW of existing
BtM capacity at VG. We note VG's existing generation equipment (turbines for electricity are also used for LNG compression),
pipeline access, and real estate differentiate its entry into the colocation market, but the market looks to have adopted a wait-
and-see approach to neoclouds in waiting that are looking to sign firm agreements with hyperscalers.
■
Demanding Nuclear Power. Deep Fission (FISN, Not Rated) signed a Master Energy Purchase Agreement with the Great Plains
Development Authority (Private) that obligates FISN to supply power once the company's DOE pilot is licensed and reaches
COD. The agreement firms up an LOI that the two parties signed last year for FISN's 150MW initial phase located in Kansas,
expected to reach COD in 3Q28. FISN can add additional SMRs to the site, pushing capacity to ~2GW under future phases.
■
Hyperscaler Bolts On More Renewable Capacity. Google (GOOGL, Not Rated) signed a new ~440MW PPA for solar capacity
from the Big Island solar plant in Arkansas as part of its partnership expansion with Entergy Arkansas (subsidiary of Entergy
(ETR, Buy, $117 PT, Analyst: Alex Kania)). The expanded partnership also includes ~1.4GW of battery storage capacity, helping
improve the reliability of GOOGL's solar offtake. Entergy noted that Google's investments are expected to generate at least
$500M in customer savings in the region, offsetting concerns around hyperscale data centers resulting in higher energy costs
for existing users.
■
Securing Offtake. Microsoft (MSFT, Not Rated) and NiSource (NI, Not Rated) have entered into a 20-year energy agreement
to power MSFT's previously announced Northwest Indiana data center campus. The agreement starts in 2028 and is expected
to generate ~$1.8B of customer savings as MSFT will pay for the new generation needed to satisfy this agreement. We note
that this has become the norm as hyperscalers (and other major data center developers) are funding grid expansion to help
facilitate deployments of large-load data centers without harming existing ratepayers.
