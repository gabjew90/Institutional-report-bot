Our Conclusion 
As of October 15, 2026 we initiate coverage of Constellation Energy (CEG) 
with an Outperformer rating and a $358 price target. We view CEG as a 
leading way to gain exposure to our positive power market outlook, 
supported by its scarce nuclear fleet, long-term contracts, and strong 
retained FCF. The recent Google and AWS agreements materially advance 
the thesis, supporting 1.1GW of nuclear uprates and illustrating CEG’s ability 
to convert data centre demand into contracted growth. While these deals 
have renewed optimism and lifted the shares, we believe the market still 
assigns insufficient value to CEG’s contracting upside, nuclear growth and 
reinvestment potential, supporting an attractive risk-adjusted upside. 
Key Points 
Nuclear At The Centre Of The Portfolio. CEG’s ~22GW nuclear fleet 
provides differentiated leverage to the growing value of carbon-free power at 
scale. The company has demonstrated its ability to monetize these assets 
and should have further opportunities as large-load customers seek long-
term supply and pricing certainty, as evidenced by the Crane restart and the 
recent Calvert Cliffs PPA announcement. With nuclear representing ~60% of 
current generation, even incremental improvements in contracted economics 
can be material. Importantly, these assets also have pricing mechanisms that 
provide downside protection, which is reassuring.  
Balance Sheet Strength Makes CEG’s FCF More Valuable. CEG’s 
financial advantage extends beyond lower leverage to the flexibility it 
provides. We forecast ~$2.4B of cumulative retained FCF in 2026–2028, 
including ~$1.1B of assumed debt repayments in 2026 (not necessary to 
meet credit metrics). This retained FCF can support additional organic 
investment (beyond the $5B we forecast), M&A and share repurchases—
CEG can remain selective rather than relying on any single avenue to 
sustain growth. Our sensitivity analysis suggests that reinvesting capital at 
low- to mid-teens returns could add approximately $0.25/sh to 2028 FCF/sh. 
Growth Runway Remains Substantial. CEG trades at a ~4.6x premium on 
EV/EBITDA to its U.S IPP peers, reflecting the market’s willingness to pay for 
its nuclear exposure, earnings protection, strong growth and balance sheet 
strength. Sustaining this premium will require CEG to translate its opportunity 
set into tangible EPS growth, including delivering the expected contribution 
from Calpine, securing attractive long-term contracts and deploying retained 
cash flow at compelling returns. We believe CEG is well positioned to do so. 
Valuation. We derive our $358 price target using an equal weighting of 
EV/EBITDA and FCF yield valuations, with both applied to our 2028 
estimates. We apply a 13.5x target EV/EBITDA multiple and a 5.75% target 
FCF yield, resulting in values of $384 and $332 per share, respectively. CEG 
currently trades at 13.4x/10.8x our 2027/2028 Adj. EBITDA estimates and 
yields 5.0%/6.4% on our 2027/2028 FCF estimates, respectively. Our upside/ 
downside scenario valuations are $597/$204.

Executive Summary 
We initiate coverage on Constellation Energy (CEG) with an Outperformer rating. Our 
price target of $358 implies potential upside of approximately 21% from the current share 
price. Our scenario analysis indicates a valuation range of $204-$597 per share, implying 
potential downside of 31% and upside of 102% from the current share price. 
In our view, CEG provides one of the most direct ways to participate in the tightening U.S. 
power market and structural demand growth thesis. The acquisition of Calpine materially 
broadens CEG’s geographic/generation mix, adding flexible gas-fired assets that complement 
the base nuclear portfolio. This broader platform provides the scale, operational flexibility and 
commercial reach to optimize the portfolio and pursue long-term contracts that can improve 
earnings visibility. The key debate recently is whether tightening market conditions and 
projected data centre load growth translate into upside for CEG, particularly as the 
Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Maryland Interconnection (PJM) and other markets evolve their 
treatment of data centre load, amid growing political sensitivity around electricity affordability. 
The recent Google and Amazon agreements provide tangible evidence that data centre 
demand can translate into long-term contracting and support approximately 1.1GW of nuclear 
uprates, modestly increasing our optimism around CEG’s growth runway. While the shares 
have rebounded from their recent lows, we believe the valuation still provides limited 
recognition of CEG’s nuclear scarcity value, broader contracting pipeline and capacity to 
reinvest retained FCF, leaving attractive upside on a risk-adjusted basis. 
Key Investment Drivers 
• 
Nuclear Fleet Supports A Durable Base + Upside. Nuclear provides ~60% of 
CEG’s annual generation output and serves as the foundation of the company’s long-
term outlook. The nuclear PTC also creates an asymmetric earnings profile, providing 
downside protection at lower power prices while preserving upside through premium 
contracting. Growing demand for carbon-free baseload power has provided and 
should continue to provide CEG with opportunities to monetize its nuclear fleet 
through long-term contracts. The company is currently progressing the 835MW 
Crane restart, while uprates provide another source of incremental generation, 
including a series of uprate/contract deals that unlock value form CEG’s nuclear fleet. 
• 
Strongest Balance Sheet Across The Peer Group. CEG combines an investment-
grade balance sheet with substantial internally generated cash flow, providing 
significant capacity to deploy FCF into value-creating opportunities without 
compromising its credit metrics. Across its most relevant peers, CEG has the lowest 
leverage. Based on our 2027E estimates, FFO/Net Debt is ~30% versus a ~18% 
peer average, while Net Debt/Adj. EBITDA of ~2.2x compares with ~3.5x for peers. 
This gives CEG greater capacity to fund new projects (including nuclear uprates and 
restarts), while retaining the capacity for M&A. CEG can deploy materially more 
capital toward growth before balance sheet constraints begin to limit that growth.  
• 
Premium Valuation Requires Delivery, Though Recent Weakness Improves The 
Setup. CEG’s premium valuation leaves less room for delays in contracting, project 
execution or the realization of Calpine synergies. While Calpine adds value, it also 
somewhat dilutes what has historically made CEG unique, shifting the story away 
from a relatively pure-play nuclear platform. This, alongside regulatory uncertainty, 
has weighed on the shares recently. However, we believe the recent pullback creates 
a more attractive entry point for investors.

Growth Drivers 
CEG’s near-term earnings step-up is driven primarily by the recent Calpine acquisition. Over 
time, the growth profile becomes increasingly supported by wholesale power prices, capacity 
revenues, data centre contracts (including the Crane restart), organic development 
opportunities and the reinvestment of retained cash flow, as summarized below and 
highlighted in the Exhibit 4 bar charts. 
• 
Asset Growth: Growth Led Initially By Calpine Acquisition. We forecast CEG’s 
EBITDA to increase from $4.6B in 2025 to $11.0B in 2028. The largest near-term driver is 
the Calpine acquisition, which contributes ~$2.8B of incremental EBITDA (net of 
dispositions) and accounts for ~45% EBITDA growth through 2028. The transaction also 
materially expands CEG’s generation platform, adding ~22.7GW of net capacity after 
required divestitures, compared with approximately 0.6GW of organic additions placed 
into service over the last three years. Organic development projects, including the Crane 
Energy Centre, nuclear uprates, storage, solar and Texas Energy Fund investments, 
provide additional visible growth beyond existing portfolio, contributing $304MM of 
incremental EBITDA from 2025 through 2028. 
• 
Upside From Existing Assets:  Organic And Market-driven Growth Become More 
Important Through The Forecast. Beyond the uplift from Calpine acquisition, stronger 
wholesale pricing, higher capacity revenues, retail growth and ~1.9GW of identified 
development opportunities drive incremental earnings growth through 2028. Wholesale 
and capacity markets represent the largest underlying EBITDA contributors, adding 
$3.0B of EBITDA from 2025 through 2028. This reflects CEG’s increased exposure to 
tightening power market fundamentals driven by rising electricity demand and constraints 
on adding new supply at a pace that keeps up with load growth.  
• 
Retained Cash Flow Provides An Engine For Further Growth. FCF is forecast to 
increase from $3.3B in 2025 to $6.5B in 2028, representing a ~26% CAGR, supported by 
higher EBITDA and healthy cash conversion. This growing cash flow also enhances 
CEG’s ability to fund capital investments, preserve balance sheet flexibility and support 
shareholder returns. Over time, retained cash flow should become an increasingly 
important source of value creation, in our view, providing capital for organic investment,

Valuation 
We believe EV/EBITDA and FCF yield are two of the most appropriate metrics for assessing 
CEG's valuation. We view the two metrics as complementary, with EV/EBITDA reflecting 
asset and earnings value, while FCF Yield captures the company's ability to convert those 
earnings into shareholder returns. 
•

reflects the scarcity value of its nuclear fleet, stronger long-term contracting potential, 
downside protection from nuclear support mechanisms, and confidence in its earnings 
growth and capital allocation.

Key Risks To Price Target 
Nuclear Fleet Operational Performance. CEG derives the vast majority of its generation 
and earnings from its nuclear fleet, making operational performance a critical earnings driver. 
Nuclear plants benefit from high capacity factors and low operating costs, but unexpected 
outages, equipment failures, extended refueling outages, maintenance projects, or regulatory 
actions can materially impact generation volumes and profitability. The financial impact is 
amplified during periods of elevated power prices, when lost output carries a higher 
opportunity cost. 
Data Center Expectations Embedded in Valuation. Investor enthusiasm surrounding long-
term load growth and direct contracting opportunities has contributed to a significant valuation 
re-rating. While management continues to benefit from strong customer interest, there is a 
risk that load growth materializes more slowly than expected, project timelines slip, or future 
contracts are executed at economics below investor expectations. Given the stock's premium 
valuation, sentiment could be sensitive to any perceived moderation in AI-related demand. 
Power Price and Capacity Market Exposure. Despite its large contracted and hedged 
portfolio, CEG's earnings and valuation remain influenced by long-term power market 
fundamentals. Lower-than-expected energy prices, weaker capacity market outcomes, 
accelerated renewable additions, or slower electricity demand growth could reduce future 
earnings expectations. Investors increasingly value CEG as a beneficiary of structural power 
scarcity, making the stock vulnerable to any easing in supply-demand conditions. 
Policy and Nuclear Support Program Risk. The investment case for nuclear generation 
benefits from favorable federal and state policy support related to decarbonization and 
reliability objectives. While nuclear power currently enjoys broad bipartisan support relative to 
many other clean energy technologies, changes in subsidy programs, tax incentives, 
environmental regulations, or market structures could impact its long-term economics. 
Calpine Integration and Transaction Risk. The Calpine acquisition materially increases 
CEG’s scale, geographic reach and exposure to gas-fired generation, but also introduces 
integration, execution and balance sheet risks. Synergies and commercial benefits could take 
longer to realize than expected, while weaker plant performance, higher-than-anticipated 
costs, required asset sales or unforeseen liabilities could reduce transaction accretion. The 
acquisition also makes CEG a less pure-play nuclear investment, which could affect the 
valuation premium historically assigned to the company.
