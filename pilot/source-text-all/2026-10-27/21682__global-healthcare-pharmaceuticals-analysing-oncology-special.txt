Heading into the ESMO 2026 conference, we have analysed the IQVIA BrandImpact 
solid tumours database as of September 2026 to identify market share and 
prescribing dynamics across key cancers, with a focus on Non-Small Cell Lung 
Cancer, Breast Cancer, Prostate Cancer, Bladder Cancer, Colorectal Cancer and 
Melanoma. Key high-level conclusions from the analysis are: (1) NSCLC: Share 
dynamics remain broadly stable in earlier treatment settings led by Keytruda (MRK), 
while targeted therapies are taking a greater share in later lines. Looking ahead, 
emerging modalities such as PD-1xVEGF could drive a more significant reshaping of 
the treatment landscape. (2) Breast Cancer: Kisqali (NOVN) leads in 1L HR+/HER2- 
and is expanding in the adjuvant setting, particularly across medium-risk and Stage 
II/III disease. In HER2+, Herceptin/Phesgo (ROP) retain leadership in 
adjuvant/neoadjuvant disease, while 1L is being reshaped by Enhertu (AZN) + Perjeta 
(ROP); importantly for Roche, Perjeta franchise volumes remain largely preserved, 
likely with a less favourable price/mix dynamic, while Enhertu maintains clear 
leadership in 2L+HR-/HER2+. (3) Prostate Cancer: Nubeqa (BAYN) leads in nmCRPC 
and continues to grow in 1L mCSPC, while Xtandi (PFE) retains leadership in 1L 
mCRPC and Pluvicto (NOVN) leads in 2L mCRPC. The recent PSMAddition expansion 
provides an opportunity for Pluvicto to move earlier into mCSPC, although this is not 
yet meaningfully captured in the dataset. (4) Bladder Cancer: Keytruda+Padcev 
(MRK/PFE) dominate this space, with incremental share gains driven by the 
subcutaneous Keytruda Qlex +Padcev combination. (5) Colorectal Cancer: The 
treatment landscape remains relatively stable and chemotherapy-led across lines, 
with targeted therapy playing a more selective role, particularly PD-1 therapies in 1L 
and EGFR-/VEGF-directed therapies in 2L, underscoring the continued need for 
innovation and more effective targeted treatment options. (6) Melanoma: 
PD-1-based therapies continue to dominate the landscape, with Opdivo + Yervoy 
(BMY) and Opdualag (BMY) leading 1L and Keytruda (MRK) leading adjuvant 
treatment, while BRAF/MEK combinations lead 2L, with Amtagvi (Iovance) emerging 
as a recent growth driver. Looking ahead, intismeran autogene (MRK/MRNA) + 
Keytruda is particularly compelling following the positive Phase III INTerpath-001 
readout in adjuvant melanoma and could represent the next significant evolution in 
the treatment landscape.

The mNSCLC market remains broadly stable, with PD-1 therapies continuing to lead 
share, while targeted therapies maintain a meaningful and increasingly established 
contribution. Keytruda (MRK) remains the clear market leader, averaging 43% of NWRx 
share from September 2025 through September 2026. This reflects its entrenched 
position as the standard of care across frontline treatment settings, despite a modest 
c.1 percentage point decline versus one year ago. Within the Keytruda franchise, uptake 
of the recently launched subcutaneous formulation, Keytruda Qlex, has helped offset 
declines in the IV formulation, with Keytruda Qlex reaching c.6% share since its 
September 2025 launch. 
The broader PD-1 combination landscape has expanded modestly over the past year 
and appears to be taking some share from Keytruda. Other PD-1 combination regimens 
accounted for 16% of NWRx share as of September 2026 and averaged 14% over the 
year. Within this segment, Opdivo-based therapies (BMY) averaged 7% share and 
remained relatively stable overall. However, use of Opdivo + Yervoy has increased from 
c.4-5% share to c.6-7% currently. Among other competing PD-1 therapies, the 
expansion has been primarily driven by Imfinzi, which increased from c.1% share one 
year ago to c.4% currently, partially offsetting a c.2 percentage point decline in 
Tecentriq share over the same period.

continues to represent the largest component of the segment, averaging 16% share over 
the year and maintaining 16% as of September 2026. Other targeted therapy segments,

contributions, while EGFR-directed therapies account for the majority of targeted

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
