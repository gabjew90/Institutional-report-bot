 
This week in Business & Information Services, a number of companies reported 3Q 
results, including RHI, ROL, MCO, MSCI and EFX, with investor conversations centered 
on the durability of improving demand and the opportunities and risks created by AI. 
Cyclical signals were mixed. RHI returned to positive staffing growth and provided an 
improving 4Q outlook, though negative contract volumes and longer-term AI 
uncertainty tempered investor enthusiasm. ROL also drew a mixed response as 
digital lead pressure and reduced 2026 guidance initially weighed on shares, 
followed by a rebound as investors viewed the selloff as overdone, saw potential 
stabilization in September lead trends and were encouraged by management’s 
reaffirmation of long-term targets. AI monetization drove the debate across MCO 
and MSCI. Investors viewed accelerating AI capex and potentially greater debt 
funding as central to the MCO bull case, but questioned the path to faster Analytics 
growth and saw valuations as full. Meanwhile, MSCI generated the most favorable 
sentiment following record net new sales, broadening demand and strong ABF 
growth, with investors seeing greater AI monetization potential. EFX discussions 
were constructive given diversified growth, government wins and AI-driven margin 
execution despite continued mortgage pressure. Separately, we updated our TRI 
estimates this week following the Global Print separation, which improves its organic 
growth profile.

Rollins (ROL, Buy): Weak 3Q as digital lead pressure persists; mitigation actions 
support long-term targets. Rollins reported weak 3Q results, with revenue and EPS 
below our estimates, organic growth decelerating, and 2026 organic growth and 
incremental margin guidance reduced. The central issue remains weaker residential 
customer acquisition, as AI and LLM-driven search further pressured digital lead flow 
following the softness that emerged in 2Q. That said, September organic website traffic 
and search leads improved, and ROL is working to strengthen visibility and conversion 
across traditional and LLM-driven search through greater pricing transparency, locally 
relevant content, improved listings and reviews and clearer service messaging, in 
addition to testing paid advertising on AI platforms. The strength of direct sales, 
door-to-door marketing and home builder partnerships demonstrates the advantage of 
ROL’s multichannel strategy. Recurring residential demand also remains healthy. We 
acknowledge near-term visibility is limited, with ROL’s 4-6% organic revenue growth 
outlook for 2026 highly sensitive to whether lead trends deteriorate, stabilize or improve 
and termite capacity constraints creating an additional execution headwind. That said, 
ROL reaffirmed its long-term targets of 7-8% organic growth and 30-35% incremental 
EBITDA margins, supported by improved conversion, investment reallocation, 
cross-selling, retention, productivity and 3-4% CPI-plus pricing. We remain Buy rated, 
supported by ROL’s recurring revenue base, channel diversification and multiple levers 
to restore growth even if digital lead generation does not fully return to prior patterns.

issuers are contributing more volume while higher-yielding leveraged-finance activity is 
moderating. In Moody’s Analytics, increasing AI usage, paying-customer adoption and

MSCI (MSCI, Buy): Strong 3Q with record net new sales and broadening growth. MSCI 
delivered strong 3Q results, with revenue above our estimate and consensus, quarterly 
recurring net new sales reaching all-time highs in Analytics and Private Capital Solutions 
and a 3Q high in Index, and ABF growth of 25% y/y, partially offset by expenses tracking 
toward the upper end of guidance. We expect subscription growth to benefit from 
broadening demand beyond traditional asset managers, with organic subscription 
run-rate growth of ~18% among hedge funds, >12% among asset owners and ~10% 
among banks and broker-dealers, compared with ~6% among asset managers. Product 
innovation provides an additional growth layer, as the contribution from new products 
to sales increased >50% y/y, Custom Index subscription run-rate growth exceeded 26%, 
and MSCI identified further white space across market makers and the broader trading 
ecosystem. We believe ABF growth will remain supported by healthy ETF inflows and 
record benchmarked AUM, with 3Q inflows reaching their second-highest quarterly 
level. Meanwhile, AI should accelerate MSCI’s product development and lower the 
growth rate of expenses required to run the existing business, driving modest ongoing 
margin expansion. We expect durable non-asset-manager demand, new-product 
adoption and continued Index monetization to drive sustained 
high-single/low-double-digit revenue growth and support valuation upside in MSCI 
shares. Link to our 10/20 note here. 
Equifax (EFX, Neutral): 3Q upside as diversified growth and AI execution temper 
mortgage headwinds. Equifax delivered resilient 3Q results, with revenue, EBITDA and 
EPS coming above consensus despite a sharp mortgage slowdown, supported by 
broad-based Diversified Markets growth and stronger-than-expected margin execution. 
EWS Verifier momentum remains strong, with diversified revenue growth of 13% y/y in 
3Q, supported by record additions, pricing, new products, customer penetration and 
government wins. Government represents an increasingly visible 2027 catalyst, as 
substantially all the $150mn of new state ACV signed year-to-date is expected by the 
company to benefit 2027, while new Medicaid and SNAP requirements are increasing 
state engagement beyond signed contracts. VantageScore adoption is accelerating, with 
~2,000 lenders using the score and volumes up >230% since April, though paid adoption 
remains limited. EFX’s 100 bps ex-FICO EBITDA margin expansion outlook reflects 
operating leverage and progress toward its $150mn multi-year AI productivity goal 
despite an $80mn mortgage revenue shortfall versus its prior outlook. That said, current 
run rates imply a high-single-digit decline in 2027 mortgage market transactions, with 
the greatest pressure in 1H, while bi-merge presents ~$80mn of annual revenue 
exposure before mitigation. We believe EFX’s valuation appropriately balances 
diversified growth, government and AI upside against sustained mortgage pressure and 
modest slowing in FI, auto and hiring activity. Link to our 10/20 note here.

Chart of the Week 
RHI Talent Solutions inflects to positive growth. Robert Half’s Talent Solutions 
business returned to growth in 3Q 2026, marking a recovery after the prolonged 
downturn that began in 2022. Talent Solutions revenue increased 2.3 y/y in 3Q, including 
1.7% growth in contract staffing and 6.2% growth in permanent placement, with 
technology remaining the strongest practice and finance and accounting returning to 
growth. Permanent placement has recovered more quickly, consistent with its greater 
historical cyclicality, and remained up 5% through the first three weeks of October 
versus 6% growth in 3Q. RHI expects Talent Solutions growth to accelerate to 3% in 4Q 
as enterprise demand remains healthy and SMB activity begins to improve, though hiring 
remains concentrated in mission-critical technology, data, cybersecurity and 
infrastructure roles. Longer term, AI disintermediation presents a risk to transactional 
and lower-complexity white-collar placements, potentially moderating the magnitude of 
the next staffing upcycle even if near-term contract volumes and permanent hiring 
continue to recover.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
