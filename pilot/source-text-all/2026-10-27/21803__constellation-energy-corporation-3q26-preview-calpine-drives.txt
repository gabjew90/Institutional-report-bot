3Q26 Preview: Calpine Drives Earnings Shape; Nuclear Contracting
Momentum Builds; TP to $341
 
RECOMMENDATION
Constellation (CEG) combines the nation’s largest nuclear fleet with a leading C&I retail platform
and Calpine’s dispatchable gas and powered-land capabilities. Recent agreements reinforce
opportunities to monetize existing nuclear output alongside customer-supported incremental
capacity. Long-term contracts with creditworthy counterparties increasingly convert merchant
exposure into durable earnings, creating decade-plus certainty and supporting a premium
valuation, in our view. We reiterate our Outperform rating and increase our target to $341/share.
3Q26 Preview - Estimates Lowered, but Still Above Street. We lower our 3Q26 adj. operating
EPS estimate to $3.90 from $4.40, still ~8% above Street consensus of $3.62 and up from $3.04 in
3Q25. The revision reflects a more measured allocation of Calpine’s summer-weighted earnings
contribution and a demanding legacy CEG comparison, with 3Q25 nuclear availability of 96.8%
and favorable commercial margins. Based on prior disclosures, we expect 3Q to capture a healthy
majority of the roughly $1/share of Calpine accretion remaining for 2H26. Higher PJM capacity
revenues and portfolio optimization provide a y/y tailwind, as well.
3Q26 Preview: Earnings Shaping and Commercial Catalysts
●
Calpine, commercial margins & market pricing. Recall, CEG raised 2026 guidance by $0.50/
share following 2Q on stronger customer margins, portfolio optimization, and buybacks,
partly offset by higher performance compensation. Commercial outperformance largely
reflects CEG’s “enhanced” earnings classification, with continued opportunities to capture
higher margins as customer contracts hit and hedges roll forward. Strong summer volatility
has also lifted PJM forwards, while ERCOT pricing remains muted. With Calpine substantially
hedged through 2027, pricing offers greater upside potential to 2028-29 hedge rolls.
●
Nuclear commercialization and remaining capacity. Recent Amazon/Google agreements
have largely committed the ~1 GW of nuclear uprates within CEG’s ~5-GW PJM Cycle 1 pipeline,
leaving ~4 GW of gas, storage and other development opportunities. With uprates proving
important to recent deal-making and additional projects likely facing longer interconnection
timelines, we expect further detail on remaining inventory and its potential to support
additional contracting. We also await greater clarity on the earnings uplift from recent
agreements (see p.3). Recall, CEG’s $0.40-$1.00/share EPS sensitivity per GW applies to
existing nuclear output, while uprates require separate capital and return assumptions.
Recent uprate commitments are split roughly evenly between Northern Illinois Hub and PJM
West, suggesting geographic differences in potential premiums. The growing portfolio of 15-
to 20-year contracts improves earnings durability, mostly in 2029+.
Updating 2026-28 Estimates. We revise our 2026 quarterly earnings cadence to better reflect
Calpine seasonality, maintaining 2026 adjusted EPS of $12.25. We make limited changes to our
2027 estimates (GAAP EPS to $12.50 from $12.54), while modestly raising 2028E to $15.50 from
$15.29 on stronger forward pricing and incremental commercial opportunities.
Valuation: Our $341 target price is based on our P/E valuation methodology (see below).

COMPANY DESCRIPTION
Constellation Energy Corporation (NASDAQ: CEG), headquartered in Baltimore, MD, is one of the nation's largest independent power producers
and the largest producer of carbon-free electricity in the U.S. The company became publicly traded in 2022 following its spin-off from Exelon
Corporation. CEG operates the nation's largest nuclear fleet, with major facilities in Illinois (Byron, Braidwood, Dresden), Pennsylvania (Limerick,
Peach Bottom), and Maryland (Calvert Cliffs), alongside hydroelectric, wind, and solar assets. Following its acquisition of Calpine in 2026, CEG
significantly expanded its generation portfolio to include natural gas, geothermal, and battery storage, while strengthening its position as a leading
competitive retail energy supplier serving commercial and industrial customers nationwide. The combined platform operates approximately
55 GW of generation capacity and serves roughly 2.5 million customer accounts. CEG is subject to oversight by the Federal Energy Regulatory
Commission (FERC), Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), and various state regulators, and participates in wholesale electricity markets
including PJM, ERCOT, NYISO, and ISO-NE. The company's strategy centers on optimizing its integrated generation and retail portfolio, expanding
long-term clean energy contracting, and developing incremental generation and powered-land opportunities to serve growing electricity demand.

Nuclear Commercialization: Contracted Capacity, Margin Upside, and Remaining Inventory
Nuclear Contracting Momentum Builds; Incremental Capacity Economics Remain Less Clear. CEG signed ~920 MW of long-term PPAs in 2Q26,
followed by Amazon‘s 690-MW Calvert Cliffs agreement and Google’s ~890-MW nuclear uprate program. These agreements increasingly combine
existing generation with customer-supported incremental capacity, with most recent earnings contributions beginning in 2029-32. CEG’s disclosed
sensitivity suggests $0.40-$1.00/share of annual EPS upside per GW of existing nuclear output contracted at a $20-$50/MWh premium to the PTC
floor. However, uprates require additional investment and carry different pricing and return economics. Recent uprate commitments are split
approximately evenly between Northern Illinois Hub and PJM West, with the former potentially toward the lower end of the disclosed premium
range and the latter toward the higher end. With most of the ~1 GW of nuclear uprates in PJM Cycle 1 now committed, additional uprates likely
face longer interconnection timelines, making the remaining ~4 GW of gas, storage, and other development opportunities increasingly important
to supporting further nuclear contracting.
Selected Announced Nuclear Contracting Agreements
●
Microsoft/Crane (835 MW; 2024): 20-year PPA supporting the restart of Three Mile Island Unit 1, now Crane Clean Energy Center; targeted
for 2H27 restart.
●
Meta/Clinton (1,121 MW; 2025): 20-year PPA covering the Clinton nuclear plant in Illinois, including ~30 MW of incremental uprates;
agreement begins in 2027.
●
Walmart/Dresden (176 MW; June 2026): Long-term nuclear agreement, including ~30 MW of uprates; combines existing output, capacity,
and environmental attributes.
●
Additional 2Q26 PPAs (~744 MW): Brings total 2Q contracting to ~920 MW, including Walmart. Average tenor of 18.5 years; commencement
during 2029-31, fully ramping by 2032.
●
Amazon/Calvert Cliffs (690 MW; September 2026): 20-year agreement comprising ~500 MW of existing generation and 190 MW of uprates
expected in 2030-32; includes a broader PJM retail supply relationship.
●
Google/Nuclear Uprates (~890 MW; October 2026): Agreement supporting incremental uprates across 11 nuclear units in Illinois,
Pennsylvania, and New Jersey, with additions expected during 2028-32. Plant-level economics and overlap with previously identified uprate
inventory remain to be clarified.
Selected Announced Contracting/Development (~4.46 GW gross): ~2.48 GW of existing nuclear output, ~1.14 GW of incremental urates, and 835
MW associated with the Crane restart. Totals are illustrative and subject to reconciliation of overlapping commitments and contract structures.
Reiterate Outperform; raise target price to $341/share. We favor CEG’s nuclear scarcity value, differentiated commercial platform, and
Calpine’s dispatchable generation and powered-land capabilities. Recent Amazon and Google agreements demonstrate the opportunity to
convert existing nuclear output into long-duration contracted earnings, while customer-supported uprates and additional PJM capacity provide
avenues for growth. The growing portfolio of 15- to 20-year agreements with creditworthy counterparties, alongside long-dated financing,
improves cash-flow visibility and reduces reliance on recurring merchant hedge rolls. We see further upside from premium contracting, Calpine
integration, and capital allocation.
Valuation: Our $341/share target price is based on ~22x 2028E adjusted operating EPS, a discount to CEG’s historical multiple to reflect regulatory
uncertainty, contract economics, and execution risk. We believe a premium to merchant IPP peers remains warranted given nuclear scarcity value,
inflation-supported Base earnings, and improving contracted cash-flow visibility. Key sensitivities include PJM pricing, Calpine hedge-roll margins,
customer contracting premiums, and incremental project returns.
Our valuation framework relies on forward P/E multiples relative to IPP peers and historical trading ranges. We assume earnings growth consistent
with our projections, upside from incremental contracting and generation monetization, and a valuation premium supported by improving
earnings durability, tempered by market structure and execution risks.

Valuation Methodology
Constellation Energy Corporation
Our valuation methodology relies on forward P/E multiples, assessed against comparable IPP peers and historical trends. We assume: 1) earnings
growth based on our forward-looking EPS projections, driven by new hyperscaler contracts; 2) a P/E re-rating reflecting CEG’s load growth profile
compared to peers, tempered by its heavy debt burden from capital-intensive operations; and 3) a stable valuation range shaped by market
conditions, typical weather, and contract execution.

Company Specific Risk Factors
Constellation Energy Corporation
CEG faces a range of risks that could impact their financial and operational stability. The AI growth theme, driving data center demand, introduces
volatility if tech adoption slows, while customer credit risks arise if hyperscaler contracts weaken. Weather risks, beyond typical patterns, may
disrupt generation or inflate costs, compounded by commodity price swings in fuel markets. The capital-intensive sector is vulnerable to interest
rate hikes, raising financing costs for projects or debt, while limited hedging exposes uncontracted power to price drops. Regulatory uncertainty,
including RTO rule-making and compliance mandates, could delay growth, and concentrated ownership or M&A missteps add strategic pressure.
We rate CEG with an RJA Suitability Rating (SR) of Moderately Aggressive Risk/Wealth Accumulation (MA/ACC). CEG is an investment-grade rated
firm with very persistent cash flows. We view the company’s profitability as significantly more durable and persistent than other IPPs, but differs
significantly from regulated utilities. CEG depends on volatile, market-driven revenues from merchant power and contracts, raising exposure to
commodity price swings that hedging may not always mitigate effectively. Additional risks persist if CEG's Calpine deal does not synergize as
expected, and/or hyperscalers back out of their contracts for any reason. Additionally, weather risks beyond typical patterns may affect operations
or PJM/ERCOT/CAISO demand. Lastly, interest rate risk in the capital-intensive sector could raise financing costs.
