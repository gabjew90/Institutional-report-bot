We provide our takeaways from the UEFW (United European 
Gastroenterology Week Congress) for ABVX which we believe 
are positive in totality given the continued demonstration of 
durable efficacy for obefazimod in ulcerative colitis (UC). Whilst 
the conference updates included positive data for GILD’s (covered 
by Salveen Richter) oral anti-integrin α4β7 emvistegrast in UC (see 
here), the competitor data should not come as a surprise to ABVX 
investors, in our view, given the validated mechanism. For 
obefazimod, ABVX presented subgroup analyses of the Phase 3 
maintenance data and 3-year Phase 2 data, supporting consistent 
benefits across patients who were exposed or naive to prior 
advanced therapy and durable long-term remission. On safety, one 
non-melanoma skin cancer (NMSC) case was observed in the Phase 
2 trial, corresponding to an incidence of 0.8%, consistent with what 
was observed in obefazimod’s Phase 3 maintenance trial where 
NMSC occurred in 0.5% and 2.2% of patients receiving obefazimod 
25 mg and 50 mg. We believe NMSC is more likely to be included in 
the Warnings and Precautions section rather than the black box 
section of the FDA label. We do not expect this to ultimately impact 
utility, as NMSC has also been observed in clinical trials of other 
approved UC therapies, including L-23 (Tremfya), IL-12/23 (Stelara), 
Integrin (Entyvio), S1P (Zeposia and Velsipity), and TNF-a 
(Remicade). For emvistegrast, we believe Phase 2 efficacy appears 
broadly comparable to obefazimod’s Phase 3 profile in cross-trial 
comparisons. We see limited impact on our obefazimod forecasts, 
as we expect obefazimod to be used largely in the 2L+ setting, after 
IL-23 and anti-integrin therapies as discussed in more detail in our 
recent initiation. Overall, we see the UEFW update as 
incrementally de-risking obefazimod’s profile building on the 
initial ABTECT 1&2 data sets and continue to see our €4bn peak 
sales estimate for the drug in UC as achievable.

ABVX obefazimod data release at UEG 
 
 
New obefazimod data further support its durable maintenance profile, with 
n
consistent benefits observed across patients who were exposed or naive to prior 
advanced therapy and sustained long-term remission in the Phase 2 trial. In the 
Phase 3 maintenance trial, obefazimod 25mg and 50mg demonstrated consistent 
efficacy in patients regardless of prior exposure to advanced therapies, showing 
placebo-adjusted Clinical Remission rates of 28-33% in advanced 
therapy-experienced patients and 45-48% in naive patients. In the 3-year analysis 
of the open-label Phase 2 trial which enrolled patients who achieved mucosal 
healing and absence of bleeding (Mayo Endoscopic subscore = 0 or 1 AND Rectal 
bleeding subscore = 0) following 1 year treatment with obefazimod, obefazimod 
25mg as the long-term maintenance therapy showed sustained clinical benefits, with 
clinical remission rates remaining at 85% at Week 144 vs. 91% at baseline. 
One new non-melanoma skin cancer case (0.8%) observed in the Phase 2 
n
extension trial. In the 3-year analysis of the open-label Phase 2 trial testing 
obefazimod 25mg, 0.8% of patients (1/130) experienced non-melanoma skin cancer 
(NMSC). The rate was broadly consistent with what was observed in obefazimod’s 
Phase 3 maintenance trial where 0.5%/2.2% of patients on obefazimod 25mg/50mg 
had NMSC including basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma. We continue 
to believe NMSC could be listed in the warnings section of the FDA label but do not 
see it as concern to physicians given that similar signals were also observed in clinical 
trials testing immunology drugs targeting IL-23 (Tremfya), IL-12/23 (Stelara), Integrin 
(Entyvio), S1P (Zeposia and Velsipity), and TNF-a (Remicade) (see our note here).

GILD Phase 2 data for oral anti-integrin emvistegrast 
 
 
Emvistegrast demonstrated similar levels of efficacy in the Phase 2 trial 
n
compared with obefazimod in Phase 3 trials in cross-trial comparisons. In the 
Phase 2 SWIFT trial where most recruited patients were naive to advanced therapy 
(78% naive), emvistegrast 200mg (the go-forward dose) demonstrated 
placebo-adjusted clinical remission/clinical response rates of 23%/29% at Week 12, 
which compares to 22.7%/28% demonstrated by obefazimod in advanced 
therapy-naive patients in the ABTECT 1&2 trials. 
We see the data release as in line with our prior expectations that emvistegrast 
n
may be used earlier than obefazimod in the treatment algorithm given the 
established status of anti-integrin injectable Entyvio in the 1L setting. As such, 
given obefazimod’s unique mechanism in UC (which we expect will require multiple 
lines of therapy and potentially combinations) we see limited impact from the 
emvistegrast data to our obefazimod forecasts which assume that obefazimod will 
be mostly used in the 2L+ setting post IL-23 and anti-integrin therapies.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.  
M&A Rank 
Across our global coverage, we examine stocks using an M&A framework, considering both qualitative factors and quantitative factors (which may vary 
across sectors and regions) to incorporate the potential that certain companies could be acquired. We then assign a M&A rank as a means of scoring 
companies under our rated coverage from 1 to 3, with 1 representing high (30%-50%) probability of the company becoming an acquisition target, 2 
representing medium (15%-30%) probability and 3 representing low (0%-15%) probability. For companies ranked 1 or 2, in line with our standard 
departmental guidelines we incorporate an M&A component into our target price. M&A rank of 3 is considered immaterial and therefore does not 
factor into our price target, and may or may not be discussed in research.
