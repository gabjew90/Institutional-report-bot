The Commodities Feed: Attacks on
Saudi Arabia fan oil supply worries
Oil prices remain well-supported after fresh attacks on Saudi Arabia, with
traders focused on risks to the outlook rather than the resilience of
Persian Gulf supplies
Source: Shutterstock
Energy - Kuwait pumping more than 2m b/d
Oil prices remain well bid, with ICE Brent back above $110/bbl this morning following additional
deadly attacks on Saudi Arabia. Drones struck the Shaybah oilfield and Dammam's
international airport. An oil processing plant was the target of the attack. Saudi Arabia’s
energy ministry said production hasn’t been impacted. The escalation prompted the US State
Department to order non-emergency US government personnel to leave the country. This will
concern markets, suggesting little improvement in Middle East tensions, and possibly further
escalation.
Despite the volatile environment, oil flows from the Persian Gulf are holding up. According to
the CEO of Kuwait Petroleum Corp, Kuwait is exporting as much crude oil as before the war and
producing more than 2m b/d. However, he admitted that the shipping of refined products is
more of an issue. While oil flows have remained elevated, President Trump has expressed
concern that oil prices remain elevated due to supply risks. This leaves plenty of uncertainty

over the sustainability of flows. The sizeable risk premium currently embedded in prices is
unlikely to fade until clear signs of de-escalation emerge and reassure investors that regional
supply will remain uninterrupted.
EIA inventory data shows US crude oil inventories fell by 3.06m barrels over the last week.
There was an additional 300k-barrel fall in Strategic Petroleum Reserves. The data still reflects
the impact of hurricane activity in the US Gulf of Mexico, with US oil production estimated to
have fallen 410k b/d week-on-week. Crude oil imports fell by 291k b/d, while exports fell by
1.13m b/d. For refined products, implied gasoline demand rose 574k b/d WoW. This
contributed to the 2.95m barrel decline in gasoline inventories. However, the diesel market saw
some relief, with distillate stocks rising by 1.68m barrels. Total distillate stocks stood at just
over 104m barrels, a seasonal low dating back 30 years.
Metals — Indonesia nickel output cuts
LME nickel prices gained significantly yesterday, settling 3.65% higher on coordinated
production cuts in Indonesia. Major Chinese nickel producers operating in Indonesia plan to
curb production to support weak nickel prices. Under the agreement, refineries would halve
production of mixed hydroxide precipitate (MHP), a key battery feedstock. Nickel pig iron (NPI)
output for stainless steel production would be reduced by about 20%. A similar proposal failed
to reach consensus in August, but persistent price weakness increased pressure on producers
to take action.
Emirates Global Aluminium, the Middle East’s largest aluminium producer, has restored half
capacity at its Al Taweelah smelter in Abu Dhabi after damage sustained amid the Iran war.
The company remains on track to fully restore operations by the first quarter next year.
However, a complete recovery in sales will also depend on the normalisation of shipping
through the Strait of Hormuz.
Agriculture — Wheat extends losses on ceasefire hopes
CBOT wheat prices fell for a fifth consecutive session, settling 2.33% lower yesterday. Prices fell
after Trump called for a Ukraine-Russia peace agreement, easing the geopolitical risk
premium. After talks with US officials, Volodymyr Zelensky adopted a more conciliatory tone,
while Vladimir Putin also met with a US envoy. No tangible progress was announced. A
ceasefire could boost grain exports from both countries, improving global supply and weighing
on prices.
