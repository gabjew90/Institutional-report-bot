Industrial Metals Weekly
Negative risk tone weighs on copper equities
Our view: Copper equities fell 7% w/w while spot copper was relatively flat, as a 3.7% US inflation print
and an AI-led equity selloff drove sentiment for most of last week. On market fundamentals, Chilean
labour risk has cleared up as Escondida supervisors ratified a 36-month contract on October 14, and
Centinela’s two unions ratified a deal on Sunday, ending a strike of more than a week. With the labour
premium removed, the focus turns to other ongoing supply challenges, the latest coming from Codelco’s
partial suspension at El Teniente after a landslide killed two contractors. We're also seeing regional prices
emerge as significant metal has been diverted to the US, with Codelco offering copper to its European
customers at a record high premium of $350/tonne, underscoring the tightness in the physical market.
Separately, in the Iron ore market, Singapore futures dipped below $90/t for the first time in two years
on soft steel demand and strong supply, although the price has firmed up in recent days on the seasonal
pick up in steel production (CRU). Meanwhile, Vale’s CEO estimates higher energy and freight costs
have made ~10% of seaborne supply, roughly 160Mt, unprofitable, which should support a recovery
(mining.com). We fine-tune estimates for CIA, HBM, LIF, LUN; see individual company pages for details.
• Base metals down: Copper prices eased 0.6% to $6.62/lb as nickel fell 2.4% to $6.88/lb and zinc fell
2.6% to $1.70/lb.
• Bulks mixed: Met coal prices declined 0.4% to $267.7/mt, while iron ore prices recovered just 0.7%
to $90.90/dmt.
• Published research: We published on CIA and LIF pre-released production results, here and here.
Chart of the Week: EV/lb valuations for select Junior Developers
Junior copper developers have re-rated roughly 2-3x since 2023. From 2023 through 2024, multiples
were stagnant despite copper rising, suggesting the market was skeptical on execution and permitting
timelines. The inflection came in mid-2025 when EV/lb jumped from $0.04 to $0.08–0.09, driven not
just by peak copper prices but by improved sentiment on scarcity of derisked projects and conviction in
the supply deficit thesis. What's notable is that even as copper has hovered in the $6-6.50/lb all-time-
high range, multiples have held at $0.08–0.10/lb rather than continuing their trajectory. We think that
as these projects continue permitting and other de-risking activities, valuations can continue to re-rate.
We were pleased to host 16 junior copper developers at our third annual Sparks Flying conference last
week, our takeaways here.

Valuation
We value Champion by applying a 50% weight to 1.0x our NAV
and a 50% weight to 5.0x EV/EBITDA using our average FY27E
EBITDA estimate. The 1.0x NAV and 5.0x EV/EBITDA are in line
with the range of the company’s historical average and peer
group. Our C$8.00/sh price target and relative return support
our Outperform rating.
Investment summary
• Champion Iron is a pure-play high-quality iron ore
producer in Canada. We think it has potential to create
value for shareholders through production growth and
projects in the Labrador Trough. We continue to see an
opportunity for CIA to re-rate higher as the company
achieves production growth and focuses on cost reduction
while progressing secondary projects. Champion is on track
to deliver consistent production of 15Mtpa following the
completion of the Phase 2 expansion with the next layer
of growth coming from the direct-reduction pellet feed
project (DRPF) which will upgrade half of Bloom Lake ore to
69% Fe. The project entered initial commissioning with first
shipments of DR quality iron ore anticipated by the end of
CY H1 2026.
• Strong financial position and organic growth pipeline: We
expect Champion to balance return of capital via its semi-
annual $0.10 div with investment in its pipeline of organic
growth projects. It is committed to delivering the DRPF
project & upgrading one of Bloom Lake's concentrators to
produce 69% iron ore by mid-CY26, which we expect to
cost C$471M. We also think further optimization work and
partnership discussions could unlock incremental value at
Kami following the recent technical report.
Potential catalysts
• Ongoing: De-stocking of inventory built up during disrupted
rail operations / Consistent commercial production at Phase
2 / Potential partnership discussions and optimization work
at Kami
• CY26: DRPF ramp up
• CY26: Successful integration of Rana Gruber and its
operations
Risks to rating and price target
• Commodity price. Champion’s fundamentals and share
price are levered to the iron ore market and overall global
growth. While we expect that Champion can generate FCF
at an iron ore price below our long-term forecast of U$100/
t (P65), shares are likely to react negatively to a falling iron
ore price in the absence of other catalysts.
• Execution risk. Champion's NAV is largely single asset
concentrated, which inherently has more risks. In the
event of an unexpected shutdown, there would be no
other sources of operating cash flow to fund sustaining
and operating costs. Furthermore, the company does
not operate its own railway to port, which has recently
pressured sales volumes.
