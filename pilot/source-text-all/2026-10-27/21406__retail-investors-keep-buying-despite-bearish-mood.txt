 
Retail Investors Keep Buying Despite Bearish Mood 
 
BY TYLER DURDEN 
WEDNESDAY, OCT 21, 2026 - 02:45 PM 
Authored by Simon White, Bloomberg macro strategist, 
Retail investors’ allocation to stocks is near a 25-year high despite their bearish outlook. 
Michael Burry, Seth Klarman, Jeremy Grantham, Ray Dalio and now Ken Griffin are some of the 
prominent investors proclaiming we are likely in a bubble. 
This hardly seems to be the zeitgeist, with the percentage of Bloomberg stories mentioning 
“bubble” declining, and well below peaks reached in late 2025 and summer 2020. 
 
The “masses” seem less concerned about a bubble, and it’s showing up in their positioning.  
The American Association of Individual Investors’ index of retail investors’ allocation to stocks 
has reached a level basically not seen since the tech bubble burst in 2000 (current allocation is 
71.8%, it briefly pipped this to 72% at the end of 2017, just before the “Volmageddon” 
mini-crash). 
 

 
 
This is despite retail investors’ sentiment on the market, which has been getting more bearish. 
Retail’s zeal for stocks despite their reservations is shown in the Conference Board’s Consumer 
Confidence Survey. Over half of respondents expect stock prices to be higher in the next 12 
months, a near 35-year high for the series. 
 
 

 
Yet the percentage expecting lower rates and lower inflation has fallen appreciably in recent 
months. 
Thus retail investors expect further stock gains and are maintaining a high exposure to the stock 
market despite expectations that the backdrop will be less favorable, and the warnings of some 
influential investors. 
As society becomes more individualistic, the hoi polloi have less respect for those in authority, 
and in retail traders’ case it may even come with a handsome pay off.
