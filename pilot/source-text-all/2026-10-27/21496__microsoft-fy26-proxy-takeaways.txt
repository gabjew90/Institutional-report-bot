FY26 Proxy Takeaways 
We analyzed Microsoft's FY26 proxy statement (filed Oct. 21) to better 
understand changes in management compensation and achievement of internal 
financial targets. Our main takeaways include (1) both total revenue and 
operating income meaningfully exceeded internal targets, with revenue 
outperforming by 1.7% and operating income by 3.6%; (2) Azure & Other Cloud 
Services revenue growth significantly exceeded management's internal 
expectations, outperforming its 34.0% growth target by 5.7pts, a notable reversal 
from last year's modest miss; (3) Microsoft's FY27 executive compensation 
program has been simplified, with annual cash incentives now entirely 
determined by financial performance, subject to an individual modifier, and (4) 
management has narrowed its FY27 performance stock award (PSA) metrics to 
better align compensation with its strategic priorities in the AI era. We discuss our 
key takeaways further herein. 
FY26 Cash Incentive Performance vs Plan 
Revenue exceeded the internal target by 1.7%, reversing last year's modest 0.5% 
miss (adj. for net impact of revenue timing effects as well as FX) and also 
comparing favorably with FY24's 1.1% beat. While revenue has generally tracked 
Microsoft's internal expectations relatively closely over the last several years, 
FY26 represents the strongest outperformance since at least FY23. 
From a profitability perspective, operating income came in 3.6% above target, 
marking the third consecutive year of outperformance following beats of 5.0% in 
FY25 and 7.3% in FY24. We attribute this to a combination of topline 
outperformance noted above and mgmt.'s continue focus on driving operating 
leverage amid the ramp in AI infrastructure related investments. 
Going forward, Microsoft has narrowed its incentive performance metrics for 
FY27 to revenue and operating income (50% weighting to each). While Security, 
Product, Customers & Stakeholders and Culture (combined 30% weighting in 
FY26) have been eliminated as standalone metrics, an individual modifier (+/- 25% 
weighting) has been added in their place that will reportedly continue evaluating 
executives in these areas.

FY26 PSA Performance vs Plan 
Azure & Other Cloud Services revenue notably came in 5.7pts above target 
despite continued supply constraints as growth accelerated meaningfully vs. the 
modest decel implied in the original target, in our view signaling very solid 
execution by the team. Microsoft Cloud revenue ex-Azure came in above target 
as well, but by a much more modest 0.5pts and with growth slowing vs. the prior 
year. Xbox Content and Services was the clear underperformer, with revenue 
declining 6% against an internal target of 4% growth. Going forward, Microsoft 
also disclosed that it narrowed its FY27 PSA metrics to emphasize a smaller 
number of strategic growth drivers but declined to identify the specific changes.

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that 
promise to pay fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these 
cash flows), increases in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus 
cause a loss. The longer the maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher 
will be the loss. Upside surprises in inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most 
common adverse macroeconomic shocks to receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client 
segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax 
policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of 
positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are also important risk factors. The sensitivity of 
fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by indexing the contracted cash flows to 
inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in emerging markets. The index fixings 
may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables they are intended to track. The 
choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating coupon rates (i.e., 
coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. Funding in 
a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk. Options on swaps 
(swaptions) the risks typical to options in addition to the risks related to rates movements.
