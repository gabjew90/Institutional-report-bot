Global Daily 
Market comment 
The New York Times reports (our geopolitical base case) that the US and Israel will strike Iran 
after the mid-term elections, as Israel just carried out military exercises up to the Iranian 
border. However, a Haaretz journalist says the visit by US Secretary of War Hegseth to Tel Aviv 
saw new intel leading to a change in the plan to a pre-midterm attack. Unless this is a bluff then 
it’s how many days vs. weeks before this war starts again – and in full. PM Netanyahu just 
reiterated his aim is to topple the regime; the IRGC says it’s preparing to go on the offensive, and 
nothing is off limits. Could that include oil and LNG facilities or desalination plants? 
We’re already there in Saudi, where Houthi strikes killed three at an airport and an Aramco 
oilfield. The US told some diplomats to depart Saudi following official travel warnings for 12 
countries in the region. Egypt stated it will defend Riyadh, following France and the UK and Turkey 
and Pakistan, but like the others hasn’t acted yet and won’t attack the Houthis in Yemen. Egypt 
also rejected the Turkey-Libya maritime boundaries deal, as did Israel, while Qatar, Pakistan, 
Indonesia, and Turkey signed the Badr MOU on collaboration in military supply chains. Hezbollah 
is rebuilding its capabilities in Beirut with Israel preparing for new fighting, Hamas gave an 
unprecedented public warning threatening Israel with another October 7-style attack, and Israel 
just made a fresh incursion into southwestern Syria. In short, the fog of war may be about to 
descend again.  
Indeed, the geopolitical logic is still that the US grinds out a win vs. Iran or ensures the region 
remains unstable and cannot swing behind Iran and its friends. That means 2026 could be the 
new market normal, a risk oil is starting to price as even if flows through Hormuz may be close to 
pre-war levels, it’s at a $40 per barrel premium. 
By contrast, we could potentially see the fog of war lift re: Russia-Ukraine. Following the 
scrambling of Ukraine’s defences by the speed cap placed on the US Starlink system, and Russian 
drone tech proving very hard to defend against, both Putin and Zelenskyy say they support 
trilateral ceasefire talks in early November with the US. Politico notes Europe now also seeks a 
negotiator-in-chief for Ukraine, a 180 from its ‘no talks’ stance. However, it appears Europe isn’t 
invited regardless, a slight to Ukraine’s primary backer: but such is realpolitik - and the difference 
between a goal of ‘strategic autonomy’ and having achieved it. 
Of course, such ceasefires attempts have collapsed before. If they do again, expect Russia to hit 
Ukraine harder and try to crush its economy into winter and Ukraine to strike more oil refineries, 
so Russia has to export crude and import refined product from Asia, pushing up refined product 
prices globally.  
Or, we could see a North-South Korea-style frozen conflict, not ‘peace’, as both sides pause to 
rearm for a future round. In the meantime, the German military is warning of a “significant” 
Russian sabotage threat; the UK press claims Russia is sending fishing trawlers to spy on it; and as 
Ukraine asks for another €70bn for its 2027 war budget following the last €90bn, the EU is asking 
Canada and the UK to help out as way to deepen overall relations – as Ottawa still plans to strike 
an accord with Europe that somehow makes two economies deeply dependent on the US less so. 
Note if the ceasefire talks fail, we could see escalation in two conflating wars at once; and if 
they succeed, it could allow US and even European attention to shift to the Middle East.

In Asia, the Hong Kong press says Taiwan’s ‘Iran-style attack drone’ could swamp PLA air 
defences, showing lessons from that war are being applied all over. However, scale matters 
most, which increases the need for AI and defence spending as well as control of upstream to 
downstream supply chains. 
In the Americas, the US accused Canada of election interference(!), and El Salvador President 
Bukele initiated a military mission to Haiti to release four Salvadoran aviation technicians held 
hostage and threatened: “Today’s attack is nothing compared to what is coming if we do not get 
the hostages back alive. Do not take our words for empty threats. We have already finished the 
gangs in El Salvador, and we can finish with you too.” This isn’t a major geopolitical development, 
but the language and actions are another notable contrast with ‘rules-based order’ inaction.  
Indeed, Germany’s Merz perhaps just advertised little grasp of the German word ‘realpolitik’. 
He stated at a public forum that while he wants an EU trade “kill switch” to help find a “new 
balance in trade” with China (i.e., Europe gets to keep industries and sell goods to China, not just 
lose them and buy from it), he doesn’t want to use it. He noted, “We are opening the toolbox, we 
are showing the toolbox, but we do not want to make use of our toolbox…. This is not the 
beginning of a trade war with China. We are looking for peaceful solutions, but the level for that is 
fair trade.”  
That’s laudable, but the old European adage is si vis pacem, para bellum – if you want peace, 
prepare for war. Few want war, but all must be able to show they’re willing to fight one to 
maintain deterrence, the lack of which ironically makes war more likely. Or did Merz try to make a 
trade threat but fail because it’s just not his technocratic style? The Hong Kong press wasn’t 
quivering in their boots if so, but only time and trade data will tell (and please see this note on 
EU-China trade relations.) 
Meanwhile, AI just managed to find a software bug that puts up to 70% of the world’s websites 
at risk – and AI is rushing to fix it before other AIs hack everything: quite a normal 2026 
headline, all things considered. 
Back in the mundane world of inflation data, following yesterday’s UK prints of 0.4% m-o-m, 3.5% 
y-o-y headline, and 3.7% for services, today saw Kiwi Q3 CPI at 0.7% q-o-q vs. 0.8% expected and 
3.7% y-o-y vs. 3.8%, with tradables up 0.2% q-o-q and non-tradables up 1.0% - worrying 
considering what could be in the pipeline on the tradables side given the geopolitical backdrop.  
Indeed, ‘War and Peace’ is long. It drags on and on and on; and here, it is dragging itself all 
over inflation and markets in the process. Thay may frustrate those who want a far less epic 
working environment. Yet as Tolstoy notes in his giant novel, “We can know only that we know 
nothing. And that is the highest degree of human wisdom.” 
Day ahead
