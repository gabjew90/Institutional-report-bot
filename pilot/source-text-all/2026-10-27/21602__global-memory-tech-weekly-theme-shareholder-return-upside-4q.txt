Global Memory Tech 
Weekly theme: Shareholder return upside, 
4Q guidance preview, resilient spot/export 
Industry Overview 
   
 
 
Expect additional dividends/buybacks from Hynix/Samsung 
We see potential catalysts in additional shareholder returns from SK Hynix (a special 
year-end cash dividend following its recent W40tn buyback) and Samsung Electronics (a 
new buyback post-3Q cash dividend of W30tn). Both could address these returns at their 
respective earnings calls on 28-29 Oct. We expect Korean memory chipmakers to step 
up dividends and buybacks, given exceptionally low P/E ratios (following severe share-
price corrections) and strong growth in FCF (despite concerns about a downturn). As of 
4Q26, annualized FCF already exceeds W200tn/W300tn for Hynix/Samsung, implying 
potential annual shareholder returns of W100tn+/W150tn+. We retain our assumed 
buyback:dividend split of 60:40 for Hynix (holdco SK Square owns only a 21% stake), vs 
40:60 for Samsung (far more retail shareholders, incl. the Lee family, than Hynix). Neither 
company has announced new dividend or buyback plans, but management at both may 
view current share prices as too low relative to fundamentals. We will host a large-group 
webcast with TrendForce on 10 Nov to reassess the memory super-cycle and AI boom. 
Bullish guidance likely; cycle differs from 2018 peak 
We expect bullish guidance at next week's earnings calls. Hynix: The successful ramp-up 
of 12-hi HBM4 (50%+ of total HBM sales by end-4Q26); additional 8-hi HBM4 
production if NVIDIA pays a price premium per bit of ~10% (or if margins are better than 
for 12-hi); further conventional DRAM ASP upside in 1H/2H27 vs the 4Q26 peak, given 
the ongoing chip supply shortage (vs AI demand), completion of phase 1 of the new 
Yongin fab in Korea in Feb’27, near-complete migration to the 1c node at Korea's 
conventional DRAM fab, and progress in preparing HBM4e samples on the 1c node. 
Samsung: HBM market-share gains; quarterly contract price upside even under LTAs, 
and strong growth in long-term orders, including agreements for chip deliveries in 2029-
30 at high prices; accelerated ramp-up at Pyeongtaek P4 and construction of P5; HBM-
focused migration to the 1c node (minimal migration for LPDDR5 even in mid-2027); 
limited support for de-spec orders (Samsung unlikely to expand 8-hi HBM capacity but to
more actively expand 12-hi HBM4, followed by HBM4e in 2027); and memory chip supply 
to remain broadly tight relative to demand even in 2028 (current channel inventories 
generally lean, vs exceptionally high in 4Q18 after the cloud boom). 
Spot prices remain resilient; likely robust exports 
Memory spot prices have remained resilient despite stock-market volatility and concerns 
about an AI bust. DDR5 prices have risen steadily, gaining 5-6% MTD. Although legacy 
DDR4 and NAND spot prices fell a further 1-2% this week, they remain up 10-20% QoQ. 
The 4Q DRAM contract prices currently under negotiation appear broadly in line with our 
forecasts for 5-10% increases or better, led by high-end LPDDR5. However, we see 
minimal contract price increases for PC/smartphone NAND (similar to softening spot 
prices), vs mid-single-digit% increases for eSSD. Separately, Korea’s semis exports 
remained strong in Oct (up 200%+ YoY during the first 20-day period).
