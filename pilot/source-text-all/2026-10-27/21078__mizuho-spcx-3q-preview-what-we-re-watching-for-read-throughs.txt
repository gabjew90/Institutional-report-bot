3Q Preview - What We're Watching For + Read-Throughs
AI is the largest swing factor for 3Q26 revenue and EBITDA. Consensus has AI revenue
of $6.30B (+146% Q/Q from $2.56B) and AI adj. EBITDA of $3.32B (from $1.15B), which
lifts consolidated revenue to $12.87B (+65% Q/Q) and adj. EBITDA to $6.59B (+86% Q/
Q), with adj. EPS of $0.15 (from ($0.09)). The key KPI is $/MW of compute. Consensus
nameplate compute is 1.74GW in 3Q26 (from 1.4GW) and 2.20GW in 4Q26, so the debate
is delivery, utilization and the price per MW. Consensus is also modeling Starlink net adds
of ~2.2MM (from 1.7MM); consensus FY26 revenue is $44.6B (+139% Y/Y) and FY27 is
$108.2B.
Key Points
Items on our focus list: (i) AI revenue and EBITDA. Cons. AI revenue of $6.30B (from
$2.56B) is split between AI Solutions & Infrastructure at $5.84B (from $2.19B) and
Advertising at $658MM (from $367MM). Cons. AI adj. EBITDA of $3.32B implies a ~53%
margin (from ~45%) and a ~58% incremental margin, with AI operating income near
breakeven at ($14MM) (from ($1.26B)). Small variances in recognition, utilization and
pricing move consolidated adj. EBITDA of $6.59B materially; (ii) $/MW of compute is the
KPI. SpaceX exited 2Q at 1.4GW of nameplate compute (from 1.0GW in 1Q; 400MW a
year ago). Cons. is 1.74GW at 3Q26 and 2.20GW at 4Q26, consistent with management's
expectation of more than 2GW by YE26. Cons. YE27 is ~5.8GW, which sits at the low
end of management's framing of capacity closer to 10GW than 5GW. Management has
cited under one year paybacks on new deployments; (iii) CRWV/NBIS pricing markers
remain constructive. CoreWeave (covered by Gregg Moskowitz) disclosed signed 3-6
month contracts at approximately $40MM/MW of annualized revenue, equivalent to ~
$40/W/year. Nebius disclosed $20-25MM/MW/year for its core 1-3 year agreements
and $40-50MM/MW/year opportunities for shorter contracts, typically 3-6 months. These
disclosures support strong pricing for newly contracted capacity. We would not apply this
directly to SPCX given duration (and hardware) differences, but it frames near term AI
capacity pricing; (iv) Contracting. SpaceX signed $14.1B of Cloud Services Agreements in
2Q and booked another $6.7B in the opening weeks of 3Q (~$20.8B combined), covering
a six-month period that begins ramping in October. Demand does not appear to be
the constraint; capacity, utilization and contracted $/MW are; (v) Starlink. Street subs
of 14.23MM (+2.23MM net adds vs 1.7MM in 2Q), ARPU of $65.05 (from $66.00) and
Connectivity operating margin of 38.3% (from 38.6%). Cons. has ARPU gliding to $62.48
in 4Q26, so the mix toward Enterprise/Gov't, Starshield and eventually mobile matters for
holding the line. Cons. Connectivity adj. EBITDA is $2.94B (60% margin); (vi) Starship and
Space. Street has 1.9 Starship launches (from 1) and total launches of 40.6 (from 38).
Cadence matters, but the milestones matter more: shorter intervals, repeatable booster
recovery, progress toward ship reuse and commercial payload deployment. That is when
Starship starts to help Starlink economics rather than consume R&D. Cons. Space adj.
EBITDA turns positive at $123MM (from ($205MM)) on revenue of $1.43B (+49% Q/Q);
(vii) Network capacity and D2C. Cons. has 10,643 satellites in orbit (from ~10.2K). We
want V3 deployment commentary and incremental network capacity as subs scale. Direct
to cell matters less for 3Q numbers but is a potential TAM expansion; we are listening for
timing, carrier additions and early commercialization.

Closer Look at AI and Compute
We believe consensus pricing assumptions remain too conservative into 2027. A central concern is that incremental supply coming
online next year will pressure rental rates. We find a sharp near-term pricing reset difficult to reconcile with current contracting, rising
industry rates and continued capacity constraints. Recently announced contracts and broader industry pricing trends give us greater
confidence in SPCX’s ability to sustain premium compute pricing into 2027, supporting highly attractive monetization as the company
brings additional capacity to market.
What consensus already embeds on $/MW. Annualizing cons. 3Q26 AI Solutions & Infrastructure revenue ($5.84B x 4 = ~$23.3B) over
average nameplate of ~1.57GW implies ~$15MM of revenue/MW, versus ~$7MM in 2Q (~$8.8B over ~1.2GW). On total AI revenue the
figures are ~$16MM (from ~$8.5MM). Cons. is therefore already modeling roughly a doubling of realized $/MW Q/Q, to ~40% of the
CoreWeave marker. This is a blunt calculation that uses nameplate rather than energized or utilized MW, so we read it as a directional
bar: a print near cons. on revenue with less capacity energized would mean even higher implied $/MW (supportive), while a print on
capacity but below cons. on revenue would point to pricing, duration or utilization pressure.

Capex per MW versus revenue per MW. Cons. AI capex of $16.9B in 3Q26 and $18.7B in 4Q26 against +342MW and +457MW of
cons. nameplate adds implies ~$49MM and ~$41MM of capex per incremental MW. A sub one year payback on those figures requires

Recent GPU deployment commentary suggests the buildout may be progressing ahead of plan. Musk recently indicated SPCX has
approximately 780k GPUs deployed across Colossus 1 and 2, including H100/H200, GB200 and GB300 systems, with another ~220k
GB300s expected to become operational near term, ~220k in November and potentially another ~220k by late December. Based on
company-disclosed cluster power densities, we estimate this could take IT load from roughly ~1.4GW currently to ~1.8GW near term,
~2.3GW in November and potentially ~2.7GW by late December if the final tranche lands. Including cooling, networking and electrical
losses, this would imply roughly ~3.1GW of facility power at the meter by year-end. We recognize complexity and execution risk remain
(supply chain, power, political backlash), but find progress versus plan encouraging.

Read Through to the Broader Coverage Universe
(i) Data center power and cooling. Cons. has nameplate compute rising from 1.4GW to 2.20GW by 4Q26 and ~5.8GW by YE27, and
total capex stepping from ~$67B in FY26 to ~$172B in FY27. That is a large, visible demand signal for electrical and thermal content.
Most relevant for VRT, ETN and HUBB (power distribution, UPS, switchgear, grid interconnect), JCI, TT and CARR (chillers and liquid
cooling), and PH on thermal management and fluid handling.
(ii) Cons. sits below management on YE27. Cons. at ~5.8GW versus management's closer to 10GW than 5GW framing means upside
risk to cons. capacity if management reiterates; that would flow through to power and cooling order assumptions across the group.
(iii) Pricing as a proxy for customer urgency. If cons. implied $/MW holds or rises, customer paybacks stay short and the incentive to
pull capacity forward persists, which supports order strength and backlog durability across data center exposed names. Normalization
would be a leading indicator for the double order and conversion risk we track.
(iv) Broadening versus concentration. SPCX adds a new, large and fast scaling buyer alongside the hyperscalers; we watch whether
this broadens demand or adds to the concentration debate.
(v) Shared execution constraints. Energization, power availability and equipment lead times that gate SPCX capacity also gate the
industry; management commentary on bottlenecks is a read on lead times and pricing power for our power and cooling names.
(vi) Physical AI and automation. Starship reuse and Starlink manufacturing scale are an indirect read on advanced manufacturing and
automation demand (ROK, EMR, AME).
Mizuho View
AI is the 3Q swing variable. Cons. already embeds a step change, with AI revenue of $6.30B (+146% Q/Q), adj. EBITDA of $3.32B and a ~
$15MM/MW implied yield that roughly doubles from 2Q, against capacity moving from 1.4GW to 1.74GW. With ~$20.8B of contracted
demand signed since 2Q began, we believe the debate is delivery and pricing rather than demand. Relative to the CoreWeave and Nebius
markers of ~$40MM/MW, cons. leaves room for upside if contract pricing and utilization hold, but the capex per MW math says the sub
one year payback claim needs realized pricing well above where cons. sits today, which is plausible based on current negotiated rates.

Price Target Calculation and Key Risks
Investment Thesis
SpaceX is the infrastructure layer of the orbital economy - three structurally distinct businesses sharing a launch platform and
compounding independently. We initiate at Outperform with a $200 price target. Starlink is the cash engine. Connectivity generates $7B
+ in 2026E adjusted EBITDA scaling toward $44B by 2029E at 60%+ margins, a recurring-revenue flywheel that funds everything else
and de-risks the consolidated thesis on its own. Direct-to-cell is the next unlock; with T-Mobile already live, Starlink's TAM expands from
fixed broadband into the 5B+ mobile subscriber market. Space is the optionality beyond 2029. Falcon 9 dominates global launch share,
DoD contracts provide a cost-plus floor, and Starship - if it achieves commercial cadence - collapses $/kg to orbit with the incremental
price being the cost of propellant ($1-2mm). AI is where the asymmetry lives. xAI's Grok and enterprise platform require ~225% revenue
CAGR to hit the management plan - the most aggressive assumption in our SOTP - but orbital compute represents genuine option value
that no terrestrial AI peer can replicate.
Base Case/PT
Connectivity achieves 90% of plan ($43.6B EBITDA) at 15.0x; Space achieves 100% of plan ($3.1B EBITDA) at 51.1x; AI achieves 90%
of plan ($107.7B EBITDA) at 16.8x. Total 2029E enterprise value ~$2,618B; equity value ~$2,618B after modest net debt and IPO
cash. At 13.064B diluted shares, base PT = $200. Meaningful upside from current; contingent on Starlink sub ramp sustaining and xAI
monetization beginning.
Bull Case/PT
Connectivity hits 100% of plan ($48.4B EBITDA) at 18.0x; Space beats plan at 130% ($4.0B EBITDA) at 60.0x on Starship commercial
cadence + DoD acceleration; AI achieves 95% of plan ($113.7B EBITDA) at 20.0x — Terafab orbital compute operational and xAI
enterprise traction confirmed. Total EV ~$3,382B. Re-rating requires AI margin scale-up, Starship on schedule, and hyperscaler capex
holding.
Bear Case/PT
Connectivity at 75% of plan ($36.3B EBITDA) at 14.5x on slower sub ramp; Space at 65% ($2.0B EBITDA) at 50.0x on Starship slippage;
AI at 60% of plan ($71.8B EBITDA) at 13.5x — Terafab delayed, xAI fails to scale near-term. Total EV ~$1,595B. Bear does not require
a disaster — multiple compression alone on AI from 16.8x to 13.5x drives the bulk of downside. Governance structure limits minority
recourse on capital allocation.
Risks
1) Hyperscaler capex retrenchment - deflates AI demand assumptions and compresses xAI multiples across all scenarios. 2) Starship
schedule slippage - each 12-month delay shifts Space EV and pushes Terafab/orbital compute optionality. 3) xAI execution - mgmt plan
requires ~225% CAGR; bear/base gap on AI alone is $35B EBITDA. 4) Governance - Musk 82.4% voting; Class A minority has limited
recourse. 5) Lock-up supply overhang - 180-day tranche releases Dec 2026; Musk's 6.4B shares unlock June 2027.
