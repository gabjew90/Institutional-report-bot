French Assets Could Be Nearing A Trough
France has been one of the factors rattling global financial markets this week and the country faces another
key test as Moody's is due to update its credit rating after European markets close on Friday. A downgrade is
widely expected: Moody’s already has a negative outlook on its Aa3 rating and the fiscal outlook has
deteriorated, and a cut would bring it down into line with Fitch and S&P, which have A+ ratings on France.
Yet this could mark something of a trough in the negative sentiment around France, even if temporarily. The
spread of French sovereign yields over the equivalent German yields has risen to 172 bp, the highest level
ever, and a gain of 33bp on the week, the largest weekly widening ever. The 10y OAT yield sits at 5.04%,
while the CAC 40 is down 2.6% this week, in line with the main European stock indexes but underperforming
the S&P, which is down 1%.
Zain Nizami, global head of equities trading, said market pricing already accounts for much of the Moody’s
decision as well as this week’s political unrest and if the social tensions ease somewhat over the weekend, it
could be enough to help French-exposed assets. This fits with his broader message that there are interesting
opportunities in short-term dislocations in markets, another being the semiconductor/hyperscaler relative-value
(read Zain’s latest note latest note here).
Barring surprises, the next catalyst for French assets would be Nov. 5, when the government is due to auction
long-dated nominal bonds with maturities over 8.5 years.
(Wayne Gouro, UBS Knowledge Network)

US Prepared For Long Stand-Off With Iran, Says US Ambassador; Oil Higher On Supply Fears - The US
is prepared for a prolonged standoff with Iran, meaning military and economic pressure will continue until
Tehran makes significant concessions on its nuclear programme, according to the US Ambassador to the UN
Mike Waltz in an interview with the FT [paywall]. While the Trump administration hopes its bombing campaign
and sanctions will compel Iran to negotiate, Waltz acknowledged a resolution may remain elusive even a year
from now. He stressed that Washington is prepared to maintain its naval blockade and intensify sanctions
indefinitely, adding that the US will not consider its objectives met until Iran’s highly enriched uranium is
eliminated and a meaningful agreement is reached on domestic uranium enrichment. Brent crude gained 0.8%
to $114.02 on Friday , with WTI up 1.1% to $100.60, as intensifying attacks on Saudi Arabia and renewed
Strait of Hormuz disruptions heightened supply risk. Brent is on track for a third consecutive weekly gain, up
nearly 4% this week, and is trading near its highest levels since May. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)

Eurozone PMIs Weaken as France Stumbles, Germany Proves More Resilient – October’s flash PMIs
point to slowing Eurozone momentum heading into year-end, with weakness broadening across services and
manufacturing. Softer employment, new orders and business expectations reinforce the more subdued growth
picture, although easing output-price pressures provide a more encouraging inflation signal. The regional
picture is increasingly divergent. France was the clear weak spot, slipping back into contraction as both
services and manufacturing deteriorated. Firms highlighted uncertainty, weaker investment and softer
spending, reversing September’s improvement and contrasting with the more upbeat business-confidence
data released earlier this week. Germany proved more resilient. While services lost momentum amid budget
pressures and delayed client decisions, manufacturing continued to strengthen, supported by exports and
relatively firm orders. UBS Economist Anna Titareva's take with all the details is available here. (Matthew
Cowley, UBS Knowledge Network)
UK Activity Strengthens Despite Sharp Deterioration in Consumer Confidence -- October’s UK data paint
a diverging picture between business activity and household sentiment. Consumer confidence deteriorated
sharply ahead of the Nov. 5 budget, with households increasingly concerned about inflation, geopolitical
uncertainty and pressure on disposable incomes. Appetite for major purchases weakened particularly
markedly, while rising inflation expectations add to the challenges facing the Bank of England. In contrast,
business activity surprised positively, with both services and manufacturing PMIs gaining momentum at the
start of Q4. Services benefited from firmer new business and improving employment, while manufacturing saw
stronger output and orders. Some of the headline improvement, however, reflected longer delivery times linked
to Middle East supply-chain disruption rather than stronger underlying demand. (Aditi Sajampati, UBS
Knowledge Network)
Japan Core Inflation Tops 2%, Backing Further Rate Hikes – Japan’s September core CPI—excluding
fresh food but including fuel costs—rose 2.1% y/y, matching the median forecast and accelerating from
August’s 1.7%. It exceeded the BoJ’s 2% target for the first time this year. While headline inflation climbed to
2.1%, core-core CPI, excluding fresh food and energy, accelerated to 2.4%. Higher fuel costs, elevated import
prices associated with the weak yen and price pressures from robust AI demand blunted the effect of
government fuel subsidies. The figures strengthen the case for further tightening, although the BoJ is widely
expected to stay on hold next week after hiking to 1.25% last month. The central bank could, however, revise
its inflation forecast higher and signal readiness for further hikes, Reuters reported. The yen attracted buying
following the release. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Aussie Flash PMI Signals Output Contraction As Price Pressures Intensify – Australia’s October S&P
Global flash composite PMI fell to 47.7 from September’s 51.3, signalling the first output contraction in five
months. The services activity index dropped to 47.8 from 51.9, while manufacturing PMI edged up to 49.7 from
49.6. Firms linked weaker demand to higher prices, borrowing costs and subdued confidence. Energy,
shipping, commodity and wage costs pushed up input inflation, while output price inflation accelerated for a
second consecutive month. Employment increased despite falling activity, driven by services. Persistent price
pressures could further threaten near-term demand. (Aditi Samajpati, Macro Sales)
India PMI Accelerates As exports Surge, Inflation Cools - India’s flash composite PMI rose to 56.7 in
October, up from 55.9 in September, signalling a faster pace of private-sector expansion and the strongest
overall output growth since June. Activity remained firmly in expansion territory above the 50 threshold,
supported by stronger performances across both manufacturing and services. The Services PMI Business
Activity Index climbed to 56.2 from 55.2, while the Manufacturing PMI Output Index edged up to 57.6 from
57.5, both indicating continued expansion. By contrast, the headline Manufacturing PMI eased slightly to 54.9
from 55.1, though it remained comfortably in growth territory. The standout feature of the report was external
demand. Total new orders accelerated, helped by the fastest increase in export business in six months, with
firms citing demand from the US, UK, Middle East and Bangladesh. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)

Hermes Q3 Miss Lowered Expectations; No Longer Immune To China Weakness - UBS Research:
Hermès reported a Q3 miss despite lowered expectations, pointing to rising cyclicality according to Luxury
analyst Zuzanna Pusz. Organic sales growth of 4% was below UBS and the consensus estimates of 5%. Most
divisions disappointed, partly offset by Leather Goods accelerating to 11% and a better Beauty performance.
More importantly, Q3 has shown that Hermès is no longer immune to the weakness in China, with APAC
ex-Japan slowing from slight growth year-to-date to flat, while the Americas also decelerated by 5 percentage
points to 9%, its first single-digit growth since 2018 ex-Covid. While Hermes undoubtedly remains a relative
outperformer, the broadening signs of brand cyclicality sit uncomfortably with demanding consensus
expectations, including 7% constant FX sales growth in the 2027 expectation. Following Zuzanna's recent
estimate cuts, she make only minor changes, as better FX offsets a further reduction in organic growth
assumptions. With the long-held resilience thesis increasingly being tested and little room for disappointment
embedded in expectations, Zuzanna remains cautious and reiterates her 'Sell' recommendation and lowers
her price target from EUR1168 to EUR1150. (Nana Antiedu, Macro Sales)
AI Debt Financing Monitor: Funding Costs Rise, Fault Lines Emerge – Credit Strategy: The AI debt
financing boom remains intact, but higher funding costs are increasingly testing the weakest borrowers and
structures. Strategist Sachin Ganesh thinks this represents a rotation across debt markets rather than a broad
retrenchment from AI financing, with secured, project-level and structured credit continuing to provide
financing where unsecured debt has become more expensive. He expects 1) Steeper 10s30s IG tech curves
as hyperscaler capex beats and the need for further debt financing re-tests the depth of investor demand
post-earnings; 2) Credit spread underperformance vs. equities likely continues; and 3) Pressure on unsecured,
earlier-stage datacenter projects, acknowledging the potential for near-term relief from earnings that ease AI
demand fears, or proof that issuers can access alternative forms of financing at a more attractive cost. (Aditi
Samajpati, Macro Sales)
Yen Gains Favour As US Dollar, Euro Risks Build – Expectations for further yen appreciation are building,
not necessarily because Japan’s underlying fundamentals have improved, but because political and economic
risks in both the US and Europe are making the Japanese currency increasingly attractive on a relative basis.
In the US, either outcome of the November midterm elections could weigh on the dollar: a Democratic victory
may raise concerns over tighter regulation and taxation of AI-related industries, potentially pressuring equities,
while a strong Republican result could revive fiscal concerns following proposals for large-scale payments to
households. Historically, the yen has tended to strengthen after US midterms as pre-election dollar positions
are unwound. Meanwhile, signs that even stronger-than-expected US inflation can no longer sustain dollar
gains have reinforced expectations that softer investment or consumption data could push USDJPY lower. The
yen is also gaining relative appeal against the euro amid political instability, fiscal concerns and unrest in
France, with broader uncertainty across Europe encouraging some investors to shift capital toward Japan.
Once US and European political events pass, however, attention is likely to return to Japan’s fiscal outlook,
BoJ policy and official willingness to resist renewed yen depreciation. See more here. (Sara Onozato,
Execution Sales)

Energy Correlation And Italian Treasury Tap Drive Inflation Flows - Strong energy correlation continued to
drive the EUR front end, with Thursday’s move higher accompanied by a duration rally that pressured the back
end of the curve. EUR 5y5y declined around 2bps, with the move described as duration-led. Flow activity was
dominated by country-risk selling in BTPei, while inflation was less of a driver. BTPei widened by roughly
1.5bps, with selling flows observed and limited buying interest in linkers, particularly in BTPei-37. The Italian
Treasury announced taps of the 2032 and 2035 linkers for next week. The choice was notable because both
bonds are off-the-run issues, with the 2035 linker first issued in 2004, last tapped in 2012, and carrying an
index ratio close to 1.60, while the 2032 linker was first issued in 2015 with an index ratio of 1.33. Market
participants were surprised by the selection, as on-the-run bonds are typically tapped. Previous asset-swap
activity in the 2035 linker over the late summer period may indicate demand for the issue and may explain the
decision to include the bond in the upcoming operation. (Joe Troszok, Rates Trading)
