FX Daily: Defensive rotation in play
It looks like markets are going through a subtle regime shift. Higher
energy prices no longer mean higher rates forever; instead, the long end
is starting to express a little more fear about growth. The upcoming US
midterms may also be playing a role. It looks like there is some defensive
rotation underway into the Swiss franc, the yen and sterling
As the US midterms approach, investors appear to be rotating into defensive currencies
USD: Midterms could be starting to play a role
Risk assets are on the back foot this European morning as energy prices stay firm and tech
stocks remain fragile. On the former, a hefty risk premium remains in oil prices as attacks
against Saudi Arabia continue. On the latter, Q3 results from Meta and Tesla and what they
mean for capex plans are still being digested, but there clearly remains a fine line to walk. The
suspicion now is that global equity investors are starting to scale back their exposure to the
tech sector and looking for more defensive plays in sectors like consumer staples, healthcare
and utilities. This will favour more geographic allocation to European equities, where tech
weights in benchmark indices are lower. The upcoming US midterms in early November may
also play a role here, where a Democrat sweep of Congress could slow/reverse deregulation.
At the same time, we are seeing a subtle change in the rate markets. During the late summer
and especially in September, higher energy prices were associated with 'higher rates forever'.

EUR: Hard to be constructive
EUR/USD is trading quite well despite higher energy prices and an unresolved French budget
crisis. One can only conclude that the biggest market driver at the moment is the heavy and
concentrated positioning in US tech and the dollar, which is being gently pared back. Expect FX
traders to keep a very close watch on the short end of the US curve now. Expectations for
further Fed tightening have stayed rock-solid at around +75bp over recent weeks. We think
that could remain the case for the coming months and that the dollar could stay strong. But
any tech wobble that prompts a dovish re-pricing at the short-end could lead to a broader
dollar correction.
There is little on the eurozone calendar today apart from the last of the ECB speakers before
the blackout period commences later today. See here for a preview of next week's ECB
meeting. 
We do not think there is a strong case for EUR/USD to trade a lot higher. But if US tech does
suffer now, a break of 1.1270/75 could open up 1.1340/50. However, we would not chase the
rally. 1.13 remains our one-month forecast as outlined in this month's FX Talking. 
Chris Turner 
GBP: Back in fashion
It seems like sterling is riding on the coat-tails of PM Andy Burnham's popularity. In reality, it
looks like there is both independent sterling strength and independent euro weakness at play
in EUR/GBP. Earlier this summer, we had felt that sterling gains would not last. But now greater
market confidence in a Bank of England tightening cycle – we look for 25bp hikes in February
and November – makes sterling a high-carry European currency.

TRY: Easing resumes as carry trade holds
We expect the Central Bank of Turkey to resume its easing cycle for the first time since the US-
Iran conflict began today, cutting rates by 100bp to 36.00%. September’s inflation data
provided a positive signal, while developments in investment funds point to downside risks for
the economy in the final quarter. The authorities’ swift response appears to have contained
the fund shock, with no signs of systemic risk or a sharp rise in dollarisation, while foreign
inflows have resumed.
However, it may further weaken domestic demand through tighter liquidity, wealth losses and
lower confidence. Repayments to smaller investors could cushion consumption, but
counterparty exposures remain the main spillover risk. Inflation sentiment has improved after
benign September data and expectations of weaker demand. We expect 100bp cuts in October
and December, taking the policy rate to 35% by year-end, versus our 29.0% inflation forecast
for year-end.
Although crowded long TRY positions offer less and less carry, they remain popular in
emerging markets, and we expect this demand to persist in the coming months despite
renewed rate cuts. The recent weekly adjustment in USD/TRY does not alter the overall FX
setup, and the carry trade continues. We expect a level of 51.50 USD/TRY by the end of the
year.
Frantisek Taborsky
