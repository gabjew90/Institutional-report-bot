META 3Q26 Results
Equity is good news for credit, but
funding overhang persists
Equity is welcomed by credit, but comments in the 14C filing
suggest massive spending ambitions. A bond deal still
appears likely in the near-term and the size is unclear.
The equity announcement has to be read positively, at least in isolation, but our enthusiasm
around it is tempered somewhat by commentary in the 14C filing that appears to suggest
massive spending ambitions. Our view is that META will likely rely on both the credit and equity
markets from here, but the absolute level of debt issuance could also be rising. We continue to
expect a bond deal in the near term, which is going to weigh on spreads for the time being. Our
base case remains that META will issue the “standard” $25bn hyperscaler deal size, but see
some risk that the company could go bigger following the equity news, discussed below.

Credit Highlights and Relative Value
• Core business remains strong.Revenue was slightly ahead of consensus ($64.6bn vs.
$63.3bn) and the top of the $61-64bn guidance range, as advertising revenue grew 26% y/y, to
$63.1bn. Operating income missed, but the gap was largely due to a $9.9bn charge tied to the
August 2026 multi state Attorneys General agreement (see State AG Settlement Reached).
 Advertising metrics remained strong, with ad impressions up 10% y/y and average price per
ad up 14% worldwide, while daily active people rose to a record 3.64bn. The company
expects 4Q26 revenue of $73-76bn.

would be the largest quarter of capex to date, consistent with management's messaging that
they will continue 'investing aggressively' to support the core business as well as their
emerging AI initiatives into next year, though they stopped short of any numeric guidance for

• New Class C shares announced. Meta announced plans to create a new class of non-voting
Class C common stock and distribute a 1:1 stock dividend to all existing shareholders,
effectively functioning as a 2-for-1 stock split. Management described the new class as a
funding tool for capital raises, employee compensation, acquisitions, and other GCP given
the significant investment needed to compete and build infrastructure during the AI
transformation. The structure also explicitly keeps the founder-led model and Zuckerberg's
voting control in place (60.6% as of October). Management said it has no specific financing
plans for now and described the move as setting up the capital structure ahead of time to
access a wider range of funding sources, but given that Class C is expected to begin trading in
late January, equity funding is unlikely before then. 
• 14C filing emphasizes scale of the build-out.  The Schedule 14C filing makes clear that the
new share class responds to large and near-term funding needs. Management's long-term
forecasts, presented to the board in May, showed Meta would need to raise "hundreds of
billions of dollars" over several years, starting as early as fall 2026. The filing pointed to
competition for capital, citing recent and expected raises at industry peers, and suggested
there is value in raising capital sooner, before the market becomes saturated. Notably for
credit, the committee concluded that Meta would not be able to issue level of financing it
needs in the debt market alone without pressuring its credit ratings. While our takeaway from
the filing is that capex will rise significantly and that funding needs are greater than expected,
the filing is not conclusive with respect to the actual amount of funding, the timeline of
raises, or the mix of equity versus debt.
• Muse off to a strong start, but compute-heavy. Meta called Muse one of its most successful
launches ever. More than 10mn people in the US and Canada set up a Muse personal agent
within about six weeks, and the app has ranked #1 in the App Store almost every day since.
About 25% of users engage with Muse on any given day with shopping the top use case, used
by more than 25% of weekly actives, with an average purchase size above $200. Volumes in
the first week were high enough that Meta had to work with Stripe to raise credit limits.
Compute is the main constraint. Muse is expensive to run, so Meta has held back a global
launch. Management expects total serving costs to rise, but is targeting a more than 10x
improvement in cost efficiency over the next year and is building capacity to serve over 1bn
users in the next few years. Because training is a large fixed cost, Meta also wants to sell high-
margin inference beyond the consumer app through a new enterprise platform. The near-
term enterprise offering is the model API, Muse Code and business agents, each described as
growing quickly, though management gave no usage or revenue figures for them.
Relative Value: Prefer DCs, but holding pattern for now 
We are reiterating our Market Weight rating on META and Overweight ratings on RPLDCI/TXEDCI.
While the equity news is positive for Meta, we are cognizant that a bond deal could be coming
near term and struggle to see META outperforming until there is clarity around the debt funding
agenda. 
 Capex is accelerating in 4Q, and if we simply assume that Meta plans to keep cash at $80bn or
above (it had $81bn of cash when it issued in the spring vs. $82bn now), it needs to come before
year-end. Of course META could hold off and use existing liquidity if it wanted, given
its $40-50bn minimum cash needs historically, but "waiting" is not the message we took from
the 14C filing.

Could it go big? We see some potential for Meta to attempt a larger deal than the “standard”
$25bn driveby. In our view, a larger deal could be absorbed by the market now that the equity
signal is out there. A massive deal (several investors have asked about a $50bn-plus trade)
would reprice the market temporarily, and we think it would need to be accompanied by some
level of messaging that the company is done for a while – although the likely concession
included in a deal that large could provide an entry point. To be clear, our base case remains a
$25bn deal with some possible EUR issuance, but a larger deal is no longer out of the question. 
Maintain preference for the “pastries.” We reiterate our Overweight ratings on Beignet/
RPLDCI and Sopaipilla/TXEDCI. Our view remains that these offer attractive discount over the
Meta curve despite being an extension of Meta credit. We see value in the residual-value
mechanism in the current rate market, providing downside support in a bearish AI scenario, and
we consider the lease terms and covenant package to be among the strongest in the data center
market (see latest DC bond comp sheet). That being said, given that DC bonds trade as a
function of their tenant spread (Figure 3), we expect risk addition in RPLDCI/TXEDCI to be
limited in the very near term, until there is clarity on META's issuance plans.
