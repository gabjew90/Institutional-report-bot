Company Update
 
Headwinds priced in while execution is 
not
We are dip buyers on yesterday’s pullback. The stock reacted negatively to a softer 
2027 outlook which is understandable. However, GM has repeatedly shown it can 
absorb a variety of headwinds and deliver upside to expectations. Moreover, the 
new $20bn buyback authorization represents ~30% of market cap and will further 
shrink the share count and support EPS growth (even in a flattish EBIT 
environment). We reiterate our Buy rating and trim our price target to $98 (down 
from $101).
Strong 3Q26 results
GM reported a third sequential quarter of strong results with EBIT higher than our/
consensus expectations. Total EBIT came in at $4.11bn vs. our $4.06bn and 
consensus at $4.02bn. The beat was driven by GMNA and lower-than-anticipated 
eliminations, partly offset by a GMI shortfall and softer GM Financial. By segment, 
GMNA EBIT of $3.59bn was above our $3.51bn, benefiting from $600m in cost 
improvement and $400m in volume contributions while mix and other also 
contributed YoY, though slightly offset by price (-$200m). Meanwhile, GMI EBIT of 
$28m fell short of our $102m. China performed well in the quarter, marking its 
eighth consecutive quarter of profitability. Adjusted FCF was also particularly 
strong in the quarter at $5.88bn, reflecting significant contributions from working 
capital, profit flow-through and the timing of capex and tariff payments. See Figure 
3 for detailed comparisons of earnings.
2026 guide raise possible if not for commodities
GM narrowed its 2026 EBIT guidance while raising the FCF outlook. EBIT-adjusted 
guidance is now $14.5-15.5bn, vs. $14.0-16.0bn previously, leaving the midpoint 
unchanged at $15.0bn but trimming the high end. We note that investor 
expectations for the year were biased towards the upper end of the range coming 
into the quarter. The $500m narrowing at both the high and low ends is driven by 
better-than-anticipated volumes, offset by higher-than-expected costs ($400m), 
most of which will flow through in 4Q. Consequently, the new guide indicates a 
materially steeper step-down in 4Q than previously anticipated. Adjusted EPS 
guidance is now $12.75-$13.75, vs. $12.00-$14.00, lifting the midpoint by $0.25 to 
$13.25. Adjusted automotive FCF guidance rises to $11.5-12.5bn from $9.5-
11.5bn, a $1.5bn increase at the midpoint to $12.0bn.

GMI ex. China performance lower due to wholesales in Middle East
As we look to 4Q, GM’s ICE volumes are anticipated to be lower than usual due to 
the launch of the new full-size trucks and associated production ramps, resulting in 
a ~35k-unit YoY headwind in full-size pickup trucks in 4Q. From a cost perspective, 
the company anticipates most of the year’s $400m in higher commodity and 
logistics costs to land in 4Q. Of the $400m, less than half is logistics, with the rest 
attributable to commodities. Emissions credit is expected to be a slight QoQ 
headwind as the company wraps up most tailwinds in 3Q, and onshoring/software 
investments are expected to step up into the last quarter. All in, we model $2.7bn 
in 4Q EBIT, leading to $15bn for the full year.

2027 growth now hinges on additional cost reduction
GM had previously signaled EBIT growth in 2027 but that outlook now looks less 
certain after the company narrowed its 2026 guide, which trimmed the high end of 
the range, and flagged higher commodity costs, mostly steel. GM buys about one-
third of its steel at spot, one-third on one-year contracts and one-third on multi-year 
contracts. On top of the $500m-1bn of commodity/logistics/DRAM headwinds it 
had previously built into 2027, GM now expects an additional $1.0-1.5bn, bringing 
the total to about $1.5-2.5bn. 1Q should be the weakest quarter: the changeover to 
the next-generation full-size pickups brings plant downtime and slower line rates 
during the ramp, on top of a full-year volume outlook GM describes as flat. To offset 
the cost pressure, management plans to pursue structural cost-reduction 
programs, a lever it has used before. The main tailwind is stronger price and mix 
from the new truck program, though management has cautioned against assuming 
a 2019-style step-up given already elevated truck transaction prices. Overall, we

FCF inflects higher in 2027; $20bn buyback authorized
GM raised its 2026 adjusted automotive FCF guidance to $11.5-12.5bn from $9.5-
11.5bn, after a 3Q that generated $5.9bn of FCF, a four-year high helped partly by 
the timing of tariff payments. We expect FCF to rise again in 2027 as cash payments 
for the EV restructuring roll off. Meanwhile, GM has completed its $6bn buyback 
announced in January and authorized a new $20bn program, signaling confidence 
in its FCF outlook and its view that the stock is undervalued. The company bought 
back $2.5bn of stock in 3Q, retiring 29m shares, plus another $1bn in October. 
Shares outstanding fell to 849m at end-3Q from 904m at end-2025. We expect 
repurchases under the new authorization to continue at a pace similar to the past 
few years, which should keep reducing the share count and support EPS growth 
even if EBIT growth slows in 2027.
Reiterate Buy and trim price target to $98
The road to 2027 is bumpier than it looked a quarter ago. Commodity costs are now 
an additional $1.0-1.5bn headwind on top of what GM had already planned for, the 
truck changeover will weigh on 1Q with downtime and slower line rates, volumes 
are expected to be flat, and management has warned against expecting a 2019-
style price/mix lift from the new pickups. But if GM has demonstrated anything over 
the past several years, it is execution. It has absorbed tariffs, unwound a costly EV 
strategy, and navigated commodity/component price spikes, yet it has beat and 
raised quarter after quarter while expanding North American margins. We expect 
the same playbook in 2027: structural cost actions, disciplined pricing on the new 
trucks, and a $20bn buyback that continues to support EPS. With the shares pulling 
back yesterday, we would buy into the weakness. Reiterate Buy, and trim price 
target to $98 (down from $101).

Macroeconomic fluctuations often account for most of the risks associated with exposures to instruments that promise to pay 
fixed or variable interest rates. For an investor who is long fixed-rate instruments (thus receiving these cash flows), increases 
in interest rates naturally lift the discount factors applied to the expected cash flows and thus cause a loss. The longer the 
maturity of a certain cash flow and the higher the move in the discount factor, the higher will be the loss. Upside surprises in 
inflation, fiscal funding needs, and FX depreciation rates are among the most common adverse macroeconomic shocks to 
receivers. But counterparty exposure, issuer creditworthiness, client segmentation, regulation (including changes in assets 
holding limits for different types of investors), changes in tax policies, currency convertibility (which may constrain currency 
conversion, repatriation of profits and/or liquidation of positions), and settlement issues related to local clearing houses are 
also important risk factors. The sensitivity of fixed-income instruments to macroeconomic shocks may be mitigated by 
indexing the contracted cash flows to inflation, to FX depreciation, or to specified interest rates - these are common in 
emerging markets.  The index fixings may - by construction - lag or mis-measure the actual move in the underlying variables 
they are intended to track. The choice of the proper fixing (or metric) is particularly important in swaps markets, where floating 
coupon rates (i.e., coupons indexed to a typically short-dated interest rate reference index) are exchanged for fixed coupons. 
Funding in a currency that differs from the currency in which coupons are denominated carries FX risk. Options on swaps 
(swaptions) the risks typical to options in addition to the risks related to rates movements.
