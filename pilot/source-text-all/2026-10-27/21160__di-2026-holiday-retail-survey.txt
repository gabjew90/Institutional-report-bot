2026 Deloitte Holiday Retail Survey
Executive 
summary
Source: 2026 Deloitte Holiday Retail Survey.
This holiday season, which marks our 41st year of 
covering holiday trends, shoppers seem determined to 
keep spirits bright, even while watching every dollar. 
Between travel and outings, festive apparel, and spiffing 
up seasonal décor, the holiday tab can add up fast—all 
before a single gift gets wrapped.
Average household spend is expected to increase to 
$1,654, up 4% YoY, as 79% of respondents believe higher 
prices are in their future. As such, the value hunt is very 
much on. Nearly 4 in 10 shoppers qualify as holiday 
value seekers who shop promotions, redeem rewards, 
and look to private label and other value alternatives to 
make gift purchases feel worth the price. Yet these 
shoppers expect to spend 12% more than other 
shoppers. 
There’s plenty of room for delight, too. Half of the 
surveyed shoppers plan to gift an experience, up 11 
percentage points since 2024, and most want to pair it 
with a physical gift. Self-gifting and luxury considerations 
are also rising, especially among younger generations. 
These younger shoppers are particularly important this 
season as Gen Z and millennials are expected to make 
up 50% of holiday spending for the first time in our 
survey. 
Consumers are also getting an earlier start, with 
22% saying they’ll shop sooner than last year. June 
promotions have already captured some holiday 
purchases for 1 in 4 shoppers, and October sales 
are gaining momentum (up 18 pp since 2023). Still, 
Thanksgiving week remains the main event. 
The holiday shopping journey favors online-only 
retailers and mass merchants, which are expected 
to capture most of shoppers’ planned spend, while 
online spending is expected to make up 55% of 
retail budgets. Generative AI is a holiday-shopper 
helper, with 39% of respondents noting it’s their 
primary tool for at least one holiday shopping task, 
but only 16% say they would permit it to complete 
a purchase. 
For retailers, an opportunity lies in meeting 
shoppers’ determination to celebrate with clear 
value, timely promotions, and easier gift discovery, 
while tapping into younger shoppers’ unique 
shopping 
preferences. 
Pairing 
products 
with 
experiences and using gen AI to build confidence 
rather than friction can help keep the season festive 
and financially manageable.

2026 Deloitte Holiday Retail Survey
Cheat sheet
Source: 2026 Deloitte Holiday Retail Survey.
Spending trends
Average holiday spend is expected to increase to 
$1,654 this season, up 4% YoY as most respondents 
believe holiday prices will be higher YoY.
The expected gains are largely from Gen Z and 
millennials who now represent 50% of the holiday 
spend.
Spending on retail goods is expected to be up 10% 
YoY ($988), while that on holiday activities is down 4% 
YoY ($666).  
Economic sentiment holds steady among 
respondents, with 45% expecting the economy to stay 
the same or improve.
Households earning more than $200K plan to spend 
$3,607 on average, more than 5x the $693 expected by 
those earning under $50K.
38% of surveyed shoppers qualify as “holiday value 
seekers,” adopting five or more money-saving 
behaviors, yet are expected to spend 12% more than 
others.
Shopping journey
22% plan to start holiday shopping earlier than last 
year, and 28% of the holiday budget is expected to be 
spent by the end of October, the highest share in six years.
1 in 4 respondents already purchased holiday items 
during June promotional events, and 4 in 10 plan to shop 
October sales events.
Shopping activity peaks in late November (77% of 
shoppers active) and stays elevated into early December 
(78%).
54% of shoppers plan to spend most of their holiday 
budget at e-commerce retailers or mass merchants, 
making these retailers the main destination for holiday 
spending.
55% of retail holiday budgets are planned for online, 
versus 45% in-store.
Consumer preferences.
39% say gen AI is their primary tool for at least one 
holiday shopping task, but only 16% would trust it 
to complete a purchase with their permission.
50% of shoppers plan to gift an experience this 
holiday season, up from 47% in 2025 and 39% in 2024.
49% of respondents consider luxury goods a 
worthy investment, up from 39% in 2025, with 
Gen Z’s luxury consideration rising to 72% (from 57% 
in 2025).
33% of shoppers plan to self-gift, led by Gen Z (49%) 
and millennials (42%).
Younger generations have unique preferences that 
may present opportunities for retailers, such as 
wish lists (79% Gen Z versus 69% baby boomers), faster 
delivery, and personalized gifts.
