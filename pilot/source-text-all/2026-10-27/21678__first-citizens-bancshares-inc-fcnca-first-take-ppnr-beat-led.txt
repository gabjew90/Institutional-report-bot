First Citizens Bancshares Inc. (FCNCA): First Take: PPNR beat led by 
top-line strength; guidance points to 4Q upside 
23 October 2026 | 8:25AM EDT
Bottom line on the fundamentals / stock: FCNCA reported solid 3Q26 results as 
PPNR came in ahead of expectations on top-line strength (NII and fees) as a larger 
balance sheet (loan and deposit growth) more than offset a slightly lower margin. 
Credit was strong as NCOs came in below expectations and helping drive a reserve 
release. Balance sheet trends were aided by the closing of the BMO branch 
acquisition while Fund banking was the main driver of the loan upside.  
In terms of its 4Q26 guidance, we believe it points to 3-4% PPNR upside largely on 
higher NII which is driven by a larger balance sheet.  We think investors will be 
focused on the path of NII from here given asset sensitivity and the FDIC note 
repayment.  Speaking of the note – FCNCA laid out a plan to have it repaid by YE 
2027 — largely driven by deposit growth and additional funding capacity. So, overall, 
this was a solid quarter and guide and we think given that positioning was mixed 
(based on conversations, although improving) we think it’s good enough to move 
shares up today.   
Four key questions for the call: 1) What are your updated expectations for the 
trajectory of net interest income and the net interest margin NIM in 4Q and during 
2027 given the move in rates? 2) How are you thinking about the pacing and mix of 
deposit growth through YE and into 2027, particularly as you look to use deposits to 
pay down the purchase money note?, 3) With 4Q expenses expected to be relatively 
flat YoY in 4Q, how are you thinking about the pacing of cost growth into 2027 and 
how much can you ‘bend the curve?’, 4) With the updated buyback authorization of 
$1.2bn, how are you thinking about the cadence of repurchases in 4Q and into 2027?  
How are you toggling that with your desire to do M&A?  
2026 Outlook: FCNCA provided updated 4Q year guidance which at the mid-point 
implies ~3-4% core PPNR upside in 4Q26 vs. the street, driven by higher NII (+1.7% 
at the midpoint vs. the street; contemplates 0-1 rate hike in 4Q) and fees (+2.0% vs. 
street at midpoint) partially offset by modestly higher expenses (+0.6% vs. street at 
midpoint). For loans, FCNCA sees ~1.5% EOP loan growth and deposit growth of 
2.8% in 4Q at the mid-point pointing to EOP balances ~3.1%/3.6% above the street 
respectively. Lastly, NCOs are expected to be 30-35bps in 4Q (vs. street at 35bps). 
Additionally, annualizing 4Q26’s implied NII points to NII of $6.883bn which is 
~60bps below the street and with an improved rate backdrop it seems like FCNCA 
will likely exceed these expectations. In terms of costs, annualizing 4Q’s costs points 
to $5.435bn which is ~2% below the street so if FCNCA can provide a solid cost 
message, cons estimates should be in a decent place.

repurchased $600mn shares in the quarter which was modestly above the street 
(expecting $578mn) and authorized an additional $1.2bn by YE 27.

price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).

other designated committee in each respective region and do not represent a change in the analysts’ investment rating for such stocks.
