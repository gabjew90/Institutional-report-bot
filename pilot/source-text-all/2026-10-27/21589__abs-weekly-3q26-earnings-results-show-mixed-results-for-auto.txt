Learnings from earnings 
Ally Bank (ALLY): Management saw resilient consumer credit performance in 3Q26 and 
continues to expect a 2026 net charge-off rate of 1.8%-2.0%. American Express 
(AXP): Management said they see no signs of meaningful credit deterioration in the 
company’s card portfolio. They continue to expect revenue growth of 10% in 2026 
compared to the original forecast of 9-10%. Bread Financial (BFH): Management 
revised their 2026 outlook. They now expect average credit card and other loan growth 
of approximately 4% and net loss rate to be below 7.0%. Capital One (COF): 
Management indicated that credit remains strong, with delinquencies tracking normal 
seasonality and charge-offs continuing to improve. The card segment saw 30+ DQ and 
the NCL rates declined YoY and QoQ while the auto segment saw mixed trends. GM 
Financial (GM): Management described consumer credit performance as resilient and 
expects retail net charge-offs in the mid-1% range. Huntington Bancshares (HBAN): 
Management noted credit performance was “top-tier” in 3Q26 and expects net charge-
offs to be in the lower half of their 25bp to 35bp range. Sallie Mae (SLM): 
Management expects Private Education Loan originations to grow 12%–14% YoY. They 
raised their net charge-off guidance to $400–$420mn due to third-party debt resolution 
practices. The company also does not expect to execute its final 2026 loan sale, 
previously scheduled for October. Synchrony Financial (SYF): Management’s 2026 
outlook includes ending loan receivables growth in the mid-single digit and net charge-
off (NCO) rate of <5.50% with delinquency and losses following normal seasonality.

 Market View 
Volume slowed during ABS Miami 
This week, only one deal priced, a $0.5bn data center transaction. The new deal pipeline 
is expected to pick up with only 8 deals in the pipeline. 
So far this year, 465 deals have been priced for an aggregate principal balance of 
$328.3bn, up 5.8% versus last year’s comparable period. The Auto ABS sector (retail & 
commercial) continues to dominate new issue volume (51.1% of total volume) while the 
broadly inclusive Other ABS sector (all sectors except auto, card, student loan, and 
equipment) has seen the highest growth (up 24.2% YoY, or $22.3bn). Within the Other 
ABS sector, personal loan ABS (up $4.4bn), franchise / whole business ABS (up $3.7bn), 
fiber ABS (up $3.6bn), aircraft ABS (up $3.4bn) and data center ABS (up $2.6bn) sectors 
have seen the greatest growth in dollars. 
For 2026, we are forecasting new issue volume of $407.0bn. Although fiber ABS 
issuance has come in higher than expected ($11.7bn actual versus $10.0bn forecasted), 
data center ABS issuance is tracking below expectations ($13.9bn actual versus $20.0bn 
forecasted). The offsetting trends leave our overall digital infrastructure ABS forecast 
unchanged at $40bn, although risks are to the downside. If realized, our current new 
issue volume forecast would be a new record and would mark the fourth consecutive 
year volume has increased year-over-year.

Spreads unchanged to wider 
ABS spreads were unchanged or wider by up to 10bp week-over-week. Spreads widened 
10bp for rental car ABS and 5bp for floorplan, aircraft, container, railcar, and 
franchise/whole business ABS. Spreads widened 3bp for retail auto lease, corporate auto 
fleet, and timeshare ABS. See Exhibit 9.  
We believe spreads continue to be impacted by several factors including broader market 
volatility, continue concerns regarding inflation, and continued uncertainty regarding Fed 
policy and upcoming elections. Further, spreads for digital infrastructure ABS continue to 
be impacted by AI companies increasing leverage to fund capital expenditures (Capex), 
including substantial commitments for data center, and reduced free cash flows. 
As we noted last week, the spread widening seen over the past several weeks has left all 
sectors trading above their respective 24-month minimums and closer to their 
respective 24-month average. The number of sectors trading 20bp or more above their 
respective 24-month minimums has increased to 15 from last week’s 13. At the low end, 
retail auto lease ABS is trading just 11bp above its 24-month minimum, while data 
center ABS sits at the high end at 55bp above its 24-month minimum. 
We continue to believe spreads will be rangebound (+/- 5bp WoW) but still feel the risk is 
to the downside (wider spreads). This likely means most sectors will continue to trade 
closer to their respective 24-average than 24-month minimum. We still selectively prefer 
the higher yielding sectors (solar, data center, aircraft, fiber, railcar, and franchise/whole 
business ABS sectors). 
The ICE BofA 1+ Year AAA-A Fixed Rate ABS Index (RAB0) widened 3bp on the week, 
while the 1-5 Year AAA-A US Corporate Index (CV10) widened 5bp, resulting in a 2bp 
tightening in the basis between the two indices. The basis now stands at 35bp, below its 
24-month average of 38bp. We still prefer ABS over HG corporates (Exhibit 3). 
The current basis between AA-BBB rated ABS and AAA rated ABS (101bp) remains above 
the 24-month minimum (93bp) but below its 24-month average (115bp) and 24-month 
maximum (133bp) (Exhibit 4). As we noted over the past several weeks, AA-BBB rated 
ABS continues to offer incremental spread relative to AAA-rated ABS. The anticipated 
widening in the basis leaves us preferring AA-BBB rated ABS over AAA rated ABS.

Sponsors updates 
Navient to implement a new corporate structure 
Navient Corporation (Navient) intends to establish a new holding company structure. 
Under the new structure, the new parent company, named Navient Holdings, Inc. will 
become the new holding company of Navient LLC and E Parent, Inc. (Earnest). The new 
corporate structure is expected to be in effect during the fourth quarter of 2026. Upon 
completion of the reorganization, Navient LLC will assume the obligations of the listed 
notes currently held at Navient Corporation and continue to be a reporting company. 
In the related press release, management states the reorganization creates “a clearer 
and more efficient organizational framework that [management] believe[s] will support 
disciplined execution, improve strategic flexibility, and enhance long-term shareholder 
value.” This includes facilitating separation of businesses if / when appropriate. 
Under the new corporate structure Navient LLC will hold all legacy loans (FFELP and 
Legacy Private) and all Earnest loans originated through December 31, 2026. Navient 
LLC will focus on maximizing cash flow generation from Navient’s portfolio of non-
strategic legacy assets that it no longer originates. The portfolio includes $26.6bn of 
FFELP loans (as of June 30, 2026), $5.4bn of legacy private loans (as of June 30, 2026) 
and an estimated $10.3bn of Earnest loans (as of December 31,2026.  
Earnest will originate and service new loans. For 2026, management is forecasting 
origination volume of $3.8bn. They expect to add additional products, overtime, that 
have marketing and cost of acquisition synergies. 
Navient outsourced most of its loan servicing to MOHELA in 2024. 
The company classified $1.4 billion of Legacy loans to Held for Sale in Q3 2026.
