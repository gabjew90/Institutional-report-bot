Initial Jobless Claims Slip Below 200K 
Headlines 
 
Initial jobless claims fell slightly in the week ending October 17. 
 
 
The four-week moving average of initial claims held below 200,000 for the 
third consecutive week. 
 
 
Continuing claims rose by 25,000 to 1.728 million in the week ending 
October 10 but continue to decline from a year ago.  
 
 
The insured unemployment rate was unchanged at 1.1% in the week 
ending October 10 for the seventh straight week. 
 
 
Initial claims were little changed in the October survey reference week 
relative to September’s survey week, pointing to continued modest job 
gains in October.       
Details
The continued low level of unemployment claims is 
consistent with a stable labor market. First-time 
jobless claims were below 200,000 for the fifth time 
in the last six weeks at 194,000 in the week ending 
October 17 (Figure 1). That was close to PNC’s 
forecast of 202,000. Initial claims are down 37,000 
from the same week one year earlier.  
 
The four-week moving average of initial claims in 
the week ending October 17 ticked down and has 
held below 200,000 for three weeks in a row. The 
four week moving average is down 37,000 from the 
same week in 2025. 
 
Continuing claims, or the number of people who 
continue to receive jobless benefits after filing an 
initial claim, rose by 25,000 to 1.728 million in the 
week ending October 10 (Figure 2). Continuing 
claims are down 219,000 from the same week a year 
ago.  
 
The four-week moving average of continuing claims 
ending October 10 edged up but is 11.4% below the 
level in the same week last year.  
 
The insured unemployment rate was 1.1% for the 
week ending October 10, unchanged for the last 
seven weeks but down from 1.3% in the same week 
last year. 
 
This week’s initial claims data includes October 12, 
which is the survey reference period for the October 
employment 
report. 
Initial 
claims 
were 
little 
changed in the October survey reference week 
relative to September’s survey week, pointing to 
continued modest job gains.

unchanged at the conclusion of next week’s 
meeting.
