Editorial 
Policy, politics and earnings  
Strategy summary: We retain our near-term defensive and 
diversified stance across assets, sectors and regions amid 
near-term uncertainty around monetary policy, politics and 
earnings. The next two weeks will be busy for major central 
banks, US political watchers and technology leaders powering 
the AI supercycle. We expect the Fed, ECB and BoJ to hold 
rates next week, but markets will focus on whether central 
banks are turning more hawkish due to the latest surge in fuel 
prices. The US mid-term elections are expected to give 
Democrats control of the House and Senate, with implications 
for taxes and the AI-led investment boom.  
Meanwhile, the consensus is expecting the US tech sector to 
report over 60% y/y earnings growth for Q3, the biggest jump 
in over two decades outside the post-COVID rebound.  Each of 
these three drivers have the potential to surprise markets.  
Big Three to hold monetary policy: We expect the Fed, ECB 
and BoJ to hold policy rates next week, after September’s 
hikes, as they assess whether higher bond yields have 
tightened financial conditions enough to offset fuel-price-driven 
inflation. We expect the Big Three to hike next in December as 
fuel prices are likely to stay elevated into early next year due to 
refining capacity constraints. Nevertheless, the Fed and ECB 
are likely to deliver less than around 60bps of rate hikes priced 
in by money markets over the next 12 months on the back of 
an eventual easing of oil and fuel prices leading to disinflation 
by H2 2027. This outlook means 3-7-year Developed Market 
investment grade bonds currently offer attractive risk-reward.  
Opportunity in Australian bonds. Other major central banks, 
such as the Bank of England, Bank of Canada and Reserve 
Bank of New Zealand are likely to hike rates by 25bps in the 
next two weeks given their focus on taming near-term inflation. 
However, we believe the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) is 
close to the end of its hiking cycle, even if it hikes one last time 
in the coming months (money markets give a 60% chance of a 
hike on 3 November). Australia’s domestic labour and housing 
markets and business confidence are softening after the RBA 
hiked its policy rate four times this year to a 15-year high. 
Meanwhile, Australia’s cash rate is 60bps higher than the US 
policy rate, supporting the AUD. We continue to see attractive 
income and FX gain opportunities from AUD corporate bonds.  
A ‘Blue Wave’ in the US? Prediction markets strongly favour 
Democrats taking the House of Representatives and possibly 
the Senate in the 3 November US mid-term elections. While an 
opposition sweep in the mid-terms has historically delivered the 
best 12-month returns for equities among all scenarios, partly 
due to the resulting policy gridlock, a decisive Democratic “blue 
wave” could increase longer-term risks of higher taxes and 
uncertainty over policy towards AI investment and data centres. 
With the Senate race still close and prediction markets fallible, 
we remain cautious on risk assets in the near term.  
Closing EU bank contingent convertible bonds (CoCos1) 
and US 10-year government bond ideas: We lock in 2% 
gains in EU CoCos and 1.7% gain in US 10-year government 
bond ideas amid French and US political risks (page 5-6). We 
continue to hedge any inflation risks through inflation-protected 
bonds and geopolitical risk through an Overweight in gold.  
Watching Big Tech earnings:  Upcoming Big Tech earnings 
raises uncertainty as market leadership remains concentrated 
and share-price reactions more mixed. With valuations less 
likely to drive returns, earnings delivery and sustainability of AI 
investment, funding and monetisation and broadening out of 
earnings beyond tech will be critical, raising the risk of volatility 
and sector divergence. We remain invested but defensively 
positioned, favouring diversification across sectors and regions.  
Staying defensive and diversified. We recently upgraded the 
defensive US Healthcare sector to Overweight, while retaining 
our Overweights to US tech and materials as well as financials 
in the US, Euro area and Japan. We also downgraded the more 
volatile Communications Services sector to Neutral and took 
profits on the semiconductor segment earlier this month before 
the latest correction. Our defensive and diversified stance 
should preserve participation in further equity upside, while 
reducing exposure to earnings disappointments and supporting 
resilience through the reporting season.  
—  Rajat Bhattacharya

Top client questions 
  What are the key takeaways from the technology sector 
earnings reported so far this week? 
Our view: Recent technology sector earnings results point to 
strong compute demand and continued capex expansion, 
supporting our positive long-term view on the AI investment 
theme. However, rising funding and execution risks warrant 
near-term caution. We prefer to avoid increasing exposure to 
higher-risk areas within the technology sector for now. 
Rationale: Earnings results reported this week suggest major tech 
companies remain committed to expanding their AI infrastructure. 
While this bodes well for AI infrastructure providers, ambitious 
expansion plans imply that an increasing share of AI capex is likely 
to depend on debt financing. We expect debt-financed AI capex to 
rise from c.25% of AI capex in 2026 to c.40% in 2027.  
Greater reliance on borrowing increases sensitivity to interest rates, 
while deployment delays could postpone revenue recognition amid 
ongoing financing costs. However, evidence of improving AI 
monetisation and adoption should strengthen operating cash 
flows and support internal capex funding. Against this backdrop, 
we will assess Big Tech earnings next week for greater clarity on 
capex guidance and funding and AI monetisation trends.  
—  Ryan Goh, Investment Strategist 
  Are you still positive on Euro area bank equities amid 
heightened political and fiscal uncertainty in the region? 
Our view: We remain positive on Euro area bank equities, 
supported by resilient earnings and shareholder-friendly capital 
return policies. While France’s fiscal uncertainty and a softer 
growth outlook warrant caution, the recent pullback in Euro 
area bank equities offers selective opportunities. We favour 
well-capitalised banks with strong deposit franchises and 
diversified earnings. 
Rationale: We expect Euro area banks’ Q3 2026 net interest 
income to grow 1.5-3.0% q/q, led by Spanish banks, driven by 
higher rates and continued asset repricing. Resilient fee income from 
wealth management and investment banking activity, as well as 
dividends and buybacks, should provide further support. Price-to-
book (P/B) valuations have eased, despite expectations for 
healthy return on equity (ROE), thus improving risk-reward. 
Fiscal uncertainty in France has widened sovereign spreads, 
pressuring banks’ bond holdings and funding costs. Although 
French banks represent 13% of the Euro area Bank Index, volatility 
could spread to regional financial markets. However, stress in the 
broader banking system appears limited, supported by strong 
capital buffers and ECB tools to contain disorderly market moves and 
sovereign market contagion. We will continue to monitor credit 
trends, sovereign spreads and banks’ 2027 earnings guidance. 
—  Robin Xie, Senior Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What has driven the recent weakness in China equities, 
and what is your outlook from here? 
Our view: We maintain China equities as a Core holding, as 
valuations remain inexpensive. Rapid technological innovation 
and investment in AI localisation provide tailwinds, but the 
potential for a re-rating appears delayed. Investors should seek 
to diversify their portfolios and reduce concentration risk. 
Rationale: China equities have weakened, driven by mounting 
scepticism 
over 
AI 
capex 
returns, 
structural 
margin 
compression and concerns about the country’s significant 
financing requirements. Another notable feature in China has been 
significant share issuance, which has diluted earnings per share and 
weighed on the MSCI China Index’s performance. These pressures 
have been compounded by geopolitical headwinds, restrictions on 
advanced chips, an enduring property sector contraction and weak 
domestic 
demand. 
September’s 
stimulus 
measures 
also 
disappointed markets in terms of their scale and pace of delivery. 
Notwithstanding these headwinds, valuations remain inexpensive, 
supported by rapid domestic innovation and structural tailwinds 
in AI localisation, leading us to retain China equities as a Core 
holding. Elevated volatility reinforces the need to be defensively 
positioned, favouring diversification rather than relying on technology 
sector concentration. We also remain Overweight China’s 
healthcare, financial and materials sectors. 
—  Cindy Lam, CFA, Senior Investment Strategist 
 
  Are you still bullish on US 10-year government bonds?   
Our view: We are closing our opportunistic bullish idea on US 
10-year government bonds at a gain of 1.7%. The risk-reward 
has become less compelling after the yield broke below 5%, the 
lower bound of our three-month forecast range of 5.00-5.25%. 
Rationale: The US 10-year yield fell over 17bps to 4.97% on 22 
October – its first move below 5% since 23 September and biggest 
one-day drop since March 2023. A sell-off in AI-related equities and 
renewed Middle East tensions drove investors towards safe havens. 
Earlier this month, the yield hit its highest level in over two decades. 
The forces behind the rally are fragile, though. Oil prices remain 
elevated amid escalating Middle East tensions, and September US 
consumer inflation rose to 3.7% y/y. Fed officials have signalled 
there is no urgency to raise rates at the 27-28 October meeting, 
but persistent price pressures keep further tightening on the table. 
We prefer to lock in gains and close our opportunistic bullish idea on 
10-year government bonds at a gain of 1.7%. 
—  Jonathan Liang, CFA, CIO, Fixed Income & FX

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  What is your outlook for French bank bonds, especially 
ahead of France’s upcoming sovereign credit rating reviews?   
Our view: The recent sell-off in French bank bonds is a repricing 
of tail risks, not a credit story. We expect further price volatility 
until the April 2027 French presidential election, particularly at 
the junior end of the capital structure, so we are closing our 
opportunistic EU bank contingent convertibles (CoCos1) idea at 
a gain of 2.0%. 
Rationale: We believe the recent sell-off in French bank bonds 
reflects domestic political and fiscal risks rather than weaker 
credit fundamentals. The sustained widening of the OAT-Bund 
spread has raised concerns about the profit and loss (P&L) impact 
on banks' sovereign debt holdings. Banks provide limited 
disclosure on these exposures. However, banks’ investment 
portfolios are generally diversified, and holdings booked at amortised 
cost should soften the P&L impact. Near-term catalysts include the 
upcoming sovereign credit rating reviews. A sovereign downgrade 
could trigger bank rating downgrades, with domestically focused 
lenders potentially more exposed than regionally diversified peers. A 
sovereign credit event is not our base case, but we expect headline 
risks to persist until the French presidential election. Against this 
backdrop, we are closing our opportunistic EU bank CoCos idea. 
—  Cedric Lam, Senior Investment Strategist 
  Will the November US mid-term elections and European 
bond volatility support a sustained Swiss franc (CHF) rebound?  
Our view: We expect a near-term CHF rebound, supported 
mainly by safe-haven demand amid European bond volatility. 
We believe the US mid-term elections will be a secondary driver. 
Rationale: European sovereign bond volatility provides the stronger 
catalyst for CHF strength. The France-Germany government bond 
yield spread remains elevated near 160bps, reflecting heightened 
French fiscal concerns and reinforcing the CHF’s safe-haven appeal. 
The US mid-terms could provide additional support for the CHF. 
A Democratic victory in either chamber is expected to constrain 
further fiscal expansion, potentially weighing on the USD. However, 
this would not necessarily lead to faster fiscal consolidation. 
The SNB’s 0% policy rate leaves the CHF at a sizeable interest-
rate disadvantage vs. the USD, constraining sustained CHF gains. 
With EUR/CHF below 0.90, excessive CHF strength could also 
increase the risk of SNB intervention. A decisive USD/CHF break 
below 0.8130 would strengthen the technical case for a more 
sustained CHF rebound.  
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Iris Yuen, Investment Strategist

Top client questions (cont’d) 
  Would worsening budget deficits and political uncertainty 
across parts of Europe affect your view on EUR/USD and Euro 
area government bond yields? 
Our view: Fiscal and political concerns constrain near-term 
EUR/USD gains, with France being the primary vulnerability. We 
expect EUR/USD to recover towards the 1.17-1.18 range over 
the next 12 months, supported by continued Euro area 
economic growth and an expected fading of USD support. We 
expect wider differentials in Euro area government bond yields, 
with political risk premium elevated in French government 
bonds (OATs) and German government bonds (Bunds) 
supported by safe-haven demand. 
Rationale: France's high public debt, large budget deficit and 
political obstacles to fiscal adjustments are weighing on market 
confidence, with the country’s 10-year government bond yield 
rising above 5%. The risk of stress spreading to Italy and Spain 
adds to regional concerns, although current evidence does not 
establish a broad European fiscal crisis. German government bonds 
should benefit from safe-haven demand, while fiscal concerns keep 
risk premia on vulnerable countries' bonds elevated. EUR/USD 
remains more sensitive to US-Germany interest-rate differentials 
and broader market confidence than to French bond yields alone. 
France’s fiscal risks, elevated energy costs and high US interest 
rates weigh on the EUR in the near term. Over the next 12 months, 
Germany’s multi-year public investment programme, resilient 
exports and sound banking-sector fundamentals are expected to 
support Euro area growth. We expect the Fed's rate-hiking cycle to 
peak by mid-2027, reducing USD support and driving the EUR  
towards 1.17-1.18 . 
The principal tail risk is fiscal stress widening government bond 
yield spreads and spilling over into bank funding conditions and 
lending activity, weakening growth and delaying the EUR's recovery. 
—  Vincent Tan, Senior Investment Strategist 
—  Cedric Lam, Senior Investment Strategist
