US Week In Review 
Energy prices edged up once again, with Brent crude rising yesterday to its highest close since 
early May amid renewed Middle East tensions and reports suggesting that President Trump is 
preparing to resume attacks on Iran following the midterm elections. A sustained rise in energy 
prices could slow the encouraging progress of disinflation reflected in the latest PCE prints.

falling from a revised 200K the previous week to 194K in the week ended October 17, extending 
their downward trend and reaching a four-year low. Continuing claims were less encouraging, 
edging up from 1,703K to 1,728K in the week ended October 10. Falling initial claims alongside 
rising continuing claims suggest that low-hire, low-fire dynamics persist.

What to Watch for Next Week 
Next week will be highlighted by the Fed’s October meeting, with the Federal Open Market 
Committee (FOMC) set to announce its rate decision on Wednesday. We expect the FOMC to 
leave the policy rate unchanged, with the upper bound remaining at 4.00%. The September core 
CPI reading was hotter than expected but while this adds to the case for caution, we think the 
reading was not too far off expectations to make a rate hike the likely decision at the October 
meeting. The downward revisions to PCE inflation have even been encouraging. We think that

The Fed’s communication before the latest inflation data also suggests a pause in next week’s 
meeting. Vice Chair Philip Jefferson suggested policymakers may need more time to assess

LatAm Highlights 
  Brazil – Elections this Sunday! Bolsonaro remains the favorite  
Brazil’s presidential run-off between President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva (Lula) and Flávio 
Bolsonaro will come to a conclusion this Sunday, October 25th.  Polls have narrowed since the 
day after the first round, from about 5% in favor of F. Bolsonaro to around 2%. In our view, the 
current gap of roughly 2%, just before the election, is close to his actual first-round margin of 
1.87%. Thus, we believe part of the move reflects mean reversion rather than real erosion. 
To be clear, we continue to believe F. Bolsonaro remains the favorite to win the election for the 
following reasons: The majority of polls continue to show higher rejection levels for Lula than for 
Bolsonaro. In PoderData, 48% say they would never vote for Lula, versus 44% for F. Bolsonaro; 
other polls show similar results. Only a handful of polls, such as Vox, have them tied at 45.3% 
each. This is key because, in our view, the election will be decided by which candidate voters 
dislike less. As we have argued before, polls and conversations with political analysts suggest 
that swing voters lean right. Among unaligned voters, Datafolha has F. Bolsonaro at 59% and 
Lula at 41% of valid votes. 
F. Bolsonaro continues to be the favorite… 
 
Source: Bloomberg,  Natixis 
Before the first round of the elections, we conducted a survey to assess expectations for Brazilian 
policy and the BRL under different electoral outcomes. The survey consisted of eight questions, 
focusing on expectations and concerns under two alternative scenarios: a victory by President 
Lula or by Flávio Bolsonaro.  
If President Lula wins… 
When asked about their main concern under the scenario that President Lula wins the 2026 
election, 76% of respondents chose fiscal responsibility as the dominant concern. An increase in 
corruption and trade tensions with the US each accounted for 9% of the responses while 
intervention in the BCB and lack of investment received little support. The results suggest 
respondents see the main market risk under Lula as fiscal rather than institutional or monetary.

  Mexico – Inflation edges higher, reducing the chances of a rate cut 
After the release of the statement for the September 24 monetary policy meeting, our reading 
was partially wrong. Recall that the meeting took place after the Fed had hiked its policy rate by 
25bps. We interpreted the emphasis on the risk of FX pass-through as a signal that Banxico would 
be more careful going forward if the Fed continued to hike. This reflected the view of one board 
member. However, at least three board members were not thinking about hiking and were open, 
to varying degrees, to discussing a possible cut, as reflected in the minutes published on October 
8. 
No one voted for a cut, and there was no commitment to one. One member said there were 
elements to discuss the merits of a “fine-tuning” cut, framing it as a discussion that should stay 
open rather than a premature decision. Another said a cut would depend on inflation continuing 
to trend down and financial conditions staying orderly. A third was more neutral, preferring to hold 
for now and decide meeting by meeting. After the release of the inflation figures for H1 October 
and the depreciation of the MXN, we believe the chances of a cut are slim. 

ACROSS THE AMERICAS 
12 
Inflation for H1 October surprised on the upside. Headline came in at 0.41% 2w/2w vs the 
market’s 0.38%. Core came in at 0.25%, also above the market’s 0.17%. The big takeaway is that 
non-core inflation is now clearly going up. Just a couple of months ago, non-core was helping to 
keep a lid on inflation, as it was around 1% y/y. Now it is solidly above 3.0%. Perhaps the El Niño 
effect is starting to have an impact. Core inflation remains low, but it seems to have touched its 
lowest level, in annual terms, in September. The H1 October data show that core inflation is 
starting to increase, albeit marginally, coming in at 3.78% y/y. 
Given that inflation has started to accelerate, the NowCast indicators for the monthly GDP proxy 
index—IGAE—came out on the strong side, and the MXN is depreciating on the back of risk-off 
sentiment in equities, we believe the room for a rate cut has materially diminished. We believe 
that the comments in the minutes about a possible rate cut were more a way of signaling that they 
don’t want to follow the Fed mechanically as it tightens.
