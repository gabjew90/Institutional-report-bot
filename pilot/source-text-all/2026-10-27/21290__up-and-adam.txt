Mortgage Rates Post First Weekly Decline Since August, But Applications Approach A Two-Year Low 
Source: FactSet
Chart of the Day: Crude Disconnect
With Treasury yields off their highs, the Mortgage Bankers Association’s average 30-year rate declined last week to 7.41%—the first drop 
after eight straight weeks of increases—though it remains more than a full percentage point higher this year. Prospective home buyers 
remain rate-sensitive: MBA’s gauge of mortgage applications has declined for seven straight weeks to the lowest level since Jan. 2025, 
while pending home sales fell to their lowest level since 2001. Bottom line: housing market frustrations aren’t going away anytime soon. 
Gold has fallen ~22% from its January all-time high, as higher bond yields have increased the opportunity cost and reduced the appeal 
of holding non-yielding assets. Notably, this decline occurred despite heightened geopolitical tensions (US/Iran, Russia/Ukraine), 
which have historically supported investor demand. It’s a reminder that gold remains a sentiment-driven commodity. Cooling yields 
could provide a boost, but shifts in investor positioning and broader risk sentiment may continue to drive volatility.
