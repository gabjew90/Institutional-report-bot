Global top three memory shares are down -42% from the 2026 peak; what 
are the share prices pricing-in? Memory shares (SEC/SKH/MU) have 
dropped sharply (down 42% from recent peaks in September on average), 
likely on negative macro developments such as softer-than-expected Frontier 
AI model lab annual-revenue-runrate trend (link), widening Neo-Cloud CDS 
spread (report), tightening risk appetite and rising doubts about AI capex 
sustainability. This likely marks the third interim share correction post the AI 
trade (the first correction in July-2024 on the Yen-carry trade unwind and the 
second correction in mid-2025 on HBM ASP correction speculation), and we 
believe the current share price action presumes the market pricing-in a down-
cycle as early as 2027 with the Q/Q memory price trend turning negative. This 
is perplexing to us since we see limited signs of a crack from a bottom-up 
memory supply/demand fundamental angle (spot price trending 
upwards, HBM ASP negotiation bias to the upside, LTA order visibility 
extended, etc.), and we believe the biggest disconnects seem to be : 1) what if 
the AI capex run-rate slows to <$1.5T in 27 from funding risks (report from US 
hardware team), and 2) what are the catalysts to reverse this dampened 
sentiment? Historically, memory stocks have fallen 49% from peak-to-trough 
over the past four downcycles (vs GFC: -71% and pre-AI: -53%) and bottomed 
at 0.9-1.2x trailing P/B (see Table 3).

What are the downcycle signals and current status quo? Each downcycle has been 
unique in itself but commonalities between the downcycles were: 1) spot/contract prices 
plateauing followed by a sharp correction; 2) unexpected inventory building both at 
memory and OEMs, an early signal of demand slowdown; and 3) signals of demand 
slowdown from other tech supply chains (wafers, foundry, memory controller, 
equipment, etc.). As of current: 1) 8Gb DDR4/5 spot prices were flat/+4% MTD; 2) 
inventory remains extremely tight with order/pricing discussions centered in 2028/2029 
(i.e. extended order visibility echoing MU’s order commentary – report); and 3) limited 
signs of order-cuts in other AI complex, while sensing continuous price hikes that support 
the tight supply thesis. CXMT’s 4F² development stoked another round of fear in memory 
stocks, but anecdotally, we believe the rudimentary cause of previous downcycles 
originated from demand equations more so than supply expansions, and we expect 
CXMT’s 4F² development to be less of a threat (see our report ). In summary, we see 
limited evidence to support any down-cycle scenario signals; we only pick up evidence 
of a stronger and longer up-cycle in the last 1-3 months.
•
Heading into 3Q earnings, near-term earnings color outweighs longer-term cycle 
evidence. Based on our conversations with investors, the memory trade has 
transformed into a battleground between momentum (largely following EPS 
upward/downward revisions and memory y-y pricing trends) and value 
investorsbanking on a higher-for-longer cycle as memory advances as the AGI enabler. 
As the AI trade enters its fourth year, investors appear increasingly focused on signs that 
the memory price upcycle is cooling, particularly after exceptionally strong DRAM ASP 
growth of +54% /+27% /+236% y-y in 24/25/26E. Sentiment also remains cautious on 
2027E pricing, with macro headwinds in focus given the linkage between CSPs’ AI capex 
sustainability (i.e., widening negative FCF into next year) and the stability of AI funding 
amidst a rate hike environment. While LTA (long-term agreement) disclosures can help 
frame the mid- to long-term cycle, we believe near-term earnings color is the primary 
driver of current share-price moves. Against investor concerns, we remain constructive 
on both the short-term and medium-term cycle and we believe any favorable 2027 
demand read-throughs (i.e. indications that HBM is being sold out, capex discipline is 
being maintained, and pricing trends are stable) could act as a near-term support for the 
sector. Fundamentally, we continue to maintain our view that the agentic CPU-related 
memory demand is the most under-appreciated source of demand upside.

Company-level catalysts and drivers to monitor going into 3Q26 earnings 
conferences. Beyond the broader memory pricing trend (a common read-through for all 
vendors), we believe HBM should remain the key differentiator across DRAM makers. 
HBM specifications and execution strategies, such as die speed, thermal management, 
yield ramp, and the approach to logic-die sourcing, vary by company and are likely to 
influence both product competitiveness and pricing/bargaining power. At the company 
level, our key watch items include: 1) SKH: HBM4 execution progress, particularly in 
light of recent market noise around potential yield hiccups; 2) SEC: Updates to HBM 
business targets and progress on HBM4 pricing negotiations; and 3) Kioxia: 4Q26 
NAND blended pricing trend and QLC NAND demand update. More broadly, we will 
assess how effectively vendors can offset B2C demand softness with B2B strength, and 
how management teams frame the shift towards a more server-centric revenue mix 
heading into 2027E. We also believe the 2027E DRAM/NAND bit supply growth 
commentary could ease over-supply concerns.
•
Narratives/catalysts that can reverse the sentiment. In the past 3M, the perceived 
detachment between larger AI context windows and slower HBM content upgrades 
raised doubts about the inelasticity of AI-driven memory demand, reinforced by the 
HBM4E de-spec in Rubin Ultra. We see four pillars that could reset sentiment: 1) HBM 
content re-acceleration – either higher capacity per SiP and/or bandwidth gains that 
directly refute the rolling de-spec narrative; 2) Supply discipline – capex/bit-growth 
restraint and a profitability-first posture that supports a more durable pricing framework; 
3) Broader AI demand drivers – new applications beyond query/coding that expand use 
cases and compute intensity; and 4) Shareholder returns (most important near-term) 
– investors have indicated prior return announcements were insufficient (report), and at 
depressed valuations a meaningful buyback is viewed as the cleanest tool to prevent 
further multiple de-rating. Both Korean and Japanese large-cap memory stocks have 
retraced to pre-buyback-announcement price levels and now trade around ~3x FTM P/E, 
below prior-cycle peak-earnings trailing multiples, implying skepticism on through-
cycle cash generation. A more progressive, credible capital return framework – 
combining buybacks and dividends – could signal management confidence and shift the 
debate from ‘how elastic is AI memory demand?’ to ‘how much downside is already 
priced in and what sets a floor under the multiple?’
•
Stock price views: DCF-based FVPS indicates a much earlier price downcycle. 
Based on our DCF exercise (report), SEC/SKH are trading at 2-3x P/E (27/28E) and 0.9x-
1.5x P/B (27/28E), pricing-in a memory downcycle without clear evidence of it 
materializing. If the share prices are correct, then we believe the market is forecasting a 
memory earnings peakout as early as 1H27E given the historical pattern of memory 
stocks reacting 6-9M ahead. Global top 3 memory shares have fallen 42% from the recent 
peak in July-26 and, in our view, this price action has already assimilated an historical 
downcycle share performance which we believe hasn’t materialized (-50% in the 14/16 
downcycle, -43% in the 18/19 server downcycle, and -47% in the 21/23 rate-hike 
downcycle, with the only exception being during the GFC, when shares corrected -71% 
from peak-to-trough). Our short-term stock preference is SKH (OW) over SEC (OW) 
based upon the former’s superior shareholder return policy and higher TSR yield. We also 
like Kioxia (OW) and NYT (OW) in Asia.
