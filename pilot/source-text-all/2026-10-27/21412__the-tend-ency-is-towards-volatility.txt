Global Daily 
Market comment 
Yesterday was not a quiet day in geopolitics at all. As is often the case --and underlying the 
point made yesterday about the different skillsets between economists, strategists, and those 
working in geopolitics/geoeconomics-- markets chose to focus on a rumour the US and Iran were 
engaged in serious discussions. They weren’t. As Iran noted, why should it help Trump by striking 
a deal before the midterms when it could then be struck after voting is over?  
What the market didn’t focus on was far more momentous: the US ambassador to Türkiye told 
the Doha Global Security Forum that the US no longer wants to own the “Grand Bargain” of oil 
in return for US protection; that it’s no longer the guarantor to the world as for the last 80 years, 
not just Europe; and that this isn't "abandonment", but the US only wants to "tend" the Middle 
East region - a process similar to "raising teenagers.” Anyone who’s raised teenagers knows the 
ruckus --and money-- involved… but America won’t be paying for it. 
This isn’t about the end of the ‘petrodollar’, which was eclipsed by the Eurodollar years ago. 
However, it matters enormously because energy still does, especially in a new Cold War along 
supply chains. The full-fat Pax Americana being over portends more Middle East instability ahead.  
Iran underlined it won’t send its enriched uranium to Russia, leaving the US with few options 
other than more war soon. A third US aircraft carrier and marine detachment of 10,000 men will 
reportedly arrive next month. That suggests the US “tend”-ency is towards more fighting, so the 
market’s will be towards more volatility. But what of the “teenagers”? 
France’s Macron started a three-day visit to the GCC with Kuwait and today he’s in Bahrain. 
Paris has already pledged to help protect Yanbu in Saudi Arabia from the Houthis. The UK will 
now support the Saudis with fighter-jets to shoot down Houthi missiles. Is that in the upcoming 
budget? 
Qatar flagged a new defence deal with Türkiye, Pakistan, and Indonesia. Given Saudi Arabia is 
already a member of the Mecca Pact with the first two, the ‘Islamic NATO’ now has men 
(Indonesia, Pakistan, Türkiye), military supply chains (Türkiye), nukes (Pakistan), resources 
(Indonesia), and money (Qatar). Yet how effective it will prove is questionable given its failure to 
help Saudi so far: a purported leak of the Mecca Pact text says it lasts at least 10 years but overall 
reads like NATO Article 1/5 rather than 5. 
Fractured Libya’s parliament agreed with Türkiye to split the eastern Mediterranean into two 
new exclusive maritime zones that cut off Cyprus, Israel from Greece and Italy despite plans for 
joint energy projects, Europe from East Africa despite plans for energy projects, and the Suez 
Canal. Greece and the EU rejected this as counter to UN maritime law but for now the Turkish 
navy has the edge as Greece is early in a rearmament cycle. At best, this is another irritant for the 
EU and the Middle East. At worst, it risks something far worse.  
Looking more broadly, one can (geographically) list emerging gangs of “teens”: India, 
Azerbaijan, the UAE, Israel, Somaliland, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Greece, and Italy; and, opposing, 
Pakistan, Qatar, the Houthis, Syria, Somalia, Eritrea, Sudan, Türkiye, and Libya. Confusingly, Saudi, 
Egypt, and even the US seem to have a foot in both camps at times if not with equal weight on 
each. The US clearly leans towards the former in terms of plans for energy pipelines, etc.

On which, the Financial Times notes ‘The unlikely winners from Trump’s Iran war’ are the 
Americas emerge for those looking to replace supply lost from Middle East war. Which is the 
Plan B part of the Trump “energy dominance” Grand Macro Strategy flagged here before: either 
flip Iran like Venezuela or see the Mid-East destabilised and emerge as the *relative* winner.   
Underlining how the Russia-Ukraine and US/Israel-Iran wars are linked, there were rumours of 
Russian air support to Iran alongside diplomatic backing --Tehran noting Russia and Iran have 
never “worked as closely” together-- but the Russians have no military muscle to spare for now.  
Iran also warned NATO over an alleged Ukrainian drone buildup near its borders, alleging 
thousands of "micro-drones" have been sent to countries near it, including Iraq’s Kurdistan 
Region, with Tehran is weighing an “appropriate response.” NATO is also sending forces to the 
Black Sea, bordering Türkiye, in response to more Russian drone attacks on commercial shipping. 
The EU unveiled an Arctic Strategy that lists ‘security and resilience’ as the first priority before 
climate change, economic prosperity, and opportunities for Arctic communities, underlining how 
Europe is under increasing geopolitical/economic pressure on many fronts – including at home. 
The UK just re-arrested two men over an Iran-linked terror plot on an RAF base, fears are 
growing over Russian grey zone sabotage in Europe, and in the wider West, Australia’s press 
reports four “major” terror plots have been foiled in the country since the Bondi massacre, with 
the public kept in the dark. 
All mentioned above in this Global Daily is going to be very expensive to address at a time 
when EM are under severe pressure and Western debt levels are mostly too high.  
Yet so is public anger, with France, Belgium, and Italy --and even India-- having their own 
issues with teenage gangs. And, according to the French PM, organised far-left attempts at 
insurrection that could even threaten the upcoming presidential election. Both France and Italy 
are now going to be deploying troops on their own streets to enforce order (as warning signs are 
of right-wing groups prepared to do the same if that doesn’t happen). 
Meanwhile, Elon Musk has been fingered as the one cutting off Starlink access to Ukraine, not 
the White House, as he floats a SpaceX and Tesla merger. That’s as Israel’s defence minister, in 
louder pre-election bombast mode than normal, noted his country is preparing for “galactic 
warfare.” All jokes aside, space is going to be a new dimension of conflict. Add that staggering 
cost of investment to AI as a burden for financial markets to be forced to bear one way or 
another, and the limited hands holding both powers to geopolitical tensions.  
The RBA’s Hauser didn’t venture into any of this territory today but still noted that the 
Australian inflation outlook is “materially worse” than in August due to higher prices in AI, tech, 
datacentre construction, and oil. Against this predictable geopolitical backdrop, who could have 
forecasted that? Not this particular central bank, clearly. 
What do you think the tendency is from here? 
Day ahead 
Next today it’s UK CPI, seen 0.3% m-o-m and 3.4% y-o-y headline and 2.8% y-o-y core, PPI seen 
7.6% y-o-y input and 4.4% output, and public borrowing. Then we have final Eurozone public debt 
to GDP for 2025, expected at 85.7% - and 2026 will be higher and what will 2027 be? 
 We also have a slew of ECB speakers.
