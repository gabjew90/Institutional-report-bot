We see developments in nuclear policy this year as a key catalyst that will accelerate progress
towards delivery (Figure 1). We have argued previously that public policy is critical in unlocking
the delivery of new capacity through restarts, uprates and new builds of conventional/advanced
fission reactors (Turning hype into reality), as well as the development of fusion over the longer
term (Fusion: Tomorrow's solution to today's problem). The most striking shift has been the
upsurge in nuclear phase-out reversals in countries that had previously adopted moratoria on
the technology, particularly:
• Switzerland: On 19 June, the Swiss parliament voted to reverse the ban on the construction
of new nuclear plants, with a national referendum now expected in 2027.1
• Italy: Italy's Senate gave final approval on 23 September to a framework law that allows a
return to nuclear nearly 40 years after it was banned, with the government now having 12
months to issue implementing decrees.2
• Taiwan: On 24 September, Taiwan's Nuclear Safety Commission cleared Taipower's restart
plan for its Maanshan plant, which could be restarted as soon as 2028,3 further cementing the
country's retreat from its nuclear phase-out plans (Asia commitment checks: Energy
transition at the crossroads).
• Belgium: On 30 April, the Belgian government signed a non-binding letter of intent with
Engie to pursue a state takeover of the country's nuclear fleet and suspend dismantling work,
following the repeal of the nuclear phase-out law last year.4
Our read-through is that policy risk is continuing to ease for nuclear developers,
supporting deployment prospects and strengthening growth opportunities for companies
across the nuclear ecosystem.
