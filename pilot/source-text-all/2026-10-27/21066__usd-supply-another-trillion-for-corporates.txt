Dollar supply stays strong as corporate issuance heads towards another US$1tr year
We forecast another hefty year for USD corporate issuance in 2027, with gross supply again
expected to be around US$1tr. While below the record-like US$1.1tr seen in both 2020 and
2026, issuance is expected to remain exceptionally strong by historical standards.
Redemptions are expected to remain broadly unchanged at US$553bn, leaving net supply of
around US$447bn. While this is a modest improvement from 2026, the USD market will
continue to face a significantly heavier net supply burden than its EUR counterpart.

Hyperscalers and tech issuers are a driving force
As discussed above, the scale of investment required for data centres, energy infrastructure
and digital capacity is unprecedented. The financing burden has spread well beyond
technology firms themselves and is increasingly influencing broader capital markets.
Already in 2026, the hyperscalers have been a major source of debt issuance, raising around
US$237bn across currencies, including US$160bn in USD and US$31bn in EUR. Issuance has
been led by the usual suspects: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle and IBM.
Looking ahead, we expect hyperscaler funding needs to increase further as investment in AI
infrastructure, data centres and power capacity accelerates. As a result, we forecast total
issuance of around US$280bn in 2027, including approximately US$190bn in USD and US$65bn
in EUR (€60bn).
The rest of the technology sector will also remain very busy in 2027, with an expected
US$200bn forecast to come to market.
M&A is substantial
M&A activity should continue to support primary market volumes. While technology has
grabbed the headlines, M&A outside the sector has also accelerated, with both US and
European deal activity reaching new highs in 2026. The pipeline remains healthy, suggesting
momentum should carry into at least the first half of 2027.
However, higher rates, rising debt costs and somewhat tighter liquidity conditions are likely to
become a greater constraint over time. As a result, we expect M&A-related issuance to remain
supportive of supply, albeit at a more moderate pace as the year progresses.

Higher rates, higher cost of debt
All-in yields in USD IG credit have risen back towards 5.2% (5yr), driven largely by higher
underlying rates rather than wider credit spreads.
Looking ahead, our rates strategists expect a modest steepening bias in 2027, with slightly
more downward momentum at the front end of the curve. The broader message, however, is
that rates should remain elevated enough to keep all-in yields attractive.
We expect only limited changes in outright rates, curve shape and swap spreads, leaving all-in
yields broadly intact. As a result, the search for yield should remain a powerful tailwind for
credit markets throughout 2027, although the same elevated rates continue to increase the
cost of debt for issuers.

Sectors supply shows the amount of tech issuance
Most sectors are expected to see relatively modest net supply in 2027. The clear exception is
TMT, which will once again dominate issuance volumes. We forecast around US$180bn of
supply from the hyperscalers alone, with a further US$850bn coming from the broader
technology universe. Telecoms should add another US$60bn, leaving TMT firmly at the centre
of the supply story.
Utilities remain another major source of issuance as rising capex requirements continue to
drive funding needs, extending a trend that has defined the sector in recent years. Healthcare
and Industrials should also remain active issuers, although a sizeable portion of gross supply
will be offset by elevated redemption profiles.
In short, while net supply remains contained across much of the market, technology and
utilities continue to shoulder the bulk of issuance growth in 2027.
