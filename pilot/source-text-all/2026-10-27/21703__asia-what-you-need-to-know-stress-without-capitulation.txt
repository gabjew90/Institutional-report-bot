Asia's October drawdown looks like broad market stress on the screen, but beneath the index-level
weakness the evidence points to a concentrated positioning/technical reset rather than systemic
capitulation. Hedge funds, particularly pods, are experiencing real stress, while long-only investors are
rotating defensively and desk flows remain relatively balanced. If that diagnosis is right, the opportunity is
not necessarily to call the absolute market bottom, but to identify where forced and tax-driven selling is
creating dislocations.
The question I have been getting all week is "where is the bottom?" I think that is the wrong question.
October's drawdown looks ugly at the index level, but the underlying evidence suggests concentrated
positioning stress and year-end technical selling rather than broad capitulation. That distinction matters
because it argues for selective re-risking into dislocations rather than waiting for an all-clear signal from
the indices.

Diverging Perspectives: This week has been particularly challenging for hedge funds, especially the pod
shops. More than two-thirds of hedge funds finished down overnight, and for the first time since COVID,
Global Fundamental L/S, Global Systematic L/S and Multi-Strats all fell more than 1% on the same day;
(Kartik Link) in Asia, Fundamental L/S managers lost 2.2%, taking the MTD drawdown to 9.3%.
(Himanshu Link) The experience for long-only investors is more nuanced: mutual funds have fared
moderately better on a net total return basis, but face a different technical as October marks fiscal year-end
for one in four US mutual funds, representing $2.3tn of active and $1.7tn of passive AUM. (Flow of
Funds) This distinction is important: hedge funds are experiencing acute positioning stress, while long-
only investors are increasingly confronting fiscal year-end portfolio considerations rather than the same
degree of forced de-risking.

Explaining the Drawdown: The initial catalyst for October’s correction was fundamental, but concentrated
positioning and fiscal year-end technicals appear to be amplifying the drawdown. Concerns around
hyperscaler free cash flow, underwhelming AI earnings, data-center disruptions and rising funding costs
are not new, but earnings season has failed to alleviate them, with AI versus the S&P 500 ex-AI suffering
its worst relative day since the DeepSeek episode; in Asia, despite Info Tech dominating Prime net selling,
exposure remains above 15% of the Global Prime Book. Concentration is also creating a challenge for
long-only managers, with TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix together accounting for more than 30% of
MSCI EM, while October marks fiscal year-end for one-quarter of US mutual funds, representing $2.3tn
of active and $1.7tn of passive AUM. Funds that previously realized gains in the year’s winners therefore
have an incentive to harvest losses elsewhere to mitigate capital-gains distributions, potentially adding
technical pressure to unrelated laggards such as Tencent and Ping An that are already trading near 52-week
lows.

GS Desk Flow Today: Today’s High Touch/Program flow does not look like panic de-risking, broad de-
grossing or market stress. It is active but largely balanced and rotational, with clear reallocations across
countries, sectors and single names rather than indiscriminate selling. Long only funds are rotating into a
defensive bias but the Hedge Fund community, especially the Pods, are feeling the pain and are stress.
There is no broad geographic liquidation as Japan, Hong Kong and Australia led gross activity, but net
country flows were modest relative to total turnover. That is inconsistent with a region-wide rush for the
exits. Stress signal is limited. The elevated gross activity shows urgency and portfolio reshuffling, but the
lack of consistently negative net flow across countries and sectors suggests rotation with pockets of de-
grossing, not systemic stress.
