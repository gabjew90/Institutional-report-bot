Global diesel prices seen staying high despite Trump's deal with Russia 
President Donald Trump's deal with Russia to supply diesel to US and global markets is unlikely to meaningfully lower 
diesel prices, market watchers and analysts said, noting that the promised volumes are too small to ease a global supply 
squeeze. 
 
Diesel prices have shot to record highs during the US-Israeli war on Iran, creating a political liability for Trump and his 
Republican Partyas they campaign to maintain thin majorities in the US Congress in the November 3 midterm elections. 
 
Critics of Trump's move said lifting sanctions on Russian diesel could help Moscow fund further attacks on Ukraine, 
undermining years of efforts to curb Russian energy revenues and raising the risk the Kremlin could profit while 
consumers get little relief from high fuel prices. 
 
Trump said Russia would immediately export 300,000 metric tons, or 2.25 million barrels, followed by 500,000 tons in 
November and 1 million tons "immediately thereafter". Analysts have said it may not be possible for Russia to produce 
enough excess diesel in the near-term to meet those ambitious goals, while a strained global tanker market may also 
make timely shipments difficult. 
 
Analysts also said the announced volumes are unlikely to meaningfully lower diesel prices. US retail diesel prices 
topped$6 a gallon in September for the first time ever and have remained above that level since. 
 
BIG PROMISES, LIMITED IMPACT 
 
Michelle Brouhard, head of policy and geopolitical risk at Kpler, said it would take an additional 400,000 barrels per day of 
diesel supply to materially ease prices in the current market. But the initial shipment of roughly 2.2 million barrels would 
amount to only about 73,000 bpd if distributed over a month. She said that volume on its own would probably not 
significantly affect global diesel prices. 
 
Caspian Conran, lead economist at consulting firm Baringa, estimated that the market would require an additional 
850,000 bpd of supply to produce an immediate softening in prices. 
 
Trump initially said Russia would immediately supply diesel to the US and global markets to help bring down fuel prices, 
but later said the Russian cargoes would be released to global markets rather than shipped to the US. This raised 
questions about whether the deal will deliver the domestic supply relief the president promised.

"The diesel that Putin was going to sell, they were going to make it available to the global market one way or the other," 
Secretary of State Marco Rubio said over the weekend. "The only difference is how much of it the US was going to get." 
 
White House spokeswoman Taylor Rogers said Trump announced that "massive amounts" of Russian diesel will supply 
the global markets and will lower prices at home and abroad. "President Trump continues to secure good deals, not just 
for the United States, but for the world," Rogers said. 
 
STRAINED REFINING SYSTEM, HIGH FREIGHT RATES 
 
It was unclear whether Russia can substantially increase diesel exports in the near term due to limited Russian export 
availability and elevated freight costs. 
 
Shipping data on Tuesday showed some Russian diesel cargoes were returning to their traditional destinations after the 
US suspended sanctions and secured the lifting of an export ban. At least two tankers were signaling trips to Turkey and 
Brazil. 
 
Data also showed Russia has resumed diesel export loadings from the Baltic port of Primorsk following an agreement 
between Washington and Moscow to supply fuel to global markets. 
 
Disruptions to diesel exports from Russia, the world's second-largest diesel exporter after the US, and the Middle East 
reached 1.3 million barrels per day in September, about 4.5% of global diesel and gasoil demand. The International 
Energy Agency has warned shortages could persist into 2027. 
 
"We question whether Russia’s refineries could even support an increase in exports with, by some estimates, up to 45% 
of Russia’s refining capacity currently offline," said Mary Melton, an analyst with tanker company Braemar. 
 
Delivering the announced volumes in addition to existing exports would require Russia to produce roughly 384,000 to 
509,000 barrels per day of diesel, the Energy Policy Research Foundation said in a note. Russia currently produces 
about 1.4 million barrels per day and consumes roughly 1.2 million barrels per day domestically, leaving little surplus 
available for export. 
 
Traders noted the country is currently importing distillates and lacks sufficient production and surplus volumes. While an 
end to attacks could allow damaged facilities to begin repairs and eventually return to international markets, sources said 
that is unlikely to happen before the end of the year, a view widely shared across the market. 
 
Ukraine resumed its attacks on Russian refineries last week with a strike on a plant in the Ryazan region. 
 
Moreover, freight rates for Russian oil and fuel exports have also risen sharply amid heightened security risks in the Black 
Sea. A tight global tanker market has also raised the cost of moving cargoes. 
 
Many tanker owners and operators are probably reluctant to move Russian diesel on their ships, fearing future customers 
may shun the ships due to worries of having moved sanctioned cargoes, a source at a tanker owner said. While Trump 
has temporarily removed US sanctions, he noted, other Western nations still have sanctions in place.

US aims to loan about a third of heating oil reserve in bid to tame prices 
The Trump administration is offering to loan up to nearly a third of the 1-million-barrel US emergency heating oil reserve, 
the Department of Energy said on Wednesday, in a bid to tame surging prices. 
 
The move, reported first by Reuters, came after senators from both political parties urged the administration to release 
heating oil from the reserve. 
 
Heating oil is similar in composition to diesel fuel, the price of which has hit records above $6.50 per gallon following 
disruptions in the Middle East caused by the Iran war. The Trump administration also blames high prices on Ukrainian 
attacks on refineries in Russia. 
 
The administration will evaluate whether further releases from the reserve are necessary, a source at the department 
said. 
 
The US has only tapped the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve once since Hurricane Sandy struck the region in 2012. 
The emergency stockpile is held at sites in New Jersey, Connecticut, Massachusetts and Maine. 
 
About one-third of New England homes use heating oil, including about half of homes in Maine. 
 
Soaring heating oil costs have emerged as a key issue ahead of the November 3 midterm elections, including in several 
tight Senate races, as Trump's fellow Republicans are trying to maintain control of the two chambers of Congress. 
 
Maine Republican Senator Susan Collins is facing a tough reelection fight, while New Hampshire's open Senate seat is a 
toss-up between Democrat Chris Pappas and Republican John Sununu. 
 
US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the administration was "committed to doing everything in our power to lower 
energy costs." The department said there would be a competitive bidding process for the loans for both fuel retailers and 
terminal holders to allow the fuel to flow into areas facing shortages. 
 
Collins said on X the move equals a 13% supply increase for New England and will push down the region's heating oil 
costs this winter. 
 
Analysts were not convinced. Tom Kloza, chief energy adviser at Gulf Oil, said on X the move was "puzzling and probably 
inconsequential" and should have no impact on prices. 
 
Average retail diesel prices have hovered around $6.32 a gallon in recent days after hitting a record of $6.52 last month, 
according to the AAA motorist organization. 
 
The department will loan up to 315,000 barrels of heating oil from Maine, Massachusetts and Connecticut, it said. 
Deliveries are set to be completed by November 13.

China snaps up Australian canola as improved ties, lower tariffs fuel demand 
Chinese oilseed buyers have purchased around 1 million metric tons of Australian canola in recent deals, as improved 
Beijing-Canberra ties, lower tariffs and stronger processing margins boost demand, three Asia-based traders told 
Reuters. 
 
The cargoes, for arrival in early 2027, were bought at about $695 to $705 per ton, including cost and freight (C&F) to 
China, said one of the sources with direct knowledge of the deals. 
 
"Attractive crushing margins in both the futures and spot markets for Australian canola prompted the purchases," said one 
Beijing-based agricultural broker. The broker and three traders did not want to be identified as they are not authorised to 
speak to the media. 
 
The purchases mark a further normalization in China-Australia trade after Beijing began reopening its canola market to 
Australian supplies around the middle of last year. 
 
Initial imports were limited to trial purchases by state-owned trader COFCO, while private crushers were excluded until 
Beijing expanded access in July this year. 
 
COFCO's purchases last year came after Beijing imposed preliminary anti-dumping duties of 75.8% on Canadian canola 
in August 2025, bringing shipments nearly to a halt. An initial trade deal with Ottawa in January led China to cut its final 
anti-dumping tariff to 5.9% the next month. 
 
China now imposes an effective duty of 14.9% on Canadian canola, compared with a standard 9% import tariff on 
Australian cargoes. 
 
Both state-owned and private buyers made the latest purchases, traders said. Australian canola is cheaper than 
Canadian supplies because of lower tariffs and freight costs. 
 
China is the world's biggest importer of canola, which is also called rapeseed. Canada is the world's biggest canola 
exporter and Australia is the No. 2 supplier of the oilseed, which is crushed to produce protein-rich meal and oil.

Crude palm oil prices to stay above $1,128 per ton in November, MPOC says 
Crude palm oil prices are expected to stay above 4,600 ringgit ($1,128) per metric ton in November on stronger export 
demand, sustained biodiesel consumption and a seasonal decline in production, the Malaysian Palm Oil Council said on 
Thursday. 
 
Output declines were expected to reduce current stock levels, with Malaysian palm oil inventories projected to trend lower 
from November 2026 through February 2027, the council said in a statement 
 
"This should provide further support to palm oil prices in the final quarter of 2026," MPOC said 
 
It said the weakness in palm oil exports in September was likely to be temporary, with shipments projected to increase 
significantly in the October to December period 
 
India and China were more likely to rely more heavily on palm oil imports in coming months, following expected 
constraints in sunflower oil and soybean oil supplies, MPOC said 
 
Elevated energy prices remain a key supportive factor for the vegetable oil market, favouring increased biodiesel blending 
globally, particularly in Indonesia, it said 
 
Main downside risks include an easing in energy costs and weaker-than-expected global vegetable oil consumption in the 
food sector due to inflation and economic pressures, it added 
 
 
 
Indonesia's imports of nickel ore from Philippines to hit record in 2026 
Indonesia's 2026 imports of nickel ore from the Philippines are set to rise for a fourth year to a record high, traders and 
analysts said, as lower mining quotas of the raw material tightened local supply. 
 
The world's largest nickel producer has sharply slashed its nickel ore production quota this year, part of efforts to curb 
oversupply and bolster prices of the metal that is used in stainless steel and electric vehicle batteries. The move has left 
local nickel smelters scrambling to secure supply elsewhere to meet production needs. 
 
Total imports from the Philippines in 2026 are projected at between 20 million and 25 million metric tons, according to 
nine analysts and two traders, amounting to an annual jump of between 30.5% and 63.1%, according to Reuters 
calculations. 
 
Indonesia's imports of nickel ore from the Philippines in the first eight months of this year have already surged by 65.8% 
from a year earlier to 13.52 million tons, official data showed. 
 
"Indonesia's lower mining quota acted as the primary driving force behind its growing nickel ore imports," said Tom Fan, 
an analyst at consultancy Mysteel.

China's state iron ore buyer agrees new annual supply contract with Rio Tinto, sources say 
China's state iron ore buyer has agreed a new supply deal for the key steelmaking ingredient with Rio Tinto, people with 
knowledge of the matter said. 
 
China Mineral Resources Group (CMRG) verbally notified some steel mills of the agreement on Wednesday without 
giving further details, they said. 
 
CMRG will start on Thursday to collect information on volumes that each mill needs to buy from the miner, two of the 
people said. 
 
CMRG and Rio Tinto did not immediately respond to requests for comment. 
 
It was not immediately clear how much Rio Tinto iron ore would be allocated under the contract. 
 
Reuters reported in early August that CMRG directed some steel mills to halt negotiations with Rio Tinto for shipments 
from September. 
 
Beijing established CMRG in 2022 as part of China's efforts to strengthen its bargaining power with major miners by 
centralising iron ore procurement. The move was aimed at securing better terms for Chinese steelmakers, which have 
faced thin margins and weak domestic demand.

UK approves long-delayed Jackdaw gas field on energy security grounds 
Britain on Wednesday approved the Jackdaw North Sea gas field, ending years of legal and political wrangling as the 
Labour government seeks to balance its climate goals with energy security concerns. 
 
Energy minister Miatta Fahnbulleh said the project would begin supplying gas by the end of this month, helping ease 
energy costs that have surged during the Iran war. 
 
Higher energy prices have weighed on households and businesses, dragging on Britain's economy and prompting US 
President Donald Trump to repeatedly urge Prime Minister Andy Burnham to expand North Sea drilling. 
 
The government said Jackdaw would raise domestic gas production by about 6% at peak output and operate for up to 11 
years, calling it "a source of national strength" during a period of global instability. 
 
Jackdaw, at its planned peak production of 40,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day, would cover just under 4% of Britain's 
daily gas demand, according to Reuters calculations based on government data. 
 
"At a time when people are really struggling, it's right that we look in a responsible way at maximising the existing energy 
reserves we have in Britain that could help," Burnham said. 
 
 
NEW ENERGY WEALTH FUND 
 
Burnham, who took office in July, has sought to balance pressure to tackle rising bills with demands from some Labour 
lawmakers to reduce fossil fuel use. 
 
Trump said on social media the decision was "Great news for the wealth and prosperity of the United Kingdom, and the 
wonderful region of Aberdeen". 
 
Oil and gas production in the British North Sea goes back to the 1960s, generating tens of billions of pounds in tax 
revenue over several decades and was a pillar of Margaret Thatcher's government. Hydrocarbon production has been in 
steep decline in the new millennium. 
 
Alongside approving Jackdaw, the government announced a new Energy Wealth Fund with an initial £2 billion ($2.7 
billion) investment to channel oil and gas revenues into the transition to cleaner energy. 
 
The move aims to demonstrate that Britain remains committed to its climate targets despite backing a new gas project, 
following the country's hottest summer on record. 
 
The Green Party called the approval "climate vandalism".

Kinder Morgan to invest $4.7 billion in two gas pipeline projects as demand surges 
US pipeline operator Kinder Morgan said on Wednesday it has approved a total of $4.7 billion as its share of investments 
in two major natural gas pipeline joint ventures, citing rising demand from power plants, data centers and LNG exporters. 
 
The projects helped lift Kinder Morgan's natural-gas project backlog to a record level, underscoring the industry's efforts 
to expand infrastructure to serve the new demand. 
 
Shares of the company rose 2.7% in extended trading after it also beat estimates for quarterly profits. 
 
Kinder Morgan would invest about $3.1 billion in Southern Natural Gas' Southeast Connector project and $1.6 billion in 
Natural Gas Pipeline Company of America's (NGPL) Permian Link project. 
 
The Southeast Connector project would transport up to 2.6 billion cubic feet per day (Bcf/d) of natural gas through about 
470 miles of new pipeline to growing markets in the US Southeast. 
 
The Permian Link is designed to transport roughly 2.8 Bcf/d of natural gas from the Permian Basin near Pecos County, 
Texas, to NGPL's Midcontinent system through about 434 miles of new pipeline.

Brazil corn exports to Europe seen up eightfold in October, brokerage says 
Brazilian maize shipments to Europe are expected to reach 1.65 million metric tons in October, jumping from around 
200,000 tons in the same month last year, brokerage firm Stag International said on Wednesday. 
The roughly eightfold increase would come as Brazilian corn gains ground in the European market amid lower exports 
from Ukraine, while European countries grapple with a smaller crop, the brokerage said in a report. 
With the expected volume, Brazil is "effectively" replacing Ukraine, which is shipping less corn to European countries as 
its export infrastructure was hit by Russian attacks 
In addition, a drought has reduced the European Union's 2026 corn output forecast to its lowest level this century, to 
around 48 million tons, the European Commission said last month 
EU corn imports surged 40% in the 2026/27 crop year through mid-October, with the US as the main supplier, followed by 
Brazil, according to data from the Commission on Tuesday 
But Stag now sees the US as less competitive than its South American rivals, mainly Argentina and Brazil; both countries 
harvested record corn crops in 2026 
"Current FOB premiums suggest South America will retain its competitive advantage through December and January, 
potentially extending into February," the report said 
 
Egyptian state buyer purchased about five wheat shipments this week, traders say 
 Egypt's state grain buyer Future of Egypt has purchased about five cargoes of wheat this week in one of its first known 
imports in months, six trading sources told Reuters, as disruptions to Black Sea exports tighten supplies. 
The wheat was purchased through private negotiations, rather than a tender. The import origin of the cargoes is still 
unclear, but four traders said the cargoes are expected to be sourced from Europe, including France and Romania. 
The purchase marks a major return to the market for Egypt's state buyer, which has largely stayed on the sidelines in 
recent months amid supply disruptions caused by the conflict between Russia and Ukraine.
