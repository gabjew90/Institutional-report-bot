 
 
 
Alger On The Money
Borrowing to Build  
The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield crossed 5% in September, reaching levels not seen since 2007. Yet higher 
inflation expectations account for relatively little of this year’s increase. Beneath the headline yield sits another 
important consideration: the enormous sum of capital required for the artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure 
buildout. Understanding that demand for capital may matter more to investors than the next inflation report.  
 
 
 
Year-to-Date Breakdown of the U.S. 10-Year Treasury Yield 
      
 
 
• 
Every few generations, a new technology demands that the economy rebuild its physical foundations. 
In the 19th century it was the railroads, whose construction absorbed as much as 4.3% of U.S. 
economic output at its peak.1 Today it is AI, and the data centers, chips and power infrastructure behind 
it could require more than $8 trillion of investment in the U.S. alone over the next five years, with annual 
investment potentially reaching 7% of GDP by 2030 and surpassing the railroads at their height.2 
Corporate cash flow is unlikely to cover that bill, so a growing share may need to be financed 
externally, putting AI’s builders in competition with other businesses and the U.S. Treasury for the same 
pool of savings.  
• 
A Treasury yield has two components, the inflation investors expect and a “real,” or inflation-adjusted, 
return. This year the 10-year yield increased from 4.2% to 5.3% through September 2026, and while 
inflation expectations have barely moved, real rates have climbed 100 basis points, as shown in the 
chart above. The rise in yields, in other words, reflects a higher real rate rather than greater inflation 
protection. As competition for capital has increased, driven by the AI buildout, heavy Treasury 
issuance, renewed Federal Reserve tightening, and higher non-U.S. interest rates, investors are 
requiring a higher return on their investments. 
• 
While several factors may be contributing to higher real rates, we believe the AI buildout represents an 
important source of demand for capital. In our view, companies across the AI supply chain, from 
semiconductors to power generation, may be well positioned for long-term growth, as projected 
spending on this buildout translates into revenue for the businesses laying its track.
