CIO View: High yield
Frederick Mellors, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
Antoine Geiller, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
Carolina Corvalan, CFA, Strategist, UBS Switzerland AG
Tom Nash, Strategist, UBS AG
• We hold an Attractive view on high yield (HY). US and EUR HY yields 
are elevated at 8.35% and 6.7%, respectively.
• Despite the recent widening in spreads driven by policy uncertainty 
and AI-related funding concerns, aggregate credit fundamentals 
remain broadly intact. Strong corporate balance sheets, supportive 
technicals, and low default expectations should help contain spread 
widening at the index level, particularly among higher-rated issuers.
• Total returns are likely to be in the high-single-digit range over the 
next 12 months, supported by elevated yields and low default activity. 
Given our view that the market is likely overestimating the path for 
policy rates, we see price upside from lower rates.
• Absolute view: Attractive; Relative preference (in comparison to other 
fixed income segments): Neutral
Central scenario
In September, the high yield (HY) market started to feel the squeeze from 
higher rates as all-in yields rose by more than 100bps over the month with 
spreads widening by around 50bps, now close to 330bps for both US and 
EUR. As a result, the US HY index returned -2.49%, while the EUR HY 
index returned -1.79%, marking the weakest monthly performance since 
September 2022. Lower-rated credits faced the greatest pressure, with 
CCC spreads widening sharply to more than 1,000bps as higher financing 
costs weighed on issuers with limited debt-servicing capacity. However, the 
weakness in the HY market has not been confined to CCCs. BB and B 
spreads widened by 43bps and 55bps, respectively, over the past month, 
indicating broader market pressure. Elevated rate volatility, uncertainty 
around energy prices, and concerns over AI-related funding requirements 
could continue to weigh on spreads in the near term, particularly given 
valuations remain tight by historical standards. Nevertheless, aggregate 
credit fundamentals remain broadly intact, which should help limit spread 
widening at the index level, especially among higher-rated issuers. Looking 
ahead, we see the asset class's high current yield and the low duration as 
its key value proposition and a solid anchor for total returns, with possible 
upside if government yields decline.

issuers. Concerns over funding costs and elevated capital expenditure 
weighed on both AI-related credit and equities. As a bellwether for AI

Bonds sold off sharply, with yields on select companies rising to over 16%. 
Weakness was not limited to neocloud players, other HY issuers linked 
to the AI ecosystem also experienced spread widening. With the earnings 
season now underway and consensus expecting S&P 500 earnings growth 
of 26.5% this quarter, investors are likely to focus closely on commentary 
from AI-related companies, particularly regarding the trajectory of capital 
expenditure, cloud demand, and expected returns on AI investments. 
Looking ahead, the key question for markets is to what extent these 
companies will continue to rely on debt financing. In theory, reduced 
bond supply should support bond valuations, particularly among higher-
quality issuers. However, the implications depend on the underlying driver. 
If the slowdown in financing reflects improved balance sheet conditions 
and reduced funding requirements, it would likely be supportive for credit 
markets. Conversely, if weaker financing activity is driven by declining 
confidence in the cash flows underpinning debt obligations, the outlook 
for credit market performance could deteriorate, creating further downside 
risk.
In terms of asset class specifics, default activity has remained low—at 
1.7% in US HY and 1.0% in Euro HY at the end of September—and we 
expect roughly 2% over the next 12 months in both regions (trailing 12m 
par-weighted default rate). HY fundamentals remain solid overall. Balance 
sheets are relatively conservative with limited post-pandemic releveraging. 
Bank of America data shows US HY leverage around 4.3x (slightly 
below the long-term average) and interest coverage near 4.3x (above 
average). Many issuers have already refinanced near-term maturities, with 
refinancing representing ~63% of US HY issuance last year and over 50% 
this year, keeping refinancing risk contained, while CCC issuance remains 
subdued. The distress ratio, a market-based measure of default risks, has 
slightly increased in the recent months but remains low in the US and 
EUR HY markets at 5.4% and 4.1%, respectively (as of end September). 
Quality has improved: BB bonds are now around 60% of the index (while 
CCC bonds have declined to 8%), and secured bonds are at a record 
of around a third of total outstanding. Ratings actions remained skewed 
toward upgrades in the last three months on average in the US. In EUR HY, 
the market remains tilted toward BBs, which comprise around 70% of the 
market, while CCC exposure is low at 4%.
While we expect overall defaults at the index level to remain low, we 
anticipate rising spread dispersion within the lower-rated segments. We 
believe this justifies an active and selective approach. In terms of upcoming 
bond maturities, around USD 201bn—or 10% of the US HY market—will 
mature by end 2028. In our view, the near-term maturity wall remains 
manageable, and the most difficult refinancing threshold is concentrated 
in CCC rated bonds rather than spread evenly across the market. BB and 
B issuers should generally retain market access, albeit at a higher cost that 
will weigh on free cash flow.
Looking ahead, spreads could temporarily overshoot our target levels. 
However, our base case remains unchanged and assumes a benign 
macroeconomic backdrop, with rates gradually declining and no material 
deterioration in growth. While uncertainty around AI-related investment 
and financing needs may continue to weigh on select segments of the 
credit market, AI-linked data center issuers now represent only around 3% 
of the US HY market. In our view, the combination of elevated yields, 
low duration, and limited default risk leaves HY bonds well positioned to 
generate high-single-digit total returns over the next 12 months. Under

Investment boom
A swift resolution to the conflict in the Middle East would allow oil prices 
to stabilize below USD 90/bbl. Disinflation resumes in the US once the 
tariff and energy impacts have been passed through. Investment spending 
and AI adoption exceed expectations. Healthy consumption, buoyant 
investment, and strong productivity growth allow the US economy to grow 
by 2.5-3% in 2026 and 2027. Residual negative tariff effects are offset by 
business-friendly policies in the US, Europe, and China.
Downside scenario
Yield pressure: ICE BofA US high yield spread June 2027 target: 
330bps/ICE BofA Euro high yield spread June 2027 target: 310bps
Oil prices remain under upward pressure due to prolonged disruptions of 
flows through the Strait of Hormuz or widespread destruction of energy 
infrastructure in the Middle East. Business investment and hiring remain 
strong in a concentrated number of economic sectors. Concerns rise about 
longer-term inflation and the credibility of monetary and fiscal policy. 
Longer-term bond yields drift higher. Central banks face pressure to raise 
interest rates despite pockets of weakness in the economy. Labor market 
softness leads to further wage growth slowing.
AI bear market: ICE BofA US high yield spread June 2027 target: 
650bps/ICE BofA Euro high yield spread June 2027 target: 630bps
AI stocks deflate due to growing concerns over low returns on investment 
and unsustainable commitments by large AI companies. Worsening 
investor sentiment leads to a broader market decline. The downward trend 
in equity markets feeds through to the real economy via the sentiment 
and wealth channels. Declining investment in AI infrastructure allows for a 
redistribution of resources into other segments of the economy, allowing 
US GDP to grow by 0.5-1% in 2026 and 2027. Inflation concerns give way 
to fears of a global economic decline, allowing central banks to cut rates 
aggressively in 2027.

Global asset class preferences definitions
The asset class preferences provide high-level guidance to make investment decisions. The preferences reflect the 
collective judgement of the members of the House View meeting, primarily based on assessments of expected total 
returns on liquid and commonly known indices, House View scenarios, and analyst convictions over the next 12 months.
Absolute view
Attractive: We consider this asset class to be attractive. Consider opportunities in this asset class.
Neutral: We do not expect outsized returns or losses. Hold longer-term exposure.
Unattractive: We consider this asset class to be unattractive. Consider alternative opportunities.
Note: For equities, we have a five-tier rating system with two additional preferences. When equities are included with the 
other asset classes in the three-tier rating system, we collapse “Most Attractive” with “Attractive” and “Least Attractive” 
with “Unattractive.”
Most Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the most attractive. Investors should seek opportunities to add 
exposure.
Least Attractive: We consider this asset class to be among the least attractive. Seek more favorable alternatives 
opportunities.
Relative preferences
For the multi asset view, we also include relative preferences to help investors compare between items.
Preferred: We view this item more favorably than others within its asset class.*
Neutral: We see this item similarly to others within its asset class.*
Least preferred: We view this item less favorably than others within its asset class.*
*In the case of cross-asset, compared to other asset classes.
Note: The tactical asset allocation (TAA) positioning of our different investment strategies may differ from these views due 
to factors including portfolio construction, concentration, and borrowing constraints.
