GS Consumer | Morning Trading Update (US)
Global equities are lower (SPX futures -1% // NDX -1.6% // RTY -75bps) as geopolitical concerns
and mixed US tech earnings weigh. Focus is wavering AI optimism and spend: META (-1.7% pre mkt)
was largely solid but capex needs and Muse monetization timing linger, LRCX (-7%) guided
underwhelmingly, pressuring semis (SMH -3%), and TSLA (-4%) disappointed on its capex
guidance cut. Macro: NYT reported the US and Israel are drawing up plans for a short, intensive
operation after midterms (WTI +2% @ $99.30). UST are bid (10yr -5.5bps @ 5.06%) and France's 10yr
yield is above 5%. Link.   
   
Beyond TSLA, earnings focus in consumer is on the strong PG (+3%) quarter which should be enough
to keep stock higher post recent run; TSCO (-4%) quarter was mixed and stock has become a favored
name of late. LVS (-3%) results surprised a bit given Singapore losing momentum while housing
results came in no worse than feared/usual with POOL (+4%) and PHM (-1%). COCO and WH also
reported while KSS (+1%) was upgraded and BROS (-2%) downgraded away.   
 
On research front here, Bonnie reiterates buy rating on PM with a $222 PT post earnings yesterday
(Link). Brooke also hosted meeting with NKE management discussing the $2.5bn PACE savings
opportunity and resetting China and Jordan and its approach to the multi-year outlook ahead of
Investor Day (Link). Jon also previews BOOT quarter tweaking comp and noting early 3Q reads
expected (link).   
   
 

 
   
Megacap  
TSLA -3%... EPS beat on better margins though revs missed slightly and capex lowered…which
is weighing on AI complex broadly. Stock was up +9% wtd heading into print so bar was a bit higher.
EPS was $0.48 (vs $0.45 cons) and revenue was $28.05B (vs $28.06B cons). Gross margin was
19.6% (vs 17.6% cons). Automotive revs beat ($20.5B vs. $19.7B cons) but energy revenue was
weaker at $2.88B (vs $3.93B cons). FCF was $1.53B on capex of $4.73B (vs $7.90B cons). 2026
capex is now expected to be in excess of $20B (lower vs $25B prior guidance). Autonomy was a
positive, with FSD on >75% of new North America deliveries and 3.1M cumulative unsupervised
robotaxi miles. SpaceX and Tesla also formed special committees to discuss a potential combination
as highlighted in press already yesterday…  
 
…Mark/GIR maintained neutral rating on stock (link): “We continue to believe that Tesla can benefit
over time from its opportunities in several large markets, including autonomy, robotics, EVs and energy.
However, over the near to medium term, we expect profitability and FCF to remain somewhat
pressured from higher opex and capex. In addition, while we expect improved growth from these areas
to support an attractive long-term EPS CAGR, we also believe the magnitude and pace of that EPS
improvement will be somewhat limited by competition (e.g. Tesla is facing several competitors in AVs,
EVs, Energy and robotics). Finally, we see valuation as full.” 
 
Staples  
PG +3%...beat across the board. They were expected to have a good print, or at least much better
than last quarter, and they got just that (top-line and cost beat). It was an across segment beat. Top-
line is moving in the right direction here, which was expected, but not this magnitude. Despite the rally
into the print (helped by the defensive rotation in the market), this should be well received. Details: 1Q

EPS of $2.00 vs Consensus $1.89 on organic of +4% vs Consensus +2%. We think the bogey had
drifted up to +3%, but certainly was not at the +4% they printed. By division, upside in every single
segment. Beauty was strong at +5.4% vs Consensus +3.9%. Grooming +4.5% vs +0.7%, Healthcare
+2.7% vs +2.1%, Fabric/Home +4.8% vs +2.5% and Baby/Family +3% vs +0.7%. Despite the beat,
they are reaffirming the core FY EPS and organic revenue guide, given they are only 1 quarter into the
year. They are raising the commodity cost headwind to $1.4B form $1B last quarter, which was
generally expected.  
   
COCO…beat and raise though usually comes down to how high the bar is. EPS was $0.58 (vs
$0.47 cons), revenue was $244M (vs $230.1M cons), and adjusted EBITDA was $53M (vs $39.3M
cons), supported by 18% organic net sales growth and the acquisition of Copra. Gross margin landed
at 38%, benefiting from lower tariff impacts. For the full year, FY revenue was $810-$820M (vs
$802.7M cons and $790-$805M guidance) and FY adjusted EBITDA was $170-$175M (vs $163.0M
cons and $154-$161M guidance), while full-year gross margin was maintained at ~40%. CEO Martin
Roper noted, "Consumer demand is very healthy, and we are therefore raising our full year Net Sales
and Adjusted EBITDA guidance." Executive Chairman Michael Kirban reiterated confidence in plans to
"maintain our growth into 2027, and our category leadership well into the future." Conf Call at 8:30am
ET.
 
Housing/Hardlines…  
TSCO -4%...A bit of a push/pull print here on the print. Comps came in “fine” and above a recently
lower 3P check that has caused a sell-off. However, it wasn’t as high as some initial hopes and they
missed on gross margins. Stock is down small (-3%) in the pre-mkt on the lack of EPS upside and that
initial move makes sense, given more ownership of the stock vs last quarter. Details: 3Q EPS of $0.43
vs Consensus $0.41, with total revenues ~2% better and SSS of +0.4% vs Consensus -0.6%. There
was a lot of debate around the SSS this quarter, with bulls looking for +1%, until recently, when a 3P
check forecasted a -1%. They sort of split the difference here, with the +0.4% neither as high as some
elevated expectations but above the check from last week that drove the stock lower. Gross margins
missed by 50 bps, with SG&A making up for it and driving a 20 bps operating margin beat. With 1
quarter to go, they are reaffirming the guide (EPS, revenues, operating margins and SSS) across the
board.  
   
PHM -1%...EPS and revenue missed with closings and orders light, though gross margin held in
line with consensus. EPS was $2.53 (vs $2.65 cons) and revenue was $3.97B (vs $4.15B cons).
Closings were 6,876 (vs 7,217 cons and 7,000-7,400 guidance) at an ASP of $561K (vs $554.7K cons
and $550-560K guidance). Gross margin was 24.8% (vs 24.9% cons and 24.5-25.0% guidance). Net
new orders were 6,208 (vs 6,664 cons) and backlog was 10,298 (vs 10,459 cons). Homebuilding pre-
tax income was $579.6M (vs $640.8M cons). The company repurchased $350M of stock. CEO: "The
rapid increase in interest rates has stretched affordability even further...and has pushed even more
consumers to the sidelines." Conf Call at 8:30am  
   
POOL +4%... top and bottom line beat with sales growth accelerating and FY guidance
reaffirmed. Details: EPS was $3.40 (vs $3.36 cons) and revenue was $1.50B (vs $1.48B cons). Sales

growth accelerated to 4% from 2% in 2Q on 3% price plus stronger maintenance and building
materials. Adjusted EBITDA was $190.5M (vs $191.7M cons), slightly light as gross margin fell 60 bps
to 29.0% on freight and customer mix, and SG&A grew 5%. Positives include inventory down 3% while
sales grew and 9-month operating cash flow of $419.8M. FY adjusted EPS guidance was reaffirmed at
$10.87-11.17 (vs $10.97 cons). CEO: "Our unmatched network, solid operational performance and
strong supplier relationships position us well to continue delivering exceptional value to our customers
and the outdoor living industry."
 
Travel/Gaming  
LVS -2%...on slowing momentum in Singapore which is the main surprise on quarter. Revenue
was $3.22B (vs $3.33B cons) and property EBITDA was $1.12B (vs $1.22B cons), as softer high-end
play across Asian gaming hubs pressured overall results. Marina Bay Sands property EBITDA was
$590M (vs $686.9M cons), representing the primary surprise, while Macao property EBITDA was
$529M (vs $551.0M cons). Capital returns remained robust, highlighted by $950M in quarterly share
repurchases and a 20% dividend increase for 2027 to $1.44 per share annually. CEO Patrick Dumont
stated, "We remain confident in our long-term strategy in both Singapore and Macao and will continue
to invest in future growth." From Lizzie/GIR recap (link): “While valuation looks somewhat attractive at
these levels, we continue to see the setup for LVS as challenging over the near term, at least until
estimates have found a definitive floor, and until the company navigates its company-specific execution
issues. While we continue to like LVS and maintain our Buy rating, we acknowledge the stock may be
range bound over the near term pending further proof points and lower our 12-month price target to
$41.”  
   
WH…solid quarter with strong US RevPAR but room growth outlook cut to 4%. US RevPAR EPS
was $1.43 (vs $1.32 cons), revenue was $410M (vs $409.0M cons), and adjusted EBITDA was $207M
(vs $199.3M cons), driven by solid U.S. RevPAR outperformance of 6%. FY guidance for full year is for
EPS of $4.84-$4.92 (vs $4.80 cons and $4.71-$4.83 guidance), EBITDA of $740-$745M (vs $741.8M
cons and $735-$745M guidance) and revs of $1.48-$1.50B (vs $1.48B cons and $1.48-$1.50B
guidance). RevPar guidance for FY by 25bps at midpoint but rooms growth lowered to ~4% (vs
4.0%-4.5% guidance). CEO Geoff Ballotti highlighted, "Our strong balance sheet and cash flow
generation capabilities provide significant opportunities to continue enhancing returns to our
shareholders over both the short and long-term," supported by an expanded $400M buyback
authorization. Solid operational flow-through is encouraging, though sequential domestic room attrition
remains a key investor debate. Conf Call at 8:30am ET. Recap from Lizzie/GIR (Link).

GS Research

NKE: Takeaways from management meeting (Brooke Roach): Brooke attended a sell-side meet and
greet with NKE's management team in New York City on October 21st, with participants including Dave
Denton (EVP & CFO) and two members of Investor Relations. The event highlighted (1) the $2.5bn
PACE savings opportunity and areas of focus, (2) continued efforts to reset China and Jordan, and (3)
NKE's approach to its multi-year outlook ahead of Investor Day. Link
 
PM: Strong Q3 results but FY26 guidance lowered due to higher costs but still implying
impressive growth - Reiterate Buy (Bonnie Herzog): Bonnie reiterates Buy on PM following a strong
Q3 with revenue growth of 5.5%, operating income growth of 9.6% and EPS growth of 2.6% ($2.36 vs.
consensus of $2.30), driven by better-than-expected strength in combustible cigs and ZYN.
Management pointed toward the middle/upper half of its FY26 topline growth guidance range but the
middle/lower half of its FY26 op income and EPS growth guidance, due to increased input cost inflation
(particularly energy) related to the conflict in the Middle East. Bonnie was surprised the stock did not
trade down more on the lowered guidance, which suggests support at these levels, and she views the
cost headwinds as likely transitory. She sees several near-term positive catalysts: further ZYN
innovation in the US and internationally, the approval/launch of ILUMA in the US, reauthorization of the
buyback program, and the launch of a next-generation IQOS in FY27, which she expects to be the
focus of the company's Q2 analyst day. She lowers her FY26-FY28 EPS estimates but maintains her
12-month price target of $222, and calls PM one of the best growth stories across broader Staples and
one of her top stock ideas. Link
 
HRMS: First Take: 3Q26 sales miss slightly on Asia (Erwan Rambourg): Erwan says Hermès
reported 3Q26 cFX sales growth of +4.3% (vs. consensus +5.2%), with the key positive being growth
in the largest division, Leather Goods (+10.8%), and strength in the Americas, Europe and Japan
offsetting weakness in Asia ex-Japan and France. Ready-to-wear was down 2% in the quarter, as were
other Hermès sectors and the small watches division. Erwan sees the outperformance as
demonstrating the strength of the high-end consumer and market share gains, and expects investor
focus on (1) Asia ex-Japan cyclicality, with sales flat at cFX (vs. +2.5% in 2Q), (2) the duration of strong
growth in the Americas (+8.8%) and Japan (+9.6%), with Europe excluding France surprisingly resilient
at +9.5%, and (3) the performance of non-volume-constrained divisions as Leather Goods and
Saddlery continue to outperform. Link
 
BOOT: 2Q27 Preview: Tweaking comp; early 3Q reads as expected (Jon Keypour): Jon trims his
quarterly comp by -50bps to +1.8% YoY but maintains his EPS of $1.63 following BOOT's September
business update, corroborated by credit card and foot traffic data. 3QTD trends indicate moderation
continued into the first two weeks of the quarter, but he expects improvement in November and
December on easier year-ago laps, with potential benefit from wetter weather. Online traffic trends
continue to strengthen, with accelerating double-digit growth across Boot Barn and exclusive brand
dotcom sites, and increased online discounting returns BOOT to its historic range of +LSD to +MSD,
normalizing to flat to slightly down into 3Q. Link
