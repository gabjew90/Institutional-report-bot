Our View 
⧫ 
On Oct 6, Google entered into its biggest power deal (~3.6 GW) with 
CEG. 
⧫ 
Google will cover the cost of nuclear power plants’ capacity expansion, 
making it the first tech giant to participate in grid co-construction. 
⧫ 
Given electricity has become the Achilles’ heel for AI development, we 
see a clear uptrend for IPPs and off-gid power supply. 
 
Google inked its biggest ever power deal with Constellation Energy; potential 
revaluation of existing nuclear power assets 
On Oct 6, Google (GOOG.US) and Constellation Energy (CEG.US) announced a 
huge 3,590 MW power deal to expand total electricity generation available 
across the PJM footprint. Under the latest arrangement, Google entered into 
a 20-year power purchase agreement for 890 MW of electricity from 11 
Constellation-owned nuclear power plants, which will be upgraded to 
produce more electricity from existing reactors. Google has agreed to invest 
over US$4.3 bn to upgrade nuclear power plants, and electricity from the 
first of the upgraded plants is expected to be delivered in 2028F. Google 
has also entered into a 15-year supply agreement for the remaining 2,700 
MW of power from Constellation in the PJM power grid. We see two key 
takeaways from this massive deal: 
1) 
The first BYOP (Bring Your Own Power) electricity contract mechanism 
across the PJM footprint may later become a template for power supply 
contracts for datacenters in the PJM market. The PJM grid occupies the 
largest datacenter market (known as “Data Center Alley”) in North 
America. Previously, due to concerns over datacenters consuming 
excessive electricity from residential users, local authorities halted the 
construction of most datacenters. Google’s investment in expanding 
power supply will alleviate regulators’ concerns. 
2) 
Revaluation of existing nuclear power assets and kickoff of a nuclear 
power expansion wave: 
a) 
Effect of other tech giants to follow suit: Previously, the 
agreements between Microsoft and CEG (to restart the Three Mile 
Island nuclear power plant), as well as between Amazon and Talen 
(to lock in power sources from the Susquehanna Steam Electric 
Station), were moves to squeeze the existing available power 
supply. In contrast, Google's recent multi-reactor capacity 
expansion partnership with CEG will ease regulators’ concerns over 
tech giants siphoning off existing power resources, and other tech 
giants are likely to follow suit in the future. (Continued on Page 2)

b) 
Coexistence of BTM (Behind the Meter) and BFM (Branch Feeder Monitor) power supply: 
Previously, the BTM nuclear power purchase agreement between Amazon and Talen 
caused a regulatory lawsuit dispute over the occupation of transmission pipelines. Talen 
mentioned in its 2Q26 earnings call that it would shift power supply focus to BTM-BFM 
hybrid power supply, with a likely trend of “grid co-construction” between tech giants 
and power companies going forward. 
c) 
Capacity expansion of existing nuclear power plants and revaluation of power 
companies: Power supply has become a bottleneck in datacenters’ construction. Due to 
long construction cycles and substantial budget overruns, building new nuclear power 
plants is not feasible in the short term. The capacity expansion approach based on 
existing nuclear power plants breaks through the bottlenecks of long construction cycles 
and massive budget overruns. Thus, IPPs with retrofittable nuclear power assets should 
see asset revaluation. 
Time to focus on the electrical equipment sector amid a restart of US electric power construction 
The key to power supply for American datacenters is off-grid power supply and IPPs. Investment 
opportunities for small gas turbines lie in rental service providers and production-related supply 
chains. We suggest focusing on Solaris (SEI.US), an American small gas turbine leasing company, 
together with Jereh Group (002353 CH; NR), a company engaged in aircraft engine derived gas 
turbines for power generation, and FTAI (FTAI.US). For IPPs, attention should be paid to Talen 
Energy (TLN.US) and Vistra Group (VST.US), two names benefitting from the US power grid reform. 
For nuclear power construction, Solstice (SOLS.US), the only nuclear fuel conversion facility in the 
US, merits attention.
