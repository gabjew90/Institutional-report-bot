01
 
Highlights 
• Prediction markets are pricing in a high chance of Democrats regaining at least some control of Congress 
• Regardless of the midterm results, historically, the gold price has reacted to the economic, fiscal and 
monetary policies that ensue, rather than to the outcome of election day itself  
• But in the current heightened geoeconomic environment, the market could be more reactive than usual 
• Overall, changes to AI regulation and war funding, as well as wider use of executive power by the Trump 
administration if Democrats gain control, will likely provide support for gold, even if some headwinds 
prevail 
• An upset by Republicans may have mixed effects but will likely strengthen the resolve of global investors – 
especially those outside the US – to continue supporting gold demand. 
Same story, new story? 
We hear a recurring question from investors: do US elections meaningfully impact the gold price? The simple answer is 
that while domestic bar and coin demand tends to increase under Democratic administrations,1 historically, the outcome 
of US elections has not had a consistent or significant effect on gold’s performance. This is not surprising. Given the 
geographic diversity of demand, what matters is whether the resulting policy changes are sufficiently consequential for 
the global gold market, and whether they can offset other global forces that drive the price. 
The market reaction to Trump’s victories in 2016 and 2024 illustrates this point. Gold initially fell and US equities rose as 
investors anticipated a pro-growth, reflationary agenda. Yet much of the initial move in gold, and to a lesser extent 
equities, was subsequently unwound as attention shifted from the election result to the policies likely to follow (Chart 1).

As we approach the 2026 US midterm elections, prediction markets currently favour Democratic control of both 
chambers (Chart 2). Democratic control of the House would likely constrain the administration’s legislative agenda and 
spending plans. Taking the Senate would broaden and strengthen that constraint. The implications for gold would 
depend on how this alters fiscal policy, market uncertainty and the use of executive power. 
We believe that, holding all else equal, gridlock could have varying consequences on US policy, which, in turn, influence 
gold’s performance in various ways: 
• 
Fiscal policy: Democratic control could limit the administration’s ability to pass deficit-expanding legislation; this 
could ease concerns that large deficits at near-full employment will sustain inflation and reignite Treasuries’ safe-
haven appeal on, and support for, the US dollar. And if tariffs come under pressure, that could also increase the 
deficit. A contracting deficit could put pressure on gold but an expanding one would further support it. 
• 
War funding: Democratic control could limit war funding, potentially encouraging de-escalation of the Iran conflict. 
In turn, this could ease energy prices, reduce inflation expectations, and soften bond yields and the dollar, providing 
some tailwinds for gold. De-escalation of the conflict could also reduce the perceived need for some central banks 
and sovereign wealth funds to mobilise their gold, as occurred in March 2026. On balance, we believe this could 
generally be supportive of gold, even though its geopolitical premium might subside. 
• 
AI: Democratic control could constrain support for AI or bring tighter regulation. Against a backdrop in which 
investors are questioning whether hyperscale spending will deliver promised returns, pressure on technology 
equities could deepen while confidence and spending weaken due to negative wealth effects. Stronger household 
balance sheets may help contain financial distress, although greater equity exposure leaves consumption vulnerable 
to falling valuations. A wealth-driven slowdown would possibly offer a more supportive backdrop for gold, with less 
pressure to sell liquid assets to raise cash. Weaker equities, softer growth expectations, lower yields, and potentially 
a weaker dollar, would likely support gold. 
• 
Executive and emergency powers: Greater Congressional constraints could push the Trump administration towards 
wider use of executive orders and emergency powers. The resulting institutional conflict, legal challenges and less 
predictable policymaking could raise both political and geopolitical risk premia, potentially supporting gold. Equally, 
recurring conflict between Congress and the administration could make US foreign policy even less predictable, 
reinforcing geopolitical risk and longer-term incentives for reserve diversification into gold. 
But…what would happen if Republicans were to exceed expectations and maintain control across the board? We can 
only wait to see and analyse the nuances should this be the result. Overall, we would expect gold investors, especially 
those outside the US, to continue supporting gold demand as they have done over the past 18 months.
