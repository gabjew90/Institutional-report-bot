We estimate Petrobras’ realized diesel price (including subsidies from the 
n

asymmetry currently favoring imported diesel over domestically refined volumes. 
If this asymmetry were to end after the elections (BRL 1.40/liter additional 
subsidies to diesel importers should be valid until mid-November), we estimate 
PBR would have to raise diesel prices to distributors by ~50% (~BRL 1.5/liter) to 
match international parity, with upside risk if management also seeks to 
compensate for the time PBR’s diesel spent below parity so far this year. Given 
our limited visibility on whether this additional incentive to diesel importers 
will expire or be renewed by the government, we assume PBR would 
maintain diesel prices unchanged until the end of the year, and acknowledge 
that a price hike could bring upside risks to our refining margins for 4Q.

Impact in the macro landscape. The inflation backdrop is already challenging, 
n
and higher diesel prices would add further pressure. Diesel is primarily an 
intermediate good and carries only a 0.26% weight in the IPCA, compared with 
0.60% for gasoline and 0.26% for ethanol. Assuming a roughly 50% increase in 
Petrobras’ diesel price to distributors raises pump prices by about 20%, all else 
equal, we estimate the direct impact on the IPCA would be 5–6bp. However, 
because diesel is a key input in producing and distributing goods and services, 
higher supply-chain and freight costs could add a more significant indirect 
impact of roughly 16–18bp. Hence, the total impact on IPCA of a 20% diesel 
price increase at the pump is estimated at 21-24bp.

Since the escalation of the conflict in the Middle East in March, Brazilian Federal 
n
government has been granting subsidies to local diesel producers and importers, 
aiming to dampen the effect of higher international oil prices to local diesel prices, 
reimbursing Petrobras and other diesel producers/importers for a discount in their 
sales price vs. international reference. However, our analysis suggests the current 
subsidy of BRL 2.12/liter to local diesel producers has not been sufficient to 
compensate for the gap between local diesel prices and international benchmarks. 
For reference, we currently see Petrobras diesel price of BRL 5.43/liter (BRL 
3.31/liter charged to fuel distributors + BRL 2.12/liter subsidy granted by the 
Federal Government) 21% below international reference (BRL 6.89/liter). 
Per PBR’s bylaws, if the company conducts business practices that would differ from 
n
any other a private peer would follow (e.g., consistently selling diesel prices below 
international reference), this should be supported by a law or regulation and 
Petrobras should be reimbursed by the federal government (e.g., through 
subsidies/payments from the government to compensate the company for the 
discount in local fuel prices vs. import parity). While government has recently 
increased subsidies to fuel importers by BRL 1.40/liter (increasing incentive for 
diesel importers up to BRL 3.52/liter), subsidies to local diesel producers remained 
unchanged at BRL 2.12/liter. As such, given PBR’s current governance in place (and 
limited indications for an increase in subsidies to local refineries), we believe it 
could be plausible to expect a hike in PBR’s diesel price in the near term, 
especially when considering the discount in PBR’s diesel price vs. import parity 
earlier this year.  
 
We also believe any price readjustment would likely take place after the 2nd 
n
round of the presidential elections. Brazil will go through the second round of 
presidential elections on October 25th. While we take no view on the outcome of the 
runoff, we believe Petrobras would likely wait for the outcome of the election prior 
to making any decision on a potential readjustment in fuel prices (given political

However, the asymmetry between subsidies for imports and local diesel 
n
producers could reduce scope for a price hike in PBR’s diesel price. As previously 
noted, Brazil Federal Government has recently increased subsidies to diesel 
importers, up to BRL 3.52/liter (from BRL 2.12/liter previously). On the other hand, 
subsidies to local diesel producers (including Petrobras) remained unchanged at BRL 
2.12/liter. This has created an asymmetry between prices of the domestic alternative 
and of the importing option, favoring importers and reducing room for a potential 
price hike in Petrobras local diesel price. 
As of today, PBR diesel price (charged to fuel distributors) stands at BRL 3.31/liter on 
n
average. Although international benchmark currently remains closer to BRL 
6.90/liter, government subsidy of BRL 3.52/liter to diesel importers brings the price 
of the importing option (perceived by fuel distributors) closer to PBR’s local diesel 
price at the refinery gate - even though Petrobras’ realized diesel price (BRL 5.4/liter, 
grossed up by subsidies of BRL 2.12/liter granted by the government) remains below 
international reference.  
 
For reference, assuming unchanged subsidies for local diesel producers, a scenario 
n
of a price hike would bring PBR’s refinery diesel price (charged to fuel distributors) 
up to BRL 4.77/liter. At the same time, fuel distributors could acquire imported 
diesel at ~BRL 3.5/liter (~BRL 7/liter import parity, deducted by BRL 3.52/liter 
subsidies to importers). This could translate into some pressure over PBR’s market 
share, an input likely taken into account in the company’s fuel price strategy, 
and reducing scope for a price adjustment. 
Given our limited visibility on whether this additional incentive to diesel 
n
importers will expire or be renewed by the government, we assume PBR would 
maintain diesel prices unchanged until the end of the year, and acknowledge 
that a price hike could bring upside risks to our refining margins for 4Q.

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics). 
For a more detailed description of how we calculate the GS Factor Profile, please contact your GS representative.
