Canadian producer prices probably increased…

Slight miss on stale NZ CPI didn’t affect RBNZ expectations

Philippines delivers mega hike, BoK holds, Turkey cut 
Markets are focused upon earnings as the mood continues to sour on tech stocks 
alongside digesting the implications of higher oil prices. Tesla beat expected EPS, but its 
stock is slipping in the pre-market after announced it would cut its capital budget. Meta is 
also selling off as investors question the return on a boost to cap-ex plans. Alphabet, 
Microsoft and Amazon are due out next week and will be followed by Apple the following 
week. Chart 1 shows updated cap-ex plans from Tesla and Meta with the rest pending.  
S&P futures are down 1% and Nasdaq futures are off by about 1¾%. TSX futures are down 
by over ½% and European cash markets are spilling red ink all over the main bourses. 
Sovereign bonds are performing unevenly, as US Treasury yields push about 8bps lower 
across most of the curve, gilts are little richer toward the longer-end, and bunds are 
rallying which makes the sell off in French bonds more painful. France’s spreads over 
Germany are about 18bps higher across the curve as French debt concerns intensify amid 
protests and ahead of France’s presidential election no later than April 18th 2027 with far-
right EU-sceptic Marine Le Pen on the rise. 
Oil prices are up by 2–3% across WTI and Brent as attacks on Saudi Arabia intensify and 
concerns about an intensification and broadening of the Iran war pick up. Canadian 
Western Select—a heavy crude proxy—remains elevated around US$66 which is well 
above pre-Iran war levels even as the discount to WTI widens. East Coast Canadian oil 
projects are reaping rich Brent prices as they sell into the northeastern US seaboard. CAD 
conversion into some local costs remains lucrative with a 1.40 loonie. 
CANADIAN PRODUCER PRICES TO SPIKE 
Canada will refresh producer prices for September and they are likely to leap higher 
(8:30amET). Key is the core industrial prices measure which has continued to point 
toward lagging upward pressures on core consumer prices via pass through effects 
(charts 2 and 3).

I was marketing to fixed income clients in Montreal yesterday. Some accounts were fully onside our market-leading Bank of Canada hike 
views, some were reconciled to begrudgingly go along with hikes, and only one account was not buying priced hikes.  
Markets continue to price about 85% of a hike next week and about half of another hike in December with about 100bps priced by next 
September.  
Bloomberg’s overly broad consensus survey (there aren’t 24 forecasters who seriously think 
about Canada!) remains stale including several primary dealers that seem to be either non-
responsive or stewing or delivering mealy-mouthed risk guidance. I can’t recall a time when 
three major domestic banks were so lagging in updating the calls for a decision that is pending 
in a matter of days from now.  
KIWI INFLATION HAD NO EFFECT ON RBNZ HIKE EXPECTATIONS 
New Zealand’s inflation remained high but slightly missed expectations. Q3 CPI was up 0.7% 
q/q (0.8% consensus) and 3.7% y/y (3.8% consensus, 4.1% prior). Chart 3 shows y/y rates. 
While the NZ$ softened overnight and kiwi yields moved slightly lower in bull flattener fashion, 
markets and local economists generally treated the figures as stale on arrival as oil prices are 
averaging higher in Q4 than Q3’s lull. Markets continue to have about 85% of a 25bps rate hike 
priced for next Wednesday and over half of another hike priced for December’s decision.  
MORE ASIAN CENTRAL BANK SURPRISES 
Asian central banks continued to deliver surprises overnight. After Bank Indonesia—the wild 
west of central banking—surprised by holding its reference rate unchanged yesterday, Bangko 
Sentral ng Pilipinas hiked its overnight rate by 50bps (25bps consensus). Bank of Korea held its 
base rate at 3% as widely expected. 
Other than Asan CBs, Turkey’s central bank cut its one-week repo rate by 100bps this morning 
as widely expected.  
The US calendar remains light with just weekly jobless claims due (8:30amET).
