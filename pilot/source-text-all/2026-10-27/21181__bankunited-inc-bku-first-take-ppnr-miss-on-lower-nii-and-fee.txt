BankUnited Inc. (BKU): First Take: PPNR miss on lower NII and fees; 
Guide points to 4Q PPNR downside
21 October 2026 | 8:40AM EDT
Bottom line on the fundamentals / stock: BKU reported mixed 3Q26 results as 
PPNR came in much lower than expected given the miss on NI and core fees while 
core expenses were a touch lower.  Provision came in lower than expected helping 
BKU meet bottom line expectations. Loan growth was slower than expected which 
led to AEA declining -1.5% QoQ (vs. expectations for up); however. Deposit costs 
outperformed, leading to a slight beat on its margin. Credit was mixed as while NCOs 
outperformed, NPAs rose 16% QoQ and BKU built reserves due to a mix of 
qualitative factors along with risk migration. In terms of guidance, BKU laid out 4Q 
expectations on most line items which pointed to ~3.6% PPNR downside in 4Q 
largely on lower NII and a touch lower fees. Additionally, BKU increased its exit NIM 
expectations to 3.20% (3.15% prior) despite the softer NIDDA growth expected and 
with the loan guide ahead of expectations, it seems like other parts of the balance 
sheet are likely to shrink into 4Q. We think the main focus on the call today will be on 
expectations for deposit costs (and what it means for NIM), expectations for NIDDA 
growth if the mortgage market remains slower, and what it is seeing within credit 
that drove the NPAs higher QoQ. So when we put it all together, this was a mixed 
quarter and guide and we think shares are likely to trade softer.  
Key questions for the call: 1) How are you thinking about the drivers of NIM 
expansion into 4Q including expectations for deposit costs?, 2) With the mortgage 
market remaining soft (impacting Title), do you expect to be able to continue to take 
share in NIDDA and can growth trend positively on a YoY basis into 2027? 3) With 
NPAs up QoQ, what are you seeing within the portfolio and how are you thinking 
about the potential for further migration into 4Q?, 4) Are there additional 
opportunities for securities sales and how do you expect to use the proceeds from 
3Qs sale? 
2026 Outlook: BKU laid out 4Q26 expectations pointing to NII of $265mn vs street 
at $268mn or ~1.2% below, as they expect a smaller balance sheet than the street 
(avg. NIDDA ~8.3% below cons., softer core loans) partially offset by a higher 4Q26 
exit NIM of ~3.20% vs. ~3.15% prior.  Additionally, the guide points to fees ($28mn 
vs. street at $29mn) and expenses ($178mn, in-line with street) largely consistent 
with consensus expectations and we think the updated guide points to -1.6% 
downside to 4Q street expectations for 4Q26 PPNR. Lastly, the $15mn 4Q provision 
guide is in-line with street expectations and BKU expects to use the remainder of its 
repurchase authorization in 4Q (~$49mn vs. street at ~$64mn).

GS Factor Profile 
The Goldman Sachs Factor Profile provides investment context for a stock by comparing key attributes to the market (i.e. our universe of rated stocks) 
and its sector peers. The four key attributes depicted are: Growth, Financial Returns, Multiple (e.g. valuation) and Integrated (a composite of Growth, 
Financial Returns and Multiple). Growth, Financial Returns and Multiple are calculated by using normalized ranks for specific metrics for each stock. The 
normalized ranks for the metrics are then averaged and converted into percentiles for the relevant attribute. The precise calculation of each metric may 
vary depending on the fiscal year, industry and region, but the standard approach is as follows: 
Growth is based on a stock’s forward-looking sales growth, EBITDA growth and EPS growth (for financial stocks, only EPS and sales growth), with a 
higher percentile indicating a higher growth company. Financial Returns is based on a stock’s forward-looking ROE, ROCE and CROCI (for financial 
stocks, only ROE), with a higher percentile indicating a company with higher financial returns. Multiple is based on a stock’s forward-looking P/E, P/B, 
price/dividend (P/D), EV/EBITDA, EV/FCF and EV/Debt Adjusted Cash Flow (DACF) (for financial stocks, only P/E, P/B and P/D), with a higher percentile 
indicating a stock trading at a higher multiple. The Integrated percentile is calculated as the average of the Growth percentile, Financial Returns 
percentile and (100% - Multiple percentile). 
Financial Returns and Multiple use the Goldman Sachs analyst forecasts at the fiscal year-end at least three quarters in the future. Growth uses inputs 
for the fiscal year at least seven quarters in the future compared with the year at least three quarters in the future (on a per-share basis for all metrics).
