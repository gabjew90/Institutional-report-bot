Why does it matter?
We are seeing signs of an inflection point. With more 
recently launched tokenised products triggering a 
surge in activity, the fledgling digital gold market has 
grown into a $45-$55 billion category. Tokenised gold 
generated $90 billion1 of cumulative spot trading 
volumes in the first quarter of 2026, more than in 
the whole of 2025. More importantly, wider financial 
markets are in the midst of a structural digital 
transformation. As a result, tokenised investments are 
increasingly mainstream.
For the purposes of this report, and to understand 
digital gold’s potential, we surveyed more than 12,000 
retail investors globally and interviewed 20 leading 
experts, helping us unpack the drivers of adoption and 
factors shaping demand. Our key finding:
The physical investment gold market is worth about $13 trillion. 
But digital gold, including vaulted digital gold and tokenised gold, 
represents less than 1% of the global market. 
Nearly 9 in 10 retail investors signal a willingness to invest in digital 
gold under the right conditions. Our modelling suggests that if 
stakeholders address the principal barriers to adoption, they can 
boost both participation and portfolio allocations, unlocking up to 
$700 billion in latent demand2. Fully realised, this would create a 
market that broadly matches physically backed gold ETFs today.
Appeal is strong   
but is not (yet) 
converting at scale.

Our survey has helped us quantify the impact of each barrier (Exhibit A). In aggregate, our calculations show 
that taking action to address all four of the key barriers (trust, access, utility and cost) could reasonably increase 
participation by 13 percentage points and allocations by 2.5 percentage points. Within trust, regulatory or similar 
protection is seen as the single most powerful lever for participation. Together, these effects account for the 
digital gold market’s total latent demand of up to $700 billion.

Having identified a total long-term opportunity of up to $700 billion, we set out three scenarios for the level of 
latent demand that could be converted into investment over the next five years (Exhibit B):
Our base case is that, as market participants address the four barriers, the digital gold market could 
grow at approximately 27% CAGR, reaching $160–$170 billion by 20313.
Base case. Stronger consumer protections, greater 
regulatory clarity and clear redemption pathways in 
leading markets foster trust, while wider bank and 
mainstream distribution expand access. Costs are 
competitive with alternative gold investments and 
new yield, collateral and payment use cases see 
limited adoption.
Bull case. Robust consumer protections, a 
harmonised global regulatory framework and 
transparent backing with clear physical redemption 
build trust across markets. Banks and scaled 
platforms provide broad access, costs fall below 
alternative gold investments and new use cases reach 
meaningful scale.
Bear case. Trust remains a challenge due to 
limited consumer protections, fragmented regulation 
and uncertainty over physical backing without a 
clear redemption path. Access through banks and 
mainstream channels is limited, costs remain higher 
than alternative gold investments and new use cases 
fail to reach meaningful scale.

Trust as the swing factor. Nearly four in 
five investors say their priority is to trust the 
provider before investing: when asked what would 
make them more open to digital gold, the most 
cited answer is evidence that the product is safe and 
legitimate. Another important trust lever is a clear 
and accessible path to redeem holdings for physical 
gold, which would give investors confidence that a real 
asset backs their position. Regulation and consumer 
protection, seen across investor segments as a key 
barrier to entry, will play an important role, too. 
2 
Access through familiar channels. 
Where digital gold is embedded in mainstream 
channels such as super-apps, payment platforms and 
banking apps, as seen in India, China and Indonesia, 
penetration is up to seven times higher than in 
markets where such distribution does not yet exist, 
awareness is more than double. For example, this 
has led to over 120 million customers in India and 5 
million in Indonesia having engaged in digital gold 
transactions. These channels provide access to large, 
established consumer bases and enable investment 
at much lower average ticket sizes than channels 
available in other markets.
3 
New utilities for gold. Unlocking latent 
demand partially hinges on what digital gold 
can be used for, not just how easy it is to buy. Yield 
generation, enabled by lending or use as collateral, is 
the top-cited unique selling point, ahead of being able 
to use it to make payments. Paxos Gold (PAXG) and 
Tether Gold (XAUT) are deployable as collateral on Aave, 
one of the largest relevant platforms that alone holds 
more than half of tokenised gold DeFi deposits. Paxos 
Labs’ newly launched gold-backed cryptocurrency token 
(PAXGy) goes a step further, building a yield directly into a 
PAXG-backed token through gold lending to institutional 
borrowers. This points to a potentially significant shift: 
product innovation could give investors new ways to use 
gold, alongside its long-standing role as a store of value. 
4 
Competitive pricing is a prerequisite. 
Even where other barriers are addressed, 
digital products must compete on price with 
alternative formats, including physical gold and 
gold-backed ETFs. While a low price is not, on its 
own, a major reason to invest in digital gold, an 
uncompetitive price point is ranked as the second 
most common barrier to entry.

Critical for near-term uptake is the activation  
of a younger audience. 
Awareness of digital gold reaches 50% among 25–34-year-olds, 
compared with just 23% among those aged 55 and older. More 
importantly, younger cohorts are twice as likely as older cohorts to 
say they would invest if barriers to entry were addressed (Exhibit D).
In conclusion, the next five years will be crucial in determining whether digital gold will break out. Success in 
addressing the four barriers will spur participation, while a failure to address them at meaningful scale could 
leave digital gold as a relative niche in the wider market. Digital gold presents both investors and providers with 
a significant opportunity. What must come next is collaboration to ensure the growing market meets the needs 
of all those who want to take part.

New money for gold.

not yet invested in gold. Among those who already hold
