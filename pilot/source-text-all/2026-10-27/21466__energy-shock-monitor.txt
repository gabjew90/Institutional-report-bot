Here's how we’re reading the latest activity and price data:
•
Global tanker imports moved broadly sideways last week (using a 28-day 
avg). The US and Asia ex China were largely unchanged near their pre-conflict 
level. China imports have started to drift upwards, but remain well below 
February levels.
•
US daily active vehicles are increasingly underperforming 2025 levels, 
weighed down by high gasoline and diesel prices. US flights are holding up 
relatively better, but globally flights are down 1.3%oya, led by weakness in 
Asia.
•
After correctly signaling a more modest gain in September consumer spending, 
our October tracking estimate from our Chase Card trackers suggests another 
soft month for nominal US consumer spending.
•
Brent resumed its ascent, rising 5%w/w and signaling a renewed leg higher in 
fuel prices. European natural gas prices continue to set new highs. TTF, 
along with Asian LNG, is up nearly threefold from its pre-war level.

Appendix
The J.P. Morgan Energy Shock Monitor provides timely tracking of the impact of the Middle East conflict on global activity and 
prices. Encompassing the full suite of the 70 economies we track, the Monitor is divided into three main sections:
1. Activity tracking: Daily data on the number of flights and vehicles on the road, as well as daily measures of US consumer 
spending
2. Price tracking: Daily / weekly changes in the price of crude oil, natural gas prices, and key petro-based product prices
3. Exposure metrics: Daily tanker imports and an overview of relative exposures
Sources and methodology
•
Daily flight numbers: Data sourced from Rystad Energy, which aggregates daily flights for a given airport. All types of 
aircraft are included and are not seasonally adjusted.
•
Daily active vehicles on road: Data sourced from Rystad Energy, which combines continuous daily road traffic from toll 
booths and highway cameras, congestion indices, CCTV images and government data. All types of vehicles are included and 
are not seasonally adjusted.
•
Daily tanker import volumes: Data sourced from IMF Portwatch and Haver Analytics. Data use satellite-captured signals of 
90 thousand ships worldwide. Tanker imports cover oil, gas, refined oil products, and chemicals. Data are not seasonally 
adjusted and subject to revisions.
•
Retail gasoline and diesel prices: Data sourced from both Bloomberg and Globalpetrolprices.com and are either daily or 
weekly, in local currency. Retail prices include taxes and fees.

Chase Card Tracker: JPMorgan Chase Company (JPMC) has aggregated and de-identified more than 25m select US 
proprietary JPMC card and debit and demand deposit account transactions used, with customer reported information subject to 
certain suppressions and segmented (e.g., industry, income and generational bands), and then indexed such information to 
demonstrate changes based on the prior time period (the JPMC Index File 2.0). The JPMC Index File 2.0 is prepared using

later, Gen X as birth years from 1965 to 1980, and Baby Boomers as birth years in 1964 or earlier. Card-not-present
