Chart of the Week – Impressive Nuclear Uprate Activity Supported by Hyperscaler Contracts and 
Government Loans
Though new nuclear build remains challenging, we’re impressed with recent nuclear uprate activity. The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) lists ~30 expected 
applications for nuclear power uprates through 2032, totaling ~2.4GW. Of our U.S. coverage, CEG-US (12), DUK-US (5), SO-US (4), and VST-US (4) lead the U.S. in expected 
applications, followed by PEG-US (2). Importantly, that data is as of a month ago, and in the last few weeks, nuclear PPA contracts with hyperscalers and government support have 
underpinned a surprising amount of additional nuclear uprate activity. CEG-US, the largest nuclear operator in the U.S., recently announced massive nuclear PPA deals signed with 
hyperscalers AMZN and GOOG, respectively (link and link to our recent reports). The AMZN deal includes a 20-year PPA covering 690MW of capacity at the Calvert Cliffs plant in MD, 
including 190MW of uprates to come online over 2030-2032, while two major deals with GOOG include 890MW of new nuclear capacity under a 20-year PPA plus another 15-year 
PPA covering 2.7GW across PJM. Also last week, the U.S. DOE announced a $4.2B financial commitment for modernization of ~4GW of baseload power and 433MW of new nuclear 
capacity at VST-US’s nuclear facilities in PA and OH (link). Finally, in September, SO-US announced 96MW of new nuclear capacity in GA in an agreement backed by GOOG and 
projected to enable $900m in benefits to customers (link). Though new nuclear build costs remain high, continued financial and commercial support from hyperscaler customers, 
demonstrated benefits to existing customers, further PJM regulatory clarity, and federal financial backing though DOE loans will improve the attractiveness of nuclear uprates as a 
solution.

Thoughts on Sentiment over the Past Week
• In the United States last week, utility stocks outperformed the S&P 500 and were among the best-performing of the 11 S&P sectors, the second consecutive week of relative 
outperformance following three consecutive weeks of lagging. Despite the recent rebound and a 5.0% gain quarter-to-date through October 9th (vs. a 2.1% pickup in the 
S&P 500 over that time), the group continues to lag the broader market by ~17% YTD and by ~13% since mid-year. In terms of investor interactions, discussions have 
increasingly centered on PA, data centers, and several high-profile company-specific announcements. Starting with Pennsylvania, investors continue to wrestle with PJM's 
market structure, regulatory uncertainty, and how policymakers will balance economic-development opportunities against affordability concerns, especially given some 
high-profile and unfavorable proposals from certain legislators. Following our recent meetings with both Pennsylvania PUC Chairman Stephen DeFrank and EXC-US 
management, we came away believing the regulatory framework remains broadly constructive, with stakeholders focused on pragmatic solutions rather than restricting 
growth outright. Nonetheless, affordability concerns, political rhetoric, and broader PJM uncertainties remain key areas of concern; we believe that we’re more bearish than 
most on PJM, but perhaps less concerned than most about PA regulatory/political risks. XEL-US filed on Friday 10/9/26 a rate stabilization plan in Minnesota that would 
freeze rates for two years in lieu of a planned general rate case application. The application seeks to allow XEL-US to keep earned Investment Tax Credits (ITCs) for ~$1.3B 
battery storage resources entering service during the 2027/2028 period. We view the application as a creative way to maintain earnings while being responsive to 
affordability concerns in the state. Next, BKH-US was very topical after the company finally announced its long-awaited contract for the 1.8GW data center opportunity in 
Cheyenne. We believe that the deal reached with Google validates the company's data-center strategy, materially improves its long-term earnings power, and enhances the 
credibility of future demand-driven growth opportunities, especially given robust community support. Similarly, we’re impressed by CEG-US’s two nuclear announcements 
related to data center deals with AMZN-US and GOOG-US. The contracts further expand the company’s leadership position with data centers among IPPs and reinforce the 
growing role of existing and uprated nuclear generation in meeting hyperscaler demand. For each of these data center announcements, we admit that we incorrectly 
expected a news flow drought until after midterm elections, making these updates seem like more positive proof points that demand for long-term nuclear and data-center 
contracts remains robust.
• Canadian equities increased 0.4% week over week. Utilities increased 0.8%, outperforming the broader market by 0.4%, while Canadian power producers increased 3.4%, 
outperforming by 3.0%. It was a busy week across the Canadian utility space, with several notable sector developments, including the announced combination of Emera and 
Canadian Utilities / ATCO. Meanwhile, Hydro One filed its 2028-2032 JRAP, outlining an approximately $37 billion five-year investment plan, with the filing coming in ahead 
of our expectations and reinforcing an attractive long-term growth outlook. Despite the positive fundamental developments, Canadian utilities significantly underperformed 
their U.S. peers during the week. Canadian power producers had a strong week, though they also sizably lagged their U.S. counterparts, with U.S. power names benefiting 
from several large power-related arrangements.
• In LatAm, the market rallied sharply over the past week following the first-round presidential election results, in which the opposition candidate emerged ahead of the 
incumbent. In parallel, congressional and senate elections resulted in a strong showing for center-right and right-wing candidates, increasing expectations that the next 
administration could benefit from broader legislative support for economic reforms. The improved political backdrop triggered a significant decline in future interest rates, 
providing a broad-based tailwind for utilities. As a result, the IEE (Electric Energy Index) advanced 10.7% during the week, outperforming the Ibovespa's 8.8% gain.
• From the trading desk. For the Utilities Select Sector (XLU-US), the number of shares outstanding increased by ~2% WoW. Nine of the 25 most shorted names in our sector 
had material increases, while five of the 25 most-shorted names had material decreases. Within our coverage, CEG-US, SO-US, VST-US, EXC-US, ED-US, WEC-US, CMS-US, 
TLN-US, and LNT-US had noteworthy increases in short interest, while none had noteworthy decreases in short interest.

CMS Energy Corporation (CMS-US, SO) – It’s the Same Old Song, but with the Same Great Meaning
• OUR TAKE: Positive. Following meetings with management, including new CFO Sri Maddipati, we remain bullish on CMS-US and believe that, 
while the story may lack fireworks and may face NT uncertainty, recent underperformance and a stellar track record of consistency create an 
attractive entry point that should generate strong LT returns for investors. Starting with earnings, CMS-US is surprised that investors appear to 
interpret the messaging as being different than it has been historically, as management is not intending to change the tone. The company 
continues to target the high end of the 6%-8% EPS CAGR range, or “7% or better.” An increase to the range seems extremely unlikely, though 
ongoing beats and “rebasing” seem probable to us. Moreover, the NorthStar restructuring shifts the growth mix toward the utility, improving the 
quality of earnings and LT visibility while reducing equity needs. On the political front, CMS-US downplayed the election risk, emphasizing MI as a 
purple state and the company’s history of working with both sides of the aisle while highlighting that the large load tariff already addresses much 
of the candidates’ concerns around data center issues. We remain bullish, especially at a ~5% P/E discount. (October 1, 2026; Link)
U.S. Independent Power Producers – Takeaways from a Wild Wednesday: Two Steps Forward, One (Big) Step Back
• OUR TAKE: Mixed. Below, we recap three meaningful IPP news items that hit over a 24-hour period. First, we view FERC’s forcing PJM to delay the 
RBP by five months as a material negative as it creates additional uncertainty and reinforces our view that PJM remains far from seeing policy 
clarity. With the delay, we believe that non-market-based solutions for capacity additions are incrementally more likely, and feel even more 
confident that ultimate capacity additions will be more material than market participants expect, weighing on LT capacity and energy prices. This 
reinforces our preference for VST-US and NRG-US over TLN-US and, to a lesser degree, CEG-US given the formers’ geographic skew toward 
ERCOT. Separately, we’re impressed with CEG-US announcing a nuclear PPA with AMZN in MD, including 190MW of uprates. Financial details are 
thin and might not be terribly material, but we tip our hats, especially during a period when we were not expecting deal flow. Lastly, we credit TLN-
US for a strong share repurchase announcement; though the FCF and leverage outlooks are generally in line, the buybacks are no doubt a bullish 
indicator. Overall, we remain bullish on IPPs and see the pullback as overdone, but expect sentiment to remain lackluster (link). (October 1, 2026; 
Link)
Constellation Energy Corporation (CEG-US, SO) – Back-to-Back Data Center Wins Catapult CEG to the Top Spot Among IPPs; We Remain 
Positive
• OUR TAKE: Positive. We’re highly impressed by CEG-US’s major nuclear deals with Google, including adding 890MW of new nuclear capacity 
under a 20-year PPA plus another 15-year PPA covering 2.7GW across PJM. We see four benefits. First, while pricing is not disclosed, it’s safe to 
assume that the PPAs will be comfortably above market pricing; we crudely estimate an uplift of as much as $2/sh of EPS (+~10%) across the 
~3.6GW deal. Second, the 890MW of uprates add incremental capacity, further contributing to earnings and cash flows. Third, adding 15–20-year 
contracts reduces market risk and improves visibility into growth. Lastly, combined with last week’s Amazon contract, the deal makes CEG-US the 
leading U.S. IPP by data center-contracted capacity, positioning CEG-US well for additional deals, and may further bolster goodwill with regulators 
and policymakers focused on affordability. Our estimates, which approximate consensus, are under review, though we see notable upside and 
look forward to updated guidance. We remain bullish on CEG-US as we’re drawn to the strong fundamentals and attractive risk-adjusted growth 
outlook; we’re hopeful that these two contracts will improve investor sentiment, which has been quite negative recently. (October 6, 2026; Link)

Black Hills Corporation (BKH-US, SO) – At Last, Google Has Come Along
• OUR TAKE: Extremely positive. The waiting game is finally over. We’re impressed by BKH-US announcing a game-changing contract with Google 
for the highly anticipated data center buildout in Cheyenne. The company will invest $1.8B in new gas generation over 2027-2029, with earnings 
beginning next year, and thanks to the unique regulatory setup in WY, the company will earn a (negotiated) return on 2.1GW of collocated third-
party contracted resources. Management estimates a net income contribution of $150m in 2030, a virtually unprecedented step-up from $300m 
in 2025A and representing a ~35% increase from our 2030E forecast (likely to be mitigated by additional equity dilution, pending the merger). 
This was in line with our/investor expectations of ~25%-50% EPS upside, though we argue that none of the upside was previously reflected in the 
stock price. It’s highly unclear how management will convert this upside into EPS guidance and a CAGR, or when (the NWE merger is on track to 
close by year-end, with the NWE team to run the combined company). Still, we expect a strong stock reaction, and, more importantly, believe that 
this landmark deal could pave the way for more. We remain bullish and applaud management for landing the jumbo jet. (October 7, 2026; Link)
North American Utilities and Power – Sentiment Seems Sour, but Funds Flows Feel Favorable, Especially in the U.S.
• OUR TAKE: Slightly positive. There’s no doubt utility stocks have been out of favor lately, with the XLU lagging the S&P 500 by >30% since the 
end of 1Q26, and by ~15% since mid-year. We remain bullish fundamentally, but are negative on NT sentiment: while demand-driven 
fundamentals are extremely attractive, higher rates, risk-on market sentiment, and election noise will likely continue to pressure utility stocks (link 
to our recent commentary). Despite the weak performance, or perhaps because of it, we note that funds have been flowing into the sector 
recently. U.S. utility ETFs have seen positive inflows in three of the past four months (total of +$2.3B), while Canadian utility ETFs have seen four 
consecutive positive months of funds flows (totaling C$1.1B). Neither is as strong as benchmark inflows, but we see the contrast between capital 
movement and stock price performance as encouraging. Additionally, there are likely cross-border shifts occurring: last week, Canada’s XUT 
recorded the largest weekly redemption since at least January 2020, while the U.S. XLU saw a sharp acceleration of inflows. Perhaps the increased 
investor attention, plus some IPP data center deals, may lead to a reversal of the recent share price declines. (October 8, 2026; Link)
Hydro One Limited (H-CA, SP) – Initial Take: JRAP 2.0 Targeting Higher Growth; Long Regulatory Review Process Begins
• OUR TAKE: Slight Positive. Hydro One filed its joint rate application (JRAP 2.0) covering the 2028-2032 period. JRAP 2.0 proposes a step-change 
in Hydro One's growth outlook, with the company seeking approval for a $27.5b capital plan over 2028-2032, including $16.7b of transmission 
and $10.9b of distribution investment, well above our expectations. The filing also includes several shareholder-friendly proposals, most notably a 
45% deemed equity ratio (vs. 40% currently) and a cumulative earnings-sharing mechanism assessed only once at the end of the rate term. While 
we expect the ultimate settlement and OEB decision to include concessions around capital spending, equity thickness and other framework 
parameters, the filing reinforces the scale of Hydro One's transmission-driven growth opportunity. Including major transmission projects outside 
JRAP, we estimate consolidated rate base growth could reach 15.4% through 2032 (13.2% ex-NCI), well above our current forecast (11.1% / 
8.3%). Read more in our previous note JRAP 2.0: More of the Same, but Growth Is Accelerating. (October 8, 2026; Link)

Northland Power Inc. (NPI-CA, SP) – Insights from the Road: Offshore Wind Projects Continue to be De-Risked
• OUR TAKE: Positive. We recently hosted members of Northland Power’s senior management for investor meetings, with discussions centered on: 
1) offshore wind project execution at Baltic and Hai Long, 2) the next wave of offshore wind growth opportunities, 3) battery storage as an 
emerging capital allocation priority, and 4) broader renewable power and gas infrastructure opportunities. Overall, we came away more 
constructive on the outlook. Management reiterated that both Baltic and Hai Long remain on track, supporting our view that execution risk 
continues to decline as the projects advance toward commissioning. We expect Baltic and Hai Long to drive a meaningful step-up in revenue and 
cash flow as the projects move through commissioning and enter commercial operation, providing an incremental catalyst for the shares. Beyond 
Baltic and Hai Long, we expect investor focus to increasingly shift toward Northland's longer-term growth runway across offshore wind, battery 
storage, renewables, and gas infrastructure opportunities. Northland is currently trading at 8.0x 2028E EV/EBITDA, below our target multiple of 
9.1x. (October 13, 2026; Link)

  Definition of Scotiabank, Global Banking and Markets Equity Research Ratings
Scotiabank Global Equity Research utilizes a three-tiered rating framework: “Sector Outperform”, “Sector Perform”, and “Sector 
Underperform”. Each Research Analyst assigns a rating that is relative to his or her coverage universe or an index identified by the Research 
Analyst that includes, but is not limited to, stocks covered by the Research Analyst.
The rating assigned to each security covered in this report is based on the Scotiabank Global Equity Research Analyst’s 12-month outlook on 
the security. Research Analysts may sometimes express in research reports shorter-term views on the securities that may impact the price of 
the security in a manner directly counter to the Research Analyst’s 12-month outlook. These shorter-term outlooks are based upon catalysts 
or events that may have a shorter-term impact on the market price of the equity securities discussed in research reports, including, without 
any limitation, inherent volatility of the markets. Any such shorter-term outlooks expressed in research reports are distinct from and do not 
affect the Research Analyst’s 12-month outlook and are clearly noted as such.
Ratings
 
 
 
Sector Outperform (SO)
The stock is expected to outperform the average 12-month total 
return of the analyst’s coverage universe or an index identified by 
the analyst that includes, but is not limited to, stocks covered by the 
analyst.
Sector Perform (SP)
The stock is expected to perform approximately in line with the 
average 12-month total return of the analyst’s coverage universe or 
an index identified by the analyst that includes, but is not limited to, 
stocks covered by the analyst.
Sector Underperform (SU)
The stock is expected to underperform the average 12-month total 
return of the analyst’s coverage universe or an index identified by 
the analyst that includes, but is not limited to, stocks covered by the 
analyst.
Other Ratings
Under Review – The rating has been temporarily placed under 
review, until sufficient information has been received and assessed 
by the analyst.
Risk Ranking
The Speculative risk ranking reflects exceptionally high financial 
and/or operational risk, exceptionally low predictability of 
financial results, and exceptionally high stock volatility. Research 
Management and the Supervisory Analyst jointly make the final 
determination of the Speculative risk ranking.
