The Commodities Feed: Brent builds
geopolitical premium as Gulf risks rise
Oil rallied yesterday as markets grow increasingly concerned about the
risk of a renewed escalation in tensions between the US and Iran
Energy – European gas storage draws start
Oil prices surged yesterday, with front-month ICE Brent settling 4% higher at just above
$113/bbl. The rally is being driven less by current fundamentals than by mounting supply
concerns. Markets are increasingly worried about renewed escalation in the Persian Gulf, a
scenario that could threaten oil flows that have so far remained remarkably resilient.
Reports suggest President Trump is considering fresh strikes on Iran, while the US is reinforcing
its military presence in the region with the deployment of a second aircraft carrier. Meanwhile,
there are reports that US troops have been evacuated from some bases in the Middle East.
Clearly, these developments don’t paint an optimistic picture. They call into question our long-
held view that the US midterm elections would bring de-escalation and normalisation in flows
and oil prices. If anything, re-escalation seems the most likely path. As a result, our
expectation that ICE Brent would average $80/bbl over 4Q26 is looking unattainable. Even if
tensions don't re-escalate, the mere possibility is likely to keep a sizeable geopolitical risk
premium embedded in prices.
European gas prices also remain extremely sensitive to developments in the Persian Gulf, with
LNG flows increasingly likely to be largely absent from the market for at least much of the
Northern Hemisphere winter. TTF rallied 3% yesterday, settling above EUR90/MWh, its highest
since December 2022. EU gas storage also appears to have peaked at just under 75% full, with

Metals — Gold falls as energy risks reignite inflation concerns
Gold prices failed to benefit from yesterday's equity sell-off and the significant fall in US
Treasury yields, with the 10-year yield falling back below 5%. Instead, gold prices came under
pressure. Spot prices fell 2.8% and are nearing $4,000/oz. The gold market appears more
focused on higher oil prices and what it could mean for inflation — and ultimately central bank
policy. With US inflation still above the central bank’s 2% target, higher energy prices could
intensify price pressures and strengthen the case for tighter monetary policy.
LME copper prices declined for a second consecutive session, falling more than 1.9%
yesterday, as rising exchange inventories and a selloff in AI-related stocks weighed on broader
risk sentiment. LME data showed copper inventories increased by 27,675 tonnes for a fourth
consecutive session to 272,950 tonnes as of yesterday, the highest level since 27 July 2026.
