Preview of October MPM and Outlook Report in view of Nikkei report
Yusuke Matsuo
Senior Market Economist
yusuke.matsuo@mizuho-sc.com
+81 3 6202 8798
In this report, we present a preview of the Bank of Japan’s 29–30 October Monetary Policy
Meeting and the accompanying Outlook Report.
BOJ may fine-tune Outlook Report in light of higher oil prices
We think the largest change in terms of economic activity and prices since the last Outlook
Report in July 2026 is the larger-than-expected rise in oil prices.
In the July Outlook Report, the BOJ made the following forecast: “Based, for example,
on developments in futures markets, Dubai crude oil prices are expected to decline
from around USD80 per barrel to around USD70 per barrel toward the end of the
projection period.” However, oil prices have recently remained above USD100, reflecting
the prolonged escalation of tensions in Iran. The Bank is therefore likely to revise its
forecasts accordingly.
On the other hand, currently available quantitative data on economic activity, prices, and
wages appear broadly consistent with the forecasts presented in the July Outlook Report.
There is persistent upward pressure on prices from the three factors identified by the BOJ
—the situation in the Middle East, AI-related demand, and exchange rates—but apart
from the increase in oil prices noted above, we see little evidence that this pressure has
intensified materially since July.
Taking all these factors together, we expect the BOJ to raise its FY2027 inflation forecast
slightly in the upcoming Outlook Report to reflect higher oil prices, while making only minor
revisions overall (Chart 1). Reuters reported on 20 October that the Bank is unlikely to
incorporate the impact of the reduced consumption tax rate on food and beverages, as the
bill has been approved by the cabinet but has yet to be enacted. As such, our forecasts
assume the BOJ will disregard the impact of the tax cut for now.

We expect the BOJ to leave its risk assessments unchanged for both economic 
activity, where risks are “generally balanced,” and prices, where they are “skewed to 
the upside.” 
BOJ likely to leave policy on hold in October despite underlying 
inflation having reached 2% 
No single indicator can precisely capture underlying inflation in its conceptual sense. 
However, many of the indicators useful for assessing it—including the BOJ’s 
measures of underlying CPI inflation, trend inflation, and inflation expectations—have 
been running close to 2%. 
The BOJ appears to have been in a position to judge that underlying inflation had 
reached 2% at any time, as hawkish members Naoki Tamura and Hajime Takata 
have already done. But based on recent comments from Policy Board members and 
media reports, we think it is likely to revise its assessment at the October MPM and in 
the accompanying Outlook Report, declaring that underlying inflation has reached 
2%, whereas before it was only “approaching” that level. There are reports that the 
Bank will signal that underlying inflation has reached the target by describing it as 
being at a level “consistent with” the 2% objective (Nikkei, 22 October). 
Even if the BOJ concludes that underlying inflation has reached 2%, it is likely to 
maintain that the target has not yet been achieved in the “sustainable and stable 
manner” specified in the official statement. The Bank appears likely to take a little 
longer to assess how firmly inflation has become anchored, including the risks of an 
overshoot or undershoot. And with the policy rate now within the estimated neutral 
range of around 1.1–2.5%, it is highly likely to avoid back-to-back hikes, which would 
give it almost no time to assess the effects of previous increases. We therefore 
expect the BOJ to leave the policy rate on hold at 1.25% at the October meeting. Few 
Bank officials appear to see a risk of inflation rising substantially above target, and 
one senior official reportedly said that “consecutive rate hikes would be inconceivable 
unless there were a serious emergency” (Nikkei, 22 October).

We expect hawkish press conference from Mr. Ueda, but... 
At the press conference following the September MPM, Governor Kazuo Ueda 
explained that the “policy phase has changed.” The Bank had previously focused on 
raising underlying inflation because it was well below 2%. But now that it is 
approaching 2%, the BOJ must also guard against upside risks. We think Mr. Ueda’s 
emphasis on this “change in phase” when the Bank accelerated the pace of rate 
hikes was meant to signal that it was considering hiking at a faster pace than the 
previous interval of roughly six months. 
If the BOJ revises its assessment to conclude that underlying inflation has reached 
2%, we expect Mr. Ueda to place even greater emphasis than in September on the 
importance of responding to this phase shift. The press conference is therefore likely 
to have a hawkish tone overall. However, we do not expect him to pre-commit to a 
specific path for the policy rate. His remarks could be interpreted as being dovish by 
a market that tends to seek clear guidance on the timing and path of future rate hikes, 
leaving a risk of renewed yen weakness against the dollar. 
We expect next rate hike in December, with terminal rate of 2% 
We expect the BOJ to continue raising rates toward a neutral level at a faster pace 
than the previous interval of roughly six months. This would allow it to reduce as 
quickly as possible the degree of monetary accommodation, which continues to 
contribute to inflation. We expect the Bank to declare at the October MPM that 
underlying inflation has reached 2%. As this will strengthen the incentive to move the 
policy rate closer to neutral sooner, we project the next hike will come at the 
December 2026 meeting. 
We expect the BOJ’s preferred measure of core inflation (ex special factors) to peak 
in 2H 2027 or beyond, reducing the need for further rate hikes. There is a substantial 
divergence of views between the reflationist and mainstream camps on the Policy 
Board, but the former group currently consists of only two members, Toichiro Asada 
and Ayano Sato. We therefore expect them to have a limited impact on near-term 
policy decisions. The two hawkish members, Mr. Takata and Mr. Tamura, are due to 
retire after the July 2027 meeting, and the resulting change in the composition of the 
Policy Board is likely to raise the hurdle for further rate hikes. 
We expect the BOJ to raise rates again at the March and June 2027 meetings, 
maintaining a pace of one hike every two meetings. We forecast a terminal rate of 
2.00%. However, since that will depend in part on the Bank’s assessment of the 
neutral rate, which takes into account indicators of financial conditions, the tightening 
cycle could end earlier than we expect. 
For now, the BOJ appears to be accelerating the pace of rate hikes only because it 
believes the policy rate should approach neutral sooner. A faster pace of tightening 
does not necessarily imply a higher terminal rate. Unless estimates of the neutral rate 
are revised higher or a case emerges for restrictive monetary policy, we do not 
expect the terminal rate to continue moving steadily higher.
